Anthropic’s IPO countdown runs straight into an AI sell-off

Anthropic has publicly filed its S-1 for a Nasdaq listing and is aiming to price as early as 13 November, with a first day of trading possible in the week of 16 November, according to Wall Street Journal and Reuters reports cited by Yahoo Finance. The filing lands in the middle of an AI stock sell-off that followed the US midterms, and Reuters reported that some potential investors warn a wider AI backlash could threaten the timetable. Kalshi odds on an IPO announcement fell after the vote.

The S-1 starts a fixed sequence: an investor roadshow, a price, and a final regulatory green light. Yahoo Finance puts the earliest date a price can be set at 13 November and the earliest possible debut at 16 November, with no official date confirmed. Caixin Global framed the listing as a test of AI’s growth story against its costs, while CNBC reported that prediction-market odds on the IPO slipped as the sell-off deepened. For enterprise buyers, the public filing is the first audited look at how much of Anthropic’s revenue comes from business customers rather than consumer chat — the number that will decide how the rest of the lab sector is valued.

GPT-6 Cyber finds 81 unknown Firefox flaws and writes exploits for 948 Linux kernel bugs

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a security-specialised model that discovered 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities and produced working local privilege-escalation exploits for 948 distinct Linux kernel flaws. BleepingComputer reports the model identified 2,308 candidate kernel bugs and successfully reproduced 1,724 of them. Many of the Mozilla patches have already shipped. Access is gated behind an approval process.

VentureBeat notes the published rate card — $20 per million input tokens and $100 per million output tokens, with cached input at $2 — sits above the comparable cyber models from Anthropic and Google. The pitch is defensive: find and validate flaws faster than human triage, then help write the fix. The dual-use problem is obvious, which is why OpenAI is restricting access rather than opening it to self-serve signups, and why the kernel work stopped at local privilege escalation in a controlled research setting.

Coforge signs on to sell Grok delivery to enterprises as a SpaceX partner

Coforge said on Friday it has joined the SpaceX Super Intelligence Partner Network as a “Transformation Partner”, building industry-specific Grok solutions and deploying them inside clients’ existing systems and workflows. Delivery runs through Momentuum Blue, Coforge’s customer-facing engineering unit, which embeds engineers and AI agents in client organisations. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The Grok portfolio covered by the arrangement spans chat, research, analysis, software development, conversational services, and image and video generation. It is the latest sign that Indian IT services firms are positioning themselves as the integration layer for whichever frontier lab wins a client.

India

Tamil Nadu and IIT Madras set up the machinery for a state AI data centre

ELCOT and the IITM Pravartak Technologies Foundation signed an MoU on Thursday to create a Project Monitoring Unit overseeing a Tamil Nadu government AI data centre. The PMU will evaluate the existing State Data Centre, prepare government applications to be “AI-ready” and plan their migration to a new facility built for AI workloads. The agreement was signed at the Secretariat in the presence of state AI and IT minister R. Kumar.

The unit is a planning body rather than an operator, which is the part worth watching: most state AI announcements stop at a memorandum, while this one assigns someone to inventory legacy applications and decide what actually moves. Tamil Nadu already runs one of the country’s more detailed state AI policies, and IIT Madras has been the state’s default technical partner on compute and skilling. If the migration plan holds, it becomes a template for other states trying to host citizen-facing models on their own infrastructure instead of renting foreign cloud.

IIT Delhi announced a four-year initiative at its Yardi School of Artificial Intelligence with Walmart Global Tech, valued at approximately ₹9 crore, of which about ₹2.3 crore is earmarked for the current financial year. The teacher-training component is the less glamorous half and arguably the more consequential, since it multiplies capability beyond a single cohort of fellows.

Policy

UK ministers seek powers to police what happens when AI agents are let off the leash

An amendment to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, introduced during the bill’s third reading in the House of Lords, would give UK ministers powers to regulate the deployment and reporting of agentic AI in certain organisations. Essential service operators and digital providers would have to guard against losing control of AI agents and report incidents when safeguards fail. The clause is not yet law.

The framing is deliberately narrow: this is resilience legislation, not an AI act, and it targets the operational risk of an agent that keeps acting after a human has stopped watching. That is the failure mode regulators keep circling — not a model saying something wrong, but a system with credentials and a queue taking irreversible actions. The Register reports the powers would be enabling rather than immediate, which means the detail will be fought over in secondary legislation. Expect UK-regulated firms to start writing agent kill-switches and audit logs into procurement terms now.

South Korea approves a single legislative framework for AI and seven task forces

South Korea’s National AI Strategy Committee, at its third plenary meeting in Seoul on 6 November, approved a framework for AI legislation across government ministries, a plan to run task forces on seven key initiatives, and a government-wide data pipeline strategy. The aim is to unify legislative direction rather than let each ministry write its own rules, and to open public data held across institutions to AI systems.

MoneyToday reports the three agenda items were deliberated and approved together, which matters because Korean AI rules have so far arrived ministry by ministry. Chosun Biz reported separately that the committee is forming a “superintelligence” task force with ten priorities due by June. MLex characterised the meeting as setting legislative priorities and staffing the hard problems. The data-sharing pillar is the practical one: public-sector data is where most national AI programmes stall, and a cross-government pipeline is the unglamorous prerequisite.

METR finds Anthropic’s AI R&D running at roughly twice last year’s pace — and most of the evidence redacted

An independent assessment by METR, released on Thursday, concluded that Anthropic is developing its AI systems around twice as quickly as a year ago, based on internal data and staff interviews the company opened to the reviewers. Anthropic, which invited the review ahead of its listing, redacted what METR called “a majority of the evidence we found most informative” for its conclusions. The Associated Press notes the finding sits awkwardly with the CEO’s public calls to slow the pace of AI development.

METR’s transparency terms let it disclose that redaction happened and whether its conclusions depended on the withheld material; it says the highest-level risk conclusions could be shared in full. Anthropic described the redactions as “aimed at removing confidential information that could accelerate other AI developers.” The episode is a preview of a harder problem than benchmarking: outside evaluators are being asked to certify safety claims for companies whose competitive interest is in not showing the workings. METR’s report is careful to say the ~2x figure is an acceleration estimate, not a claim that recursive self-improvement has begun.

Research

OpenAI’s new benchmark scores agents on messy lab data, not tidy paper figures

OpenAI and biotech company Ligo released BioBasicsBench, a benchmark of 100 routine wet-lab tasks that measures whether agents can interpret raw experimental data. The tasks are built around the conditions that actually occur at the bench: failed experiments, ambiguous images and conflicting observations. OpenAI argues that existing benchmarks, drawn from figures selected for publication, miss the ambiguity and troubleshooting that lab work requires.

The framing is that agents will not speed up biology by reading papers better; they will do it by being useful on the unglamorous middle of the process, where a gel looks wrong and nobody knows why. That requires reading evidence rather than reproducing conclusions. Building the tasks with an enzyme-design company rather than an academic group is a deliberate choice — it keeps the benchmark close to industrial workflows where an agent’s error has a cost. OpenAI has not published headline scores for frontier models in the announcement, so the useful comparison will come when independent groups run it.

Frameworks

Zyphra open-sources FP8 attention kernels that make AMD’s MI355X noticeably less awkward

Zyphra released ZYFA, a set of FP8 and BF16 prefill attention kernels for AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs, under Apache 2.0. On real model activations the FP8 kernels run 1.16–1.76x faster than AITER, AMD’s own kernel library, with 62–78% lower FP8 error at head dimension 128. It drops into existing code through the same flash_attn_func interface, on ROCm 7.2.3 and PyTorch 2.12.

The diagnosis is the interesting part: FP8 attention on MI355X is softmax-bound, not matrix-bound, because the softmax runs on the vector unit at the same speed in either precision. Zyphra’s “Lean P” replaces the exponential with one multiply-add and an integer conversion, cutting softmax vector cycles per block from 896 to 384, then overlaps matrix and vector work with a two-wave ping-pong schedule. At 32K tokens the kernel reaches 2,672 TFLOP/s FP8 — 53% of datasheet peak against AITER’s 39%. The honest caveats: prefill only, no decode or paged KV, MI355X only, and gains on real models are smaller than on synthetic benchmarks because AITER’s sparser probabilities let the GPU clock higher.

Source: MarkTechPost, 6 Nov 2026

RAG

Pinecone argues retrieval’s next limit is authority, not similarity

In a webinar recapped on 6 November, Pinecone VP of engineering Jörg Schad described what he calls the design-doc problem: the two nearest neighbours to a query may be a three-year-old design document and a scan of this morning’s codebase, and a similarity score cannot tell you which one describes the system that runs today. Recall@K can look healthy while retrieval returns a policy that has been superseded, or a document the caller is not permitted to see. Pinecone’s answer is a context layer above retrieval that resolves authority, freshness and permissions.

The talk walks the lineage — inverted indexes and BM25, then word2vec, HNSW and BERT for semantic match, then RAG as grounding at query time — and argues each era fixed the previous era’s limit while inheriting a new blind spot. RAG’s blind spot is that grounding is unchecked: the model gets whatever comes back, with no step verifying that it is the right result or that the model actually used it. For teams building agents over enterprise corpora, the practical implication is that adding a better embedding model will not fix a ranking that ignores recency and access control. Pinecone is, of course, selling the next layer; the critique is still the sharpest framing of the problem published this week.

Microsoft puts Kling, LTX and MiniMax video models inside Foundry through fal

Microsoft and fal are rolling Kling, LTX and MiniMax video models into Microsoft Foundry in public preview as a first-party Azure service, covering text-to-video and image-to-video. fal’s platform exposes APIs for more than 1,500 generative media models across image, video, audio, 3D and generative worlds. The pitch is picking a different model for each step of a workflow rather than standardising on one.

The reasoning Microsoft gives is that video workloads do not start from the same input or aim at the same output: a creative team wants several interpretations of a brief, a retailer wants an approved product image turned into motion, an application developer wants description to frame to scene. Those need different trade-offs on quality, motion, control, turnaround and cost. What changes with this announcement is procurement, not capability — generative video becomes something an enterprise can buy on an existing Azure agreement with the data-handling terms already negotiated.

Vision

Kling’s new model family argues video generation is now bottlenecked on meaning, not pixels

Kuaishou’s Kling team released Kling-MLLM on 4 November in three variants — 27B dense, 35B-A3B MoE and 397B-A17B MoE — all initialised from Qwen3.5 to Qwen3.8 checkpoints. The accompanying technical report argues that the constraint in short-form video generation has moved upstream from rendering quality to semantic understanding. The models are designed to sit above a generator, handling data, conditioning, alignment and evaluation.

The report’s central artefact is a “semantic contract” for captions, built from six families: subject and action, space and composition, cinematography and light, time and shots, visible and audible text, and aesthetics. Each family is tied to a named downstream failure mode — drop the cinematography fields and you get camera behaviour that looks appealing but cannot be reproduced. Reference-grounded captioning then turns video pairs into relational supervision data. It is a useful corrective to the assumption that better video means more compute on the renderer.

Source: Kuaishou Kling technical report, 4 Nov 2026

Perplexity ships always-on “Teammates” for work, priced at the top of the market

Perplexity launched Teammates, always-on agents that take on multiple roles inside a workspace and pull projects, skills and memory from the Perplexity tools a user already has. They build on Perplexity Computer, which deploys agents on a user’s device, and are aimed at ongoing, large-scale work rather than one-off tasks. The Verge reports the feature requires the $200-a-month Max plan, making it more expensive than comparable offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic.

Teammates puts Perplexity into the same category as OpenAI’s Dots and Claude’s Duet: a persistent worker that holds context across sessions instead of a chat window you return to. The differentiation claimed is collaboration — assigning different jobs to different agents that run simultaneously. The pricing is the story for teams evaluating this seriously: at $200 a seat per month, the agent has to replace a meaningful slice of coordination work, not just speed up drafting. Perplexity also shipped a fully local Computer on Mac and a voice mode in the same release.

Source: The Verge, 5 Nov 2026
