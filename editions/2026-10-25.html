★ Top Story India

India moves to map ‘AI-proof’ sectors as Goyal scales the sovereign AI push

India’s commerce and industry ministry is working to identify sectors that are ‘AI-proof’, commerce minister Piyush Goyal said, as concerns grow about the technology’s effect on jobs. The remark lands alongside the government’s ₹10,372-crore IndiaAI Mission, which is contracting GPU compute for domestic model builders and backing 20 indigenous foundation-model projects. NITI Aayog has warned that routine technology-services roles risk displacement by 2031, while projecting that up to 4 million new AI-era jobs could be created over five years.

Speaking on an official visit to Germany and the Netherlands, Goyal said the government would also write to Indian embassies and high commissions to push greater participation in global AI, startup and semiconductor events. The IndiaAI Mission has already cleared support for foundation-model efforts including Sarvam AI, Gnani.AI, BharatGen and Avataar AI, and MeitY is contracting high-performance GPUs to subsidise compute for startups, researchers and public institutions. The framing is political as much as industrial: New Delhi wants visible action on AI-led job disruption before it becomes a campaign issue. NITI Aayog’s 10 October roadmap argued that with “the right skilling, reskilling and innovation pathways”, India could become a hub for AI-first roles such as ethical-AI specialists, AI trainers and AI DevOps engineers.

Telangana opens an applied-AI and green-skills centre with Microsoft and MeitY

The Telangana government, Microsoft, the 1M1B Foundation and MeitY Startup Hub have opened the Telangana Green Skills & Applied AI Centre of Excellence in Hyderabad. Inaugurated by IT and industries minister D Sridhar Babu, it will train science and technology graduates in applied AI, green pharmaceuticals, solar, EV and wind energy, and link them to mentors, internships and jobs. At launch, 1M1B’s Jobs Express announced 100 job offers and eight MoUs were signed with companies and labs.

The Hyderabad centre is part of a planned five-centre national network targeting 100,000 young people by 2030. For India’s startup ecosystem, the pitch is talent supply: applied-AI training tied to industry mentors and placements could ease the hiring squeeze for AI and climate-tech ventures. Launch events included an ‘AI Talkies’ session with young changemakers and an #AIForMyCity Challenge inviting AI-powered fixes for civic and environmental problems.

AI startups top India’s weekly funding charts with $93.9 million across six deals

Indian startups raised just over $184 million across 17 deals in the week to 23 October, down about 22% on the previous week, according to Inc42. AI was the best-funded sector, pulling in $93.9 million across six deals, led by martech startup Different’s $48 million seed round. AuxoAI raised $20.7 million in a Series A led by NTTVC, while Hunar, Vectorial, Karta AI and Firstwork also closed rounds.

The AI total comes as headline startup funding cools: deal count fell about 32% week on week even as AI’s share of the pie grew. Venture-debt firm Trifecta Capital and early-stage investor Blume Ventures were the most active backers, each funding two companies. The pattern mirrors a broader shift in Indian venture capital toward AI application-layer companies with near-term enterprise revenue rather than capital-heavy model training.

Global

Moonshot’s Kimi K3.1 Pro puts an open-weight model within striking distance of the frontier

Chinese AI startup Moonshot AI has unveiled Kimi K3.1 Pro, which scores 55 on the independent Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — fourth overall and just behind Claude Opus 5.5 (58), Claude Sonnet 5.5 (56) and Meta’s Muse Wonder Full (56). OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and Google’s Gemini 4 Argon sit at 53. Moonshot says it will publish the model’s weights by early November, which would make it the strongest open-weight model available.

The 11-point jump over its predecessor, Kimi K3 (44), narrows the gap between the best open and the best proprietary model from 12 points to about three, per Artificial Analysis. Among open models, Xiaomi’s MiMo-V2.6-Pro currently leads at 46, ahead of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (45) and Kimi K3 (44); the best US open model, Meta’s Muse Spark Open, scores 39. API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with a ‘Highspeed’ variant that Moonshot says is two to three times faster at double the price. Caveat: the index scores are independent, but the model’s own launch claims are not yet independently benchmarked.

Research

An RL-tuned 8B model beats a 400B model at choosing the right tool

Researchers from Meta and Carnegie Mellon University report that a Qwen3-8B model, post-trained with reinforcement learning, picks the correct tool 41.5% of the time on the ToolBench G3 benchmark — ahead of Meta’s own roughly 402-billion-parameter Llama-4-Maverick at 30.6%. Even the 1.7B variant cleared the larger model, at 37.6%. The work appears in an arXiv paper, “Training Small Language Models for Transferable Tool Selection”.

The method, called Plan-Retrieve-Select, has the model first work out what it needs, retrieve a shortlist from a corpus exceeding 10,000 tools, then choose one. Training combined supervised fine-tuning with GRPO-based reinforcement learning over about 21,000 ToolBench G3 instructions. The practical upshot is cost: running a 400B model for every tool call is expensive and slow, while an 8B model is far cheaper to host. The caveats matter — results come from a single benchmark family that also supplied the training data, and a 41.5% success rate still means the best model misses more often than it hits.

Source: arXiv via CryptoBriefing, 23 October 2026

Research

DeepSeek’s Engram pairs neural compute with a hashed memory table

A DeepSeek-AI and Peking University paper introduces Engram (also described as Deep Sparse Embedding), a memory module that uses hashed N-gram lookups for static patterns alongside a model’s usual computation. In the authors’ tests, a 26.7-billion-parameter model using it outperformed a matched mixture-of-experts baseline, with reported gains of about 5 points on BBH, 3 on HumanEval, 3.7 on ARC-Challenge and 2.4 on MATH. A 100-billion-parameter memory table added under 3% inference-throughput overhead when held in host memory.

In the headline comparison, the team reallocated parameters from 72 routed experts down to 55 and added a 5.7B memory module, keeping the total at 26.7B. For long-context work, the paper reports 97.0 versus 84.2 on a Multi-Query NIAH test in a comparison matched on pre-training loss. The results are the authors’ own, and the paper notes that behaviour after supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning remains underexplored — worth remembering before treating the memory as a drop-in database.

Source: DeepSeek-AI via dstld.news, 24 October 2026

Policy

US Senate moves on two fronts: a governance framework and federal cyber-safety bills

Senate Commerce ranking member Maria Cantwell has released a framework for “comprehensive AI governance” built on six principles, including enforceable frontier-AI standards crafted by NIST, “continuous testing, reviewing and auditing”, and “transparency and accountability”. Separately, new Senate AI bills would set up federal safety mechanisms for AI-related cyber threats. Together the moves signal Congress returning to AI legislation after months of stalled efforts.

Cantwell’s principles are pitched as the guide for any comprehensive AI bill, and stakeholders had been watching for her position amid concerns about a separate, high-profile governance effort. The emphasis on NIST-written enforceable standards and continuous audit is notable: it favours a standards-led regime over purely voluntary commitments. None of this is law yet, and the bills’ scope, funding and enforcement teeth remain to be worked out in committee.

Shanghai AI Lab publishes Atria Dawn, a 744B open-weights agent model under MIT

Shanghai AI Laboratory has released Atria Dawn on Hugging Face, a 744-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts agent model built on GLM-5.2, licensed MIT and carrying a 512K-token context window. Its model card claims wins on CyberGym, WideSearch and AutomationBench, and clear losses on SWE-bench Pro. It lands days after the lab’s smaller Sage-A1.5, a 35B Apache-2.0 agentic model.

The MIT licence and open weights matter for teams weighing self-hosting: they permit commercial use with minimal restriction, and the 512K context is aimed at long-horizon agent tasks. The mixed benchmark picture — strong on some agentic suites, weak on software-engineering tasks — is a reminder to test on your own workloads rather than trusting a single card. Shanghai AI Lab is emerging as a steady publisher of large open agent models, a pattern worth tracking alongside Meta and Mistral.

Frameworks

Microsoft’s Agent Framework lays groundwork for MCP 2.x

Microsoft released version 1.23.0 of its Agent Framework for Python, adding a compatibility foundation for MCP 2.x as a breaking change and migrating MCP transports, errors, schemas and lifecycle handling. The update also forwards allowlisted function-invocation and client arguments from A2A request metadata. A third, opt-in feature adds a synthetic trailing user turn for providers that return empty text after an assistant message.

The Model Context Protocol is the standard agents use to reach tools and data, and the A2A protocol handles agent-to-agent messaging, so the release is plumbing that enterprise teams will feel at integration time. The breaking nature of the MCP migration — touching core, declarative, hosting-mcp and AG-UI packages — means existing deployments should test before upgrading. Microsoft positions the framework as the successor to Semantic Kernel and AutoGen.

RAG

Qdrant 1.20 folds BM25 keyword scoring into the vector database

Qdrant’s v1.20 release adds native BM25 scoring inside payload text indexes, letting the engine compute keyword relevance during the index scan and blend it with dense vector scores in a single query. It also introduces TurboQuant vector types (turbo8 and turbo16) that shrink vector storage by roughly 60–75%, and a code-aware tokenizer for source-code retrieval.

Hybrid search — dense embeddings plus exact keyword matching — has become the default for production RAG, but it usually meant running a vector store alongside a separate full-text engine and fusing results at the application layer. Qdrant’s move removes that second service for teams that only needed BM25, cutting network hops and cross-engine sync risk. The new code tokenizer targets agentic coding tools, preserving identifiers like camelCase names and namespace operators that ordinary tokenizers split. Recall and cost still need measuring on your own data.

Source: Qdrant via DEV Community, 24 October 2026

Vision

Tencent open-sources Hy-Nomas, an interactive video world model you can steer mid-scene

Tencent’s Hunyuan team has released Hy-Nomas under Apache 2.0, a video world model that builds a scene segment by segment and accepts new text instructions or camera paths between segments. It outputs 720p at 24 fps in eight denoising steps and can run on a single RTX 4090 in a quantised configuration. Weights, training code, LoRA adapters and a web demo are public, along with Hy-Nomas-Bench, a 280-case benchmark.

The architecture combines a segment-autoregressive diffusion transformer with a Qwen2.5-VL text encoder, camera-conditioning modules and a PixelUMM video decoder, using a persistent KV cache as memory so each new segment attends to everything generated so far. Tencent has published no benchmark scores and lists the technical report as coming soon, so quality claims are unverified — a notable gap for a model competing with closed video tools. The single-GPU mode is the most interesting part for researchers, since it lowers the hardware bar for world-model experimentation.

Kling 4.0 generates 30-second clips with stereo audio and 10-language lip sync

Kuaishou’s Kling 4.0 has launched with up to 30-second single-pass clips, Omni Reference (up to 15 multimodal references and 10 keyframes), stereo audio and lip sync in more than ten languages. A cheaper Flash tier is limited to 720p and 20 seconds. The model is being distributed through partners including Runway, fal, Higgsfield, Adobe Firefly and ElevenLabs.

The pitch is professional-grade control: stable subject motion during fast action, complex camera moves, and targeted edits such as swapping a character or restyling a background in existing footage. The catch is that the broadcast specs used to announce it — 4K 10-bit HDR and a developer API — are still listed as coming soon, and launch material has been inconsistent on capabilities. Kling 4.0 is also unavailable to ElevenLabs users in the United States.

Vmake Labs ships a Halloween social-video toolkit for small businesses

Vmake Labs has launched an all-in-one Halloween social video toolkit aimed at small and medium businesses, e-commerce sellers and social teams. It bundles seasonal short-form video templates, UGC-style product formats and AI video and image generation so brands can turn existing product assets into campaign content. The workflow runs inside the company’s Vmake Labs Workspace.

The tool is a template-first play: instead of scripting, filming and editing a seasonal concept from scratch, teams drop their product into a structure already tuned for short-form platforms, then refine output with the company’s watermark remover and video enhancer. It reflects a broader pattern in AI marketing tools — packaging generation behind ready-made, platform-native formats rather than exposing raw models. As with any template pipeline, brand fit and output quality still need a manual review pass.
