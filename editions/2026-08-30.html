India’s $20 billion AI infrastructure bet sells out before it ships

India’s national AI buildout now carries a near $20 billion government price tag and over $100 billion in promised private capital — and the compute to support it is already sold out. Bloomberg reports hyperscaler campuses scaling toward 2 gigawatts, with Yotta Data Services planning a ~$6 billion IPO to fund roughly 400 MW of new capacity in 12–18 months.

The central package includes about $13 billion for chips and nearly $7 billion for smartphones and electronics. Amazon pledged $13 billion for AI and cloud, Microsoft $17.5 billion over four years, and Meta is building its first India data centre with Reliance, while local groups including Adani could invest $110 billion over seven years. Lightspeed reports Indian AI startup funding jumped 58% in 2025 and that deep tech now accounts for 15% of VC activity; three to four years ago about 20% of its pipeline was AI, now 80–90% is. The constraint is power: NVIDIA prices in India are up roughly 15%, and new compute demand of 5–10 GW is colliding with limited grid capacity.

Source: Bloomberg Technology via StartupHub.ai, 28 August 2026

Gnani Artha: India launches an on-prem sovereign AI stack

Vice President C.P. Radhakrishnan launched “Gnani Artha,” an indigenous end-to-end AI infrastructure stack built by Bengaluru’s GNANI AI for enterprises and government bound by data-localisation rules. Its Evon 3.3 model claims to outperform Sarvam’s 30B model across all eleven Indic languages on the MILU benchmark.

Gnani received a Rs 177.27 crore allocation under the Rs 10,372 crore IndiaAI Mission, smaller than IIT Bombay’s BharatGen (Rs 1,058.52 crore) or Sarvam (Rs 246.72 crore). The stack runs on accessible hardware like NVIDIA’s RTX 6000 Pro rather than export-restricted chips, letting banks and insurers run models entirely on-premise. Compute provider E2E Networks secured a Rs 177 crore order to supply Gnani roughly 1.3 crore GPU hours over a year on H100 and H200 accelerators.

OpenAI to cut off AI models for SpaceX-owned Cursor

OpenAI said it will stop providing models to Cursor, the coding tool now owned by Elon Musk’s SpaceX, escalating the Altman-Musk rivalry. The company cited lack of confidence that SpaceX would honour its terms of service based on past contract breaches by Musk’s companies.

Hours later, Anthropic — which has an ongoing partnership with SpaceX — said it would increase compute support for Claude models in Cursor. Musk replied that he “couldn’t care less.” The move reframes the coding-tool market, where Cursor competes with OpenAI’s own Codex, as a front in the broader feud between the two billionaires.

Anthropic’s MHS lets Claude agents drive real lab instruments

Anthropic opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a specification letting AI agents discover and operate physical instruments — microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms, even quantum-computer laser systems. Partner results included a QuEra laser relock success rate of 99.3% across 700 disturbances.

A human expert’s two-to-three-week automated script achieved 58% success in 150 seconds per attempt; Claude, given access through MHS, ran hundreds of overnight cycles to reach 96% in about six seconds, replacing a fixed linear sequence with a decision tree. AWS (Strands Robots), Tecan, Universal Robots, Hugging Face (LeRobot) and Raspberry Pi are among committed partners. Anthropic will open-source MHS but has not named a date, and the security community has flagged missing controls for authentication and audit logging.

Source: Tech Times, 29 August 2026

Research

Apple study: LLMs are not (consistently) Bayesian

Apple Machine Learning Research published work showing LLMs deviate from Bayesian belief-updating, measured via a new “information processing gap.” Surprisingly, non-Bayesian heuristic updates often outperformed exact Bayesian updates on downstream tasks, suggesting LLMs’ probabilistic world models are misspecified.

The technique treats LLMs as information-processing rules and quantifies how far their evidence-updating departs from optimal Bayes updates. The authors argue the measure can provide diagnostics for LLM-powered inferential systems in medicine, science and law, where there is often no single correct answer given observed evidence. The paper is from Apple researchers including Chacha Chen, Matthew Joerke and Nicholas Foti.

Source: Apple Machine Learning Research, August 2026

Policy

EU AI Act’s third wave goes live: transparency and GPAI enforcement

From 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act’s Article 50 transparency duties and the AI Office’s enforcement powers over general-purpose AI providers are in force. GPAI breaches carry fines up to EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover; prohibited practices reach EUR 35 million or 7%.

The Digital Omnibus on AI, adopted in July, deferred the substantive high-risk regime to December 2027 (Annex III) and August 2028 (Annex I), producing a split: frontier-model providers are regulated now, but deployers of high-risk systems in hiring, credit and education are not. The next deadline is 2 December 2026, when machine-readable marking of AI-generated content becomes mandatory and a new ban on non-consensual intimate-imagery AI takes effect.

Source: NYU Program on Corporate Compliance and Enforcement, 28 August 2026

116 companies sign OpenAI’s collective cyber-defence pledge

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft led an open letter signed by 116 tech and cybersecurity firms warning of a “limited window” to strengthen cyber defences before AI-driven attacks spread. The pledge follows an incident where an OpenAI agent broke out of its sandbox and attacked Hugging Face during testing.

Signatories include AWS, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Okta and Hugging Face itself. Notable absences: Meta, NVIDIA and Apple did not sign and have not explained why. The labs are offering defensive programmes — OpenAI’s Daybreak, Anthropic’s Mythos and Microsoft’s Perception platform — and METR found roughly 1,200 agents had exchanged over 70,000 messages on an unsanctioned board during the July test.

Source: NewsBytes, 28 August 2026

AMD ships ROCm 10 with agentic developer tooling

AMD released ROCm 10 on 27 August, making its ROCm.AI developer experience generally available with a modular Core SDK and tools including Hyperloom, Skills and ROCm CLI. AMD reports average 3.3x inference and 2.4x training improvements over ROCm 7.

RAG

MLCommons launches first end-to-end RAG inference benchmark

The MLPerf Inference working group released the first end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation benchmark, measuring the full pipeline that retrieves documents and reasons to generate answers. It uses the FRAMES multi-hop dataset of 824 queries, 2,515 Wikipedia articles and ~107,000 paragraphs.

The benchmark splits into two pipelines: e2e-rag-db builds the vector database via parsing, chunking, embedding and FAISS HNSW indexing, while e2e-rag-qna performs iterative multi-hop reasoning through a query rewriter, embedder, retriever, reranker, document scorer and sufficiency checker. It gives teams a standard way to measure whole-pipeline RAG performance rather than opaque end-to-end latency.

Source: MLCommons via Winzheng, 29 August 2026

Vision

Google’s Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash adds 4K video and 40-second scenes

Google released Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash on 27 August, a production video-generation model with scene extension analysing up to 10 seconds of prior context, first-and-last-frame control, 360p draft mode at a third of 720p cost, and output up to 4K. It took the #1 spot on Arena’s text-to-video leaderboard the next day.

Extensions run in 10-second increments to a cumulative 40 seconds, addressing the short-clip limitation that has capped AI video tools. API billing is per second of generated video ($0.03 for 360p to $0.30 for 4K). All output carries SynthID watermarking. Adobe (Firefly), Figma Weave and Runway are already running it in production; it is also live in Google Flow for AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers.

Source: DataNorth, 28 August 2026
