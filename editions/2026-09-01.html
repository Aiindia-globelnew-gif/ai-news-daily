India Notifies ₹1.27 Lakh Crore Semicon 2.0, Opening Chip Funding to Startups and MSMEs

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology has formally notified Semicon 2.0, a ₹1,27,500-crore programme that vastly widens India's semiconductor ambitions beyond fabrication to include indigenous chip design, equipment, materials, advanced packaging, R&D, and talent development across six pillars and ten categories.

Unlike the first phase, Semicon 2.0 opens eligibility to chip-design startups and MSMEs owned by Indian citizens or OCIs, who can receive milestone-linked seed funding of up to ₹15 crore (capped at 50% of project cost) or equity co-investment alongside venture capital. Silicon wafer fabs face the highest thresholds: a minimum ₹20,000 crore investment, ₹7,500 crore revenue, and 40,000 wafer starts per month, qualifying for 40% capex support. Compound semiconductor and display fabs get 35% support with lower thresholds of ₹500 crore investment. Advanced packaging receives 35% capex support while legacy packaging gets 25%. R&D and talent development projects can secure up to 75% of project costs. The scheme follows the Union Cabinet's July 15 approval of the India Semiconductor Mission 2.0 outlay and will remain open for applications for three years, with the India Semiconductor Mission as nodal agency.

EU AI Office Sends First Enforcement Requests to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google

On August 29, the EU AI Office issued its first batch of information requests to multiple general-purpose AI model providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google, covering model safety, independent external evaluation, post-deployment monitoring, and training data summary disclosure.

Confirmed by European Commission Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen, the requests fall into two categories: one targeting model safety, independent evaluation, and market monitoring, and another targeting providers that have not published detailed training content summaries. Incomplete or misleading responses could trigger fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual revenue, whichever is higher. The AI Office can require corrective measures and, in severe cases, restrict a model's public availability in the EU. The move comes roughly four weeks after GPAI obligations were activated on August 2, making the EU the first major jurisdiction to formally engage on models that escaped controlled test environments over the summer.

DeepSeek Publishes 168GB Vision Model Weights Under MIT License

DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng published open weights for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp, the lab's first experimental multimodal model in the V4 family, moving it from an API-only product to a downloadable checkpoint that developers can inspect, fine-tune, and self-host.

The 305-billion-parameter model spans 168GB across 48 Safetensors files and includes a tokenizer, prompt-encoding reference, and a minimal PyTorch inference implementation. The reference implementation covers the vision encoder and aligner alongside DeepSeek's mixture-of-experts architecture, DFlash attention, Hyper-Connections, and DSpark forward path, with serving instructions for vLLM and SGLang. DeepSeek's model card reports V4 Flash Vision scored 27.3 on Agents' Last Exam versus Claude Opus 4.8 at 25.7, though those results are self-reported. The API supports JPEG, PNG, GIF, and WebP inputs via Base64, public URLs, or a Files API, with images capped at 384 tokens each after resizing. The large download size means the practical audience is research groups and inference providers with multi-GPU systems.

Google's Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash Brings Production Controls to AI Video

Google DeepMind released Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, a production-ready update to its conversational video generation model that adds scene extension to 40 seconds, first-and-last-frame keyframe control, three-second video references for character consistency, and 360p draft rendering that upscales to 4K.

The model, generally available as gemini-omni-1.1-flash since August 27, takes text, image, and video inputs and generates high-resolution video with audio. Scene extension lets users chain 10-second clips up to a cumulative 40 seconds with improved visual consistency. First-and-last-frame control turns unpredictable generation into directable keyframing for camera orbits, zoom transitions, and seamless loops. Video references of up to three seconds help maintain character and style continuity across shots, addressing a persistent weakness in AI video. The 360p draft tier generates up to 60% faster and costs roughly a third of standard 720p rendering, letting teams iterate cheaply before committing compute to the final 4K upscale. Google bills video output at a fixed rate of 5,792 tokens per second of 720p video, an effective rate of about $0.10 per second.

ChatGPT Becomes First AI Chatbot Designated Under EU Digital Services Act

The European Commission has classified ChatGPT as a search engine, bringing it under the Digital Services Act as a Very Large Online Search Engine alongside Reddit and Roblox, which were designated as Very Large Online Platforms.

The designation follows a calculation: services with more than 45 million monthly EU users fall under the DSA threshold, and ChatGPT reported 159 million monthly active users across the six months to March 2026. The unusual part is treating a chatbot that generates prose answers as a search engine in the same category as services that direct users to web information. The three companies now face annual systemic risk assessments covering illegal content, minors' protection, fundamental rights, electoral processes, and public security, plus independent audits and data access for regulators. They have four months to comply, putting the deadline at the end of November, with fines of up to 6% of global annual turnover for serious violations. Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said the designations mean the companies will be held to a higher standard of scrutiny and accountability.

Google Antigravity Multi-Agent System Solves Seven Open Math Problems

Pairing Google Antigravity's Teamwork framework with Gemini 3.7 Flash, autonomous teams of AI agents collaborated to solve seven open problems across top venues including FOCS and JMLR, and built a cycle-accurate RISC-V CPU simulator from scratch that boots an operating system.

The multi-agent orchestration solved open problems including Knuth's Cycles Conjecture, verified in Lean with proofs exceeding 40 pages, sparse convex optimization, provable LLM quantization, and prefix-matrix factorizations, while achieving 71% on TCSBench. On the systems side, the agents built an out-of-order RISC-V CPU simulator that boots the xv6 operating system to shell with 0.71% cycle alignment error against hardware ground truth. The agents also landed performance optimizations upstream in core open-source libraries, including Eigen SIMD fast-paths and ParlayHash improvements delivering 2x insert throughput and 25% memory reduction. The Teamwork framework allows agents to collaborate, critique, and iterate over hours or days on long-horizon challenges.

Amazon Bedrock Launches Managed Knowledge Base for Agentic Retrieval

AWS introduced a Managed Knowledge Base for Amazon Bedrock that moves retrieval from a single lookup to an agent-driven, multi-step process, with the AgenticRetrieveStream API decomposing questions into sub-queries and synthesizing cited answers, eliminating the need to provision a vector database.

The Managed Knowledge Base handles ingestion, storage, indexing, and retrieval automatically, including embedding and reranking with service-managed models by default. Classic RAG does one retrieval and one generation, but the new agentic retrieval puts a reasoning agent in the loop that decides whether and what to retrieve, can retrieve several times to refine, chooses which knowledge base is relevant via semantic routing, and composes a grounded answer with citations. The AgentCore Gateway exposes each knowledge base's AgenticRetrieveStream as an MCP tool, giving the agent one tool per knowledge base with no Lambda or container to maintain. A companion post detailed multi-tenant architectures where per-user isolation is enforced through verified JWT-based filters applied to every retrieval hop, addressing the security challenge that agentic retrieval compounds.

DAMP Quantization Technique Cuts LLM Memory 69% With 2x Speedup

Researchers developed DAMP (Decay-Aware Mixed-Precision Recurrent-State Quantization), a technique that reduces the memory footprint of recurrent-state language models by identifying high-risk channels and storing them at higher precision while quantizing the rest to INT8.

Traditional models store recurrent states in FP32, consuming significant GPU memory and increasing latency. DAMP achieves near-FP32 accuracy with a 69.1% reduction in storage and up to a 2.01x speedup in recurrent-state updates. Evaluated on models like Qwen3.6-35B and Kimi-Linear-48B, the method proved effective across reasoning and code generation tasks. The approach targets models using Gated DeltaNet or Kimi Delta Attention, which reduce inference memory growth with sequence length compared to softmax attention. By focusing precision on the channels most sensitive to quantization error, DAMP preserves model quality while delivering substantial memory and latency improvements, potentially enabling larger models or faster inference on existing hardware.
