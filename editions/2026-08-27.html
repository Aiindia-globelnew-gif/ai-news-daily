Meta Opens Weights on Muse Glimmer, a 30B Model Built to Run on a Single Consumer GPU

Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model optimized for local, always-on AI agents. Nvidia has tuned the model to run on a PC with a single consumer GPU, and CEO Mark Zuckerberg said the more powerful Muse Spark 1.2 foundation model weights will follow soon.

Muse Glimmer is the latest salvo in Meta's push to make increasingly capable AI widely available rather than concentrated in a few institutions. The dense 30B model targets coding and agentic tasks on local hardware, a space where Chinese open-weight models have been gaining ground. Muse Spark, introduced in April as the first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs, is a natively multimodal reasoning model supporting tool use and multi-agent orchestration. Opening its weights would mark one of the largest open foundation-model releases to date.

Tamil Nadu Unveils AI and Deep-Tech Package with Rs 50 Crore Vetri Innovation Fund

The Tamil Nadu government announced a suite of AI and deep-tech initiatives, including the Vetri Tamil Nadu Deep-Tech Innovation Fund with an initial Rs 50 crore corpus managed through the iTNT Hub, targeting startups and PhD scholars in AI/ML, semiconductors, and robotics.

The initiatives span multi-crore funds, state-of-the-art infrastructure, public-private partnerships, and specialised AI tools across multiple sectors, positioning the state as a national leader in AI and deep-tech innovation.

Centre Approves Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 with Rs 10,000 Crore Corpus

DPIIT has approved the Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 with a Rs 10,000 crore corpus, targeting deep-tech startups and early growth stages through Alternative Investment Funds across the 16th and 17th Finance Commission cycles. SIDBI will serve as the implementation agency.

The scheme allows access to venture capital for startups across stages and sectors, encouraging manufacturing based on new ideas and supporting technologies that require longer development horizons. It also assists smaller VC funds investing at the early stage. Each AIF proposal will be reviewed by a Venture Capital Investment Committee set up by DPIIT before receiving funding.

EU AI Office Gains Enforcement Powers to Investigate and Sanction Frontier Model Firms

On 2 August the European AI Office gained powers to investigate major technology firms and sanction them for violating the EU AI Act. Nature's editorial board is urging more researchers to participate in the Office's technical work.

The AI Act categorises systems into four risk levels, with 'high risk' systems facing enhanced transparency and oversight requirements. Frontier models classified as systemic-risk, such as Anthropic's Claude Fable 5, must provide the regulator with training data overviews, demonstrate copyright compliance, and show their models can prevent harmful manipulation of the public. The Office is calling on the research community to help with technical evaluation of these systems.

US House Receives Bill H.R. 10146, the AI Advertising Disclosure Act

The US House of Representatives received H.R. 10146, the AI Advertising Disclosure Act, which would require disclosure when AI is used to generate or substantially alter advertising content.

The bill represents a growing bipartisan push in the United States to bring transparency to AI-generated commercial speech, complementing broader regulatory conversations at the FTC and state level. If passed, it would establish a federal disclosure requirement for AI-assisted advertisements, putting the onus on advertisers rather than platforms.

Ray 2.58 Adds Native gVisor Sandboxing for Secure Agentic AI Workloads

The Ray distributed computing framework introduced native gVisor sandboxing in version 2.58, integrating secure, isolated execution environments directly into Ray clusters. The feature targets agentic reinforcement learning where untrusted, model-generated code requires secure isolation.

Users can create gVisor sandboxes from OCI container images, execute commands, transfer files, and scale environments through Ray's existing APIs, with sandboxes treated as Ray Actors. The release follows Nscale's July acquisition of Anyscale, which committed to expanding Ray investment and maintaining open-source governance under the PyTorch Foundation. Upcoming enhancements may include GPU support and REST APIs for sandbox management.

Perplexity and Nvidia Launch Portable Computer, a Fully Local AI Agent with Zero Token Costs

Perplexity launched Portable Computer, a version of its agentic platform that runs entirely on hardware users already own, starting with Nvidia's DGX Spark and Linux machines with RTX GPUs. Work completed locally consumes no billing credits, with cloud escalation only when the local model hits its limits.

The system bundles local models, an agent harness, inference engine via vLLM, tools, app connectors, and an OS-level security sandbox. Perplexity published a research paper arguing that effective local agents require the model and harness to be co-designed, since compact models struggle beyond roughly 100,000 tokens of context. At launch, users can run Qwen 3.8 27B or PPLX 27B, with Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Lightning coming soon. A 24GB VRAM floor applies.

Accel-Backed Keenable Emerges from Stealth, Indexing 100B+ Web Documents for AI Agents

Keenable, founded by former Yandex search lead Andrey Styskin and German AI scientist Matthias Petri, came out of stealth with $26 million in seed funding led by Accel. The startup has built a web search index of more than 100 billion documents, with its API already in production at several AI labs.

Keenable is building proprietary retrieval capabilities including an upcoming Web Query Language to help AI systems combine information from multiple web sources. The founders argue that AI chatbots perform better when grounded in source documents, creating a new flywheel distinct from Google's human-centric search. With Google and Microsoft shutting down existing search APIs, Keenable sees an opening to become the default web-scale retrieval layer for agentic workloads.

Alibaba Launches Wan3.0, Doubling AI Video Generation to 30 Seconds from Documents and Slides

Alibaba began general availability of Wan3.0, an AI video generation model that can read text, images, documents, spreadsheets, slides, and web pages to produce videos up to 30 seconds long in a single generation, double the previous limit.

Wan3.0 maintains consistency in faces, clothing, backgrounds, and props across scenes, a key challenge in multi-shot narrative video generation. The model can create promotional videos by reading product planning slides, opening up marketing use cases. Its predecessor, Wan2.7-Video, was limited to 15-second clips, forcing creators to stitch multiple generations together.
