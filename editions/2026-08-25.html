OpenAI Cuts GPT-5.6 Sol API Prices by Over 20% in a Three-Month Salvo

OpenAI cut developer prices for its frontier GPT-5.6 Sol model by more than 20% for three months, dropping input tokens to $4 per million and output tokens to $20 per million from $5 and $30, as it faces sharpening competition from Anthropic and Chinese AI models. The discount covers the API plus credits for the agentic ChatGPT Work product and the Codex coding tool, while Pro, Plus and Business subscriptions stay unchanged.

The move follows price reductions across OpenAI’s smaller models in late July, when the mid-tier Terra model was cut 20% and the lower-cost Luna model was slashed 80%. Sol’s new $4/$20 rate now undercuts Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5 ($5/$25) on both rails, and sits well below the premium Claude Fable 5 at $10/$50. Analysts note the three-month window runs straight through the most concentrated frontier-model release season of the year, suggesting a tactical bid to lock in developer workloads while rivals ship their next flagships. DeepSeek’s V4 Pro, after a 75% permanent cut, lists output at $0.87 per million tokens — anchoring a price war some call a race to zero.

Peak XV Says Venture Capital Is Going ‘Marginal’ in the AI Cycle

Peak XV Partners MD Shailendra Singh told the ET World Leaders Forum that VCs are becoming somewhat marginal in the AI investment cycle because the sector demands far larger pools of capital, arguing that large enterprises with deep balance sheets — like HCLTech in Sarvam AI or Amazon in Anthropic — will need to become the bigger funding source. Specialist funds are simultaneously widening into deeptech, with Omnivore, Aavishkaar and Incubate Fund all moving beyond their traditional sectors into robotics, spacetech and AI.

Speaking at The Economic Times World Leaders Forum 2026 in New Delhi on August 21-22, Singh said corporate balance sheets can support investments beyond the reach of most VC funds, pointing to HCLTech’s investment in Sarvam AI and Amazon’s backing of Anthropic as templates. Menlo Ventures partner Deedy Das, on the same panel, said India faces challenges building globally competitive foundational AI companies given the concentration of top research talent in San Francisco and limited appetite for high-risk capital. Das was also bullish on Anthropic ahead of its public listing, saying a potential $2 trillion valuation would still undervalue the company. Tracxn data shows funds including Omnivore, Ankur Capital, Avaana Capital, Aavishkaar Capital and Incubate Fund Asia India have increased investments beyond their traditional sectors since 2024.

MeitY Launches GENESIS EIR 3.0 With Up to Rs 10 Lakh for Tier-II, III Startups

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, through MeitY Startup Hub, launched the third cohort of its GENESIS Entrepreneur-in-Residence programme, offering up to Rs 10 lakh each to early-stage innovators in Tier-II and Tier-III cities working on AI/ML, IoT, semiconductors, cybersecurity and quantum technologies. The scheme sits under a Rs 490 crore GENESIS budget outlay covering EIR, Pilot, Investment and DeepTech funding components.

GENESIS EIR 3.0 was launched at the GENESIS NextGen Startup Summit in Coimbatore on August 4, with applications open until September 3, 2026. To qualify, applicants must be Indian citizens with innovations at Technology Readiness Level 1-4; incorporated startups must be less than two years old and based in a Tier-II or Tier-III city. Selected innovators receive financial assistance through participating GENESIS incubators along with mentorship, incubation and access to the wider startup ecosystem. The programme targets technology areas including AI/ML, IoT, semiconductor and VLSI, cybersecurity, blockchain, quantum technologies and AR/VR.

India’s Airbound Raises $37M to Build a Truck-Rivaling Drone Network

Indian autonomous drone startup Airbound raised $37 million in a Series A led by Greenoaks with participation from DoorDash, Lachy Groom, Lightspeed and Humba Ventures, less than a year after an $8.65 million seed. The three-year-old company has signed an agreement with the Andhra Pradesh government to build a drone delivery network connecting three cities, targeting 10,000 flights a day for retail, e-commerce and healthcare.

Airbound wants to make moving goods through the air as cheap as trucking them by road. The startup’s agreement with Andhra Pradesh does not involve a government contract or subsidy, with the state working with Airbound on the regulatory framework for the network. The bigger bottleneck is securing approvals for beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) operations, critical to operating delivery networks at scale. Those regulatory constraints have limited Airbound’s commercial revenue, and the startup remains broadly pre-revenue despite a team of more than 150 employees. With this round, Airbound has raised nearly $50 million total.

Anthropic Chases a $2 Trillion IPO as Its Premium Fable 5 Stalls

Anthropic’s annualised revenue run rate surged from roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025 to over $65 billion by July, and the company is projecting $190-200 billion of revenue in 2028 to justify an October IPO potentially valued at $2 trillion or more. Ramp economist Ara Kharazian said Fable 5 ‘disappointed both in adoption and real-world application given price + data retention requirements imposed by regulators.’ Fable 5 also carries a 30-day data-retention requirement flagged as a drag. If customers increasingly decide cheaper models are good enough, the path from $65 billion today to $200 billion in 2028 becomes much harder.

Study Finds Design Beats Raw Scale in Cutting AI Hallucinations

A Nature Scientific Reports study evaluating seven models from one to seven billion parameters found that non-scale factors — architecture, training data quality and alignment methodology — account for nearly a quarter of performance variation, enough for a well-designed three-billion-parameter model to match a seven-billion-parameter predecessor. Returns diminish severely beyond three billion parameters, with marginal gains collapsing up to sixteenfold.

However, fundamental limits persist: about a quarter of carefully designed prompts defeat all tested models regardless of scale or architecture, with questions requiring multi-step reasoning and temporal tracking proving universally problematic. The authors argue the language-model field’s fixation on ever-larger sizes carries massive computational and environmental costs that are not justified by the reliability gains they deliver, and that design efficiency is the more productive frontier.

South Korea passed a revision of its privacy law to permit the use of personal data in AI development, alongside setting national principles for AI ethics covering human-centricity, safety and accountability. SGLang v0.5.18 Cuts LLM Cold Starts 2.4x; FreeToken Serves 753B on One GPU

The open-source LLM serving engine SGLang released version 0.5.18 with an overlapped startup engine that stages model checkpoint pages from storage concurrently with CUDA graph capture, cutting cold-start latency by 2.4x. The release comprises 710 pull requests from 212 contributors and adds native serving engines for diffusion video and image models including SANA-Video, LTX-2.5 and Cosmos3 Edge.

In a parallel thread, researchers from UC Berkeley and UT Austin released FreeToken, an Apache-2.0 edge-native MoE serving engine that runs the 753B GLM-5.2 on a single workstation GPU by continuously mapping computation and model state onto whatever GPU, CPU and memory bandwidth a machine has. It sustains 39.3 tok/s for a 35B model on an 8 GB laptop GPU and reports 1.5-2.3x decode throughput over llama.cpp, Ollama and KTransformers, with no router modification or precision relaxation.

Production Vector Compression Compared: SQ, PQ and RaBitQ Weighed on Recall and Memory

A detailed engineering analysis compared scalar quantisation (SQ), product quantisation (PQ) and the newer RaBitQ compression schemes across architecture, recall retention and memory economics for production retrieval pipelines. The work addresses the central tension in large-scale RAG deployments: shrinking the memory footprint of billions of vectors without losing the recall that makes retrieval useful.

NVIDIA separately published an enterprise RAG retrieval scaling and sizing guide covering embedding, vector database and reranking stages, finding that retrieval performance is not significantly affected by vector database sizes ranging from 100K to 40M embeddings. The guide recommends splitting large enterprise data across multiple collections and sizes nodes at 2-4 data nodes plus 5-10 query nodes for 10M-100M vectors, signalling that the engineering of RAG is maturing into a well-documented infrastructure discipline.
