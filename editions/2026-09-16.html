OpenAI Confirms It Is Working With Anthropic and Google on AI Safety

OpenAI's chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane said on Tuesday that the company has been in talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on safety for several weeks, confirming reports of an unprecedented coordination effort between the industry's fiercest rivals. Lehane said the companies do not believe they need an antitrust waiver to cooperate, days after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call to slow frontier development drew support from Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis.

The discussions trace back to a July proposal by Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis for a US-led "Standards Body" modelled on the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA), which oversees US brokers. Speaking at a Washington briefing, Lehane told reporters the collaboration had been under way for several weeks and that "it's better to try to work together to prioritize safety." He also said OpenAI is backing the bipartisan FRONTIER Act from Reps. Jay Obernolte and Lori Trahan, which would create the first federal AI safety framework and require top labs to embed independent verification organizations. Amodei had argued the US government should consider a narrow antitrust waiver for certain safety conversations, but Lehane said such a move is unnecessary. No formal agreement or standards document has been published yet, and Reuters could not independently verify details of the talks.

Sanskrit Heritage Model Launched at Madras Sanskrit College

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman launched a Sanskrit Heritage Model and Platform built with enterprise AI firm Articul8 in Chennai, calling for AI that makes India's literary treasures accessible "to everyone anywhere in the world at any time." The college says it is the first institute in the country with an AI-powered knowledge model.

Articul8 founder and CEO Arun K. Subramaniyan said the system encodes Panini's Ashtadhyayi — the roughly 2,500-year-old grammar of Sanskrit — as a "live compiler and verifier" so every response is generated and verified by the same rule-based machinery before it reaches a scholar. Sitharaman also suggested AI could help trace how far Indian languages, including Tamil and Sanskrit, spread based on manuscripts, palm leaves and copper plates. Articul8 says the approach is designed to extend to other Indic languages and knowledge systems, and the platform will keep evolving through feedback from Sanskrit scholars.

Flam Raises $40 Million Series B, Shah Rukh Khan Joins the Round

AI interactive content startup Flam has raised $40 million in a Series B led by QED Investors, with actor Shah Rukh Khan's family office among the participants. The company, founded in 2021 with teams in India and Japan, builds interactive video, 3D and digital-character formats used by more than 100 enterprise customers including Google, Reliance and Hyundai.

The company holds five AI models and more than 15 patents, and will use the capital for model R&D, product expansion and global enterprise sales. "Content has moved in waves, from text to images to video, and every wave flowed one way, creator to viewer. We think interactive is the next default for the internet," founder Shourya Agarwal said. Existing investors RTP Global and Dovetail also followed on.

India

Pine Labs Launches India's First Agent-to-Agent Payment Protocol on UPI

Pine Labs has launched the Pine Labs Payment Protocol (P3P), described as India's first agent-to-agent payment system. By extending UPI's standing mandate framework to machines, it lets users authorize a one-time mandate to an AI agent that can then monitor markets, negotiate prices and execute transactions on their behalf.

The system's flagship example is autonomous purchasing: a user can set a mandate for an agent to buy gold when prices dip, without real-time human authentication at the point of sale. The launch is an early glimpse of agentic commerce on Indian rails — building on UPI's ubiquity rather than new card networks — and raises fresh questions about fraud controls, liability and revocation as software agents gain spending authority over real bank accounts.

Source: TechShots, 16 September 2026

US AI Regulation Faces Deadlock as Trump Calls Safety Concerns a "Hoax"

Even as tech CEOs escalate warnings about AI risk, Washington is not moving. President Trump dismissed calls for AI limits as a "conspiracy" and "hoax," the Republican-controlled Congress is unlikely to break from him, and the House heads into recess this week until after the November midterms.

Polling by the University of Maryland's Program for Public Consultation found 85% of Democrats and 79% of Republicans want a new federal agency to regulate AI, yet lawmakers remain divided on specifics — proposals range from independent audits and a federal "kill switch" to Senator Bernie Sanders' moratorium on data centres. Senate Majority Leader John Thune is working on a bill with Sens. Ted Cruz and Amy Klobuchar that would force some AI companies to submit to federal oversight, while Reps. Lori Trahan and Jay Obernolte's Frontier Act would require independent audits of AI labs. House Speaker Mike Johnson has largely deferred to the White House, calling first for AI companies to self-regulate. With the clock and the calendar against it, most observers expect no new US safety legislation before the midterms.

Microsoft Research Nearly Doubles Agent Task Success and Halves Memory Cost

A Microsoft Research paper proposes giving enterprise agents a background "memory curator" with limited read-only access to the live environment before committing anything to long-term storage. On a production-like GitHub Copilot benchmark, the technique lifted pass rates from 39% to 73% and roughly halved per-task inference costs — with no model retraining.

The paper, "Grounding Agent Memory: Environment-Probing Curation for Enterprise Agents," was submitted to arXiv on 10 September by a five-person Microsoft team in Redmond. On the CLBench benchmark — whose schema changes midway through to expose stale-memory failures — environment-probing memory achieved a 73% pass rate at $1.68 per question versus 39% at $3.38 for a stateless baseline, while cutting tool calls from 8.8 to 4.7 per question. The gains held across both Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.7 foundation models, and the architecture is designed to slot into existing Copilot Studio and Model Context Protocol deployments. It is the third major agent-memory release from Microsoft Research this year, after STATE-Bench in May and Memora at ICML 2026.

Research

IBM Tackles the Hidden Unreliability of AI Agents

IBM Research published work showing that an agent succeeding 77% of the time on average may pass every repeated run for only 53% of tasks — a 24.4-point "consistency gap" most benchmarks hide. Its new Consistency Analyzer and consistency guidelines cut that gap roughly in half without sacrificing average accuracy.

The diagnostic resamples an agent's own recorded trajectory to find flip-prone decision points — steps where the model was one token-sample away from doing something different — using a single trace and no ground truth. Turning that diagnosis into automatically distilled guidelines raised same-task Pass-5 from 53.0% to 69.0% on AppWorld with a ReAct agent on GPT-4.1, while Mean-5 rose slightly from 77.4% to 81.0%. Gains transferred to similar tasks (+13.0 points) and held on the weaker gpt-oss-120b, suggesting the guidelines capture reusable failure patterns rather than memorized fixes. The toolkit is open source in the ALTK-Evolve repo.

Source: IBM Research / Hugging Face Blog, 15 September 2026

NVIDIA Squeezes a 10.4x MoE Training Speedup From JAX and Transformer Engine

NVIDIA says combining the JAX Python library with its Transformer Engine library lifted Mixture-of-Experts training on DeepSeek-V3 671B from 103 to 1,068 TFLOPS per GPU on GB200 systems — a 10.4x improvement, with 97% scaling efficiency up to 1,024 GPUs. The optimizations ship in the NGC MaxText container.

The bottleneck NVIDIA attacked was communication: in an unoptimized DeepSeek-V3 MoE run, inter-GPU communication consumed 84% of accumulated kernel time, partly because a learned router distributes tokens unevenly across experts, creating "ragged" tensors that standard kernels handle poorly. Specialized grouped-GEMM and expert-parallel kernels, plus JAX host offloading of intermediate activations and XLA multistreaming collectives, closed the gap. Because MoE is now the dominant architecture for open frontier-class models, the work has direct implications for anyone training or fine-tuning large models on NVIDIA hardware.

Frameworks

Alibaba's Accio Team Open-Sources Occamy-1.0, a 3B-Active-Parameter Agentic Model

Alibaba's Accio Team has released Occamy-1.0, an open-weights AI agent built on the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Mixture-of-Experts base that activates only about 3 billion of its 35 billion parameters per token. The team claims frontier-competitive scores on sustained multi-step "co-work" benchmarks, and released the Dressage training infrastructure alongside it.

Occamy-1.0 is post-trained for long-running agentic work — updating databases, running code, and managing workflows across dozens or hundreds of tool calls — and was trained and evaluated across three harnesses (OpenClaw, Hermes Agent and Accio Work). Dressage, open-sourced in June, uses a Token-In-Token-Out proxy that records exact token IDs for every model call, preserving trajectory fidelity when an agent's history gets compacted mid-session. The catch: no inference provider hosts it yet, so teams must self-serve with SGLang or vLLM on eight-way tensor parallelism to use its full 262,144-token context.

RAG

Google Research's Retrieve-for-Train Cuts Search Inference Latency 12-20x

Google Research detailed an ICML 2026 framework that replaces expensive inference-time "thinking" with a one-time reinforcement-learning step compiled into a lightweight diffusion retriever. The 53.9-million-parameter model generates a full slate of expert-level search sub-queries in a single non-autoregressive pass — 12 to 20 times faster than autoregressive fan-out.

The Retrieve-for-Train pipeline first trains a fan-out language model (Gemma3-4B and Qwen3-4B were used) with RL against a set-level reward balancing diversity, alignment and groundedness, then distills the learned behaviour into the small diffusion retriever that maps a query embedding directly to a complete set of target embeddings. At scale, autoregressive fan-out latency grows to nearly 50 seconds under large context batches, while the diffusion retriever stays between sub-second and a few seconds. The researchers frame RL as a one-time "objective transducer" rather than an online inference engine — a data-efficient recipe for retrieval pipelines where property-aligned training pairs are scarce.

Creatify Launches Boreal: Frontier-Quality AI Video at a Cent a Second

Creatify Labs launched Boreal, an advertising-focused AI video model that generates text-to-video and image-to-video at one cent per second, rendering in realtime — a five-second clip costs about $0.05 and is ready in roughly five seconds. The company says it rivals top-tier closed models at up to 40 times the speed and a fraction of the price.

Boreal is built on LTX-2.5, Lightricks' open-source 22-billion-parameter video model that generates audio and video jointly, tuned with a single post-training recipe on advertising footage. In blind side-by-side review, evaluators preferred Boreal over its untouched base in 81% of decisive comparisons, with gains in image quality (MUSIQ, p<0.001) and fine-detail retention. The company is candid about trade-offs: output is 720p-class, so resolution-sensitive measures favour 1080p rivals like Seedance 2.0, which scored higher on a blind ad-quality judge (80% vs 70%) but at more than 30 times the cost. Boreal is available via Creatify's API, Model Playground, AdFlow and fal.ai.

Vision

fal's H3 Max Topped Sora and Veo — Then Its Price Quadrupled

H3 Max, fal's post-trained version of MiniMax's open-weight H3 video model, ranks first on Artificial Analysis' image-to-video leaderboard with audio (Elo 1,201), ahead of Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Grok Imagine 1.5 and Kling 3.0 — while rendering five-second clips in under three seconds. On 15 September, fal's 75% launch discount expired, quadrupling the price of a five-second 768p clip from $0.10 to $0.40.

The story is a case study in open-weights economics: fal achieved roughly 35 times the throughput of MiniMax's own hosted endpoint by post-training the open model for speed, and a distilled H3 Max Turbo variant (previewed 13 September) runs at twice the speed for half the price — though it drops reference-to-video consistency. Because the underlying weights are open, nothing stops a rival infrastructure provider from post-training the same model and undercutting fal on price, which is exactly how fal undercut MiniMax's own API in the first place. Holding prices down once promotional discounts expire, the piece argues, is the harder problem.

Fotor Agent Turns Prompts Into Editable, After Effects-Grade Motion Graphics

Fotor launched Fotor Agent, an AI production system that assembles complete, editable video projects and 4K motion graphics from a brief or raw assets — ranked Product of the Day on Product Hunt. Unlike video models that bake graphics into uneditable pixel streams, Fotor Agent keeps text, charts, colours and animation timing fully parametric.

The agent handles long-horizon planning — generating supporting assets, organizing scenes, and assembling a multi-track timeline — while a built-in editor allows element-level refinement through natural-language prompts. Fotor's internal benchmarks claim motion-graphics production costs cut to 1/200th of traditional After Effects workflows with 50-fold faster generation, and end-to-end production of a two-minute video compressed from 3-5 days to under an hour. The launch extends Fotor's "AI Vibe Marketing" platform, which the company says serves more than 800 million users worldwide.
