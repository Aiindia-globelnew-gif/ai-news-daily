★ Top Story Global

OpenAI and Anthropic chiefs tell UN Security Council: no one company or country should control AI

Sam Altman, Dario Amodei and Hugging Face's Clem Delangue told a UN Security Council session on AI and international security that the technology's risks demand international coordination — common capability and risk standards, incident reporting, and human oversight. Their appeal ran straight into the Trump administration's opposition, with White House advisor Michael Kratsios telling the same forum that AI development should not be constrained by "new global governance structures."

Altman called for "national and international" standards for measuring AI capabilities and safeguards, plus "speedy incident reporting, classification, and reporting protocols so the world can learn from failures before they become catastrophes." Amodei said AI "if managed poorly... could be a risk to humanity as a whole" and pledged that Anthropic would "slow down as much as necessary" to keep each release safe. Yoshua Bengio, co-chair of the UN's Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, pushed back on the industry's framing of an unavoidable race: "The race is not a law of nature; it is the product of choices, choices made by the companies themselves." The session, convened by France alongside the UN General Assembly, drew foreign ministers including those of France and the UK, who backed common frameworks for controlling the technology.

Policy

Sanders bill would ban "superintelligence" outright and create a US Department of AI

Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar unveiled legislation that would permanently prohibit artificial superintelligence — systems exceeding human cognition or capable of disempowering humanity — and pause advanced AI development until a new federal Department of Artificial Intelligence sets safety rules. Violations could carry up to 20 years in prison.

The bill would require firms to submit pre-development plans and obtain federal approval before deploying advanced systems, bar violators from the AI industry for 10 years, and pursue international agreements — including export controls on AI computing infrastructure — toward a global ban. Sanders compared the needed US-China cooperation to Reagan and Gorbachev's nuclear arms control. The proposal faces long odds in the Republican-controlled Congress but signals where the progressive wing stands heading into November's midterms, and it drew endorsements from multiple employees at leading AI companies.

Source: PBS News (AP), 23 September 2026

India

MeitY narrows government agentic AI platform to CoRover and Kyndryl; DigiLocker first use case

The National e-Governance Division has shortlisted CoRover and Kyndryl Solutions for a government-wide "Conversational and Agentic AI Bot Framework" — a reusable ask-do-escalate platform that departments can configure rather than rebuild from scratch. DigiLocker will be the first implementation, with an agent that fetches, shares and manages documents on a citizen's behalf.

Cactus Technology Solutions and NEC Corporation India were disqualified in the technical evaluation opened on 7 September. The DigiLocker agent will be built over a four-month implementation phase followed by 12 months of operations and maintenance. Notably, the stack is required to use open-source and open-weight components and support both SaaS APIs and fully on-premises deployment — a deliberate hedge against dependence on any single proprietary AI or cloud provider — with authentication via Parichay and multilingual support.

India

Gujarat rolls out statewide GPU-as-a-Service for AI projects

Gujarat has launched a GPU-as-a-Service framework with immediate effect, creating a shared pool of GPU computing for state departments, public institutions and selected AI startups. Gujarat Informatics Ltd will manage provisioning, operations and administration, with capacity procured from cloud providers empanelled under the IndiaAI Mission.

State government departments, government institutions and educational institutions get access on a 100% subsidy basis, as do startups selected through the AI Centre of Excellence's AI Innovation Challenge for production deployment of approved use cases; other startups pay GPU service costs plus GIL's 7% consultancy fee. The framework operationalises the infrastructure pillar of Gujarat's six-pillar AI action plan and is designed to stop every department building and maintaining its own high-performance computing.

Global

Antitrust suit claims Big AI's "slowdown pact" was an illegal agreement

A federal lawsuit filed in California alleges that Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google violated antitrust law by coordinating to slow AI development, arguing the arrangement would reduce the value consumers get from paid AI subscriptions. The complaint traces the coordination to a 12 September essay by Dario Amodei urging industry-wide deceleration, and same-day endorsements by Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis.

The suit also cites a July statement signed by high-ranking employees across leading labs acknowledging "intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow" development. Amodei had anticipated the antitrust problem, writing that it would help for the US government to mediate or issue a narrow waiver for safety conversations; Altman responded that OpenAI does not believe it needs to wait for an antitrust exemption to begin the work. Regulators in the EU and US are already watching whether safety coordination shades into collusion on pace; none of the companies commented on the suit.

Research

Claude agents discover a CRISPR-like system hidden in phage DNA

Anthropic says roughly 950 Claude agents spent 21 hours scanning more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases and surfaced a previously uncharacterised enzyme system — array-associated reverse transcriptase (ART) — in bacteriophages. The arrangement, a reverse transcriptase beside a long array of evenly spaced DNA repeats, resembles CRISPR, though its function is not yet known.

The agents consumed about 210 million tokens, collected over 200,000 reverse transcriptases, identified roughly 3,500 candidate systems and narrowed the field to 20 candidates for detailed reports — work the company says would take an expert scientist weeks or months. Initial laboratory experiments found that the ART repeat array is expressed as multiple distinct short RNAs, as CRISPR arrays are, which is what helps make CRISPR-Cas systems programmable. CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang of MIT and the Broad Institute called it "an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery." Anthropic has opened a Bay Area molecular biology lab to test candidates, with human scientists running all experiments.

Source: Interesting Engineering, 23 September 2026

DeepSeek details DSec, the sandbox fabric for training AI agents at scale

A new arXiv paper from DeepSeek — with founder Liang Wenfeng among its 130-plus authors — describes DeepSeek Elastic Compute (DSec), a sandbox platform that unifies function-call, container, microVM and full-VM sandboxes and coordinates their lifecycle with reinforcement-learning workloads.

A production-scale unit spans around 160 nodes and supports about 3 million sandboxes per day, more than 380,000 concurrent sandboxes, and creation rates above 5,000 sandboxes per second. The infrastructure speaks to the operational reality of agent training: tool-using agents need safe, disposable execution environments at enormous volume, and DeepSeek is now publishing the systems design behind it.

Frameworks

AWS open-sources Strands harness, a token-sipping agent runtime

AWS has introduced Strands harness, an Apache 2.0-licensed runtime that turns its Strands Agents SDK into a ready-to-use agent harness for shells, files and web services. AWS claims it required about 28% fewer tokens on average across six benchmarks with the same Claude or GPT models as the harnesses it was compared against, without a noticeable drop in accuracy.

The savings come from pre-configured context management: tool outputs are truncated from around 1,500 tokens, the conversation history is summarised when the context window passes 85% full, and needed context is restored on overflow so long logs stop eating the budget. It ships as Python and TypeScript libraries, supports Bedrock, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Ollama and LiteLLM backends, and runs anywhere Linux containers do — including ECS, Cloud Run, Azure Container Apps and Modal. The figures are manufacturer claims; AWS did not publish complete per-benchmark results in the announcement.

Source: heise online, 23 September 2026

Frameworks

NVIDIA-backed SoL-Pi cuts coding-agent token traffic by up to 49%

A team from NVIDIA, NTU and MIT released SoL-Pi, an MIT-licensed extension for the open-source Pi coding agent that cuts token traffic by 44.7–49% on the 51-task EdgeBench evaluation while keeping scores within about 6% of the original harness on both GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5 backends.

Rather than cheaper kernels or quantisation, SoL-Pi modifies the harness layer: it fuses file edits with follow-up commands into a single tool call, compacts context only when projected savings justify rewriting the prompt cache, archives tool outputs above 10 KiB behind stable handles, and routes build and test logs to a cheaper model guarded by a deterministic verifier. On GPT-5.6 Sol, a full evaluation run used 1.10 billion tokens versus Pi's 2.15 billion, with API cost falling from $1,339 to $894 — evidence that meaningful cost reduction in coding agents can come from the layer teams already control.

Source: Complete AI Training (NVlabs), 23 September 2026

RAG

Rethinking retrieval: AI search needs answer-oriented contexts, not ranked documents

An arXiv paper argues that AI search changes the retrieval objective itself: because the consumer of retrieved documents is now a generation model rather than a human, ranking for "satisfaction" must give way to constructing reliable contexts. The authors propose a three-stage framework — answer support, content trustworthiness, and context organisation under a finite budget.

The industrial workflow pairs prior optimisation — LLM-based answer-supporting document ranking, trustworthiness assessment from source, temporal and factual signals, and set-wise context selection — with a posterior optimisation simulator that uses model-based proxy judgments to attribute answer quality back to contributing source documents, addressing the sparse answer-level feedback typical of AI search. For RAG teams, the framing is a pointed reminder that the quality ceiling is often set at the context-construction step, not the generator.

Source: arXiv, 22 September 2026

Vision

Black Forest Labs open-sources FLUX 3 Action, a fine-tunable 7B world action model

FLUX 3 Action is an open-weights 7B diffusion transformer that takes a camera frame plus a text instruction and returns the next two seconds of actions — predicting future frames and actions together. Fine-tuned on DROID, it places first on the RoboLab benchmark at 42.92% success, ahead of the 16B Cosmos 3 Nano policy at 36.8%.

The model was trained on NVIDIA GB200 systems with custom kernels written in NVIDIA's CuTe DSL, uses a frozen video VAE and a frozen Qwen3-VL-4B for instruction encoding, and ships with PEFT fine-tuning recipes and a LeRobot integration — both DROID and SO-101 arm checkpoints are supported. The team also trained the same model to control a drone and play video games from a few hundred recorded episodes each. Weights are released under the FLUX Kommunity License v1.0, with code on GitHub.

Source: Hugging Face Blog, 23 September 2026

Vision

Meta's Muse Realtime Avatar turns any image into a live, talking character

Meta AI Research introduced Muse Realtime Avatar, which extends its Muse Realtime Voice model into expressive, interactive avatars that stay synchronised and coherent through a live conversation. It streams 448x768 portrait video at 25 frames per second with roughly 870 ms of latency from the end of a user's turn to the first byte of response.

The system is an audio-driven diffusion transformer conditioned on the speech-token stream, reference media and a rolling window of recent video latents; a distillation recipe compresses a 120-evaluation teacher into a two-step student, and four-bit quantisation-aware training plus cache-aware routing yield an 8x serving-capacity gain — 12 concurrent real-time sessions per GB200. In blind comparisons, raters preferred it over Runway Characters and HeyGen LiveAvatar overall and across most dimensions. Meta embeds a Video Seal watermark in all generated video.

Marketo co-founder launches Phave, marketing automation that reasons

Jon Miller, co-founder of Marketo and Engagio, has taken the wraps off Phave, an AI-native marketing automation platform built to replace rule-based stacks like Marketo, Pardot, Eloqua and HubSpot. Its "Maestro" engine computes a playlist of touches for each person, account and buying group, ordered and timed for the objective the marketer sets.

Marketers describe what they want in plain language instead of translating it into configuration, and Phave can be driven through its own interface, an existing AI client, or headlessly via MCP and a versioned REST API — with permissions and approvals enforced whichever door a request comes through. Now generally available and used by enterprise teams including SambaNova, SPS Commerce and mabl, it starts at $36,000 a year, priced on the number of people reached per month rather than database size.

Upfluence goes agentic: brief to creator campaign in five minutes

Upfluence launched an agentic creator-marketing platform whose "Jaice" system handles campaign strategy, creator discovery, outreach, approvals, payments and reporting. The company claims brands can go from a campaign brief to a programme launch in as little as five minutes, versus a typical process of six weeks and up to 20 hours of manual review.

Upfluence says creators receive personalised outreach with response rates more than 20% higher, and that brands are 28% more likely to work with an AI-selected creator than one found through manual searches. The platform is MCP-enabled, so its creator data and campaign functions connect with environments like ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor, and a human-in-the-loop design lets teams decide which workflow steps stay under direct human control — including configurable brand-safety screening across major social platforms before outreach begins.
