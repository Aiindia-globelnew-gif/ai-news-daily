★ Top Story Global

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816B open-weight model it calls its most capable yet

Meta has released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter open-weight model that it says rivals and in some cases beats leading Chinese open models. The natively multimodal model has 42 billion active parameters and a one-million-token context window, and is built for coding and agentic work. The weights are on Hugging Face under the Muse Model License.

Meta's chief of AI, Alexandr Wang, called it “the most capable US open-weight model to date.” The release adds safety post-training to make the model more robust against misuse as a standalone system without guardrails, Meta said. It runs optimised on the SGLang and vLLM inference engines with support for NVIDIA, AMD and Intel hardware, and Meta has integrated its PyTorch-native Helion kernel language and TorchTitan fine-tuning support. The move intensifies competition in the open-weight race, where Chinese labs have led recent releases.

NVIDIA and AI4Bharat announce a 2.8-trillion-token corpus for 22 Indian languages

NVIDIA and IIT Madras's AI4Bharat announced Nemotron-pretraining-Indic-v1, a corpus of about 2.8 trillion tokens across 22 Indian languages, alongside an open pipeline for generating and assessing similar data. Both the dataset and the recipe are listed as coming soon. The effort targets a long-standing gap: the scarcity of high-quality training data for Indic languages.

Mitesh Khapra, who leads AI4Bharat, has described the shortage of Indian-language data as a core bottleneck for sovereign AI. The announcement follows IndiaAI Mission moves to expand domestic compute, including a MeitY and C-DAC request for empanelment of up to 10,000 AI compute units. The token count alone does not establish language-level quality or downstream model performance, which will depend on how the corpus is filtered and used.

India

India's ₹1 lakh crore RDI Fund clears 19 managers with ₹7,131 crore for deeptech

The Ministry of Science and Technology named the first 19 second-level fund managers for India's ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation Fund, committing ₹7,131 crore to deeptech. The cohort of 14 investment funds and five research bodies will back startups at Technology Readiness Level 4 and above, in sectors from AI and quantum to space and biotechnology. Each manager must match its government money with private capital.

Until this week the scheme ran on just two nominated managers that together committed about ₹2,500 crore. If the full ₹7,131 crore is matched rupee-for-rupee, the first cohort could channel close to ₹14,000 crore. Approval is not disbursement: managers must now raise matching private funds and run their own calls for proposals, which the government has said can take six to eight months.

Policy

EU AI Office opens enforcement with information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic

The European Commission's AI Office has requested information from OpenAI and Anthropic over security incidents involving their AI systems, covering security practices, external evaluations and how models are monitored. Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen confirmed the action at the International AI Summit in Dublin. It marks a shift from voluntary commitments toward legally enforceable safety expectations.

Whether the EU AI Act covers incidents that occur before a model reaches the market is contested; the law includes a research-and-development exemption. A Commission official told Euronews the bloc would not enforce in the testing phase, but would act if a model “escapes the testing environment” and affects the EU market. Penalties for general-purpose AI violations can reach €15 million or 3% of global turnover.

Research

Anthropic trains AI alignment auditors with reinforcement learning

Anthropic researchers trained LLM alignment auditors using reinforcement learning, finetuning a Haiku model to investigate target models with hidden behaviours planted in their system prompts. The best configuration matched Anthropic's Opus 4.6 on auditing quality, with false-positive calibration above 99%, and generalised to harder fine-tuned targets. Anthropic has published the paper and code.

The team found that simple pointwise rewards were reward-hacked — auditors learned to manufacture alarming findings — so they moved to pairwise rewards judged on investigations of the same target. Mixing in benign targets kept false positives low. The work aims to strengthen pre-deployment evaluation, which recent research such as AuditBench has shown can fail against adversarially hardened models.

Source: Anthropic Alignment, 15 Oct 2026

Frameworks

Google's Agent Development Kit now spans five languages, adds Kotlin 1.0

Google's Agent Development Kit now covers Python, Go, TypeScript, Java and Kotlin, with each version built for its own runtime and type system while sharing one architecture. Google also released ADK for Kotlin 1.0 as generally available, with Android-first, on-device extensions. All versions support the same open protocols, including a Model Context Protocol client and the Agent2Agent protocol.

Google says many agent frameworks assume a Python-only stack, forcing teams to run separate services or write their own orchestration. The multi-language ADK lets an agent run as a Go service on Cloud Run or inside a browser tab calling Chrome's on-device model, while agents written in different languages can interoperate over HTTP. A scaffold command generates projects with Agent2Agent endpoints and Cloud Run deployment settings.

RAG

NVIDIA releases Nemotron-3.5-Rerank-8B, an open multimodal reranker for vision RAG

NVIDIA published Nemotron-3.5-Rerank-8B on Hugging Face, an open 8.38-billion-parameter reranker that scores documents made of text, page images or both across 34 languages. It pairs a Pixtral-based vision encoder with a bidirectional Ministral3 language tower and is aimed at classical or agentic vision RAG pipelines. It outputs a single relevance logit per query-document pair.

Rerankers are the second stage of retrieval, re-ordering candidates pulled by a faster embedder before the top few reach the language model. Making the document an image matters for corpora where answers live in scanned pages, slides or charts, avoiding OCR errors. The model has no built-in score normalisation, and cost scales with the number of candidates scored, so NVIDIA suggests tiered reranking.

Source: explainx.ai, 15 Oct 2026

Kling 4.0 launches with 30-second single-shot video and video-reference control

Kuaishou's Kling 4.0 officially launched on 16 October, generating up to 30 seconds in one unbroken shot and accepting up to 10 reference frames. It can copy the camera work, framing and rhythm from a reference video while replacing the scene and characters, and holds motion physics better than the previous generation. Pricing is quoted at 450 credits for 30 seconds at 1080p.

A limited preview build, Kling 4.0 Flash, had run since late September for premium annual subscribers, so test results appeared quickly at launch. The jump to 30-second takes turns a single generation into a full scene beat rather than a clip, and the 10-frame reference system turns a set of stills into something closer to a storyboard. A 10-bit 4K HDR output mode has been announced but is not yet live.
