India Weighs ₹20,000 Crore National Frontier AI & Compute Fund

The government is considering an anchor investment of ₹15,000–20,000 crore in a proposed National Frontier AI & Compute Fund (NFAICF) that would finance frontier-model developers, GPU clusters and specialised data centres. The proposal was discussed at a closed-door Delhi meeting on “Financing India's Frontier AI & Compute Ecosystem” attended by MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan, Sarvam AI co-founder Vivek Raghavan, Peak XV's Rajan Anandan and E2E Networks CEO Tarun Dua. An anchor commitment near ₹20,000 crore would be almost twice the ₹10,372 crore outlay of the entire IndiaAI Mission.

The fund could be structured as a SEBI-regulated Category I AIF with a possible 50:50 government-private capital split, investing through venture and growth equity, convertible instruments, infrastructure equity, fund-of-funds commitments and direct co-investments, according to an Economic Times report. Officials are examining demand-linked compute financing — capacity commitments and pre-agreed commercial terms — to avoid stranded GPU capacity, and whether institutions like the International Finance Corporation could participate. The proposal remains under deliberation: structure, corpus and governance are not finalised. It would sit on top of the existing IndiaAI Mission, which has onboarded more than 38,000 GPUs through empanelled providers and is funding indigenous foundation models from Sarvam, Soket, Gnani and Gan AI.

Source: ET Government / Inc42, 25–26 September 2026

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic Near Launch of Joint AI Safety Body SAFA

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are finalising plans for an independent self-regulatory group tentatively dubbed the Standards Authority for Frontier AI (SAFA), which could launch by late 2026 or early 2027, The Information reported. The body would turn the labs' public safety pledges into testable benchmarks and support pre-deployment evaluation of frontier models.

The companies have approached former White House AI policy adviser Sriram Krishnan and former OSTP director Arati Prabhakar for the chief executive role, with former Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice under consideration for board chair. The push follows Demis Hassabis's proposal for a FINRA-style authority and Dario Amodei's call for an industry slowdown. Notably, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg and Nvidia's Jensen Huang have publicly opposed industry-wide coordination, preferring company-level responsibility. Critics warn self-set standards could entrench the big three and freeze out smaller open-source developers.

Source: The Information via TechRepublic / Times of India, 25 September 2026

Anthropic to Pay Akamai $11.6 Billion Over Seven Years in Cloud Deal

Anthropic will spend $11.6 billion over seven years on Akamai's cloud infrastructure — more than six times the size of their earlier $1.8 billion arrangement. Akamai also issued Anthropic a warrant for up to about 5% of its stock that vests as spending grows, and its shares rose as much as 17% in after-hours trading.

The commitment depends on Akamai meeting delivery and availability requirements, and either side can exit under certain conditions. Akamai expects $150–300 million of revenue from the deal in 2027, reaching an annual pace of roughly $1.7 billion by the end of 2028, and plans to spend about $5.5 billion building out capacity. The warrant structure flips the usual circular AI financing pattern — here the supplier gives its customer equity that grows with spending, similar to AMD's chip-milestone warrants with OpenAI. The deal could expand to about $20 billion in total.

Source: TechCrunch, 25 September 2026

Gemini 4 Enters Post-Training as Google Races to Catch Up

Google DeepMind's new chief Koray Kavukcuoglu said Gemini 4 is in post-training and he hopes to release a first version “much earlier” than the end of 2026, in his first public interview since taking over from Demis Hassabis. The disclosure comes after a September in which OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic released two flagship models while Google's public lineup stayed on the Gemini 3.8 generation.

Kavukcuoglu signalled a staged release: a first post-training output shipped “as soon as possible” rather than a fully polished model, followed by quick updates. Pre-training for Gemini 4 — described by Google's Logan Kilpatrick as the “most ambitious pre-training run yet” — began on July 21, meaning the project moved from base training to fine-tuning in just over two months. Google has given no date, benchmarks or pricing, and the unshipped Gemini 3.5 Pro episode is a cautionary precedent. Inside Google, engineers already use Gemini 4 to power the Antigravity agentic development environment.

White House Asks OpenAI and Anthropic to Hold Models Back from UK Testers

The Office of the National Cyber Director has asked OpenAI and Anthropic not to share their new models with the UK's AI Security Institute until the US government has tested them first, Politico reported. Anthropic has already withheld its Mythos 5.1 model from the UK institute, saying it is “only available to a set of U.S. organizations” for now.

A senior administration official told Politico the request reflects standing policy: “Because they're American companies and this has been our policy with every new frontier model that comes out.” UK institute director Henry de Zoete acknowledged the lack of access in a letter to a parliamentary committee, while noting the institute had tested OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra before release. The request lands the same week Prime Minister Andy Burnham used the UN General Assembly to call for a “single set of global principles and standards” for AI — a pitch the US has rejected, insisting it will encourage AI rather than rein it in.

Source: Politico via The Next Web, 25 September 2026

OpenAI's MentalHealthBench Exposes Gaps in AI Mental Health Support

OpenAI released MentalHealthBench, an open evaluation dataset built with more than 80 licensed psychologists and psychiatrists across 22 countries, spanning 1,215 synthetic conversations from everyday stress to emergencies. GPT-6 Astra led with a 57.3% task-clipped score, but every frontier model showed weaknesses in context-seeking and urgency calibration.

Experts across nearly 20 subspecialties crafted 5,262 rubric criteria weighted from –10 to +10 by clinical importance, with GPT-5.6 Sol as the automated grader across 10 behavioural dimensions. GPT-6 Sol scored 53.9% and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 52.4%, while older GPT-4o trailed at 32.1%. A companion survey of 44 adults who have used AI for emotional support revealed a rift: clinicians favour cautious context-gathering while users want rapid, practical next steps. OpenAI stresses the scores measure synthetic conversations, not real-world outcomes.

LangChain Interrupt: Engine v2, Managed Deep Agents and Agent Fine-Tuning

At its Interrupt 2026 conference in New York, LangChain shipped a suite of agent-infrastructure releases: LangSmith Engine v2 with proactive red teaming, Managed Deep Agents v0.8 with identity-scoped memory and built-in web search, and a fine-tuning CLI that turns production trajectories into custom models. The new smithtune CLI handles the full fine-tuning loop — dataset creation from trajectories, training via Fireworks or Baseten, and evaluation in LangSmith; on an internal code-review set, fine-tuning Qwen-3.8-27B lifted F1 from 48.9% to 53.7% while cutting model calls by nearly 30%. The moves cement what observers call the “harness pattern” as the baseline architecture for production agents.

Tencent Open-Sources 770B-Parameter Hunyuan Hy4 Preview

Tencent released the weights for Hy4 preview, its most advanced Mixture-of-Experts model, under the Apache 2.0 license. The model totals 770 billion parameters with 49 billion active per token and a one-million-token context window — more than twice the capacity of the previous-generation Hy3.

In Tencent's own evaluations — which should not be read as independent — Hy4 preview outperformed GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5, GLM 5.3, Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro on 10 of 12 benchmarks, and in an internal blind test of 203 engineering tasks scored 2.99/4.00 against 2.92 for GLM 5.3. BF16 and FP8 variants are downloadable from GitHub and Hugging Face and deployable with vLLM or SGLang; the model ranked third by tokens processed on OpenRouter's September 21 leaderboard. The release extends a wave of Chinese open-weight models, from DeepSeek V4 Pro to Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash.

Elasticsearch Adds GPU Vector Indexing: 138M Vectors in Under 10 Minutes

Elastic introduced GPU-accelerated vector indexing built on NVIDIA cuVS, offloading HNSW index construction to GPUs for up to 7x higher indexing throughput. In a benchmark, 138 million 1,024-dimensional vectors from MS MARCO — roughly 4 TB of multimodal documents — were indexed in under 10 minutes on eight RTX PRO 6000 GPUs, versus about an hour on CPUs.

The GPU-built indexes show the same recall profile as CPU-built ones, and because indexing moves off the CPU, search latency improved 6x when both run concurrently; force-merge time fell from roughly four hours to five minutes. The speed-up matters most for teams that rebuild indexes regularly — e-commerce catalogues refreshed nightly, or corpora re-embedded whenever an embedding model is updated — and for faster RAG pipelines and semantic search at petabyte scale.

Source: Elastic Search Labs, 25 September 2026

RAG

Perplexity Rebuilds Its Retrieval Stack: Photon and CobbleDB

Perplexity detailed Photon, a retrieval and ranking engine built from scratch whose new “fast preset” cut p99 retrieval-and-ranking latency from roughly 800 ms to 65 ms. The company also replaced Amazon DynamoDB with CobbleDB, a home-grown Rust key-value store that cut median batch-read latency from 31.4 ms to 5.6 ms and lowered storage costs by at least 20%.

Photon separates index building from query serving on different nodes, uses compact inverted indexes and a per-document “docblob” record so ranking needs one lookup per document, and stores 2.5 times more data per document than before. The Search API fast preset delivers 160 ms at p50 while cutting cost per task by an estimated 68%. CobbleDB's 40,000 lines of Rust were written in two months by two systems engineers working with an autonomous swarm of AI coding agents, and Perplexity plans to open-source the codebase.

Source: Perplexity blog / InfoQ, 24–25 September 2026
