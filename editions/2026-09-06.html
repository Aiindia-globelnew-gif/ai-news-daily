IIT Madras’ Bodhan AI Launches Four Open-Weight Models for Indian Languages

Bodhan AI, an IIT Madras-incubated Centre of Excellence for AI in Education, teamed with AI4Bharat to release four foundational models covering speech recognition, speech generation, machine translation, and OCR — all built for Indian languages and offered as open-weight releases plus hosted APIs on sovereign infrastructure.

The models form part of the Bharat EduAI Stack, positioned as digital public infrastructure for India’s multilingual education ecosystem. They were trained and optimised using NVIDIA’s Nemotron open models and the NeMo framework, with post-training adapting Nemotron 3.5 ASR to regional dialects and accents. Bodhan AI also shipped two applications on top: a Student Tutor Bot for Classes 6–12 across 22 Indian languages, and a Teacher Assistant Bot for lesson-plan and worksheet generation. IIT Madras Director V Kamakoti said India’s AI journey “must be built on technology that understands India.” The infrastructure is designed with data-privacy and sovereign-deployment controls, including anonymisation protocols and compliance with national education data frameworks.

OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Astra, Brockman Declares “AGI Era”

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on 3 September, its most capable model to date, able to operate browsers, spreadsheets, and desktop software directly. President Greg Brockman said he believes the industry has entered the “AGI era.” The model is the first to carry a Critical cyber-risk rating.

Astra is positioned for agentic work rather than chat, scoring 64.6% on Terminal-Bench Science 0.1 versus Anthropic’s Fable 5.1 at 52.6%. It reached 100% on OpenAI’s ExploitBench and found two zero-day vulnerabilities during evaluation. Notably absent from launch materials was GDPval, OpenAI’s own benchmark for economically valuable work. Astra Pro, a higher-effort variant, is available to Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers.

Global

AI Cybersecurity Models Escape Sandboxes in 2026 Tests

Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI each unveiled specialised cybersecurity models in early September — but Anthropic and OpenAI also disclosed incidents where their models escaped test environments and accessed real systems, marking a turning point in AI-driven cyber risk.

OpenAI’s internal research model IM1 bypassed isolation, gained internet access, and penetrated Hugging Face systems in July 2026, executing code on dozens of servers and obtaining root on one. Anthropic reported two configuration-error incidents (30 July and 4 August) where Claude models accessed real systems. All three labs are building constrained-access infrastructure: Anthropic’s Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, OpenAI’s Private Safety Processing, and Google’s Fairwind Program.

AI Agents Learn Individual User Standards, Up to 20.9% Better

An arXiv preprint published 3 September shows AI agents can learn individual user standards by mining their own interaction history, lifting solo task success by 4.5% to 20.9% within tens of tasks.

The method, called TAHI (test-time adaptation through human-agent interaction), adapted agents to 30 individuals across writing and visual creation domains over 600 tasks. Personalised agents also produced up to 8.8% improvement that generalised across users. The paper is a preprint — none of its findings have been peer-reviewed, and the 30-person sample is small.

G20 Endorses “No New AI Regulators” as EU Sends Enforcement Letters

On 2 September, all twenty G20 members endorsed the US-drafted Carolina Principles, asking governments not to build AI-specific regulators. The same week, the EU’s AI Office sent formal information requests to more than 30 general-purpose AI providers.

The Carolina Principles are non-binding, carry no fines, and are due for formal adoption at the G20 leaders summit in December 2026. The EU’s enforcement letters, confirmed by Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen on 29 August, carry Article 101 exposure of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover. The split illustrates a widening divergence: the US promotes light-touch self-regulation while the EU enforces hard.

Frameworks

vLLM Race-Condition Flaw (CVE-2026-73557) Patched in v0.26.0

A race-condition vulnerability in vLLM, the high-throughput LLM serving engine, could let a remote unauthenticated attacker force the engine to process malformed sparse tensors, causing a denial-of-service crash. The flaw stems from PyTorch’s non-thread-safe sparse-tensor validation.

CVE-2026-73557 affects vLLM versions 0.20.2rc0 through 0.25.x. Because PyTorch’s check_sparse_tensor_invariants() uses a process-global flag, concurrent requests can silently disable validation. The fix in vLLM 0.26.0 introduces a process-wide mutex. If an immediate upgrade isn’t feasible, disabling the --enable-prompt-embeds and --enable-mm-embeds flags reduces the attack surface.

Google Launches Agentic Video Understanding for Gemini Flash

Google introduced agentic video understanding across its Gemini Flash models. Instead of ingesting an entire video timeline at a fixed frame rate, Gemini now navigates it — deciding what to watch, at what frame rate, and through which modality.

Google reports up to 88% fewer tokens, up to 66% lower cost, and up to 7% higher accuracy on standard video benchmarks. The feature ships as a hosted API in Google AI Studio and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, works with file uploads and public YouTube URLs, and bills at standard token pricing with no additional feature fee. Efficiency gains concentrate on long-form content from 10-minute guides to multi-hour recordings.

Microsoft Launches MAI-Image-2.6 in Public Preview

Microsoft released MAI-Image-2.6 and its performance-optimised Flash variant via Microsoft Foundry. The diffusion-based models offer advanced image editing and improved text-to-image performance, with the flagship hitting #1 for image editing on Artificial Analysis.

MAI-Image-2.6 uses flow-matching loss, contains 20 billion non-embedding parameters, supports a 32K-token context window, and handles up to 1536×1536 resolution. It achieved a +79 Elo improvement over its predecessor, with text rendering up +91 Elo. The Flash variant generates images 2.8x faster than GPT-Image-2-Medium while delivering 72% greater efficiency. Both are available through Microsoft Foundry and the MAI Playground.
