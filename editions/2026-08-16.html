★ Top Story India

PM Modi announces AI training for 10 million youth

In his Independence Day address from the Red Fort, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced a programme to provide AI training to 10 million (one crore) youth over the next year, framing it as a pillar of the Viksit Bharat push.

The scheme marks a sharp expansion of India's existing AI skilling efforts, and Modi tied it to the country's broader ambition to become a developed economy by 2047. The announcement came alongside a separate ₹1 lakh crore Innovation Fund for startups unveiled the same day. With India's registered startup count now above 2.47 lakh, the government is pairing capital access with a workforce-readiness pitch aimed at keeping AI talent domestic.

Source: Business Standard, 15 August 2026

India

MeitY says no extension on DPDP Act deadline as startups scramble

MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan told a startup audience that there will be no further extension of the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act compliance deadline, urging firms to begin data-protection work immediately.

Speaking on 14 August, Krishnan stressed that the window for voluntary compliance has effectively closed and that the rules now bite for the firms handling personal data. The DPDP Act, passed in 2023, sets out consent, data-fiduciary and breach-notification obligations that many early-stage Indian companies have not yet fully operationalised. The firm stance signals that India's data-protection regime is moving from policy drafting to enforcement, with sectoral consequences for AI products that ingest personal data.

Source: Fortune India, 14 August 2026

India

Karnataka unveils AI hubs and one lakh jobs target by 2047

On Independence Day, Karnataka's Chief Minister announced new schemes to create one lakh jobs, build dedicated AI hubs, and steer the state toward a $3 trillion economy by 2047.

The Udyoga Sankalpa and Udyoga Nidhi schemes form the job-creation core, while a fresh push for AI in education signals Karnataka's intent to retain its position as India's tech capital. The AI hubs are intended to cluster research, startups and skilling in a single physical footprint, echoing the state's long-running electronics and IT cluster model. The plan was announced from Bengaluru on 15 August.

Source: The Hindu, 16 August 2026

Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash, its coding-and-agent workhorse

Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash on 13 August, calling it its most intelligent workhorse model yet for coding and agents, arriving just three weeks after the 3.6 Flash release.

Google describes 3.7 Flash as an algorithmic improvement built on top of 3.6 rather than a wholly new architecture or a fully disclosed training run. The accelerated cadence reflects a market where labs are now differentiating on speed and agent suitability rather than raw benchmark leaps, with Gemini 3.7 Flash pitched as the cheap-but-capable backbone for production agent workloads.

OpenAI previews Ultrafast tier running GPT-5.6 Sol at 750 tokens/sec

OpenAI and Cerebras Systems unveiled an Ultrafast API tier that runs the flagship GPT-5.6 Sol model at a claimed 750 output tokens per second, up to 14x faster than the Standard tier.

Ultrafast is not a new model; it runs the existing GPT-5.6 Sol on Cerebras hardware, leaving intelligence and context unchanged while changing only the latency profile. The tier is currently waitlist-only with no published price or general-availability date, but it signals that response speed is becoming a product feature businesses will pay for separately from model quality.

Source: TechTimes, 14 August 2026

Research

Google's Gemini Robotics 2 walks a humanoid from one set of weights

Google's latest vision-language-action model, Gemini Robotics 2, can walk a humanoid robot across a room, crouch to a low shelf, and grasp a light bulb, all from a single set of weights.

Google says it is the first model in its robotics family to run both locomotion (legs) and manipulation (hands) from one checkpoint, a simplification that moves it closer to true multi-embodiment control. The work points toward generalist robot policies that transfer across morphologies rather than being trained per-robot.

Source: DeepLearning.AI The Batch, 14 August 2026

Frameworks

DeepSeek open-sources Harness, a plugin-first agent runtime

DeepSeek open-sourced the DeepSeek Harness (dsh) under an MIT license, an extensible Node.js agent runtime where the model adapter, tool registry, session log and agent loop are all swappable plugins.

The harness ships four presets (Standard, Minimal, Code and Creator) and is built on the Cordis meta-framework for composability. It is model-agnostic, supporting Anthropic, OpenAI, AWS Bedrock, Azure, Google and custom OpenAI-compatible endpoints alongside DeepSeek's own. The release arrived alongside the general-availability of DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, which the company benchmarks at 87.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1 using Harness in minimal mode.

Source: The New Stack, 13 August 2026

Frameworks

Meta's Muse Glimmer runs a full agent on a single consumer GPU

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter model that runs entirely on one consumer GPU and outperforms Gemma4 and Qwen3.6 on agentic tasks, under an Apache 2.0 license.

With 4-bit quantisation the model compresses below 20GB, putting a capable agentic system within reach of a gaming-class machine. The release is part of Meta's broader open-weight push, which this week also included Muse Code, a command-line coding harness, and Muse Spark 1.2, the low-cost-per-task model behind it.

Source: Memeburn, 14 August 2026

RAG

Voyage AI launches voyage-code-4 embedding model for coding agents

Voyage AI released voyage-code-4, a next-generation code embedding model purpose-built for coding-agent retrieval, claiming it beats Cohere Embed v4 and Gemini Embedding 2 by wide margins.

On a new benchmark designed to measure how coding agents should retrieve code, voyage-code-4 outperforms Cohere Embed v4 and Gemini Embedding 2 by an average of 28.25% and 31.03% respectively, and by 19.21% and 16.01% across 28 standard code-retrieval datasets. The release targets the retrieval layer that increasingly determines whether agentic coding tools return the right context.

Source: Voyage AI Blog, 13 August 2026

RAG

MongoDB unifies vector search and agent memory in one platform

MongoDB announced integrated retrieval and memory capabilities aimed at closing the gap between agentic promise and production impact, combining vector search, session memory and metadata in a single datastore.

The company argues that stitching together separate databases, vector stores and caches is what breaks agent reliability, since a retrieval error compounds across later steps. By keeping agent memory and retrieval in one system, MongoDB is positioning its document model as the substrate for production agentic workloads rather than just a vector-search add-on.

Source: MongoDB Blog, 13 August 2026
