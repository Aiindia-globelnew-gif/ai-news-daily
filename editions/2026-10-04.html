India's AI innovation push widens as MeitY's Kerala centre of excellence opens with 15 startups

The MeitY-IndiaAI Centre of Excellence in Artificial Intelligence, Thiruvananthapuram (CAIK) was launched on September 30 with a first cohort of 15 AI startups and two partnerships, managed by Digital University Kerala under the IndiaAI Mission. Separately, Karnataka's Innoverse Foundation announced the first cohort of its DeepTech Enabler programme for growth-stage deep-tech firms.

CAIK's AI Startup Studio and Garage will run incubation and acceleration programmes over four years, offering startup-studio tools including an agentic AI platform and synthetic-data simulators, and plans to train 1,600 students, faculty and professionals and develop 40 responsible-AI prototypes. The inaugural cohort spans healthcare, climate and AgriTech, legal technology, enterprise and document AI, industrial and critical-infrastructure AI, Edge AI and indigenous camera and perception technologies. Innoverse's first cohort covers autonomous systems, indigenous navigation, regenerative medicine, orbital computing, counter-drone systems and solid propulsion, and targets doubling the cohort's combined annual revenue from about ₹35 crore to ₹70 crore and mobilising growth capital from roughly ₹150 crore to more than ₹300 crore.

Global

Frontier labs converge on $2/$10 as the October model price war sharpens

In a nine-day stretch, OpenAI, Anthropic and Google landed on identical $2 per million input and $10 per million output pricing for their mid-tier flagships — GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon — a sign the labs are pricing against each other in real time. The gap to top-end models, by contrast, has widened.

Google's Argon price is explicitly introductory, set to rise to $4 and $20 after an unannounced period, and the model is not yet broadly on sale — it is rolling out first to trusted cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind Program. OpenAI charges more for GPT-6.1 Sol prompts above 272,000 tokens, and effort settings can shift the bill, so headline rates are not the whole cost.

Policy

Trump orders US agencies to replace 'AI' with 'Super Intelligence' in official usage

A presidential order published on 2 October directs executive departments and agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports and policy documents, to the maximum extent permitted by law. It does not require altering previously issued regulations or historical documents.

Within 60 days, the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology is to submit proposed legislative language establishing a federal definition of "Super Intelligence" that reflects the capabilities described in the order, including any conforming amendments to existing statutory references. The order frames the terminology change as reflecting capabilities that "far exceed" what the term artificial intelligence first described.

Source: Federal Register, 2 Oct 2026

Policy

20 governments and the EU back frontier-AI controls as China issues governance framework 3.0

Twenty governments and the European Union adopted "A Call for Control of Frontier AI Models," urging human oversight, pre-deployment testing and the sharing of information on serious incidents, and inviting UN members to consider an international body to monitor compliance. China's National Cybersecurity Standardization Technical Committee released AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0.

China's framework classifies AI risks as inherent, application or derivative and sets out technical countermeasures and a regulatory sandbox mechanism, while the Supreme People's Court issued an opinion holding that generative AI use of names, images or voices without consent infringes personality rights. Australia introduced an AI Kill Switch and Data Centre Control Bill requiring providers of advanced systems to maintain the ability to stop inference and shut down systems, conduct red-teaming and report critical incidents within 24 hours. Italy signed a decree implementing EU AI Act provisions on liability.

Source: Tech Policy Press, 2 Oct 2026

Research

Meta's 'Thinking Before Thinking' lifts agentic performance without more budget

A Meta Superintelligence Labs paper, arXiv:2609.38147, adds a meta-reasoning controller that decides how an agent should spend its calls before it does the work. Holding workers and call budget fixed, it lifted ProgramBench for GPT-5.5 from 63.7% to 71.5% and beat matched direct control in every model-benchmark pairing tested.

The authors report average gains of 4.0 points on IMO ProofBench-Advanced, 4.2 on ARC-AGI-2 and 3.6 on LongCoT-mini across three models, and note the controller's own quality judgments outperformed worker self-confidence. The paper also flags a caveat: at low call budgets the approach can underperform, so the extra deliberation has to be worth its cost.

Source: arXiv:2609.38147, Meta Superintelligence Labs (reviewed 2 Oct 2026)

Frameworks

Aleph Alpha releases Kolibri, a sovereign open-weight model under Apache 2.0

Aleph Alpha released Kolibri, an English-German mixture-of-experts transformer with 78B total parameters and 3B active per token, supporting context lengths up to 1M tokens. The full weights are downloadable on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 licence.

Kolibri follows the smaller Kolibri Origin (30B total, 3B active, 65k context) and scaled in three months from 30B to 78B parameters, from a 65k-token context window to up to 1M, and from 7.5T to 20T training tokens. It is served via the aleph-alpha-inference package, which provides a vLLM plugin, and supports four reasoning effort levels.

Source: Aleph Alpha, 3 Oct 2026

Frameworks

Microsoft Agent Framework 1.20.0 adds Foundry hosting and vector-store connectors

Microsoft released version 1.20.0 of its Agent Framework Python library, letting developers host workflows over Foundry and connect agents to DuckDB and SQL Server vector stores. The Foundry hosting package runs native workflows over two Foundry interfaces, Responses and Invocations.

The core library gains response-stream gates and buffering, which control how an agent's streamed reply is handled, and session-scoped file-access isolation so file access is tied to a single session. Other additions include a typed dictionary for saving the state of an agent step and optional injection of a function-invocation context into skill scripts.

Source: Drop Agentic (Microsoft Agent Framework v1.20.0), 2 Oct 2026

RAG

Cohere launches Embed 5 for multimodal enterprise retrieval

Cohere released the Embed 5 family — embed-v5.0-pro and embed-v5.0-fast — with native multimodal support for text and images up to 2 million pixels, a 128K-token context window and coverage for over 100 languages. On the ViDoRe V3 benchmark, Embed 5 Pro scored 85.8, an 8.8-point gain over Embed 4.

The two tiers share a single embedding space, so developers can index a large corpus with the higher-quality Pro model and run low-latency queries with the Fast model. Using Matryoshka representation learning, both support output dimensions of 256 to 2048. Embed 5 is generally available via the Cohere API, Microsoft Foundry and Amazon SageMaker, priced at $0.12 per 1M text tokens for Pro and $0.08 for Fast.

Source: TheNextGenTechInsider, 3 Oct 2026

Vision

NVIDIA releases PixelUMM, an encoder-free multimodal model that works in pixel space

NVIDIA released PixelUMM, a 15.2B-parameter model that treats RGB images as raw 16×16 pixel patches rather than routing them through a separate pretrained vision encoder, unifying text, image and video understanding and generation in one decoder-only transformer. It is published under a non-commercial research licence.

The model uses a Qwen3-8B language backbone and supports text-to-image, image-to-text, video-text-to-text understanding and video generation, with iterative denoising for image and video output. It is intended for technical verification and research on shared representations spanning text, images and video, and requires a Linux environment, an NVIDIA GPU and CUDA-compatible PyTorch.

Source: Local Model Watch (NVIDIA PixelUMM), 2 Oct 2026

Vision

Black Forest Labs ships FLUX 3 Image with layout boxes and 4K output

Black Forest Labs released FLUX 3 Image on 1 October, adding bounding-box layout controls on a 0–1000 coordinate grid, composition from up to ten reference images and output up to 4K resolution. It is the image-generation and editing component of the broader FLUX 3 family the company first described in July.

BFL describes its editing as "pixel perfect," though an independent examination of the company's own samples found some changes outside the intended object, so teams should inspect their own output before using it for client work. Each reference image receives a token in order starting with ref_image_0, which means a production brief should give each reference a specific role to keep results reproducible.

Source: Lapaas Voice, 2 Oct 2026

Shopify launches Canvas, an AI design tool that builds stores by conversation

Shopify introduced Canvas, a conversational design interface powered by its Sidekick AI assistant that lets merchants describe desired changes in chat instead of dragging template blocks. The system renders live, executable code in real time rather than static image previews.

Merchants can inspect page animations, test interactivity across screen sizes and navigate the storefront with pan and zoom controls, while Sidekick captures automated screenshots to evaluate layout adjustments. The current release is a desktop beta; support for third-party themes, app extensions, localisation and multi-market configurations is planned for later. Shopify says Sidekick executed over 25 million theme modifications in the first half of 2026.

Contentstack launches Canoe to show brands what AI engines tell their buyers

Contentstack launched Canoe on 1 October, a tool that scans a brand's own website, drafts the questions buyers are likely to ask and runs them across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity to show whether and how the brand appears. It reports which of up to ten named competitors appeared instead and which sources the engines cited.

Paid plans include a remote MCP server so Contentstack's own Agent OS, Claude, Cursor and other MCP clients can read Canoe reports directly, draft fixes for a team to review and re-run the analysis to measure what changed. A Free tier runs ChatGPT and Gemini on a monthly cycle, the Growth plan adds Perplexity and Claude at $279 per month, and an Enterprise plan is scheduled for early 2027.
