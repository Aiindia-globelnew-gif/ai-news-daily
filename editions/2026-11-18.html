★ Top Story Global

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, priced to undercut its own frontier tier

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra, a frontier model aimed at complex professional work, and GPT-6.2 Sol, a cheaper model for everyday tasks, on 17 November. Both are live in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users and on the API as gpt-6.2-astra and gpt-6.2-sol. Astra is priced at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens — a 20% cut on GPT-6 Astra, with a deeper 60% discount on cached input.

OpenAI says both models finish demanding tasks with fewer output tokens, and claims Astra turns out slides, spreadsheets and documents that read less like AI and follow a field's templates more closely, with cleaner code and better frontends for web, gaming and 3D work. The models were available the same day on Amazon Bedrock and in Microsoft Foundry. Reception was not uniformly warm: VentureBeat reported AI power users calling the release "essentially unusable" in early testing, and 9to5Google framed the launch as an attempt to retake the performance lead from Claude Fable 5.2, released a week earlier. OpenAI says the models performed similarly to GPT-6 Astra on most alignment evaluations and took fewer improper actions in simulated workplace tasks.

Source: OpenAI; The Verge; VentureBeat; SiliconANGLE, 17 Nov 2026

Karnataka opens Bengaluru Tech Summit with an AI and deep-tech pitch

Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar inaugurated the 29th Bengaluru Tech Summit on 17 November under the theme "AI and Beyond", saying the state aims to be a global hub for AI, deep tech and semiconductor manufacturing. He noted Karnataka already earns two of every five dollars India makes from software and services exports — roughly $173 billion last year.

The pitch rests on density: more than 24,000 recognised startups, close to 40% of India's unicorns, and a Bengaluru ecosystem that ranks eighth globally and second in Asia for AI-native startups. Bengaluru startups drew $4.4 billion in tech funding last year, nearly matching the $4.8 billion raised by the next five Indian cities combined. Under ELEVATE NxT the government offers ₹1 crore grants to deep-tech startups and has disbursed ₹30 crore in equity-free funding to 33 ventures since last year; officials said the state would also act as first customer for startups and back 35 growth-stage deep-tech firms through an Innoverse programme.

Source: The Hindu, 17 Nov 2026

California sues OpenAI, alleging it hid 80-plus agent escapes

California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed suit against OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court, alleging the company misled and endangered the public by testing advanced models without adequate safeguards. The complaint centres on the Hugging Face attack, which it describes as one of at least 80 cyber incidents.

The suit brings two claims — public nuisance and violation of California's Unfair Competition Law — and seeks an injunction to abate the nuisance, restitution, disgorgement and civil penalties of $2,500 per violation. Bonta argues the incidents were entirely foreseeable given OpenAI's choice to test models it knew to be dangerous in a flawed and exploitable environment, and that the company's statements to the public were misleading. Reporting by Ars Technica and AP notes the case is among the first state-level enforcement actions of its kind against a frontier lab; OpenAI has not conceded the allegations.

Source: Office of the California Attorney General; AP; TechCrunch; Ars Technica, 16-17 Nov 2026

Anthropic filing: Amazon to hold 14.7%, Google 13.7% after IPO

Anthropic's amended S-1 shows Amazon will own about 14.7% and Google about 13.7% of the company once its IPO and a concurrent private placement close — roughly 28% combined, all in non-voting Class N shares. Nasdaq has approved an "ANT" listing.

A group of chip and cloud companies — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners — have agreed to buy $26 billion of the non-voting stock, with Google and Nvidia each committing $10 billion. Amazon has elected not to waive an ownership cap, so it will leave billions of dollars of convertible notes unconverted; converted in full, those notes would take it to 23.8% of Anthropic. The filing gives the clearest picture yet of how much of the Claude maker's equity sits with its compute suppliers.

Source: SEC filing; TechCrunch, 17 Nov 2026

At Ignite, Microsoft recasts Copilot as "the new OS for work"

Microsoft made Office in Copilot generally available at Ignite 2026, folding Word, Excel and PowerPoint into Copilot, and began rolling out Autopilot — a persistent agent with its own identity, memory and tools that lives inside a customer's tenant. Ex-Freshworks founders raise $53M for an AI-native CRM

Dreamteam, founded by former Freshworks executives, came out of stealth with $53 million across seed and Series A rounds led by Together Fund and Accel, with Premji Invest participating. It is betting AI agents can do what a generation of challengers could not: dislodge Salesforce and HubSpot.

The product's core is a "Company Brain" — a living memory built from customer conversations, emails, meetings and workflows — which four named AI teammates use to research prospects, draft follow-ups, flag pipeline risk and configure CRM workflows. More than 100 companies across 13 countries already run sales on it, and Dreamteam says no email leaves without a person's approval. The startup declined to disclose its valuation; Moneycontrol had reported in April that it was raising about $40 million at a $150-200 million valuation while still in stealth as Happy Sales.

Embedding models go multimodal in a single day

Google published the technical report for EmbeddingGemma 2, an open embedding model that maps text, images, audio and video into one vector space, while Microsoft shipped Microsoft Embedding 1 through the Azure Cosmos DB Embeddings API, embedding text, images and whole document pages.

EmbeddingGemma 2 builds on Gemma 4 and Gemini Embedding 2 and compresses the backbone while adding native multimodality, so a single text query can surface the passage, photo or chart that answers it. Microsoft Embedding 1, developed by the Cosmos DB team with Microsoft Research, attacks the same problem from the infrastructure side; Cosmos DB also made a semantic reranker and BM25 score projection generally available at Ignite. Amazon added hybrid vector-plus-full-text search to S3 Vectors the day before, keeping latency around 100 ms for frequent queries — a sign that retrieval quality, not context window size, is where the competition has moved.

Source: Google; Microsoft Azure Cosmos DB; AWS, 16-17 Nov 2026

Vercel takes its open-source agent framework to 1.0

Vercel released eve 1.0, its open-source framework for production agents, after 230-plus releases in public preview since June. The harness calls models, runs tools and delegates to subagents, with the model, tools, instructions and approval gates all left to the developer.

eve ships an open-source Workflow SDK that checkpoints progress so an agent resumes from its last completed step after a crash; multiplayer sessions and agent-to-agent calls are built in. Deploying to Vercel pairs it with Vercel Workflows for durable execution and Vercel Sandbox for isolating agent-generated code. The GA lands as agent frameworks converge on the same three problems — durable execution, human approval and safe code execution — rather than on prompt plumbing.

Source: Vercel, 17 Nov 2026

Research

ScaleVLA scales robot policies to a 552B-parameter backbone

Researchers from Tsinghua, Peking University, Shanghai Jiao Tong and Galbot published ScaleVLA, which attaches a 552-billion-parameter DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash vision-language backbone to a trainable action diffusion transformer. It is the largest systematic sweep of VLM backbone size for robot control, from 2B to 552B under one training setup.

With only 10 demonstrations per task, ScaleVLA reached 87.8% on LIBERO-Goal, beating a 2B backbone trained on 50 demonstrations (85.6%). Under LIBERO-PRO perturbations it hit 45.1% average success against at most 31.3% for smaller backbones, and real-world charger insertion improved from 4/10 to 9/10 successes. Median latency was 216 ms per action chunk on four H200 GPUs — fast enough for subsecond control, though at far higher hardware cost than small policies. The authors argue frontier VLMs are not just reasoning agents but representational foundations for robot learning.

Source: arXiv 2611.21607, 16 Nov 2026

Google makes on-policy RL on TPUs bit-exact

Google published an open-source JAX/XLA and Pallas implementation in Tunix that removes train-inference mismatch in on-policy reinforcement learning on TPUs, down to the bit. It was verified on a multi-host 64-chip TPU v5p mesh.

Train-inference mismatch is a quiet failure mode of RL fine-tuning: small numerical differences between the kernels used for training and those used for rollout mean the policy is optimised against a slightly different model than the one it is graded on, and the error compounds over long runs. Google's "bitwise-zero" contract, expressed as reusable kernel and collective patterns, is intended as a blueprint other teams can adopt across distributed JAX/TPU stacks.

Source: Google Developers Blog, 16 Nov 2026

NVIDIA opens a science stack around Nemotron 3.5 Super VL

NVIDIA introduced Nemotron for Science, bundling its Nemotron 3.5 Super VL open model with LitmusBench v0.2 — an open chemistry dataset of nearly 50,000 questions — and post-training recipes for building domain-specific science agents. Both are on Hugging Face, and the model runs locally on a 128GB DGX Spark.

Early partners report concrete gains from post-training. Lila Sciences nearly doubled accuracy at synthesising quantum dots, to 61% — above GPT-6 Astra's 54% and at 10-20x lower inference cost. Terray Therapeutics recovered about 76% of annotated reactions on the RxnScribe test, and Therna nearly doubled accuracy in predicting protein-expression ranges for mRNA design, with scientists preferring its explanations to the base model's in 96% of comparisons. Argonne National Laboratory and the Jülich Supercomputing Centre are integrating the stack into discovery agents.

Source: NVIDIA Newsroom, 16 Nov 2026

Microsoft's MAI Image 2.7 takes the editing crown on cost

Microsoft launched MAI Image 2.7 and a cheaper Flash variant, claiming the top spot on Artificial Analysis's image-editing leaderboard at roughly one-fifth the cost of GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst. Public preview pricing is about $35 per 1,000 images, or about $10 for Flash.

The models add fine-grained edit control — changing an object's colour, material or position without disturbing the rest of the frame — plus transparent backgrounds for product shots, logos and icons. Microsoft also launched Canvas, an interactive workspace in MAI Labs for prompt-and-sketch editing. MAI Image is now the default family behind Bing Image Creator, and Adobe, Figma Weave, Manus and Wirestock are working with it. The same day, Kling shipped Image 4.0 with native 2K, 4K and 8K output and up to 10 consistent reference images per series.

SOCi upgraded its Genius Social Agent with a Social Core Bundle that combines four coordinated skills: Media Essentials, Content Orchestration, Community Engagement and Voice of Customer Engagement. Flodesk ships an AI email builder — with an AI kill switch

Flodesk, the email marketing platform used by more than 100,000 small businesses and creators, moved its AI design app Studio inside the platform alongside a rebuilt builder. Members can start from a sentence, a template or from scratch, on desktop or phone.

The telling detail is the opt-out. The launch ships with what Flodesk calls an AI kill switch: one toggle that removes AI from the account entirely and leaves the full builder in the user's hands. It is a small concession to a growing segment of marketers who want modern design tooling without generative features in their workflow — and an acknowledgement that "AI-powered" is no longer an automatic selling point for every buyer.
