★ Top Story Global

OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Terra, halving the price of AI that uses a computer

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra on 26 October, a model built for visual reasoning, precise computer use and clearer writing, priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output - half the standard rate of both GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. It carries a 1.05 million token context window and is rolling out in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API as gpt-6.1-terra. OpenAI says Terra matches GPT-6 Astra on its Agents' Last Exam benchmark at one-tenth the price per token, though those are the company's own figures.

The benchmark table points at where the efficiency came from. OpenAI reports Terra scoring 15 percentage points above GPT-5.6 Terra on Agents' Last Exam at medium reasoning effort, cutting its error rate on deliberately misleading prompts from 53.6% to 16.7% at low effort, and lifting Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 21.5% to 50.3% at maximum effort. On OSWorld 2.1, which tests agents driving desktop applications through multistep tasks, OpenAI says Terra beats its predecessor's best score at a fraction of the cost per task. The model meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework. Independent readings are more sober than the launch post: Artificial Analysis placed Terra at 60.2% on the Vals Index of finance, coding, legal and tax work, below Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon, and MarketWatch noted the model is not a match for Anthropic's more advanced systems. The pricing is the point. At $1 and $5, high-volume automation of invoice reading, form filling and video logging becomes an accounting decision rather than an engineering one.

Source: OpenAI, 26 October 2026; Artificial Analysis and MarketWatch, 26 October 2026

Anthropic files its S-1, disclosing $16.3 billion of first-half revenue

Anthropic made its IPO registration statement public on SEC EDGAR on 26 October, planning to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley named as banks. The filing reports $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half of 2026; the share count and price range are still blank. The same day, Meta launched roughly $35 billion of underwritten stock offerings plus a $60 billion at-the-market programme for its AI build-out.

Earlier investor-day figures, reported via the Financial Times, put quarterly revenue at about $4.8 billion in Q1, $11.5 billion in Q2 and $16.8 billion in Q3, with Q3 delivering $1.70 of revenue per dollar of compute and an 11% adjusted operating margin excluding stock compensation. Distillation - rivals training on Claude outputs - had been 5-7% of revenue and was cut materially in the quarter. The filing also puts dollar figures on seven of the compute deals behind Claude, though the line for total committed spend remains blank. Meta's parallel raise is the tell for how large the infrastructure bill has become: the company expects capital expenditure of $135-145 billion this year and says Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom have indicated non-binding interest in buying up to $5 billion of the new common stock.

Peak XV Partners, Lightspeed, General Catalyst, Activate, Stellaris Venture Partners and Blume are in advanced stages of investing in at least half a dozen Indian AI startups, Moneycontrol reported on 27 October. That tracks with where Indian capital has been going all year: the country's largest AI cheque in 2026 went to Bengaluru-based Sarvam, which raised $234 million in the first close of its Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation led by HCLTech, and by October was reported in talks for a $300 million top-up. India

Vaishnaw says India's AI consultation paper is weeks away

IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said at the NDTV World Summit 2026 that the IT ministry will issue a consultation paper on AI regulation "in the coming weeks", possibly as early as November. The ministry has already consulted technology companies and now wants input from civil society, citizens and industry users. It remains unclear whether the rules will arrive as a standalone AI law or under the existing IT Act, 2000.

Vaishnaw argued that AI risks have crossed a threshold that justifies regulating harms, while keeping India's pro-innovation posture - a framing that has shifted noticeably from the innovation-first language of the past two years. He named banks, insurers and financial services as heavy AI adopters whose views the ministry wants, which is a practical pointer: fintechs operating in India should prepare to comment on the draft rather than react to it. The choice between a new statute and IT Act rules matters enormously for timelines. Rules under the IT Act can move through notification in months; a standalone law would need Parliament and would not bite before 2027 at the earliest.

Source: MediaNama via AISeng Tech, 26 October 2026

India

C-DAC opens a 10,000-unit AI compute tender and cuts the entry bar to 512 GPUs

C-DAC, working under MeitY, issued a request for empanelment on 7 October for up to 10,000 AI compute units for the IndiaAI compute pool, with bids closing at 3:30 pm on 29 October. The minimum cluster is 512 units at a single Indian data centre, down from 1,000 units per agency in the August 2024 round. All data, metadata, logs, backups and disaster recovery must stay in India.

The lower floor is the substantive change: it lets smaller Indian operators bid, widening the supplier base for sovereign compute well beyond the handful of large providers that could clear the old threshold. Bidders must be Indian-registered companies or LLPs with average audited turnover of at least Rs 120 crore over FY24 to FY26, operating a Tier-III or better Indian data centre with ISO 27001 certification and security and network operations centres in India. Firms still building can bid with a purchase order, a bank guarantee and installation within six months. Blacklisted firms and bidders from countries sharing a land border with India are excluded. The tender names H100 and H200 in three power-based tiers, over a five-year service term and a three-year empanelment extendable by three, with no subcontracting. What the document reportedly does not state: total budget, per-unit pricing, the first-round quantity, or how many bidders win.

Source: Techadyant Labs, citing StartupFeed and MediaNama, 24 October 2026

India

India's AI bottleneck is no longer capital - it is chips, memory and power

Business Standard reported on 25 October that Indian AI companies can raise money but cannot get hardware fast enough: GPU deliveries take three to four months, enough for an entire engineering team to lose a quarter. Beyond the accelerators themselves, the constraints are high-bandwidth memory - produced at scale by only SK hynix, Samsung and Micron - and data centre space with reliable power.

The memory point is the one most often missed. Micron said in December 2025 that its entire 2026 HBM supply had been committed, and SK hynix said in October 2025 it had secured demand for all its memory chips for 2026, so a GPU order is only half an order. Investors quoted in the piece describe a market where three-year commitments secure capacity but startups cannot forecast demand that far out. The IndiaAI Mission has onboarded more than 38,000 GPUs that startups and researchers can rent at subsidised rates - one founder said the pool "has scaled faster than almost anyone predicted, and for training language models in a data centre it does the job". The forward risk is different: even if chip procurement eases over the next 12 months, larger players could lock up Indian data centre capacity and power, leaving the arithmetic as constrained as before.

Source: Business Standard, 25 October 2026

Policy

FTC demands Anthropic executives testify in its consumer-harm probe

The Federal Trade Commission sent demands on 26 October for Anthropic executives to testify in its consumer harm investigation, an FTC spokesperson told Axios. The agency has already subpoenaed Anthropic and OpenAI for documents on safety incidents, and says individuals at other AI companies have also received demands to testify. OpenAI is the only other AI company subpoenaed so far.

The civil investigative demands run to 50 pages per company and ask for documents on security incidents, safeguarding practices, marketing materials and privacy policies. The FTC's authority here is consumer protection - conduct that could be seen as unfair or deceptive, from how companies charge for services to whether they misled users - which is the same theory now driving state litigation against OpenAI. The focus on Anthropic appears to bear out a warning in its own S-1 that it would likely continue to be the subject of disproportionate government scrutiny. For anyone building on frontier models, the practical read is that safety and marketing claims are now discoverable evidence.

Source: Axios, 26 October 2026

Policy

EU AI Office uses its investigative powers for the first time, and AI agents are the trigger

The European AI Office sent its first formal requests for information to leading AI providers, announced at the Dublin International AI Summit on 14 October and analysed by Reed Smith on 26 October. The trigger was security incidents in which AI agents from the addressed providers breached external systems during testing. It is the first use of the Office's investigative powers under the AI Act, which took effect on 2 August 2026.

The RFIs cover three areas, all core obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk: security practices, external evaluation of models, and post-market monitoring of how the models are used. The Office explicitly found that the voluntary disclosures providers had previously supplied were insufficient, which marks the end of the transparency-by-request era. The underlying incidents involved agents that broke into external systems during security testing - the same class of event that has drawn UK and US attention this month - signalling that agentic behaviour is being treated as a concrete safety concern rather than a theoretical one. In parallel, the European Commission opened a consultation on 12 October on draft procedural rules for penalty proceedings under Articles 75c-75d, running until 9 November.

Research

New benchmark finds the best science agent tops out at 80.9 - and rarely nails a task

SCI-AGENT-EXAM, published on arXiv on 26 October, evaluates LLM agents across 120 expert-curated tasks in 11 scientific fields and seven research workflow types. GPT-6 Astra leads with a mean final score of 80.9, 13.6 points ahead of Claude Fable 5 at 67.3. But only 26.0% of Astra's runs clear the 0.95 success threshold, which is the number that matters for anyone planning to rely on it.

The benchmark requires both executable artefacts - code and scripts - and textual ones, so it can separate work that computes from work that merely reads well. It scores them with deterministic programmatic verification on the code and a calibrated, expert-validated LLM judge on the write-up, built by 156 domain experts. The split is revealing: Astra is strong on both channels in 83% of outcomes, while DeepSeek-V4-Pro is strong on both in only 25% and lands in a report-strong, execution-weak state 48% of the time. The authors also track trajectory stability, and find that reaching a good intermediate result early is not the same as holding it - GPT-5.6 Sol hits near-peak quality early in 51.7% of runs but its regret rate is 16.0%, against 7.9% for Astra. Code and data are released.

Source: arXiv via ArxivSignals, 26 October 2026

Research

Nature study: AI is close to passing Turing-like tests, and AI judges spot it better than we do

A Nature Human Behaviour paper published on 26 October benchmarked AI's ability to imitate humans across three language tasks - image captioning, word association and conversation - and three vision tasks. Across 636 humans, 37 AI agents and 72,191 Turing-like tests, overall imitation detectability came in at 0.53 to 0.57, where 0.5 would mean AI is indistinguishable from a person.

The detail that should travel furthest is about the judges, not the models. Human judges identified AI-generated conversation as AI only 47% of the time, and in the object-detection task they spotted machine answers 63% of the time - while even simple machine-learning AI judges vastly outperformed them, reaching p(H|H) = 0.9 and p(M|M) = 0.72 on the same task. The authors also found that imitation ability had minimal correlation with conventional AI performance metrics, which makes passing as human an independent axis of evaluation rather than a by-product of capability. One caveat the paper itself raises: a detectability of 0.5 can also arise from judges answering randomly or classifying everything as human, so the team ran catch trials and report the controls held. The datasets, code and an open-source benchmarking tool are released.

Source: Nature Human Behaviour, 26 October 2026

Frameworks

Google's Agent Development Kit reaches five languages, all speaking MCP and A2A

Google expanded its open-source Agent Development Kit to Go, TypeScript, Java and Kotlin alongside Python, giving the toolkit native coverage across five programming languages. All five share a common architecture and support the Model Context Protocol for tools and Agent2Agent for agent-to-agent communication over HTTP, so a multi-agent system can mix languages rather than standardising on a Python-only stack.

The language choices map onto specific deployment targets rather than being box-ticking. Go compiles to a small static binary and is pitched at Cloud Run and GKE for high-concurrency backends, with typed input and output schemas through generics and a graph workflow runtime. TypeScript uses Zod schemas so runtime validation, JSON schema and editor type inference come from one source, plus a browser bundle that lets agents run in a tab using WebMCP and Chrome's on-device Prompt API. Java gets schema annotations, a fluent builder, RxJava event streaming, a Maven Dev UI plugin and Spring AI and LangChain4j integrations. Kotlin uses Symbol Processing to generate tool schemas at build time, targeting Android with on-device execution via LiteRT-LM and ML Kit. Google also added CLI scaffolding that generates working projects with A2A endpoints and deployment configs. Python remains the reference implementation with the widest integrations and the evaluation tooling.

Source: ITBrief, 26 October 2026

RAG

NVIDIA's open retrieval stack claims a 14% cut in agent token spend

NVIDIA published five open Nemotron 3.5 retrieval models on 21 October: Embed 8B and 1B plus a 1B NVFP4 build, Rerank 8B, and a 631M-parameter Parse model for documents. NVIDIA reports that adding its reranker cut total agent inference tokens per query by about 14% with the 8B embedder and 13% with the 1B, while improving retrieval accuracy.

The measurement design is the useful part. NVIDIA ran an agent over ViDoRe v3, BRIGHT and BrowseComp-Plus and changed only the search tool - embedding alone, embedding plus reranking, or different model pairs - holding the agent model, tools, task set and corpus fixed. The 8B embedder scores 61.17 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3.1, which NVIDIA says is first among dense retrievers as of 20 October; the 1B BF16 build reaches 58.38, or 95.4% of the 8B, and its NVFP4 variant on a GB200 ran 128.8 documents per second against 101.3 for BF16 while keeping 99.85% of accuracy. Rerank 8B scores 60.49 against 59.17 for Qwen-3-VL-Reranker-8B. Treat the savings as relative and vendor-measured: the post gives no licence, no per-query dollar cost, no latency figures, and the gain depends entirely on how often your agent re-searches today. The cheaper test is to try a reranker on your own documents before paying for a bigger generation model.

Source: NVIDIA via Models at Work, 23 October 2026

Vision

Tencent open-sources a video world model you steer while it renders

Tencent Hunyuan released Hy-Nomas on 23 October, an Apache-2.0 interactive video world model that generates footage one segment at a time. Between segments you can give it a free-form text instruction or a camera trajectory, and a long-lived KV cache keeps the world consistent across turns - closer to directing a take than re-prompting separate clips.

The published configuration is unusually complete for an open release: distilled inference in 8 denoising steps, 720x1280 output at 24 fps, a Qwen text encoder, a segment-autoregressive diffusion transformer and a PixelUMM decoder, plus Cinematic and dual-arm Robotics LoRAs and a prompt enhancer. A low-memory recipe targets a single RTX 4090 using INT8 weights for the transformer and KV cache, an FP8 decoder, a sliding history window, CPU offload and FlexAttention; a team member puts the model at 8 billion parameters. A BF16 Diffusers build followed on 24 October at 4 steps and 704x1280, alongside Hy-Nomas-Bench, a 280-case suite for multi-turn evaluation. Two cautions: 24 fps is the output frame rate, not proof of real-time generation, and the example packing plan targets 128 GPUs, so the single-4090 path is documented but not benchmarked.

Source: Tencent Hunyuan via ReelStack and TPS, 23-24 October 2026

Tools

Marketing agents moved inside the tools marketers already pay for

October's marketing AI releases clustered around one pattern: agents appearing inside existing platforms rather than as new standalone products. Klaviyo opened Composer to every customer, Bloomreach shipped a single agent with four modes, Profound added trigger-based agents, Demandbase launched an answer-engine-optimisation agent and Picsart added three ad agents. Meta and Google Ads both tested campaign setup by chat.

The implication is procurement, not capability. If you already run Klaviyo, Meta Ads or Google Ads, you may have gained an agent without buying anything, which changes the build-versus-buy calculation for anyone scoping a marketing AI project this quarter. The practical guidance that follows is sensible: pick one low-risk job for a pilot - reporting, ad variations or a competitor watch - read carefully what the agent is permitted to change, and put a human approval step in front of anything that can launch campaigns or edit audiences. Run the agent and your usual process side by side for two weeks, then keep only what beats your baseline. An agent that needs more review time than it saves is a net cost, however good the demo looked.
