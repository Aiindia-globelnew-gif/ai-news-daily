★ Top Story Global

Google releases Gemini 4 Argon, its first frontier model since Gemini 3

Google has launched Gemini 4 Argon globally for developers, enterprises and paid consumers, claiming it outperforms rival frontier models on coding, cybersecurity, science, finance and legal benchmarks. The model ships with an industry-leading 1M unified input-output token limit and an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — roughly a fifth the cost of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic's Fable 5.

Argon arrives after months in which Google trailed Anthropic and OpenAI at the frontier, and it lands a day after Meta's Muse Wonder Full. The Verge reports Google says Argon beats GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 across a range of benchmarks, and that Google is already using it internally — to find and patch cybersecurity vulnerabilities, halve the compute needed for YouTube's comments section, and debug a drone physics simulator for Wing. The model is rolling out to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, and to Ultra subscribers on the agent-first development platform Antigravity; it is not yet clear when free or Plus users will get access. Prices double to $4 and $20 per million tokens after the introductory period ends on 30 November 2026.

Source: The Verge / Google Blog, 13 October 2026

India

Sarvam AI in talks to raise up to $300 million at a $3-billion valuation

India's Sarvam AI is reportedly in talks to raise $250–300 million in fresh funding at a $3-billion valuation, roughly double the $1.5-billion mark it reached four months ago. If completed, the round would make the Bengaluru startup India's most valuable AI company.

The Economic Times reports Sarvam raised more than $300 million in June, led by HCLTech with $150 million, with participation from Bessemer Venture Partners, Peak XV Partners and Khosla Ventures; a later $75-million extension drew Nvidia, Glade Brook Capital, Gaja Capital and IndiGo Ventures. Founded in 2023, Sarvam builds Indian-language models and applications for government and enterprise clients including LIC, SBI Life, IDFC First Bank, Tata Capital and Cred, and was among the first companies selected under the IndiaAI Mission to develop indigenous foundation models.

Source: Economic Times / ET EnterpriseAI, 13 October 2026

India

IndiaAI Mission lets GPU bidders sell compute abroad as C-DAC tenders 10,000 units

Fourteen companies that won bids to onboard GPUs under the ₹10,372-crore IndiaAI Mission will be allowed to sell compute capacity globally at commercial rates, after the government accepted an industry request to keep providers profitable while domestic demand matures. Separately, C-DAC has issued a tender for up to 10,000 AI compute units.

Bidders include Jio Platforms, Tata Communications, Yotta Data Services, CtrlS, E2E Networks, Locus and NxtGen. Officials said overseas customers cannot be offered the subsidised L1 rates — which Business Standard puts at nearly ₹115 per GPU hour, and about ₹140 for Nvidia H100s — because Indian demand is currently low. In the latest bidding round the government will buy five years of GPU time from winners and allocate it to startups, researchers and academia. The C-DAC Request for Empanelment requires all infrastructure, data, logs and backups to stay inside India, with capacity organised in clusters of at least 512 units per data centre.

Source: ET Datacenters / Medianama, 13 October 2026

India

Government commits ₹7,131 crore to 19 fund managers under ₹1 lakh crore RDI scheme

The government has committed ₹7,131 crore to the first group of 19 fund managers selected under its ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation scheme, which prioritises artificial intelligence, robotics, quantum, space and biotech. The group comprises 14 alternative investment funds and five research organisations.

Managers include Blume Ventures, Chiratae Ventures, Kalaari Capital, Speciale Invest, YourNest, IvyCap, Ideaspring and TVS Capital, alongside research bodies such as ARTPARK at IISc Bengaluru, C-CAMP, IIT Madras Research Park, BITS Pilani's PIEDS and IIT Bombay's SINE. The Empowered Group of Secretaries approved the first group at its 8 October meeting, per a government release dated 12 October. Selected managers are expected to raise private capital matching the fund's contribution; the scheme is managed within the Anusandhan National Research Foundation under the Department of Science and Technology.

Source: Times of India, 13 October 2026

Global

Meta unveils Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full frontier models

Meta introduced the Muse Wonder series — Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full — which it calls its most capable models yet, claiming state-of-the-art performance on personal and professional agentic tasks. Meta says the models rival those of OpenAI and Anthropic and bring it "to the frontier".

Muse Wonder is available through the Meta Model API and Muse Code, with Muse Wonder Full for Muse Code subscribers. The launch came a day before Google's Gemini 4 Argon release.

Policy

UK sets out new AI defences and resumes pre-release model testing

Britain announced a package of measures to defend against growing AI risks, including putting frontier AI to work on cyber defences, exploring stronger legal protections and preparing for serious incidents involving autonomous AI agents. Scientists at the UK AI Security Institute are again evaluating the latest generation of advanced models.

The plan builds on two pilots that identified and fixed more than 1,000 potential cyber vulnerabilities across government systems. The government will explore strengthening the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill to cover AI loss-of-control risk, where autonomous systems evade human oversight, and will work with the National Cyber Security Centre on how AI-agent incidents should be reported, including near-misses. The announcement followed a US policy reversal that restored UK access to pre-release OpenAI and Anthropic models; AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said: "If you cannot adequately control the risks, do not release the system."

Source: GOV.UK, 12 October 2026

Policy

EU AI Office requests information after "loss of control" incidents

The European AI Office has requested and received serious-incident reports and sought information about the security practices of companies whose models were involved in recent incidents the Commission describes as involving "loss of control". It has not publicly identified the companies or models and has not established an AI Act infringement.

Further information requests will combine questions prepared by the AI Office with those of the independent Scientific Panel, the 60-expert group supporting AI Act enforcement, which held a special meeting on 9 October. Experts recommended stronger governance of internal models used during development and testing, greater European capacity to detect and investigate incidents independently of companies, and closer scrutiny of training techniques that make model behaviour harder to monitor. The Commission has announced no sanction or corrective measure at this stage.

Source: 2eu.brussels, 13 October 2026

Research

Agents' Last Exam finds below-1% full-pass rates on its hardest tier

A new living benchmark, Agents' Last Exam (ALE), reports average full-task pass rates below 1% on its hardest tier across mainstream agent harnesses and backbone models. Built with more than 250 industry experts, ALE tests long-horizon, economically valuable workflows with verifiable outcomes.

ALE organises non-physical work around the US O*NET/SOC 2018 occupational taxonomy, covering 13 industry clusters, 55 subfields and more than 1,000 tasks, with runnable instances targeting Linux and Windows virtual machines. Its authors argue that strong benchmark scores elsewhere should not be read as evidence of reliable autonomous performance on extended, GDP-relevant tasks. The benchmark is designed to grow continuously as new workflows and industries are onboarded.

Source: SOTA Papers (arXiv:2606.05405), 13 October 2026

RAG

Google patent filing describes retrieval folded into the model itself

A newly published US patent application describes NatRAG, a Google architecture that lets a generative model signal when it needs evidence and use its own internal representations to find documents, instead of handing the task to a separately trained retriever. The filing claims lower latency and a smaller footprint but supplies no benchmark results and has not been granted.

Application 2026/0310702 A1, published 8 October, centres on a special "need to retrieve" token: the generative portion emits ordinary tokens until it decides more context is required, then the retrieval portion reads signals from selected decoder layers to form a document-search representation, appending retrieved tokens without re-encoding earlier output. The filing also proposes freezing attention weights inherited from a pretrained decoder while training the layers that connect to retrieval. It contains 20 claims and remains pending.

Source: INNOVOX, 12 October 2026

Vision

Kandinsky 6.0 Video goes open source with synchronized audio and 1080p output

The Kandinsky research team released Kandinsky 6.0 Video under the MIT licence, generating five-second clips with synchronized 44 kHz stereo audio, native lip-sync and Full-HD 1080p upscaling. It is available in a 3B Lite variant and a 29B Pro flagship.

The model uses a dual-stream CrossDiT architecture that couples a pretrained video stream with a newly trained audio stream through bidirectional cross-attention, aiming to fix the temporal drift that plagues pipelines which generate silent video and patch in audio afterwards. The team reports a 62.4% reduction in audiovisual alignment error against cascaded baselines, with the distilled 3B model running on 24 GB of VRAM. Code, weights and Diffusers integration are released openly (arXiv:2610.05608).

Source: AICoder / arXiv, 12 October 2026

Tools

Klaviyo opens its Composer AI marketing agent to all customers

Klaviyo has made Composer, its AI marketing agent, available to every customer. Composer analyses business data, answers performance questions with cited evidence, automates recurring tasks and applies team-defined processes through reusable "Skills".

Composer can be asked plain-language questions about customer, marketing and commerce data, and it surfaces the sources behind each answer so teams can verify them. It also runs scheduled tasks — such as a weekly flow review or a monthly lifecycle comparison — in the cloud without a user logged in. Klaviyo says the agent is grounded in data from its 205,000 customers, and is offering extra usage for 30 days at launch.

Source: Klaviyo, 13 October 2026
