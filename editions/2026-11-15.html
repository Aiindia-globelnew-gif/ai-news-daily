★ Top Story Global

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; Kress takes interim charge as the AI trade loses its anchor

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, chief executive and board member effective immediately, the board said, naming chief financial officer Colette Kress interim CEO and appointing former Synopsys chief Harvey Jones as the company's first-ever board chair. The exit followed revelations of extramarital relationships, one involving a businesswoman US intelligence officials have concluded was recruited by Chinese intelligence — an allegation Beijing denies. Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% on the day; the company said operations, customer commitments and product roadmap continue without interruption.

Reuters reported that Huang, Nvidia's fourth-largest shareholder with a 3.61% stake, also leaves the board, and that the board has begun a search considering both internal and external candidates. The scandal built over more than a year, from a late-2025 New York Times report linking Huang to a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia cloud partner, through an independent board probe and an emergency board meeting. For India the read-through is concrete: Moneycontrol reported on 14 November that Counterpoint Research counts $4-5 billion in commitments toward Nvidia GPU-powered AI data centres in the country, that Nvidia's first direct Indian startup investment was Sarvam AI in August 2026, and that Nvidia GPUs make up the overwhelming majority of the IndiaAI Mission's subsidised pool of more than 25,000 GPUs — with no revised schedules yet for Yotta's 20,736-chip project or the mission's clusters.

Source: NVIDIA Newsroom and Reuters, 12 Nov 2026; NPR, 12 Nov 2026; Moneycontrol, 14 Nov 2026

India

Centre steers defence and aerospace toward homegrown AI models — with most mission funds unspent

The Union government is encouraging central and state departments to adopt indigenously built AI models and agents in critical sectors including defence and aerospace, officials told Business Standard. Twelve startups are building 20 approved models under the IndiaAI Mission, among them Sarvam, BharatGen, Soket's EKA-1, ZenteiQ's Dhir 2B and 5B, and a video-generation model called Varya. One official said some domestic models have reached about 80% of the capability of leading frontier systems.

The money tells a different story. The mission carries a five-year outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore, but MeitY told the Rajya Sabha it released ₹21.79 crore in 2024-25 against revised estimates of ₹173 crore, ₹379.15 crore in 2025-26 against ₹800 crore, and nothing at all for 2026-27 against a budget estimate of ₹1,000 crore. Gnani, Gan AI, Fractal, Tech Mahindra and Shodh AI are expected to release LLMs and SLMs over the next 12-15 months, and the government says it is in talks with selected firms to widen the scope of what they have already built.

Source: Medianama, 12 Nov 2026; Business Standard via SignalDesk, 13 Nov 2026

India

OpenAI begins processing Indian API traffic inside India, starting with GPT-6 Luna

OpenAI said on 12 November that data sent through its API from India will be processed in the country, with inference for supported workloads also running locally. It covers GPT-6 Luna at first, through the Responses and Chat Completions APIs, and eligible customers opt in by creating a new API project with India as the storage and processing region and calling the India regional endpoint. Nitin Bawankule, OpenAI's head of enterprise for India, said some Indian businesses had held back on important deployments because their data had to be processed abroad.

Only one model is included at launch, and the coverage is text workloads first. The move arrives alongside a broader sovereignty push that includes the government's indigenous-model directive and a MeitY draft framework proposing pre-approval vetting of AI models. Analysts have been quick to note the limit: local inference keeps data in the country but does not by itself build domestic model capability, and the IndiaAI Mission's own buildout still faces a funding gap against hyperscaler spending.

Source: Business Standard, 12 Nov 2026; Arthat.ai, 14 Nov 2026

India

Sarvam says it has raised $300 million with Nvidia and government backing, as the FT weighs India's sovereign bet

Sarvam co-founder Vivek Raghavan told the Financial Times that the Bengaluru startup has raised $300 million in a round backed by Nvidia and the Indian government, arguing that low costs, a large home market and Indian languages give the country a route to an alternative to US and Chinese technology. Sarvam has deployed a large language model trained on Indian languages and is developing a larger version, reported to be around one trillion parameters. Its servers still depend on US semiconductors, Raghavan acknowledged.

The FT frames Sarvam as central to India's attempt to avoid becoming a “digital colony” reliant on foreign models. It was valued at $1.5 billion in an August round, became Nvidia's first direct investment in an Indian AI startup, and in November signed a five-year MoU with Headquarters Integrated Defence Staff to build and deploy AI applications across the armed forces. Its 30B and 105B open-weight models, trained on IndiaAI compute, are published on Hugging Face and AI Kosh. The counterweight, as the FT notes, is scale: Sarvam's spending is tiny next to US rivals, which are giving Indian consumers premium AI free.

Source: Financial Times, 13 Nov 2026 (via AI123 and KKReckons, 14 Nov 2026)

Global

Anthropic's IPO is six times oversubscribed ahead of an 18 November price that could top SpaceX

Anthropic will price its initial public offering on 18 November and start trading on Nasdaq as “ANT” the next day, selling 825 million shares at $70 to $78. Bloomberg reported demand at about six times the shares on offer, with bankers saying existing backers will be favoured in allocation. Combined with a $26 billion private placement from Alphabet, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, the deal would raise $90.4 billion at the top of the range — above SpaceX's $86.2 billion June listing.

At $78 the company would be worth about $1.02 trillion on post-IPO shares outstanding, or roughly $1.4 trillion counting options, restricted stock units and shares reserved for employee plans — well below the $2 trillion once floated. Bloomberg noted the deal uses a conventional roadshow rather than SpaceX's take-it-or-leave-it fixed price, giving institutions more influence over the final number in a choppy market: the Nasdaq 100 is down about 7% over three months after a rate hike, worries about AI debt supply and a midterm result in which data centres drew voter anger. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead with 20 other banks.

Source: Bloomberg, 13 Nov 2026; Reuters, 12 Nov 2026; Quartz, 13 Nov 2026

Global

OpenAI lets its agents pay; Google lets Gemini read your spending

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on 13 November: a user adds a card once, then approves each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint, and the payment network issues a token so card details are never shared with the agent or the merchant. It is built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa and starts with ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US. The same day, Google began rolling out a read-only, opt-in Google Wallet connection for Gemini's Personal Intelligence to US users on web, Android and iOS.

The two are not the same product — OpenAI is moving money, Google is reading it. OpenAI says every purchase needs human biometric approval by design, and that spending limits and pre-approved purchase rules are coming; it also says sites must stop inadvertently blocking agents and teach payment and risk systems to recognise agent-initiated purchases. Google's version charts spending, finds recurring charges and flags price increases, can optionally link bank and card accounts through Plaid, cannot move or invest money, and can be switched off at any time — though Google says it may still train on summaries of transactions when activity keeping is on.

Source: TechCrunch, 13 Nov 2026; NPDev Daily AI Briefing, 14 Nov 2026

Policy

Four nonprofits ask a California court to freeze frontier AI at five labs

A complaint dated 12 November and filed in San Francisco Superior Court asks a judge to bar Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX's Grok entities from developing or deploying any system more capable than their best on the filing date, and to stop AI being used to autonomously conduct or direct research into more capable AI. The single cause of action is California's Unfair Competition Law, with public nuisance supplying the “unlawful” prong. No hearing date and no response from the companies has been reported.

The plaintiffs are the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and Future Tech Insights, which operates as the Alliance for Secure AI; counsel includes former New Jersey attorney general Matthew Platkin. The complaint points to agent incidents it attributes to the companies, including the July 2026 intrusion into Hugging Face infrastructure and three incidents Anthropic disclosed on 30 July. The weak point is standing: since Proposition 64, section 17204 limits private suits to plaintiffs who have lost money or property, which is why the filing spends pages on resources each nonprofit says it had to divert. A state court is being asked to halt a technology across five groups on the basis of a competition statute.

Source: JURIST, 14 Nov 2026; Le Latent, 14 Nov 2026; TechTimes, 14 Nov 2026

Research

Ai2's XYBench: models answer the question you asked, not the problem you actually have

The Allen Institute for AI published XYBench on 13 November, testing whether a model solves the stated request (Y) or the underlying goal (X). Every model evaluated — GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3.5-9B and DeepSeek-V4 Pro — was likelier to include a fix for the stated request than for the underlying goal: 75-86% versus 59-71% on Stack Overflow questions. Top human answers beat every model on steering users away from a mistaken approach.

On emphasis the gap widens. Models focused primarily on the underlying goal in only 35-41% of their Stack Overflow responses, against 55% for the best human answer; on steering the user to the goal rather than the mistaken approach, top human answers scored 79% against 60% for the best model, GPT-5, and 47% for Claude Sonnet 4.6. Ai2's framing is that a passing mention of the real fix is not the same as making it the answer — a measurable failure mode for assistants deployed in support, troubleshooting and IT helpdesks.

Source: Allen Institute for AI, 13 Nov 2026

Research

Anthropic's field guide says most safety evals are easy to game and hard to reproduce

Anthropic published “Building strong safety evaluations” on 13 November, arguing that a safety claim is only as sound as the eval behind it. The guide sets sample-size floors of 100 items for a complex multi-turn eval and 1,000 for a simple single-turn one, warns that multi-turn confidence intervals can exceed ±10 percentage points, and recommends preregistration plus teams mixing methods experts with domain experts. It also advises importing the replication reforms psychology adopted a decade ago.

Its sharpest illustration of the problem: Claude Fable 5.1 scored up to 34 points apart on multi-turn safety evals depending on whether it ran through the API or through claude.ai. On grading, the guide asks for atomic yes/no criteria, agreement checked against human labels using a chance-corrected measure such as Cohen's kappa, and consideration of a grader model different from the one under test, since model graders can favour their own outputs. No product ships with the post — it is methodology only.

Source: Anthropic, 13 Nov 2026 (via Fervor AI briefing, 14 Nov 2026)

Frameworks

Google open-sources TPU Sync to shuttle KV cache and weights between TPUs, DRAM and storage

Google Cloud open-sourced TPU Sync, an Apache-2.0 C++ library that moves KV cache blocks and model weights between TPU HBM, host DRAM, shared storage and the data centre network. It targets two workloads where accelerators wait on data: long-context and disaggregated LLM serving, and reinforcement-learning loops pushing updated weights to rollout samplers. For Qwen3 235B, Google reports median KV transfer latency falling from several seconds to about 230 ms.

The reported gains are substantial: average time-to-first-token down 60% for that model and 90% for a 480B model at low concurrency, and up to 90% lower average TTFT on 64K-context workloads with prefix caching. Python wheels ship for JAX and PyTorch with KV cache manager, KV store and weight synchroniser APIs; it acts as the KV connector for vLLM-TorchTPU and the disaggregation backend for SGLang-TorchTPU. Read the caveats first: the repo warns APIs are changing rapidly, only development builds are on PyPI, the PyTorch wheel needs a matching torch_tpu build that is not on a public index, and the storage tier and RL weight sync remain experimental.

Source: InfoQ, 12 Nov 2026; Google Cloud, Nov 2026

RAG

IIT Patna's READ drops embeddings entirely and beats chunk-and-embed RAG by 43 points

Researchers at IIT Patna and the independent lab TwoSpoon published READ (Reliable Embedding-free Agentic Document-search), which replaces vector retrieval with an agent given three tools: keyword search that returns matching line numbers, an outline tool listing section headings with line numbers, and a reader that fetches specific line ranges. On 51 questions about a 780-page Gujarat government financial report, all systems running Gemini 2.5 Pro, READ answered 58.8% correctly.

The baselines were RAG-style chunk-and-embed retrieval at 15.7% and the same retrieval inside an agentic loop at 27.5%. The target is documents where meaning is not self-contained — a number in a table whose units sit lines away, a legal term defined paragraphs earlier, a figure that only makes sense against a fiscal-year label. The agent refines its query when it gets too many hits, too few, or too little context, then reads from the table header down to the match. The Batch places READ alongside RLM, Search-R1 and IRCoT as evidence that letting models write their own search queries can beat fixed chunking.

Source: DeepLearning.AI The Batch, 13 Nov 2026

Vision

ByteDance opens a cheaper video API today: Seedance 2.5 Flash at $0.152 a second

BytePlus, ByteDance's cloud unit, opens the Dreamina Seedance 2.5 Flash API on BytePlus ModelArk on 15 November, priced at $0.152 per second for 720p text-to-video and image-to-video — about 34% below Seedance 2.5. BytePlus says results are close to the flagship at nearly twice the generation speed. The ModelArk price list puts it at $7.00 per million tokens for 480p and 720p against $10.70 for Seedance 2.5, with 1080p and 4K unsupported.

The workflow this formalises is drafting on Flash and finishing on the full model; Higgsfield's changelog added it on 12 November and says it is not available in the US. The week's other Vision release cuts the other way on licensing: MiniMax's open-weight H3-Image is a 33.1B checkpoint that handles text-to-image and editing in six sampling steps at up to 16 megapixels with up to 16 reference images, but its community licence excludes the US, EU, UK and South Korea, and commercial use above $1 million in annual revenue needs written permission. There is no technical report or independent benchmark for it yet.

Tools

DomoAI's InkoAI Agent turns one sentence into a ready-to-post video

DomoAI, which says it has more than five million creators, launched InkoAI Agent on 13 November — a creative assistant that runs the image and video workflow inside a single conversation, writing the prompt, setting format and length, and generating clips for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. It works across 15 image and video models including GPT Image 2.5, Nano Banana Pro, Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3, and shows what it will generate before running anything.

It reads text, images, documents and library video — character sheets, product photos, scripts, storyboards — and remembers earlier results in the chat, so an image can be redrawn, varied or animated without re-uploading. One-click “Skills” cover short drama, anime video, cartoon style and general story, with approval at the script, character and shot stages. DomoAI says video ads from a product link or photo, tutorials and AI music videos are next. The pattern is the same one showing up across marketing software this week: the agent does the work, the human keeps the approval step.
