★ Top Story Global

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; Kress takes interim charge as the AI trade loses its anchor

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, chief executive and board member effective immediately, the board said, naming chief financial officer Colette Kress interim CEO and appointing former Synopsys chief Harvey Jones as the company's first-ever board chair. The exit followed revelations of extramarital relationships, one involving a businesswoman US intelligence officials have concluded was recruited by Chinese intelligence — an allegation Beijing denies. Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% on the day, and the company said operations, customer commitments and product roadmap continue without interruption.

Reuters reported that Huang, Nvidia's fourth-largest shareholder with a 3.61% stake, also leaves the board, and that the search for a permanent successor will consider internal and external candidates. The scandal built over more than a year: a late-2025 New York Times report linking Huang to a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia cloud partner with priority chip access, then an independent board probe led by outside counsel, then a second report and an emergency board meeting that ran into the early hours. The read-through for India is concrete. Moneycontrol reported on 14 November that Counterpoint Research counts $4-5 billion in commitments toward Nvidia GPU-powered AI data centres in the country, that Nvidia's first direct Indian startup investment was Sarvam AI in August 2026, and that Nvidia GPUs make up the overwhelming majority of the mission's subsidised pool of more than 25,000 GPUs — with no revised schedules yet for Yotta's 20,736-chip project, TCS's Hyderabad data centre or the state AI clusters that depend on that supply chain.

India

Centre steers defence and aerospace toward homegrown AI models, with most mission funds unspent

The Union government is encouraging central and state departments to adopt indigenously built AI models and agents in critical sectors including defence and aerospace, officials told Business Standard. Twelve startups are building 20 approved models under the IndiaAI Mission, among them Sarvam, BharatGen, Soket's EKA-1, ZenteiQ's Dhir 2B and 5B, and a video-generation model called Varya. One official said some domestic models have reached about 80% of the capability of leading frontier systems.

The money tells a different story. The mission carries a five-year outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore, but MeitY told the Rajya Sabha it released ₹21.79 crore in 2024-25 against revised estimates of ₹173 crore, ₹379.15 crore in 2025-26 against ₹800 crore, and nothing at all for 2026-27 against a budget estimate of ₹1,000 crore. Gnani, Gan AI, Fractal, Tech Mahindra and Shodh AI are expected to release LLMs and SLMs over the next 12-15 months, and the government says it is in talks with selected firms to widen the scope of what they have already built. The pitch is technological sovereignty in sectors where dependence on foreign providers is a security question, not just a procurement one.

Source: Medianama, 12 Nov 2026; Business Standard via SignalDesk, 13 Nov 2026

India

Sarvam says it has raised $300 million with Nvidia and government backing as the FT weighs India's sovereign bet

Sarvam co-founder Vivek Raghavan told the Financial Times that the Bengaluru startup has raised $300 million in a round backed by Nvidia and the Indian government, arguing that low costs, a large home market and Indian languages give the country a route to an alternative to US and Chinese technology. Sarvam has deployed a large language model trained on Indian languages and is developing a larger version, reported at about one trillion parameters. Its servers still depend on US semiconductors, Raghavan acknowledged.

The FT frames Sarvam as central to India's attempt to avoid becoming a “digital colony” reliant on foreign models, and notes its deliberate choice not to chase the hardest problems: it builds cheaper models for routine work such as customer calls at banks and insurers. It was valued at $1.5 billion in an August round, became Nvidia's first direct investment in an Indian AI startup, and in November signed a five-year MoU with Headquarters Integrated Defence Staff. Its 30B and 105B open-weight models, trained on IndiaAI compute, are published on Hugging Face and AI Kosh. Sridhar Ganapathy of the think tank Artha Global told the FT the Indian-language focus makes sense because global companies will not build for every Indian language.

Meta buys Conductor, the workspace that runs several coding agents side by side

Meta has acquired Conductor, a two-year-old Y Combinator startup whose Mac app lets developers run Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and OpenCode in parallel, each in its own Git worktree, then compare the results in a diff viewer and merge the one that worked. Conductor's founder Charlie Holtz announced the team was joining Meta to work on a new interface for coding agents. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The deal is best read as a hiring and roadmap shortcut rather than a product absorption. Meta shipped its first Superintelligence Labs coding agent, Muse Code, on the Muse Spark 1.2 model in August 2026, positioned as a cheaper rival to Claude Code and Codex; Conductor had spent two years building the orchestration layer on top of exactly those competitors, and Meta, Google and Spotify engineers were already users. Conductor stopped taking new sign-ups, put its cloud product into read-only mode on 13 November and is winding it down, while the local Mac app keeps receiving model support and fixes. Whether the future interface keeps supporting third-party agents is unresolved.

Global

Anthropic makes Opus 5.5 the default and tells users to stop writing “think hard”

Claude Opus 5.5 is now the default model on Pro, Max, Ultra and Team plans, and Anthropic is recommending it as the daily driver for research, analysis and document work rather than reserving Opus for heavy projects. Its guidance says the most common mistake is carrying over prompting habits from earlier models: phrases like “think hard” or “think carefully” should be removed, because the model always reasons and scales depth to the selected effort level. In Anthropic's tests, responses without those phrases started faster with no measurable quality drop.

The second recommendation is to start at Medium effort, the new default. Anthropic says Opus 5.5 at Medium matched the previous Opus 5 at High on coding and knowledge-work evaluations, and that higher settings cost more without always producing better results. A change that will break integrations: thinking can no longer be switched off, and a request with thinking disabled now returns a 400 error, so applications that relied on it must move to low effort and measure. List pricing is $4 input and $20 output per million tokens, against $5 and $25 for Opus 5, with output more than 30% faster.

Source: the-decoder, 14 Nov 2026; Claude Blog, 12 Nov 2026; TokenPost, 12 Nov 2026

Kenya's High Court declines to halt public-sector algorithms, and sets a 2027 review

Kenya's High Court dismissed a petition seeking to halt automated systems used in public services, according to reporting published on 13 November. The court in Kerugoya found no evidence of specific rights violations and cited protections in the Data Protection Act, leaving systems including the Social Health Authority's proxy means test in operation. A mention has been scheduled for 13 May 2027 to review progress toward an AI policy and legislation.

The ruling is a useful counterpoint to the litigation-heavy week in the United States, where four nonprofits have asked a San Francisco court to freeze frontier development at five labs. Kenya's route is regulatory and legislative rather than injunctive: the court declined to intervene on the systems themselves while putting the state on a timetable to produce policy and law. For governments deploying automated decision-making in welfare and health, the practical question the case leaves open is what evidence of harm a petitioner would need to show before a court would act.

Research

Anthropic's field guide says most safety evals are easy to game and hard to reproduce

Anthropic published “Building strong safety evaluations” on 13 November, arguing that a safety claim is only as sound as the eval behind it. The guide sets sample-size floors of 100 items for a complex multi-turn eval and 1,000 for a simple single-turn one, warns that multi-turn confidence intervals can exceed ±10 percentage points, and recommends preregistration plus teams mixing methods experts with domain experts. It also advises importing the replication reforms psychology adopted a decade ago.

Its sharpest illustration of the problem: Claude Fable 5.1 scored up to 34 points apart on multi-turn safety evals depending on whether it ran through the API or through claude.ai. On grading, the guide asks for atomic yes/no criteria, agreement checked against human labels using a chance-corrected measure such as Cohen's kappa, and consideration of a grader model different from the one under test, since model graders can favour their own outputs. No product ships with the post — it is methodology only, aimed at the labs, auditors and regulators who will have to defend an eval number in public.

Source: Anthropic, 13 Nov 2026 (via Fervor AI briefing, 14 Nov 2026)

Research

SO-Arena scores 21 single-pass models and finds capability does not buy reliability

A paper posted on Hugging Face on 13 November scores 21 models on 2,919 tasks across 11 domains, grouped into semantic understanding, knowledge and reasoning, safety and policy compliance, evidence assessment, and agent task completion. Fastino's GLiDE (27B) and PelaAI's KnowLine-31B tie at the top of the System One table at 70.2, against GPT-6 Sol at 82.3 and GPT-6 Luna at 79.7 as System Two references. SimpleJev's JevAny-27B is named the most reliable System One model despite ranking eighth in capability.

Reliability is split in two. Option Order Stability measures how often an answer survives 12 reorderings of the choices — 14 of 18 System One models score above 0.8 — while Null Discrimination tests whether the model notices the correct option is absent, and there the paper reports a range from near chance to roughly 0.8. The authors argue a performance score says much about order stability and little about whether a model notices a missing answer. Domains split three ways: System One matches System Two on knowledge, sentiment and retrieval; lands within a few points on intent routing, safety, tool use and web interaction; and trails on maths, scientific reasoning and code, where the auto-switching StartLux Auto jumps from 50.8 to 92.5 once thinking is enabled.

Source: SO-Arena paper via AI Weekly, 13 Nov 2026

Frameworks

Cloudflare open-sources Kitesurf, a browser built for agents to drive

Cloudflare open-sourced Kitesurf on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 licence on 13 November, following through on a promise made when the stateless browser for AI agents launched in August. Kitesurf runs entirely on Workers: it parses HTML, applies CSS, executes page JavaScript and renders screenshots without a browser container, and it speaks the Chrome DevTools Protocol, so clients such as Puppeteer can connect. Developers can run it locally, modify it and deploy their own instance.

It is assembled from open-source Rust compiled to WebAssembly — Blitz for HTML, DOM, layout and painting, Stylo for CSS, Boa for JavaScript as a last resort, plus Linebender libraries for text and 2D graphics and Krilla for PDF output. The trade-offs are stated plainly: it is experimental, it is a CDP endpoint rather than a complete Chrome implementation, it still does not suit video, WebGL or bot-challenge handshakes that need real TLS fingerprints, and self-hosting requires a Workers Paid plan for Dynamic Workers. It also ships with no authentication of its own, so Cloudflare tells operators to put Access or another layer in front of the URL immediately.

RAG

Hugging Face trains retrieval models to survive compression, cutting 75% of stored vectors

Hugging Face published a pooling-aware training method on 11 November that optimises a multi-vector retrieval model against both full and compressed document representations. On its NanoBEIR@13 evaluation, the method retained 97.8% of its own unpooled nDCG@10 score while storing 74.8% fewer document vectors at factor-four pooling. The ordinary trained baseline retained 94.6% at the same compression.

The target is the storage bill of late-interaction retrieval, which keeps contextual vectors for individual document tokens and scores them with MaxSim rather than collapsing a document into one vector. The pooler uses Ward hierarchical clustering, repeatedly merging the token-vector groups that add least within-group distance and replacing each with a normalised mean, while queries stay unpooled. Pooling-aware training adds the compressed representation to the objective instead of applying pooling after training, with both losses updating the same encoder. At a 16-token cap the pooling-aware model scored 49.26 nDCG@10 against 46.61 for the baseline; the unpooled score also rose 0.55 points, which means the compression objective acts as a regulariser. No independent replication was found.

Source: Hugging Face via Data Phoenix, 14 Nov 2026; CASRAI, 14 Nov 2026

Vision

Runway opens Praxis-1, a 27B video model repurposed as a robot controller

Runway released the weights and source code for Praxis-1 on 11 November under the Apache 2.0 licence, including commercial use, after months of partner-only access. The 27-billion-parameter model is what Runway calls a world action model: it takes camera images, text instructions and robot position data, predicts how the scene will change, and computes the matching movements, adjusting continuously as new frames arrive. It is fine-tuned from a video model that learned how objects move from ordinary footage.

On Nvidia's RoboLab-120 benchmark — 120 tabletop tasks, 10 trials each — the DROID-fine-tuned checkpoint completed 555 of 1,200 episodes, a 46.2% success rate against 42.9% for FLUX 3 Action, which Runway says makes it the top world action model and the top open robot foundation model on that board; four entries still rank higher overall, two of them agents that call frontier models directly. On a real Franka arm it succeeded 88 times across ten tasks to FLUX 3 Action's 80. The release is unusually complete — weights on Hugging Face, inference and LoRA fine-tuning code, a policy server, MuJoCo simulation, LeRobot support and vLLM-Omni integration. Runway's own report flags the limits: it confuses look-alike objects, struggles with vague instructions, and cannot reliably chain steps without being told each one.

Source: the-decoder, 14 Nov 2026; Runway release notes via DEV Community, 14 Nov 2026

DomoAI's InkoAI Agent turns one sentence into a ready-to-post video

DomoAI, which says it has more than five million creators, launched InkoAI Agent on 13 November — a creative assistant that runs the image and video workflow inside a single conversation, writing the prompt, setting format and length, and generating clips for TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. It works across 15 image and video models including GPT Image 2.5, Nano Banana Pro, Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3, and shows what it will generate before running anything.

It reads text, images, documents and library video — character sheets, product photos, scripts, storyboards — and remembers earlier results in the chat, so an image can be redrawn, varied or animated without re-uploading. One-click “Skills” cover short drama, anime video, cartoon style and general story, with approval at the script, character and shot stages. DomoAI says video ads from a product link or photo, tutorials and AI music videos are next. The pattern is the one showing up across marketing software this month: the agent does the work, the human keeps the approval step.
