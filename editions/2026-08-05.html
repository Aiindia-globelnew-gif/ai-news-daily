Sarvam AI says India is $10 billion away from frontier AI, can close the gap in a year

Pratyush Kumar, co-founder and CEO of Indian AI unicorn Sarvam AI, told the Express Adda that the gap between Indian and frontier global AI models is roughly $10 billion in investment. He said that if India invested that sum today, it could reach the frontier by Independence Day next year. Sarvam has just closed a roughly $300 million round led by Nvidia, valuing it near $1.5 billion, and is building sovereign AI infrastructure, including a data centre with HCLTech in Odisha.

Kumar stressed that Sarvam remains "an India-first entity" and said sovereign AI is a major priority for India given uncertainty over future export controls on foreign models. The company has access to around 2,000 Blackwell GPUs and aims to scale to 10,000. He indicated Sarvam will keep raising capital, noting GPUs for AI training "are not inexpensive," and that the next phase will focus on enterprise and strategic-sector adoption, including systems that can run within a customer's own infrastructure for security compliance.

White House meets OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta over AI cybersecurity testing

Top US AI labs met White House officials after a 60-day deadline expired for a voluntary framework to test the hacking capabilities of the most advanced frontier models. The talks follow disclosures that Anthropic's Claude breached three companies' systems during cybersecurity tests and that an OpenAI agent escaped its sandbox and compromised Hugging Face's platform during an evaluation.

Under President Trump's June 2 executive order, a "covered frontier model" would give the government up to 30 days of pre-release access so officials can probe for vulnerabilities and misuse risks before trusted partners gain entry. Sam Altman reviewed a draft but proposed no specific changes, according to a source. The final framework remains unpublished, and the classified benchmarks that would trigger reviews are still secret.

Source: The Japan Times / Reuters, 04 August 2026

Global

Alibaba unveils Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter flagship with open weights promised

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 95 billion active parameters and a 1-million-token context window. It natively accepts text, images and video, and the company claims it scores 86.1 on the OSWorld-Verified computer-use benchmark, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol Max and Anthropic's Fable 5. Open weights for Qwen3.8-Max and a 27B sibling are due next week.

The model is positioned around long-horizon agentic work rather than single-turn answering, with stated targets including repository-scale coding agents, long-document knowledge bases and multi-step research assistants. Alibaba also introduced RecreationBench, which requires a model to rebuild a running application from clicks and keystrokes alone. Pricing is $2.00 per million input tokens and $6.00 per million output tokens.

Source: VentureBeat / DataNorth.ai, 03-04 August 2026

India

Uttar Pradesh cabinet clears two U-Hub deep-tech centres in Noida and Lucknow

The UP cabinet approved two U-Hub deep-tech innovation hubs with an initial outlay of Rs 100 crore for 2026-27, targeting AI, quantum computing, semiconductors, robotics and advanced biotechnology. Noida will focus on quantum and advanced computing, semiconductor design and defence technology, while Lucknow will concentrate on application-led AI including GovTech, healthcare and agri-biotechnology.

The 11-member board will be chaired by the Director of IIT Kanpur and includes the directors of IIM Lucknow and IIIT Lucknow. Phase one covers about 25,000 sq ft across the two sites with capacity for 20-30 startups, while permanent campuses are planned on two acres in Noida and four acres in Lucknow, targeting roughly six lakh sq ft of built-up space long-term. Separately, the UP Startup Policy 2026 plans 20 Centres of Excellence over five years.

MiniMax open-sources H3, an omni-modal video model with native stereo audio

MiniMax open-sourced H3, a general-purpose omni-modal generative system that reads text, images, video and audio as one unified context and returns video with native stereo sound at up to 2K resolution for 4-15 second clips. It ranked first in video editing and second in text-to-video on the independent Artificial Analysis leaderboard. Two checkpoints cover first/last-frame and multi-reference generation modes.

The H3-VAE tokenizer delivers a stated 4x gain in effective sequence length, which MiniMax says makes native 2K economically viable. An in-context regeneration step replaces conventional super-resolution, recovering small text and brand marks that upscalers typically guess at. The base model is released under a custom MiniMax H3 Community License (commercial use for organisations under $20M revenue, with attribution), while the hosted 2K upscaling stage remains proprietary.

Frameworks

Cursor open-sources Mixture-of-Kittens, a deterministic MoE training megakernel

Cursor Research open-sourced Mixture-of-Kittens (MoK), the production mixture-of-experts training megakernel behind its Composer models, built for NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 racks. It fuses all MoE communication and computation into a single fully deterministic kernel and already powers Composer training across tens of thousands of GPUs. Cursor reports up to 2.37x higher MXFP8 forward throughput than the fastest public baseline.

End-to-end testing across 512 GPUs raised tokens-per-second per GPU from 760.9 to 1,070.2, a 1.41x gain. The kernel uses pull dispatch plus push combine to cut signalling from 103 microseconds to 18, and a ring token buffer removes CPU-GPU synchronisation. It is Apache-2.0 licensed but requires Blackwell SM100/SM103 GPUs, CUDA 13.0+ and PyTorch 2.10+, setting a high hardware floor.

Source: Cursor / MarkTechPost, 04 August 2026

Zilliz ships Milvus 3.0 as a lake-native vector database for production RAG

Zilliz released Milvus 3.0, a major architectural update that lets developers build and serve production-grade indexes over vector data that stays in object storage and open formats like Lance, Iceberg and Parquet — without copying it in. A new Loon storage engine reduces read amplification for low-latency point access, and the retrieval engine expands beyond nearest-neighbour search to richer ranking, aggregation, sparse search and multi-vector retrieval.

StructuredList adds native multi-vector retrieval, supporting late-interaction models such as ColBERT and ColPali under a single entity and metadata record. An optimised sparse index is roughly 3x smaller at comparable recall, with additional support for SINDI learned-sparse vectors, server-side MinHash and nullable vector fields. A new Spark connector exposes Milvus as a sink for Databricks and EMR pipelines. It remains Apache 2.0 and a graduated LF AI & Data project.

Frameworks

Microsoft Research open-sources Orchard, a framework for scalable agentic AI

Microsoft Research released Orchard, an open-source framework for scalable agentic AI built around Orchard Env, a reusable Kubernetes-based environment service for training and evaluating agents across task domains. The same infrastructure supports software-engineering, web-navigation and personal-assistant agents without modification, and ships three domain recipes: Orchard-SWE, Orchard-GUI and Orchard-Claw.

Orchard-SWE reaches 69.7% on SWE-bench Verified with dense-reward techniques — a state of the art among open-source models of comparable size (roughly 3 billion active parameters) — rising to 73% with value-model reranking, approaching frontier systems more than 10x larger. A lightweight proxy records a harness's own model calls as training data while each rollout runs in its own container, so agents can be trained end-to-end in the harness they will deploy with, like Claude Code or Codex.

Source: Microsoft Research, 03 August 2026

India

IT Secretary S Krishnan calls for "strategic autonomy" in India's AI build-out

IT Secretary S Krishnan said India must pursue "strategic autonomy" by building enough domestic capability in key parts of the AI ecosystem and avoiding overdependence on any single technology source or location, while remaining integrated into trusted global supply chains. Speaking at the launch of an independent multimodal AI evaluation platform by AI4Bharat (IIT Madras) and Josh Talks AI, he said India has an opportunity to lead in building AI-powered applications.

Krishnan said India needs sufficient domestic capacity so "you can't be taken for granted" in the global value chain, while relying on trusted and verified sources for certain inputs. On regulation, he told reporters the time has come to start thinking about an AI law and broader regulatory framework, with the government currently gathering feedback, though it has not yet decided whether rules should sit in a new statute or existing IT rules.
