★ Top Story Global

Anthropic splits its frontier model in two with Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on 9 November, describing them as the same underlying model shipped on two access tracks: Fable carries strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards and is generally available, while Mythos is restricted to organisations verified under the company's Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. The models improve on coding, reasoning and vision, run up to about 30% faster than the 5.1 generation, and cost less at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens.

Anthropic said it also implemented its strongest anti-distillation protections to date, cutting data harvested by distillers by 90% to 99%, and extended preserved thinking to all accounts. During training the company deliberately held accelerator-kernel optimisation and LLM architecture research capabilities to roughly the level of Opus 5.5 while leaving agentic coding, hardware development and interpretability work intact. Reported benchmarks include FrontierCode 2.0 at 49.1% at Xhigh effort, FrontierSWE v2 at 69.9% and Humanity's Last Exam with tools at 74.2%. An optional fast mode raises token generation by up to about 2.5x at $16 and $80 per million tokens. Fable 5.2 is available on AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure under the model id claude-fable-5-2, and Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 as the everyday default.

Source: Anthropic announcement, via PoYo AI Daily, 10 November 2026

India

Centre moves to make Indian AI models the default in defence and other critical sectors

The central government is likely to push for greater use of indigenously developed AI systems, large language models and AI agents in government-run critical sectors such as defence and aerospace, senior officials told Business Standard. Departments at both the central and state level could be asked to avoid frontier models from non-Indian companies altogether. Defence, which already runs air-gapped LLMs for edge use, has been asked to review how IndiaAI Mission systems can be deployed.

Under the Rs 10,372 crore IndiaAI Mission, 20 models have been approved for development by 12 startups, spanning general-purpose LLMs and sector-focused small language models. Sarvam and BharatGen have already released models, alongside Soket's Varya video model and EKA-1 foundation model, and ZenteiQ's Dhir 2B and Dhir 5B. Gnani, Gan AI, Fractal, Tech Mahindra and Shodh AI are expected to ship over the next 12 to 15 months. One official said some domestic models have already reached up to 80% of the capacity of leading frontier systems.

Source: Business Standard, 9 November 2026

Sarvam's $234M first close puts sovereign AI at the centre of India's funding map

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI closed a $234 million Series B first close as part of a targeted $300 million round, taking its post-money valuation to $1.5 billion and confirming unicorn status. Tracxn data cited by World Startup News shows Sarvam alone accounted for roughly 87% of venture capital flowing into India's Indic AI ecosystem over the year, drawing in about $345 million to $346 million.

The round lands as large Indian IT services firms move from building AI practices to buying into the model layer. HCLTech has committed $150 million to Sarvam, part of a wider pattern of IT companies taking stakes in AI startups so they can resell domestic models to enterprise clients. The concentration is a double-edged signal: it validates investor appetite for full-stack Indian language AI, but also shows how thin the field still is beyond the leading player.

India

Indian banks weigh a Rs 3.5 trillion data centre lending book, and the chip risk inside it

India's data centre build-out could add up to Rs 3.5 trillion to bank loan books by 2030, but lenders face two very different propositions, according to Communications Today: the physical facilities, which look like some of the safest infrastructure credit available, and the AI accelerators inside them, which promise higher returns at far greater risk. Operational capacity is expected to reach about 2 GW by December 2026, with 2030 forecasts at 5 GW to 8 GW.

Nvidia chips alone cost roughly $25 million to $40 million per megawatt, several times the cost of the building, and can look outdated within a few years. Filling even 1 GW with Indian-owned accelerators would need $25 billion to $40 billion, or Rs 2.4 trillion to Rs 3.8 trillion, more than all the buildings combined at 2 GW. RBI rules cap a bank's exposure to a single corporate group at 25% of Tier-I capital, so a single gigawatt would breach the limit even for SBI, pushing deals toward syndication and structures borrowed from aircraft leasing in GIFT City.

Source: Communications Today, 9 November 2026

Global

Google adds TPU 8tx and 8i Max, taking its eighth-generation line to four systems in a year

Google introduced TPU 8tx for confidential inference and small-scale training, and TPU 8i Max for very large context windows and rack density, expanding the eighth-generation portfolio to four systems in a single year alongside 8t for training and 8i for real-time reasoning. TPU 8tx builds confidential compute into the hardware layer and pairs with 5th Gen AMD EPYC hosts; 8i Max places two 8i chips in one package to double compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth. Google said TPUs now have nearly 100 external customers.

The pitch for 8tx is that organisations can fine-tune on private data, distill larger models into smaller ones and train agents with reinforcement learning on a single system, without moving sensitive data between clusters. Google says it delivers up to about 2x lower latency than 8t with a near-zero performance penalty. Rather than waiting for the next generation of silicon, Google has carved two new systems out of the same eighth-generation chips, one wrapped around confidentiality and one around memory bandwidth.

Source: Google Cloud blog, 9 November 2026

Global

Meta's Muse Ember matches Claude Haiku 5.5 at roughly a third of the cost per task

Meta released Muse Ember, its smallest and most efficient model, built for the work agents repeat all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction and long document parsing. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index it scores 36 at maximum reasoning effort, the same rounded score as Claude Haiku 5.5, at about 14 cents per benchmark task against 41 cents for Haiku. It is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens up to 200K tokens.

The model has a 1,048,576-token context window, up to 524,288 output tokens, and natively accepts text, images, audio, video and PDFs. Meta's own results show Ember fully completing 37.8% of AutomationBench-AA business workflow tasks on the first attempt, against 20.2% for GPT-6 Luna and 13.9% for Haiku, though Haiku leads on FrontierCode 2.0 at 34.9% to Ember's 27.2%. Meta positions it as the cheap executor under a larger planner: Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume. It ships as muse-ember-1.0 on Muse Code and the Meta Model API.

Source: Meta AI, 9 November 2026

Policy

Australia to legislate mandatory AI safety duties and a dedicated AI regulator

The Australian government said strong mandatory safety and security protections will be legislated through the AI Standards and apply to all frontier AI companies wishing to operate in the country. It will propose a new targeted regulator for AI safety, working with the Australian AI Safety Institute, with powers to enforce significant penalties for non-compliance.

Companies would be required to maintain security controls across development and deployment, identify and manage serious model risks, allow independent evaluation, notify the government rapidly of incidents, publish safety and security frameworks, and respond to updated standards as technology changes. The measures follow a rapid review into a June breach involving OpenAI and Medicare, which recommended considering mandatory, time-bound AI safety reporting covering misaligned behaviour and agentic cyber incidents, and a possible failure-to-prevent offence.

Source: Prime Minister of Australia, 10 November 2026

Policy

Abu Dhabi and OpenAI turn government rules into machine-readable code

Abu Dhabi partnered with OpenAI on Machine-Readable Government, an initiative that converts laws, policies and regulations into formats AI systems can process, so routine applications and compliance checks can be assessed in minutes rather than days. The emirate says it is the first government to optimise a defined set of laws and policies for AI, as part of an ambition to become the world's first AI-native government by 2027.

The system initially covers routine regulatory assessments such as permits and certificates, with every outcome linked to the specific regulation or clause that informed it, creating an audit trail. Officials set standards, review complex cases and retain final decision authority, and the framework is explicitly not intended to decide high-stakes matters or cases affecting rights, eligibility or access to essential services. A secondary benefit is conflict detection: structured policies let departments see where rules across agencies intersect, and test proposed changes before they land.

Research

Next-Concept Modeling beats diffusion language models at 10-31x the speed

A new paper proposes Next-Concept Modeling, which compresses a passage into a stack of discrete code levels and predicts one whole level at a time rather than token by token. On LM1B it reaches better generative perplexity than the strongest diffusion baseline while running 21 times faster; on XSum it matches the best baseline's ROUGE-L at 31 times the speed.

The design follows VAR, a 2024 image-generation method that predicts images scale by scale, with three changes for text: per-level codebooks, nested dropout so early levels carry meaning on their own, and a token-level reconstruction objective. On Sudoku the approach is striking, reaching 99.5% on easy puzzles and 86.1% on hard ones with 12 function evaluations, against 81.3% for FMLM+ at 16. The authors are explicit about the limits: models are in the tens to hundreds of millions of parameters, speedups are measured against diffusion baselines on a single H100 rather than a production serving stack, and the work has not been peer-reviewed.

Source: NYU Shanghai RITS, 9 November 2026

Research

NVIDIA's GR00T 2 predicts the next video frames and the robot's actions together

At the Conference on Robot Learning in Austin, NVIDIA introduced GR00T 2, a 2-billion-parameter world action model built on Cosmos that denoises predicted future frames and an action chunk in a single diffusion pass, then executes the actions it has just imagined. The company reports 40 of 50 evaluated tasks completed at least once with no task-specific demonstrations, 71% average success with one demonstration per task and 95% with five.

Adaptation takes a 30-minute LoRA fine-tune on a single RTX 5090, and the five demonstrations add up to under three minutes of robot data. GR00T 2 Turbo keeps the parameter count but uses four times fewer spatial video tokens, running a prediction in 124 milliseconds on a Jetson AGX Thor aboard a Unitree G1. Pretraining mixed 402,730 hours of human egocentric video, 10,184 hours of handheld gripper demonstrations across 2,897 tasks and 23,500 hours of teleoperation. Foxconn, Siemens, Nexuni and MicroAGI are testing it; weights are due in December, with licence terms not yet published.

Frameworks

PyTorch 2.15 adds a Flight Recorder for distributed training hangs

PyTorch 2.15 adds automatic post-mortem diagnostic dumps for stalled distributed GPU training jobs. When a collective operation hangs or times out, Flight Recorder saves diagnostic state so engineers can inspect it after the stall, rather than losing the job's context when the process is killed. The feature works across communications backends.

The release also makes the redesigned NCCL backend the default for NCCL process groups, a path that was optional in 2.14. Teams that need the previous behaviour can use fallback options, which are scheduled for removal in the next release. PyTorch 2.15 additionally adds faster ways to reconfigure a training cluster after a failure; those require NCCL 2.29 or later, or RCCL 2.30.7 on ROCm. Anyone running multi-node jobs will recognise the problem this solves: a hang with no traceback.

Source: TokenPost, 8 November 2026

RAG

Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base adds single-shot RAG and retrieval-only modes

Amazon Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base now supports single-shot retrieval-augmented generation and retrieval-only requests through AgenticRetrieveStream, alongside the existing agentic retrieval that plans and runs multiple searches before answering. Single-shot RAG searches once and uses a foundation model to generate an answer from the passages it finds; retrieval-only returns the matching passages with no generation at all.

The practical effect is that one API now covers both ends of a real workload: a checklist lookup that needs a direct answer, and a cross-policy comparison that needs the system to plan several searches. Retrieval-only is the more interesting addition for teams building on top of their own models or UIs, since it removes the generation step and hands back source text. Exposing all three modes behind a single call also cuts the amount of orchestration code a team has to own.

Source: AWS Machine Learning Blog, 9 November 2026

Vision

ByteDance's Seedream 5.5 targets 100-round editing and native transparency

ByteDance Seed introduced Seedream 5.5, an image creation model built around three upgrades: stable multi-turn editing across 100 rounds, native RGBA output with transparent backgrounds, and more realistic rendering of lighting, materials and skin texture. The model preserves unedited regions at the pixel level and reduces the colour shifts and detail loss that accumulate over successive edits.

Native alpha channel output is the practically useful change: generated elements can enter a design workflow as standalone assets without a separate background removal or edge cleanup pass. Text rendering also improves, with more accurate complex character shapes and densely arranged small text, a persistent weakness in image models. The 100-round editing stability claim is the one to test on your own assets before building a workflow around it.

Source: ByteDance Seed, 9 November 2026

Ogilvy announced ECHO, a proprietary consent-first AI platform that turns a single celebrity or ambassador shoot into an ongoing creative content system. Built with a WPP and Creative Artists Agency partnership, it operationalises verified talent scans stored in The CAA Vault, covering digital likeness, voice and IP, so brands can generate localised, platform-native assets from one hero shoot.

For marketing teams weighing generative content at scale, that is the pattern worth copying: contract terms encoded as machine-checkable rules, with a human approval gate before publication rather than after.
