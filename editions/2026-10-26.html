★ Top Story India

India's AI bottleneck is no longer money — it is chips, memory and power

Venture capital and free cloud credits mean Indian AI companies can mostly afford compute; what they cannot get is the hardware. Founders and investors tell Business Standard that GPU deliveries take three to four months, high-bandwidth memory is sold out through 2026, and data-centre capacity and power are being committed to hyperscalers.

An engineering team can lose an entire quarter waiting for a graphics processing unit, and industrial-grade chipset pricing can swing two- to three-fold. “With demand outstripping supply, companies are increasingly looking to secure capacity in advance,” said Rahul Agarwalla, managing partner at SenseAI Ventures, adding that three-year commitments help but startups struggle to forecast demand that far out. The government's IndiaAI Mission pool, described in the report as more than 38,000 GPUs rentable at subsidised rates, has helped: growX Ventures' Ashish Taneja said it “has scaled faster than almost anyone predicted, and for training language models in a data centre it does the job.” The deeper constraints sit upstream. Only SK hynix, Samsung and Micron make high-bandwidth memory at scale, and Micron said in December 2025 that its entire 2026 HBM supply was already committed. Bengaluru fintech Juspay now runs about 95% of its AI-assisted coding on open-source models on GPUs it controls, because renting an eight-node GPU cluster costs ₹25–35 lakh a month against a token bill that can be five to ten times higher. Taneja's warning: when a card takes three or four months to arrive, “a whole engineering team can lose a quarter, and that hurts far more than a chip being a generation old.”

NCERT adds a 20-hour AI and computational thinking module for classes 9 to 12

The National Council of Educational Research and Training has released an activity-based module on computational thinking and AI for classes 9–12, developed with IIT Madras and IIT Jodhpur. It covers how machines learn, the limits of AI, deepfake detection and the ethics of personal data.

NCERT said the module is designed for students with no prior knowledge of AI and aims to make them “careful, confident, creative, and responsible users and evaluators of AI systems, rather than relying on its outputs without verification.” Activities can run with or without digital devices, which matters for schools without lab infrastructure. Special emphasis sits on data privacy, misinformation, fairness, digital safety and critical verification of AI-generated information, with the council stressing that “human judgement, creativity, and ethical responsibility remain central to learning.” It is one of the clearest moves yet to put AI literacy into the mainstream Indian school timetable rather than an elective.

Source: The Hindu, 25 October 2026

Global

OpenMobile-2 shows open-source phone agents can match proprietary models

Researchers from Nanjing University, SenseTime and other institutions released OpenMobile-2, two phone-operating agents built on Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-27B, trained in a simulated environment covering more than 110 apps. The larger model reaches 79.9 points on AndroidWorld and the best score among open models on real Android devices.

Training agents on real apps is painful — logins, real purchases and no way to reset state — so the team rebuilt 35 apps popular in China, including Taobao, JD, DiDi and Meituan, plus Slack and Google Drive, and combined them with existing simulations and an Android emulator. Human reviewers refined each rebuilt app until workflows matched, and every simulation resets automatically. From that setup came about 11,600 example workflows and more than 2,000 practice tasks, generated with Gemini 3.1 Pro as the teacher. The larger model improves from 67.1 to 79.9 on AndroidWorld, from 35.9 to 50.4 on the harder MobileWorld, and from 31.9 to 59.6 on SPA-Bench with real devices. The most transferable finding is about data diversity rather than volume: holding data constant and only adding apps lifted success on unseen applications from 60.7% to 67.5%. Training environment, data and reproduction instructions were all released.

Source: The Decoder, 25 October 2026

Global

Anthropic opens a free vulnerability scanner to critical open-source projects

Anthropic launched OSS Scanner, a free, opt-in vulnerability scanner for critical open-source projects that runs the company's strongest models, including Claude Mythos, against enrolled codebases. Reports go straight to maintainers without human review.

The scanner is part of the Cyber Mission Anthropic announced on 8 October, which also includes a Critical Infrastructure Defense Program aimed at operators of essential systems. Bypassing human review is the notable design choice: it means findings reach maintainers quickly, but it also means the model's output is treated as actionable intelligence rather than a draft. The launch sits alongside a related effort in which Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, working with Theorem, had Claude write machine-checked proofs that 239 fuzzing entry points across 123 OSS-Fuzz libraries never crash on inputs up to 64 MB, at the level of compiled RISC-V binaries — a formal-verification approach that complements pattern-matching scanners.

UK commits more than £600 million to AI security, including a £100 million biosecurity lab

Ahead of Wednesday's Budget, the Chancellor set out a package worth over £600 million covering AI security, sovereign AI investment and skills. Research

GPT-6 Astra solves 49 of 50 unpublished theoretical physics problems

A preprint called TheoPhysEval tested off-the-shelf AI agents on 50 never-published research-level problems across 11 physics subfields. GPT-6 Astra solved 49 with web search enabled, at a run cost of $116.28, and 46 of 50 with the internet cut off; Claude Opus 5.5 scored 46 on both tracks.

The work was led by physicists from ICTS, Google DeepMind and other institutions, with 35 authors, and posted to arXiv on 9 October. Using unpublished problems is the design choice that gives the result weight: there is little chance of recall from training data, so a correct answer is more likely to reflect reasoning. Perhaps the more striking finding is that the models' work led to corrections of some human-written reference solutions. One problem defeated every model tested. Caveats are real — the paper is not peer reviewed, 50 problems is a finite sample, and solving a well-posed problem is not the same as choosing which problem to solve, which remains much of what theoretical physicists actually do.

Source: CryptoBriefing, reporting the TheoPhysEval preprint, 25 October 2026

Research

RExBench: the best coding agent finishes only a third of realistic research extensions

A new ACL 2026 benchmark of 12 realistic AI research-paper extensions found that the strongest LLM coding agent completed roughly 33% of tasks autonomously with no hints. Adding human-written hints lifted the best score, but it stayed below 44% across all agents tested.

The authors — Nicholas Edwards, Yukyung Lee, Yujun Audrey Mao, Yulu Qin, Sebastian Schuster and Najoung Kim — evaluated 12 LLM agents across two frameworks, aider and OpenHands, against expert-authored extension instructions with automated scoring. “All agents fail to autonomously implement the majority of the extensions,” the paper reports, concluding that “current agents are still short of being able to handle realistic research extension tasks without substantial human guidance.” The value here is as a public yardstick: any vendor pitching self-driving research now has a number it can be asked to beat, and the current ceiling is one task in three.

Microsoft open-sources Agensh, a harness where 1,024 agents coordinate with no boss

Microsoft Research released Agensh, a decentralised multi-agent harness in which every worker claims its own sub-task, posts findings and merges code into a shared repository. On ProgramBench it scaled from 1 to 1,024 agents across 16 nodes, with more workers raising scores on all five of the hardest tasks.

There is no orchestrator: each worker runs the same gather, claim, act, verify, merge loop, capped at 272k input and 128k output tokens and 64 GiB of memory. The shared workspace is Gitea, messaging runs through Mattermost, and a typed findings board adapted from DeLM carries context between workers. The best single-task result was a 79.59% pandoc pass rate using 32 agents on GPT-6.1 Sol. Thin adapters connect Agensh to existing single-agent harnesses such as GitHub Copilot CLI and Claude Code, which makes it testable against work you already run. It lands in a week of agent-infrastructure releases, and the practical question it poses is whether coordination overhead or model quality is the real ceiling on long-horizon coding.

Frameworks

Anthropic open-sources the OS-level sandbox behind Claude Code

Anthropic published sandbox-runtime, the OS-level sandbox used by Claude Code, which restricts file and network access for agents, MCP servers and CLI tools using Seatbelt on macOS and bubblewrap on Linux — with no container required.

The design argument is that most agent tools need a narrow, predictable set of paths: a workspace directory to read, a temp folder to write, a few outbound ports. Wrapping that in a container or a VM adds isolation overhead the workload does not need, so sandbox-runtime attaches lightweight profiles directly to the process at launch. Seatbelt declares allow and deny rules for file metadata, network sockets and system calls; bubblewrap uses mount namespaces and seccomp filters, dropping privileges after setup. Startup falls to milliseconds and memory overhead to a few megabytes per agent, enough for hundreds of concurrent workers on one node. The trade-off is that profiles must be written carefully, and network rules that omit DNS resolution and localhost services break tool calls an agent is legitimately allowed to make. Container isolation remains the better fit for untrusted third-party code.

Source: Anthropic, reported by MarkTechPost, 24–25 October 2026

RAG

Azure SQL makes DiskANN vector search generally available inside the database

Microsoft has taken its DiskANN-based vector index and vector search to general availability on Azure SQL Database, Azure SQL Managed Instance and SQL database in Microsoft Fabric. Approximate nearest-neighbour search now runs inside the SQL engine, with no separate vector database to operate.

The GA adds full INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE support with asynchronous index maintenance, iterative filtering so a query can still return the requested number of results after relational predicates are applied, and optimizer-driven execution that chooses between exact and approximate search based on query context. Vectors sit beside relational data and inherit the filters, joins, row-level security policies and transactional behaviour an application already relies on, which is the part that matters most for multi-tenant RAG. Applications opt into approximate results with TOP (N) WITH APPROXIMATE, and the query surface is a VECTOR_SEARCH table function taking a column, a query vector and a metric such as cosine. DiskANN's point is scale: it keeps large indexes on SSD-backed storage rather than requiring everything in RAM.

Vision

Canva ships Craft, an image model trained on design templates rather than photographs

Canva released Craft, its first in-house image generation model, built on a two-stage architecture that plans a design before producing the final image. It outputs up to 2K across more than 30 aspect ratios, renders text in multiple languages, and is reachable through Canva's Dream Lab and a public API.

The distinctive choice is training data: Craft learned from design templates rather than photographs, which reflects Canva's design-first identity and sidesteps some of the copyright exposure that surrounds photo-trained models. That also positions it for graphic output rather than photorealism — layouts, editable elements and planned compositions, the things a designer actually needs to iterate on. Shipping its own model moves Canva off third-party image providers and gives it cost control and tighter integration with its editing tools. For marketers, the interesting part is the API plus planned layouts: it shifts the product from 'generate an image' to 'generate a layout I can edit', which is where most brand work actually happens.

Tools

Peec AI adds an agent that turns AI-search visibility data into tasks

Peec AI, whose AI search platform is used by more than 3,500 companies, launched Peec Agent alongside an expanded Actions system. Recommendations are ranked by a five-level relative impact rating and carry the evidence behind them, then hand off to a brief, a page edit or a task in the team's own tools.

Actions cover four kinds of work: technical fixes such as a blocked AI crawler or an invalid sitemap, improvements to owned content including missing summaries or unsupported claims, opportunities in third-party publications and review profiles cited by AI answers, and inaccurate claims about a brand traced back to the pages that contributed to them. Teams can filter by AI model, topic, country and type, and add their own actions. The agent ships with a library of 30 skills and more than 25 connectors spanning Webflow, Contentful, HubSpot, Klaviyo, Notion, Linear, DataForSEO, Moz, SISTRIX, Cloudflare and PostHog. Each connection uses an individual's own login and reaches only what that person allows, and the agent requests approval before making changes by default — the approval line being the unresolved question across this whole product category.

Source: Peec AI, via MarTechSeries, 23 October 2026
