★ Top Story India

India's AI bottleneck is no longer money — it is chips, memory and power

Venture capital and free cloud credits mean Indian AI companies can mostly afford compute; what they cannot get is the hardware. Founders and investors tell Business Standard that GPU deliveries take three to four months, high-bandwidth memory is sold out through 2026, and data-centre capacity and power are being committed to hyperscalers.

An engineering team can lose an entire quarter waiting for a graphics processing unit, and industrial-grade chipset pricing can swing two- to three-fold. “With demand outstripping supply, companies are increasingly looking to secure capacity in advance,” said Rahul Agarwalla, managing partner at SenseAI Ventures, adding that three-year commitments help but startups struggle to forecast demand that far out. The government's IndiaAI Mission pool — described in the report as more than 38,000 GPUs rentable at subsidised rates — has helped, with growX Ventures' Ashish Taneja saying it “has scaled faster than almost anyone predicted.” The deeper constraints sit upstream: only SK hynix, Samsung and Micron make high-bandwidth memory at scale, and Micron said in December 2025 that its entire 2026 HBM supply was committed. Bengaluru fintech Juspay now runs about 95% of its AI-assisted coding on open-source models on GPUs it controls, because renting an eight-node GPU cluster costs ₹25–35 lakh a month against a token bill that can be five to ten times higher.

Source: Business Standard, 25 October 2026

AI startups take half of India's weekly funding as Different raises a $48 million seed

Indian startups raised just over $184 million across 17 deals in the week to 23 October, down 22% week on week, but AI companies pulled in $93.9 million — about half the total — for the second consecutive week. The largest cheque went to AI martech firm Different, which emerged from stealth with $48 million led by General Catalyst and Lightspeed.

AuxoAI followed with $20.7 million in a Series A led by NTTVC, Firstwork raised $15 million led by Earlybird, and smaller rounds went to Hunar.AI (₹40 crore, Blume Ventures), Vectorial ($3 million, SenseAI Ventures) and Karta AI ($3 million seed). Healthtech was second with $60.6 million, almost all of it Even Healthcare's $50 million Series B led by Khosla Ventures. Seed-stage companies took $51.4 million, roughly 28% of the weekly pie, and Trifecta Capital and Blume Ventures each backed two startups. The AI share matters because it is holding up while overall deal count fell 32%.

India

NCERT adds a 20-hour AI and computational thinking module for classes 9 to 12

The National Council of Educational Research and Training has released an activity-based module on computational thinking and AI for classes 9–12, developed with IIT Madras and IIT Jodhpur. It covers how machines learn, the limits of AI, deepfake detection and the ethics of personal data.

NCERT said the module is designed for students with no prior knowledge of AI and aims to make them “careful, confident, creative, and responsible users and evaluators of AI systems, rather than relying on its outputs without verification.” Activities can run with or without digital devices, which matters for schools without lab infrastructure. Special emphasis sits on data privacy, misinformation, fairness, digital safety and critical verification of AI-generated information, with the council stressing that “human judgement, creativity, and ethical responsibility remain central to learning.” It is one of the clearest moves yet to put AI literacy into the mainstream Indian school timetable rather than an elective.

Source: The Hindu, 25 October 2026

GPT-6 Astra, Sol and Luna arrive inside Google Cloud's Model Garden

OpenAI and Google Cloud have put GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna into Google Cloud's Model Garden alongside OpenAI's Codex agentic coding workflows, now in private preview for selected enterprise customers. Charges appear on a single Google Cloud invoice and count toward existing spending commitments.

Astra is positioned for demanding coding, computer use, professional tasks and scientific research; Sol offers near-Astra intelligence at lower cost; Luna is the fast, cheap option for high-volume work. Google says customer prompts, context and outputs stay inside their designated data perimeter and are not used to train foundation models, with isolated gateways, centralised access controls and audit logging. Zero Data Retention and Confidential Compute support are still in development. Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner, but the arrangement means Google Cloud can now claim access to all three leading closed-source model families on one platform, and removes a procurement excuse for Google Cloud shops that wanted OpenAI models. Further integrations with Antigravity and Gemini Agent are planned.

Global

Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro puts a 3-trillion-parameter open model on a 4 November deadline

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro on 23 October, a 3T-class model with a 1-million-token context, priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output on the Kimi API. The company says full open weights will ship by 4 November.

If the weights land on schedule, the gap between open and closed frontier models narrows sharply — Moonshot's own framing is that K3.1 Pro approaches Claude Opus 5.5 on its benchmarks, and those are vendor runs rather than independent ones. The release has not been smooth: on 25 October Moonshot apologised for rate limits and quota burn on its K3.1 Pro subscription plan, attributing it to its own error, and returned a quarter of every subscriber's quota. A Highspeed variant runs at $4/$20 per million tokens. On Vals AI's Legal Research Bench, K3.1 Pro scored 37.5% all-pass, well behind Claude Opus 5's 55.29% — a reminder that headline scale does not transfer evenly to every task.

Source: TopFive and ArtificialWatch, 23–25 October 2026

UK commits more than £600 million to AI security, including a £100 million biosecurity lab

Ahead of Wednesday's Budget, the Chancellor set out a package worth over £600 million covering AI security, sovereign AI investment and skills. Its centrepiece is a £100 million AI Biosecurity Lab, backed by up to £200 million in model credits from OpenAI and Anthropic and partnerships with Google DeepMind and Basecamp Research.

The £165 million National AI Biosecurity Initiative splits into the lab and £65 million for bio surveillance and medical countermeasures against priority pathogens. Elsewhere: £120 million more for the AI Security Institute, £60 million for a National Cyber Shield using agentic AI plus a £25 million challenge fund, and £70 million for a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre. On the industrial side, the Sovereign AI Fund gets £60 million more and expands into physical and industrial AI, £18 million goes to an Advanced Chip Design Academy, and £23 million over three years goes to the AI Economics Institute. AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said Britain “cannot afford to rely entirely on technology built overseas.” The government also cited NHS use of AI to analyse over 210,000 chest X-rays a month.

Research

GPT-6 Astra solves 49 of 50 unpublished theoretical physics problems

A preprint called TheoPhysEval tested off-the-shelf AI agents on 50 never-published research-level problems across 11 physics subfields. GPT-6 Astra solved 49 with web search enabled, at a run cost of $116.28, and 46 of 50 with the internet cut off; Claude Opus 5.5 scored 46 on both tracks.

The work was led by physicists from ICTS, Google DeepMind and other institutions, with 35 authors, and posted to arXiv on 9 October. Using unpublished problems is the design choice that gives the result weight: there is little chance of recall from training data, so a correct answer is more likely to reflect reasoning. Perhaps the more striking finding is that the models' work led to corrections of some human-written reference solutions. One problem defeated every model tested. Caveats are real — the paper is not peer reviewed, 50 problems is a finite sample, and solving a well-posed problem is not the same as choosing which problem to solve, which remains much of what theoretical physicists actually do.

Source: CryptoBriefing, reporting the TheoPhysEval preprint, 25 October 2026

Research

RExBench: the best coding agent finishes only a third of realistic research extensions

A new ACL 2026 benchmark of 12 realistic AI research-paper extensions found that the strongest LLM coding agent completed roughly 33% of tasks autonomously with no hints. Adding human-written hints lifted the best score, but it stayed below 44% across all agents tested.

The authors — Nicholas Edwards, Yukyung Lee, Yujun Audrey Mao, Yulu Qin, Sebastian Schuster and Najoung Kim — evaluated 12 LLM agents across two frameworks, aider and OpenHands, against expert-authored extension instructions with automated scoring. “All agents fail to autonomously implement the majority of the extensions,” the paper reports, concluding that “current agents are still short of being able to handle realistic research extension tasks without substantial human guidance.” The value here is as a public yardstick: any vendor pitching self-driving research now has a number it can be asked to beat, and the current ceiling is one task in three.

Source: AI Weekly, covering the ACL 2026 RExBench paper, 25 October 2026

Frameworks

TorchTPU and TorchNeuron make TPUs and Trainium first-class PyTorch targets

At PyTorch Conference North America in San Jose, Google and Meta open-sourced TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend for Google TPUs that replaces the PyTorch/XLA bridge. AWS put TorchNeuron, the equivalent for Trainium, into public beta under Apache 2.0.

The pitch is that porting can be as simple as changing model.to("cuda") to model.to("tpu"). TorchTPU supports eager execution and torch.compile, with validated integrations for TorchTitan in training and vLLM and SGLang in inference, plus Ray and llm-d for orchestration; four repositories were open sourced. PyTorch Foundation executive director Mark Collier said inference has overtaken training as the largest data-centre workload, creating “an incredible hunger for tokens.” Both backends register through PyTorch's PrivateUse1 extension point. The caveats are substantial: PyTorch says peak performance still needs TPU-specific tuning, and TorchTPU's default Skinny Collectives approach can cost 30–40% end-to-end on distributed workloads. TorchNeuron does not yet support dynamic input shapes.

Source: InfoQ and the PyTorch Foundation, 22–25 October 2026

Anthropic hands the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation

Anthropic has transferred Agent Skills — the folder-plus-SKILL.md format for teaching an agent a task — to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, giving a format used by more than 70 agent platforms a neutral governance home.

Anthropic's figures put a million skills published and nearly 280 million installed, with integrations across ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code. The donation covers the name, logo, the agentskills GitHub organisation and agentskills.io, with engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and elsewhere maintaining it as individuals; David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag are lead maintainers. The proposed roadmap is versioned releases, a formal proposal process and security guidance for people who write and install skills — the last of which matters, since a skill is executable instruction an agent will follow. The AAIF already hosts MCP, so two of the agent stack's most-used interoperability layers now sit under one foundation.

Source: Anthropic, via AI Engineering Digest, 22 October 2026

RAG

NVIDIA releases open Nemotron 3.5 retrieval models and claims a 14% token cut

NVIDIA published five open retrieval models — Embed 8B and 1B (plus an NVFP4 build), Rerank 8B and a 631M-parameter Parse model. It reports that adding its reranker cut total agent inference tokens per query by about 14% while improving retrieval accuracy.

The argument is economic: an agent with weak retrieval re-searches and opens extra documents, which burns tokens. NVIDIA ran an agent over ViDoRe v3, BRIGHT and BrowseComp-Plus, changing only the search tool, and held the agent model, tools, task set and corpus fixed. Embed 8B is claimed at 61.17 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3.1, first among dense retrievers as of 20 October, while the 1B model reaches 58.38 — 95.4% of the 8B score — and its NVFP4 build ran 27.1% faster on a GB200 with 99.85% of accuracy. The reranker is claimed at 60.49 nDCG@10 against 59.17 for Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B. Every figure is NVIDIA's own; the post gives no licence, no per-query dollar cost and no latency impact, and the token saving depends on how often your agent re-searches today.

Source: NVIDIA, reported by Models at Work, 23 October 2026

Vision

ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 stretches AI video to 30 seconds with 50 references

Dreamina Seedance 2.5, ByteDance's new video and audio model, generates clips up to 30 seconds and accepts up to 50 multimodal references — images, video, audio and text — for a single clip. A companion image model, Dola Seedream 5.0 Pro, adds arrow-and-circle editing and layered elements.

The headline capability is granular editing: creators can target specific timestamps, characters or other elements inside a video rather than regenerating the whole clip, which is what makes iteration practical for short-form work. Seedream 5.0 Pro improves text rendering and translation for charts, diagrams and presentation pages in more than 10 languages including English, Spanish, Arabic and Japanese. Separately, ByteDance's Volcano Engine released a Seedance 2.5 4K variant aimed at film and television production, with a Draft mode for cheap 480p previews before committing to a 4K render and 10-bit colour support. Rollout starts with subscribers over 16 across Europe, Asia, the Middle East and South America, with C2PA Content Credentials and visible AI labels built in.

Source: Dreamina (ByteDance) and TechNode, 23 October 2026

Picsart folds Google Ads and three AI agents into a single marketing studio

Picsart launched Marketing Studio, which combines creative production, ad strategy, creative testing and paid distribution. It ships with a Google Ads integration that lets businesses build and launch campaigns inside Picsart, plus three agents: Pax for Google Ads, Draper for ad strategy and Slava for campaign management.

The target user is the solopreneur or small-business owner with limited marketing expertise, and the product is framed around four questions: where to invest, what to create, how to launch and what is working. Creative testing comes from a partnership with Zappi, whose agent Vera scores concepts against consumer-insight models before spend is committed; Picsart says Zappi's model identified stronger creative 86–93% of the time in validation against hundreds of consumer-evaluated ads. Mika Peterman of Google said “creative is the biggest driver of ad effectiveness” and that bringing creation and distribution together lets small businesses “launch faster, iterate quickly based on real performance.” Plain-language performance insights then feed back into the next round of creative.

Source: Picsart, via Business Wire, 23 October 2026
