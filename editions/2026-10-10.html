Indian AI Startup Funding Jumps 265% as AI Becomes the Country's Most-Funded Sector

Indian AI startups raised $438 million across 35 deals in the July–September quarter, up 265% year on year, making AI the most-funded sector in the quarter for the first time. The surge stands out against a subdued wider market, where overall startup funding rose just 5%.

Inc42's Indian Tech Startup Funding Report Q3 2026 puts AI startup deals at 35, up 35% from 26 a year earlier. Indian AI startups raised $676 million across 57 deals in the first half of 2026 — more than four times the $162 million raised in the same period last year — and another $438 million in Q3. Investors surveyed by Inc42 said 63% see some froth in Indian AI valuations, and one warned that a global AI bubble bursting could trigger a correction. The report points to enterprise AI moving from pilots to production, hyperscaler investment in Indian AI infrastructure and sovereign-model efforts as structural drivers.

IIT Delhi Team Designs India's First Homegrown Micro-GPU, Targets 65nm Fabrication

A two-student team at IIT Delhi has developed what it calls India's first indigenously designed graphics chip — a small 'micro-GPU' demonstrated on a Spartan-7 FPGA. Guided by Prof Jayadeva and Prof Kaushik Saha, the researchers now want to turn the design into silicon using a 65nm process. It is a concept rather than a product, but the team hopes it becomes a starting point for more Indian chip designs.

The micro-GPU is not meant to compete with Nvidia or AMD. The team is targeting edge applications — displays in electric vehicles, medical diagnostic screens and e-rickshaw meters — where extreme graphics performance is not needed. India currently lacks a 65nm ASIC fabrication facility, so the team may need to manufacture abroad or wait for fabs being built under the semiconductor mission in Gujarat, Assam and Odisha to come online. The design will be shared to encourage startups and students to build on it.

India Backs BigEndian's ₹260 Crore AI-Vision Chip With ₹130 Crore in Public R&D Funding

The Technology Development Board, under the Department of Science and Technology, has committed ₹130 crore to Bengaluru-based BigEndian Semiconductors for Project VeerAI, a ₹260 crore effort to build an indigenous AI Vision System-on-Chip. The chip integrates AI, image signal processing and edge connectivity on a single piece of silicon for camera-focused edge devices. The funding, from the national Research Development and Innovation Fund, is structured as optionally convertible debt.

Project VeerAI aims to move the technology from TRL-5 (laboratory validation) to TRL-9 (proven operational deployment), a transition that deep-tech hardware startups often struggle to finance. Target applications include secure CCTV and surveillance, defence and aerospace, automotive ADAS and industrial IoT. The government has framed the project as a step toward semiconductor sovereignty and reduced reliance on foreign hardware in security-sensitive environments.

New 'Sixth Sense' Benchmark Finds Frontier Models Far Behind Humans on Intuitive Visual Reasoning

Researchers introduced Humanity's Sixth Sense (HSS), a benchmark for the intuitive visual reasoning people perform at a glance — inferring causes, trajectories, social hierarchy and unwritten rules from images and short video. Human participants reached 93.1% accuracy, while the strongest model tested, GPT-6-astra, reached only 53.6% even at maximum reasoning effort. The authors argue that scaling on existing benchmarks has left this capability behind.

HSS organises items under a structured taxonomy and pairs each with human-written prompts probing implicit temporal, spatial, social and abstract structure. The team also tested an agentic setup that applies dynamic visual manipulation, which narrowed but did not close the gap. The paper positions intuitive visual reasoning as a measurable axis distinct from expert-level analysis and low-level perception, both of which current benchmarks already target.

Source: arXiv, 8 October 2026

Stanford Audits 56 AI Benchmarks and Finds Most Do Not Measure What They Claim

A Stanford HAI team adapted a century-old psychometric method to ask whether AI benchmarks measure the concepts they name. Across 56 capability and safety benchmarks and 53 models, they found that safety tests claiming the same concept often disagree on model rankings, and that 'reasoning' and 'knowledge' benchmarks correlate almost as strongly with each other as within their own categories. One bias benchmark correlated more strongly with reasoning benchmarks than with its own assigned concept.

The study applies convergent and discriminant validity — standard tools from the social sciences — at benchmark and item level, and releases the full dataset of model outputs and scores so the findings can be checked. The authors frame the work as timely because benchmark scores increasingly influence which models get funded, bought and regulated. The paper is 'What AI Benchmarks Actually Measure' (arXiv:2609.08812), announced by Stanford HAI on 5 October.

Source: Stanford HAI via Everyday Data Science, 9 October 2026

China's AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0 Gives Autonomous Agents Their Own Risk Category

China's national cybersecurity standards committee, TC260, published AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0, which adds a dedicated category for AI agents — systems that plan, call tools and act on a user's behalf. It specifies that each agent should carry a unique identity, hold only task-relevant privileges and hand control back to the user before high-risk operations. The framework is voluntary, but a filing regime for sensitive sectors is taking shape around it.

The framework expands its risk list to 54 distinct items from 30 in version 2.0, adding agent risks such as misuse of identities and permissions, goal hijacking, poisoned tools and contaminated memory. It sorts agent decisions into three tiers — reserved for the user, requiring authorisation, or autonomous — and calls for agent behaviour to be observable, traceable and auditable. An associate professor quoted by CCTV via China Daily said that although it is not mandatory, 'it serves as a benchmark'.

Hugging Face Transformers v5 Drops TensorFlow and JAX, Going PyTorch-Only

Hugging Face's Transformers v5 release has removed its TensorFlow and JAX code, focusing exclusively on PyTorch. The change eliminates classes such as FlaxBertModel previously used to compute sentence embeddings in JAX. An open-source library called eqx-zoo now offers a way to compute those embeddings in JAX using Hugging Face checkpoints, loading them as Equinox modules.

The move simplifies the library's maintenance surface but disrupts teams that standardised on JAX or TensorFlow. eqx-zoo replicates the pooling and normalisation steps defined for each model to keep embeddings consistent with the PyTorch reference. The change is the latest sign of PyTorch's consolidation as the default framework for open-weight model work.

Kiln AI Open-Sources Seahaven for Reproducible Synthetic Agent Environments

The Kiln AI team released Seahaven, an open-source Python framework for building isolated, stateful synthetic environments for agent evaluation and reinforcement learning. Each agent connection gets a private SQLite database cloned from a fixture in milliseconds, and the framework grades agents on the database rows they change rather than on text transcripts. It is released under the MIT licence and supports the Model Context Protocol.

Seahaven targets a common bottleneck: production and staging systems are shared and cannot be reset, while simple mocks lack statefulness. The framework is compatible with OpenEnv, so its worlds can be used as training environments in tools such as TRL, and a demonstration 'Stripe World' sandbox mimics a billing engine with 24 tables and 155 API operations. Controlling the clock, seeds and fixtures lets developers replay any agent run exactly.

AWS Adds Metadata Pre-Filtering to S3 Vectors, Claiming Up to 5x Better Recall

AWS made metadata pre-filtering generally available for Amazon S3 Vectors, evaluating metadata filters before running the vector similarity search. Under the new 'enhanced' index mode, applications can isolate relevant data domains with up to 5x higher recall on selective queries, with no data re-ingestion or query-syntax changes. The feature targets multi-tenant RAG platforms and agentic retrieval pipelines.

A single query supports up to 100 filter constraints, with equality, numeric ranges, set membership, boolean logic and a new $startsWith operator for hierarchical keys and paths. AWS says pre-filtering provides database-enforced multi-tenancy without sacrificing semantic richness, and that it is available at no additional charge in all commercial regions where S3 Vectors is supported. The company frames it as a more reliable memory layer for autonomous agents.

Alibaba's Qwen Ships an 8-Step Image Model, Cutting Denoising Work Fivefold

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo, an accelerated checkpoint of its open-weight Qwen-Image-2.1 that generates and edits images in 8 denoising steps instead of 40. It keeps the same 7B visual generator and a Qwen3-VL 8B text encoder, and supports 2K output, multi-reference editing and native transparency. A hosted API version costs CNY 0.1 per image, 2.5x cheaper than the Pro tier.

The checkpoint ships with its recommended 8-step schedule and uses prefix KV caching to reuse text and reference-image context across steps. The base model supports up to 10 reference images and local edits via masks or painted annotations. The weights are released under a research licence, so commercial self-hosting needs separate permission.

Kandinsky 6.0 Video Open-Sources Synchronized Audio-Video Generation Under MIT

Kandinsky Lab released Kandinsky 6.0 Video, a family of diffusion models that generate video and audio together, under the MIT licence. A 3B 'Lite' model and a 29B 'Pro' model produce five-second clips with synchronized 44 kHz audio, including lip-sync, in text-to-audio-video and image-to-audio-video modes. A separate super-resolution model raises output to Full HD.

The models use a dual-stream CrossDiT architecture that connects a pretrained video stream and a newly trained audio stream through bidirectional cross-attention. In side-by-side human evaluation the Pro model was preferred over Kandinsky 5.0 Video Pro for overall visual quality, 0.58 to 0.16. Code, weights and a Diffusers integration are released under MIT, though the 29B model needs substantial GPU memory and several minutes per clip.
