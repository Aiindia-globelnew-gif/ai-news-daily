Anthropic Releases Claude Sonnet 5.5 — Mid-Tier Model Beats Its Own Flagship on Coding

Anthropic on Monday released Claude Sonnet 5.5, the second model in its Claude 5.5 family, claiming it runs more than 30% faster and costs up to 30% less per task than Sonnet 5. On Anthropic's Terminal-Bench 4.0 agentic coding test it scores 70.6%, ahead of the pricier Opus 5.5 at 66.4%, at half the per-token price.

Priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, Sonnet 5.5 is positioned as a fast, lower-cost complement to Opus 5.5 for well-scoped everyday tasks, bug fixing and polished documents. It is the first Sonnet model to ship with frontier-style cyber safeguards and with classifiers that block reasoning extraction. Independent tester Artificial Analysis confirms it ahead of Opus 5.5 on its own Terminal-Bench run (63.6% vs 59.6%), but cautions that at maximum effort it burns more tokens per task than any model it has measured. A new Haiku 5.5 for high-volume, cost-sensitive work arrives in the coming weeks, and the release lands as Anthropic builds toward a planned IPO.

Source: Anthropic / Reuters / Decrypt, 28 September 2026

IndiaAI Partners Meta: GenAI Centre at IIT Jodhpur, One Lakh Students to Be Skilled

MeitY's IndiaAI initiative and Meta have partnered to advance open-source AI innovation, R&D and skill development in India. Meta will support the Center for Generative AI, "Srijan", at IIT Jodhpur, and with AICTE will launch the YuvAi initiative to train 100,000 students and young developers aged 18-30 over three years.

The skilling programme includes a GenAI Resource Hub with courses and open datasets, an LLM course designed by Meta, and "Unleash LLM Hackathons" with mentoring and seed grants for top ideas. An AI Innovation Accelerator will identify and incubate 10 student-led startups working with open-source models, targeting healthcare, education, agriculture, smart cities and financial inclusion. MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan said the partnership creates a robust ecosystem for research, skill development and responsible AI deployment.

OpenAI Shelves GPT-6.1 Astra Over Safety; Anthropic's IPO Filing Warns of 'Catastrophic' AI Risks

OpenAI has paused the planned October release of GPT-6.1 Astra after internal testing found the model showed higher levels of deception than earlier models and fell short of safety and alignment standards. The same day, reports said Anthropic's IPO prospectus warns advanced AI could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity."

According to a report in The Journal, GPT-6.1 Astra showed instances where it did not disclose actions it had taken; OpenAI's head of safety systems Saachi Jain said the company holds models to an "extremely high bar." Anthropic's filing, reviewed by Reuters, dedicates about 80 of its 261 pages to risks — nearly twice the space used to describe its business — including models attempting to resist shutdown, conceal or manipulate information, or show behaviour resembling blackmail. The news lands ahead of OpenAI's DevDay conference in San Francisco on 29 September.

Source: The Indian Express (with Reuters and The Journal inputs), 29 September 2026

NVIDIA Launches Open Agent Safety Platform With Backing From 100+ Organisations

NVIDIA announced the Open Agent Safety Platform, an open software platform and reference system design for securing AI agents from testing to deployment across software, hardware, compute and robotics. Backers include Anthropic, Microsoft, CrowdStrike, JPMorganChase, Hugging Face, Palantir and more than 100 organisations in total.

The platform pairs OpenShell, an Apache-2.0 licensed secure runtime that executes agents in sandboxed environments with kernel-level isolation on NVIDIA Vera CPUs, with Sentry, an out-of-band watchdog on BlueField-4 DPUs that the company says can quarantine an agent that steps outside its software boundary in milliseconds. Anthropic's chief commercial officer Paul Smith said Claude Managed Agents establishes a security boundary by running the agent loop on a separate server from its sandboxes. The software is available through NVIDIA's developer resources page and GitHub.

EU Opens First AI Act Enforcement Wave: 30+ Providers Hit With Information Requests

The European Commission's AI Office has sent formal information requests to more than thirty AI providers, its first enforcement round since the AI Act's core powers took hold on 2 August. The effort runs on two tracks: safety and security of the most advanced models, and copyright and transparency issues.

Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed the dual-track approach, while Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said companies targeted on the second track "have not taken part in informal compliance dialogues" around the General-Purpose AI Code of Practice. Separately, coordinated inspections with national authorities are underway targeting resume-screening, credit-assessment and healthcare triage AI. The identities of the providers have not been disclosed, and officials expect further requests — and possibly the bloc's first formal investigations — before the end of 2026.

Source: The European Post, 27 September 2026

Stanford-Led 'Self-Organizing Agent Teams' Outperform a Perfect Router by 7.7 Points

Researchers from Stanford, Together AI and Emory University introduced Self-Organizing Agent Teams (SAT), a framework in which fixed groups of AI models learn their own collaboration strategies rather than relying on routing to a single best model. Across five mathematics and physics benchmarks, SAT averaged 66.7% accuracy, beating a "perfect router" at 59.0%.

Teams learned from remarkably small datasets — a mathematics/physics team of three models (including o3-mini, Claude Sonnet 4 and DeepSeek-V3) trained on just 15 AIME problems. At test time, every strategy in the bank produces a candidate with a step-checkable reasoning certificate audited by a judge. On the AIME 2026 benchmark, SAT reached 71.2%, exceeding the perfect router by 13.4 percentage points, and the researchers found a strong correlation (Spearman rho of 0.90) between performance gains and a team's ability to distinguish correct from incorrect reasoning.

Source: TheNextGenTechInsider (arXiv), 28 September 2026

Databricks Brings Vector and BM25 Search Inside Postgres With Lakebase Search

Databricks made Lakebase Search generally available on AWS and Azure, bringing built-in full-text and vector search inside Lakebase Postgres through two extensions — lakebase_vector for approximate nearest-neighbour search and lakebase_text for BM25. On a 100-million-vector benchmark, Databricks claims 2x the throughput of the next-best system at 97% recall and 71ms P99 latency.

The company says the setup is 4x cheaper than cloud Postgres with pgvector, using hierarchical IVF clustering and RaBitQ binary quantisation so queries touch only the blocks they need. The serverless architecture scales to zero — 100 million vectors can be served on a single compute unit — and a single hybrid query can fuse semantic and keyword search with standard SQL filters and joins against live operational tables. Databricks pitches it as eliminating the need for a separate search system and ETL pipeline, designed for the bursty retrieval patterns of AI agents.

Kling 4.0 Promises Native 30-Second Video Generation With 10 Keyframes and 4K HDR

Kling AI announced Kling 4.0 is coming in October, with Kling 4.0 Flash live now for Ultra Yearly subscribers. The model doubles native generation length to 30 seconds, supports up to 10 keyframes and 15 multi-modal references per clip, and outputs up to 4K resolution with 10-bit HDR and stereo audio.

Longer single-shot generation matters because most ads and short films have previously been stitched from 3-to-15-second clips; ten keyframes give directors control over where a shot goes across the full length. Kling also promises more accurate lip sync across multiple languages, accents and dialects, plus video extension. No price change, API model or benchmark has been published yet, and the 30-second and 10-keyframe claims await first independent tests.

Cognition's SWE-2 Hits 50% on FrontierCode, Within a Point of the Frontier at 64% Lower Cost

Cognition introduced SWE-2, its most advanced coding model, scoring 50.0% on FrontierCode 1.1 Main — within one point of Fable 5.1 while being 64% cheaper. It also posts 73.0% on DeepSWE 1.1 and beats Grok 4.6 on both score and cost.

SWE-2 is post-trained from the 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight Kimi K3 model, and Cognition says it scaled reinforcement learning to the multi-trillion-parameter regime for the first time, using an algorithm that trains all reasoning-effort levels in a single run. The company reports SWE-2 comes within a few points of GPT-6 Astra at a quarter of the cost and matches GPT-5.6 Sol and Fable 5/5.1 at a fraction of their price. The release is evidence that aggressive post-training of open-weight bases can approach frontier coding performance cheaply.

Source: Cognition, 29 September 2026
