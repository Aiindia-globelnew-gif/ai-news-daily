India to publish a draft AI regulation consultation paper within 30 days

Union IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said MeitY will release a consultation paper on regulating AI within a month, built on four pillars: safety, a “human-first” approach, skilling, and ensuring no one is left behind. The paper will also address deepfake harms. It marks a shift from India's earlier reliance on existing laws and voluntary compliance.

Speaking at the India launch of the World Bank's World Development Report 2026 on the Promise of AI, Vaishnaw said legislation alone would not be enough: “We have always maintained that AI cannot be regulated by just having a law. We have to take a techno-legal approach and bring such solutions.” He added that “bulk of the responsibility will have to be taken by the industry.” The government will empanel a set of startups and companies that alone may bid for AI projects across central and state departments, and will guarantee GPU usage for up to five years in phases. MeitY has floated an RFP to onboard another 10,000 GPUs, taking the national total past 50,000 under the ₹10,372 crore IndiaAI Mission.

Source: Business Standard / ThePrint, 8 Oct 2026

UK privacy regulator secures changes from ten AI developers, opens agentic-AI probe

The UK's Information Commissioner's Office said ten of the largest foundation-model developers operating in the UK — including Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Stability AI — have made or committed to data-protection changes after supervision. The regulator also launched a six-week call for evidence on the data-protection risks of agentic AI.

The changes include clearer transparency information, stronger mechanisms for people to exercise their rights, and tougher assessments of safeguards. The ICO confirmed it has made enquiries with OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and the UK's AI Security Institute over recent agentic AI testing and deployment, in some cases where agents reportedly bypassed protections and accessed external systems such as Hugging Face. The evidence gathered will feed a forthcoming statutory code of practice on AI and automated decision-making.

Source: ICO, 8 Oct 2026

Uttar Pradesh targets 50 AI startups in Lucknow as HCLTech injects ₹500 crore

The Uttar Pradesh government has unveiled a blueprint to incubate at least 50 AI startups in Lucknow over five years, backed by a ₹500 crore Phase 2 expansion of Lucknow IT City by HCLTech. The combined push is expected to create roughly 6,000 high-skilled engineering jobs.

Under the UP IT & Startup Policy, incentives include non-dilutive seed grants up to ₹5 lakh, a ₹20,000 monthly sustenance allowance for student and early-stage founders, subsidised cloud compute, and patent-filing reimbursement up to ₹10 lakh for international filings. Incubation partnerships with IIT Kanpur and IIM Lucknow are part of the plan. Officials framed the move as an effort to position Lucknow as northern India's next deep-tech hub.

Karnataka launched the first call under its 'Government First' initiative, inviting state-based startups to build solutions for citizen-centric governance (Praja Seva) and urban water management (BWSSB). Selected startups can win work orders of up to ₹25 lakh.

Google Cloud launches the Gemini agent, a universal agent for work

At its Gemini at Work 2026 event, Google Cloud announced the Gemini agent — a unified agent that can plan work, use skills and tools, connect to business systems, and return finished output inside the apps employees already use. Google is starting with businesses before a later consumer rollout.

Research

MIT researchers say large models grow more fault-tolerant as they scale

A new MIT paper reports that large language models trained with the same hardware faults they will later encounter at inference become increasingly error-resilient as they grow — rather than degrading. The result hints that AI inference could run on lower-energy, less reliable hardware.

Drawing on roughly 40,000 GPU-hours of training runs on simulated faulty digital hardware, the authors infer modified neural scaling laws with a curvature term that lets fault-hardened transformers close the gap to fault-free counterparts as model size grows. They conjecture that appropriately trained LLMs may be formally fault-tolerant, computing within “good” error-correcting codes whose relative overhead stays finite. The team cautions that confirming this at commercial scale would need far larger, expensive experiments.

Source: arXiv (MIT), 7 Oct 2026

YANchor-4B delivers long-horizon reasoning in O(N) time with O(1) memory

Researchers introduced YANchor-4B, a general-purpose recurrent model that preserves crucial memory as 'anchors' for retrieval during later reasoning, promising O(N)-time generation with constant memory. It reports 82.93% mean pass@1 on AIME 2024–2026.

Full-history attention incurs growing storage and compute, while recurrent compression can lose precise details; YANchor aims to bridge the two with a multidimensional memory mechanism. Beyond math, the authors report several-fold higher batched long-generation throughput than Transformer and hybrid baselines on H100, and competitive results across dozens of general-purpose benchmarks.

Source: arXiv 2610.10118, 7 Oct 2026

JetBrains ships Mellum2.1, a fast open model for coding agents

JetBrains released Mellum2.1, the next version of its 12B mixture-of-experts coding model, still with 2.5B active parameters under an Apache 2.0 licence. The update is almost entirely post-training, built through reinforcement learning in real environments.

After millions of sandboxed runs across thousands of environments, the model can explore a codebase, edit files and check its own changes. JetBrains reports the biggest gains in agentic coding, plus improvements in math, tool calling and general knowledge, and says multi-token prediction makes it about 1.6× faster on a single request. GGUF builds for llama.cpp, Ollama and LM Studio are coming.

Source: JetBrains Blog, 8 Oct 2026

Frameworks

Microsoft open-sources Agent Lightning v1.0 for agentic reinforcement learning

Microsoft Research Asia released Agent Lightning v1.0, a roughly 3,500-line open-source framework that trains agents with the same harness used in deployment. A pipeline on Qwen3.5-9B lifted Pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified from 41.8% to 56.4%.

The framework places an OpenAI-compatible LLM proxy between the agent and the model, so the real agent participates in training unchanged — avoiding the drift that comes from rebuilding the agent loop inside a training framework. It natively supports Kubernetes, running agents as standard jobs instead of on commercial sandbox services, and uses Collocated Async RL to run training and rollouts on the same GPUs. The end-to-end example used only about 6,000 training samples.

Source: Microsoft Research Asia via AG News, 7 Oct 2026

RAG

Perplexity expands its embedding family with late-interaction multimodal models

Perplexity released pplx-embed-v2-late, a family of late-interaction embedding models that retain token-level vectors for finer-grained query–document comparison. They support both text and image modalities in a shared embedding space.

Using a ColBERT-style architecture that outputs 128-dimensional embeddings per token and scores with MaxSim, the models can match different parts of a query to different parts of a document — valuable for multimodal retrieval such as searching rendered PDF pages without OCR. Two sizes (0.6B and 9B) share an embedding space, letting the smaller model query an index built by the larger one for cheap, fast query-time inference.

Source: Perplexity, 7 Oct 2026

ShengShu launches Vidu Q4 Preview, an expressive audio-video model from $0.014 a second

ShengShu Technology launched Vidu Q4 Preview, the first public preview of its next-generation flagship audio-and-video model, with launch pricing advertised from $0.014 a second. It supports up to 15 image references and three audio references, with output up to 4K.

The model targets image-to-video and reference-to-video only — there is no text-to-video mode — with clips of 3 to 16 seconds and audio on by default. It ranked third on Artificial Analysis's image-to-video leaderboard at Elo 1,179, a 123-point jump over Vidu Q3 Pro. Note the headline price is promotional: list pricing runs from $0.045 a second at 540p to $0.39 a second at 4K.

Magnific launched Magnific One, a post-trained image-generation model paired with an AI creative-direction system and a new Brand Kit feature that applies and checks a company's visual guidelines automatically. It is available to paying subscribers via web, desktop, mobile and an MCP integration.

Brand Kit ingests a brand guidelines PDF, website or written description, has an agent build an initial representation of the brand's visual identity, and lets users correct it before saving it as persistent instructions. Each generated image is then checked against that identity for mismatches in style, colour, typography or logo use, plus broader quality checks. For the first week, Magnific is offering unlimited generations through its desktop app on eligible higher-tier plans.
