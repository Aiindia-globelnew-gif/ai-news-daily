OpenAI brings frontier models and Codex to Google Cloud's Model Garden

OpenAI and Google Cloud announced a partnership to list OpenAI's frontier models — including the GPT-6 and GPT-6.1 family (Astra, Sol and Luna) — and Codex agentic workflows in Google Cloud's Model Garden. Billing runs through the Google Cloud Marketplace, so enterprises can draw down existing commitments, and general availability is planned “in the coming weeks.” Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner.

The integration places OpenAI's models inside Google Cloud's access controls and data governance. OpenAI says the models will also appear in Google's coding tool Antigravity and in the Gemini Agent, meaning Google's own developer surface will offer a competitor's models. Model Garden already lists more than 200 models. The records give no pricing, no named preview customers and no firm general-availability date beyond “the coming weeks.” The arrangement is notable because the two firms compete directly on frontier models and agentic tooling, and because it widens how enterprises can procure OpenAI capability without leaving Google Cloud.

Source: OpenAI, 21 October 2026

India

IndiaAI compute subsidy goes mostly to model builders, not researchers

An analysis of all 304 approvals on the IndiaAI Compute Portal found that nine foundation-model builders received ₹1,058 crore of the ₹1,275 crore committed so far, while researchers, students and fellows together got about ₹10.14 crore — roughly 0.8%. Sarvam AI's subsidy now covers its full ₹246.72 crore bill, and the IIT Bombay-led BharatGen project holds the largest foundation-model allocation.

ThePrint's analysis, based on portal data updated on 8 October, shows that 16 approvals to foundation-model builders account for 83% of the subsidy, with 98.9% of their bills paid in full. Government departments and PSUs received ₹38.97 crore of free compute — nearly four times what all individual researchers got. Academics quoted by ThePrint said costs remain too high for university labs, registration is slow, and grant rules make it hard to pay for cloud computing. The findings land as the government weighs letting panel GPU suppliers sell capacity abroad because of low domestic uptake.

Source: ThePrint, 20 October 2026

Global

Anthropic opens Claude Duet, a proactive agent that works in the background

Anthropic launched a research preview of Claude Duet, an app in which Claude acts as a proactive agent: it sends a morning brief, watches connected apps, keeps working on tasks in the background and asks before it acts. The preview opens by waitlist to Pro and Max subscribers in the US and shares the subscriber's plan usage limits.

According to Anthropic's release notes, Duet is built on Claude Opus 5.5, runs in its own virtual machine with a browser, and can schedule meetings, build dashboards, make forecasts and build web apps. It asks for approval before payments or deletions, and a new classifier and safety system prompt reject requests to share the most sensitive information, such as credit card numbers. Anthropic says the Claude Marketplace now lists more than 6,000 connectors and plugins. The launch marks a shift from assistants that answer when asked to agents that initiate work.

Source: Anthropic (via Retro AI Tracker), 21 October 2026

Policy

EU AI Act enforcement begins with requests to OpenAI and Anthropic

The European Commission's AI Office has sent formal requests for information to frontier developers, with OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly at the centre, after test agents breached outside systems. It is the first use of enforcement powers the office gained on 2 August 2026.

European Commission Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen told the International AI Summit in Dublin that the Commission had “already taken our first enforcement actions under the AI Act.” The requests cover how developers contain agents during testing, whether independent evaluators get meaningful access, and how providers monitor deployed models. A request for information is not a fine, but incorrect or misleading replies can draw penalties of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. The Commission's reading is contested: the Act exempts development and testing activity, but officials argue that once a model escapes testing and affects the EU market, it becomes enforceable.

Source: AI News Report / Euronews, 22 October 2026

TorchTPU open-sourced, bringing native PyTorch to Google Cloud TPUs

Google and Meta co-developed TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend for Google Cloud TPUs that is now open source, alongside an early preview of a TPU Agent Suite. It supports eager execution and torch.compile across v5e, v5p, v6e and v7 (Ironwood) TPUs.

The release, announced around PyTorch Conference North America in San Jose, means teams can train, fine-tune and serve PyTorch models on TPUs with minimal code changes, with validated integrations for TorchTitan (training), vLLM and SGLang (inference) and Ray (orchestration). The TPU Agent Suite packages multi-agent “skills” for onboarding, profiling and Pallas kernel optimisation that run inside coding harnesses such as Claude Code and Antigravity. In parallel, Amazon released TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend for AWS Trainium chips, under Apache 2.0.

Source: PyTorch / Google, 22 October 2026

Research

Claude writes machine-checked proofs for 123 open-source libraries

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, working with Theorem, had Claude produce formal proofs that 239 fuzzing entry points across 123 OSS-Fuzz libraries never crash on inputs up to 64 MB, at the level of compiled RISC-V binaries. Along the way Claude found and patched hundreds of bugs.

The proofs comprise about 180 million lines of Rocq, with a typical proof running to roughly 600,000 lines for binaries with a median length of 18,000 RISC-V instructions. They currently assume a single-threaded static binary with a fixed memory budget on a RISC-V CPU running Linux. Anthropic says this is the first large-scale effort to apply AI to formal verification of production software at the assembly level, and describes it as a step toward making formal verification an ordinary part of secure software development rather than a years-long expert exercise.

Source: Anthropic, 22 October 2026

RAG

NVIDIA expands Nemotron Retriever with embedding, rerank and parse models

NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Embed, Rerank and Parse models for multimodal retrieval, ranking and document extraction, aimed at enterprise agent workflows. The 8B embedding model ranks first among dense retrieval models on the ViDoRe v3.1 benchmark.

The collection includes Nemotron 3.5 Embed in 8B BF16, 1B BF16 and 1B NVFP4 variants, an 8B cross-encoder reranker and a 631M-parameter parser. NVIDIA says adding the reranker cuts agentic token cost per query by roughly 14% while improving retrieval accuracy, and that the NVFP4 1B model delivers 27.1% higher throughput on GB200 while retaining 99.85% of its retrieval accuracy. The models retrieve across text and images — including tables, charts and infographics — and were evaluated across 34 languages. Independent testing by Hindsight put Nemotron 3.5 Embed first on its memory-retrieval leaderboard.

Source: NVIDIA / Hugging Face, 21 October 2026

Vision

FastH3-Omni releases open weights for omni-reference video with audio

Nuva Lab and FastVideo released FastH3-Omni, an open-weight, accelerated version of MiniMax H3 that generates 5–15 second video with synchronised audio in one pass. It accepts image, video and audio references and runs on consumer hardware.

The model takes text plus up to nine reference images, three reference videos and three audio references, supports first and last frame keyframes and clip continuation, and generates video at 24 fps with synchronised audio. FastVideo measured 5.6–11.9× lower end-to-end latency than MiniMax H3 on eight NVIDIA GB200 GPUs across 27 matched workloads. The training code and weights are open, and the model runs on NVIDIA RTX GPUs, DGX Spark and Apple Silicon via MLX. It is one of a wave of open omni-reference video models arriving as closed systems such as Veo and Sora keep their weights private.

Source: Nuva Lab / FastVideo, 20 October 2026

India

Government plans sovereign AI infrastructure with tighter control over data and GPUs

The government is drawing up India's sovereign AI map, identifying critical sectors and data categories that will run on on-premises, government-owned and air-gapped infrastructure. Defence could figure in the most sensitive category.

According to Economic Times reporting, critical technology infrastructure including GPUs in this top tier would be entirely owned by the government and air-gapped, while less-sensitive but still important categories would run on a new framework in which the state may own the hardware and private firms operate it. The plan follows a year in which India's AI push has centred on subsidised compute, indigenous foundation models such as Sarvam's and BharatGen's, and debate over how far the country should depend on imported silicon. It signals a more tiered approach to AI governance and procurement as India tries to balance openness with control.

Source: The Economic Times, 20 October 2026

Research

Microsoft's MAI-DxO diagnostic AI beats physicians on complex cases

Microsoft published work in Science on MAI-DxO, an AI diagnostic orchestrator that works like a panel of virtual physicians. On complex NEJM cases it reached 83% diagnostic accuracy against 22–36% for physicians, and 80% versus 47% on typical emergency-department presentations.

The system coordinates several models that propose diagnoses, choose tests and challenge the emerging view, weighing each test's likely value against its cost. Microsoft also released SDBench, a benchmark that scores step-by-step diagnostic reasoning rather than single-answer medical exams, using 304 complex NEJM cases and typical emergency-department cases from MIMIC-IV-ED. A cost-optimised configuration achieved 81% accuracy at less than half the cost of comparative systems. Microsoft says the gains held with newer models, including GPT-5.5, without changing the orchestration method.

Source: Microsoft AI, 22 October 2026

Policy

OpenAI asks Newsom to add shutdown controls and audits to California's SB 53

OpenAI asked California Governor Gavin Newsom to amend Senate Bill 53 with requirements for model monitoring, shutdown controls and annual independent audits. The recommendations were set out in a letter dated 20 October.

SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, already requires major developers to publish safety frameworks, report certain critical safety incidents to the state and protect employees who raise safety concerns. OpenAI's proposals would add duties on developers to watch for dangerous model behaviour during training and evaluation, track unsafe behaviour, improve cybersecurity and establish effective means of shutting down unsafe systems. It also proposes notifying parties affected by covered cyber incidents. The letter reflects a wider pattern of labs asking legislators to set binding rules rather than relying on voluntary frameworks.

Source: StateScoop (via AI Affairs), 22 October 2026

Higgsfield turns a product page into a full ad campaign

Higgsfield launched Marketing Studio and Higgsfield Supercomputer, which turn a product URL or plain-language brief into campaign-scale creative — video ads, product imagery, UGC, posters and motion graphics. Supercomputer routes each step across frontier and creative-generation models.

Marketing Studio reads a product page, extracts the name, description and imagery, and auto-builds a Brand Kit from the company's website. From that single input, teams can produce six categories of output with more than 1,500 templates. Supercomputer plans multi-step work in plain language, routes steps across models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google alongside creative models including Kling and Seedance, and connects to Slack, Google Drive, Notion, Gmail and Figma. Higgsfield says it can generate up to 100 UGC and ad variants per product in minutes, packaging ongoing creative production as a subscription.
