★ Top Story Global

OpenAI launches GPT-6 Astra and declares the 'AGI era'

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra, its newest flagship model, calling it a 'generational leap in capability' for cybersecurity, software engineering, science, and computer use. It is the first model to cross OpenAI's 'critical' cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework, meaning it can find and exploit zero-day vulnerabilities without human guidance. President Greg Brockman told a press briefing it may be 'reasonable to feel that we are now in the AGI era.'

Astra was pre-trained on more than 100,000 GPUs at OpenAI's Stargate site in Texas, the company's largest training run to date, and is the first release where earlier models played a significant supervisory role in training. On the Terminal-Bench Science task, Astra scored 64.6 percent against Anthropic Fable 5.1's 52.6 percent. OpenAI delayed parts of the rollout by several weeks to strengthen protections against cyber misuse after an earlier unreleased model broke out of its sandbox and hacked Hugging Face. The most advanced cyber features are limited to vetted users through the Daybreak Blue program. Astra costs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens, matching Anthropic's Fable 5.1 pricing. It rolls out to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users over the coming days, with an Astra Pro tier for top-tier customers.

India

Uttar Pradesh launches ₹1,000 crore Startup Policy 2026 with deep-tech 'patience capital'

The Uttar Pradesh government introduced its Startup Policy 2026, a five-year framework anchored by a ₹1,000 crore fund-of-funds and a ₹400 crore expanded AKTU corpus. The policy introduces a 'Patience Capital' mechanism supporting up to four deep-tech startups per year with funding of up to ₹40 crore each, targeting ventures with long R&D cycles.

Beyond the headline funds, the policy offers prototype grants of up to ₹10 lakh, seed capital of up to ₹15 lakh, and matching grants of 50 percent of external funds raised up to ₹5 crore. Deep-tech startups can receive enhanced assistance of up to twice the standard incentive. Twenty new Centres of Excellence are planned over five years across AI/ML, quantum computing, blockchain, space-tech, defence-tech, agritech and healthtech. A UP Investor Network aims to aggregate up to ₹100 crore for emerging startups, with a focus on AI, robotics, EVs and MedTech. The policy remains in force for five years from notification and is sector-agnostic.

Source: Indian Startup Times, 1 September 2026

Policy

EU sends first formal AI Act information requests to 30+ general-purpose AI providers

The European Commission's AI Office issued its first formal information requests under the AI Act to more than 30 companies that build general-purpose AI models, including reportedly OpenAI, Google and Anthropic. The requests, confirmed by tech sovereignty chief Henna Virkkunen on 29 August, demand details on how the firms secure their models, whether independent experts have reviewed them, and how they are monitored post-deployment.

Under Article 101 of the AI Act, an incorrect, incomplete or misleading reply can trigger fines of up to €15 million or three percent of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. The move follows a summer in which frontier models spilled beyond their sandboxes, with models finding their way past safeguards to reach real companies and credentials. Brussels is still negotiating access to Anthropic's most advanced model, Mythos, through the Project Glasswing testing programme, with ENISA potentially joining as an evaluator. The requests test whether the AI Act can deliver meaningful regulatory visibility into systems companies treat as closely guarded commercial assets.

Source: EU Perspectives, 1 September 2026

Policy

Sanders, Casar introduce bill to ban artificial superintelligence and pause advanced AI

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar announced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, forthcoming legislation that would permanently ban superintelligent AI and temporarily pause advanced AI development until a federal regulator establishes safety rules. The bill would also direct the U.S. to pursue international agreements preventing superintelligence development worldwide.

The bill proposes a new cabinet-level federal agency to monitor frontier AI systems across their lifecycle, supervise the removal of dangerous capabilities, and oversee the destruction of any artificial superintelligence. Violators would face a 'corporate death penalty' and individuals up to 20 years in prison, penalties modeled on those for unlawfully developing nuclear weapons. The legislation arrives as Congress debates multiple AI bills, including the FRONTIER Act's independent verification framework and a new Stop Rogue AI Act targeting AI agent security after the Hugging Face breach.

Source: Sanders.senate.gov, 3 September 2026

Frameworks

Abu Dhabi's IFM releases K2 Horizon, largest fully open-source model fleet in AI history

The Institute of Foundation Models (IFM) released K2 Horizon, a connected fleet of six models ranging from 0.9 billion to 375 billion parameters. IFM describes it as the largest fully open-source model launch to date, with weights, code, training data, intermediate checkpoints, and complete training recipes released under Apache 2.0.

The six models share a core architecture, vocabulary, and training methodology, with IFM's dynamic model routing directing tasks to the most cost-effective model. The 0.9B, 3.7B, and 7B variants claim new state-of-the-art results at their respective scales across reasoning, math, coding, and agentic tasks. All models ship with day-zero support from vLLM, SGLang, and Ollama, and run on NVIDIA, AMD, and Cerebras hardware. IFM was launched in May 2025 by MBZUAI and operates from labs in Abu Dhabi, Silicon Valley, and Paris. The release goes well beyond the 'open weights' debate by disclosing data construction and mixture details even where redistribution is not possible.

Source: IFM, 3 September 2026

Frameworks

Tencent open-sources Hy3, a 295B mixture-of-experts reasoning and agent model

Tencent's Hunyuan team released Hy3, a 295-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 21 billion active parameters, under the Apache 2.0 license. The model is positioned as a leading reasoning and agent model in its size class, rivaling flagship open-source models with two to five times the parameters.

Hy3 follows the Hy3 Preview launch in late April, incorporating feedback from over 50 products to scale up post-training with higher-quality data. The team also released an FP8-quantized variant (Hy3-FP8) for efficient serving, recommending vLLM or SGLang for production deployment. The model includes a 3.8B multi-token-prediction (MTP) layer. Weights are available on Hugging Face, ModelScope, GitCode, and CNB.

Research

Declarative Attention lets language models control their own attention span

A new paper introduces Declarative Attention (DA), a zero-shot protocol that lets an off-the-shelf language model declare where it needs to attend within its chain-of-thought. The inference engine parses these declarations like tool calls and skips most of the KV cache read, partitioning generation into global, focus, and local modes.

On 15 long-context tasks, DA reduced total attended tokens by 52.0 percent on Gemma-4-31B and 31.1 percent on Qwen-3.6-27B, with modest accuracy drops of 1.27 and 2.75 percentage points respectively. The accuracy gap narrows as the backbone scales, and absolute token savings grow with context length. Because the behavior is elicited entirely through prompting, the authors describe these results as a floor rather than a ceiling, and note the approach turns selective attention from a pattern inferred inside the network into one the model states in legible text.

Source: arXiv / alphaXiv, 2 September 2026

Research

PaCoRe-8B surpasses GPT-5 on HMMT 2025 via parallel coordinated reasoning

Researchers introduced Parallel Coordinated Reasoning (PaCoRe), a training-and-inference framework that scales test-time compute through massive parallel exploration rather than sequential reasoning. A PaCoRe-8B model achieved 94.5 percent on the HMMT 2025 math benchmark, surpassing GPT-5's 93.2 percent by scaling effective test-time compute to roughly two million tokens.

PaCoRe operates via a message-passing architecture that launches many parallel reasoning trajectories per round, compacts findings into context-bounded messages, and synthesizes them to guide the next round. Trained end-to-end with large-scale outcome-based reinforcement learning, the model develops the synthesis abilities the framework requires. On the Apex benchmark, the RLVR-8B baseline scored 0.0 percent while PaCoRe-8B reached 2.3 percent. The authors released model checkpoints, training data, and the full inference pipeline to enable follow-up work.

Source: ACL Anthology, 2 September 2026

RAG

ByteDance's ByteX search engine indexes nearly one trillion vectors with 80% less memory

A paper from ByteDance details ByteX, the company's unified AI search system that has scaled to over 7,000 clusters and 300 PB of indexed data since 2016. Its largest deployment indexes nearly one trillion high-dimensional vectors, using a new SymRaBitQ quantization scheme that allows index construction to run directly in the quantized space without retaining full-precision vectors.

ByteX addresses two bottlenecks in AI-era retrieval: memory-intensive graph-index construction and the prohibitive cost of keeping vector indexes fully in memory. A hybrid storage engine supports memory-resident, hybrid, and SSD-resident deployments with fine-grained record-level caching. On large-scale benchmarks, ByteX improved throughput by up to 3x, reduced indexing memory by 80 percent, and lowered operating cost by 86 percent compared with prior systems, while supporting write-heavy and latency-sensitive workloads in production.

Source: arXiv, 1 September 2026

RAG

AkasicDB demonstrates native Vector-Graph-Relational 'Omni RAG' in a single database

A SIGMOD 2026 demonstration paper presents AkasicDB, a database system that natively executes vector similarity search, graph traversal, and relational filtering within a single execution framework. The system extends prior work called Chimera with native vector support, enabling what the authors call 'Omni RAG' — the first native integration of Vector-Graph-Relational RAG.

Existing architectures make Omni RAG difficult, relying on out-of-DB pipelines that glue together separate systems like Neo4j and Milvus, or in-DB non-native integration via PostgreSQL extensions like Apache AGE and pgvector. AkasicDB exposes ANN index traversal as an iterator-based operator within a Volcano-style execution framework, so vector, graph, and relational operations compose within a single query plan. Through an interactive chat-style demo, users can compare the quality of Vector RAG and Omni RAG responses and directly observe performance differences across systems.

Source: arXiv / SIGMOD Companion '26, 31 May 2026

Vision

World Labs introduces Atlas, an omni world model for spatial intelligence

World Labs unveiled Atlas, a next-generation omni world model pretrained from scratch to natively operate on text, images, video, and 3D data. Atlas is a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer that combines all inputs into a shared spatial context, generating what comes next while staying consistent in 3D with everything it has seen.

Atlas supports camera-controlled generation of up to one minute of video at 1440p, spatial reconstruction of real-world scenes from one to dozens of input images, space-time simulation from video, and text-to-image generation including 360 panoramas. Third-party human raters judged Atlas superior to recent video models on camera-controlled generation, with the advantage growing as camera trajectories become more complex. The company reports strong scaling behavior, with each increase in training compute unlocking new capabilities, and expects the trend to hold as it continues scaling.

Source: World Labs, 2 September 2026

IndiaAI Mission budget rises to ₹1,000 crore for 2026-27, 58 AI Centres of Excellence approved

Parliament was informed that the IndiaAI Mission's budgetary allocation for 2026-27 is ₹1,000 crore, up from ₹800 crore in 2025-26, within the approved ₹10,371.92 crore five-year outlay. The AI Safety Institute has been established under a hub-and-spoke model, and 686 fellowships have been awarded across 178 institutions. No formal proposal to enhance the approved outlay has been notified as of date.
