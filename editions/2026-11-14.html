★ Top Story Global

Anthropic lifts its Microsoft cloud commitment to as much as $49.2 billion, days before its IPO

Anthropic raised its minimum spending commitment on Microsoft cloud services by $2.3 billion to $47.2 billion, with the ceiling now $49.2 billion, according to an amended SEC filing dated 10 November. The agreement runs from November 2026 to June 2033 and is payable regardless of how much capacity Anthropic actually uses. The disclosure lands about a week before Anthropic's IPO, which Axios reports is oversubscribed.

The same filing puts Anthropic's aggregate contractual commitments at roughly $900.9 billion, almost all of it computing infrastructure, up from $895.4 billion on 2 November; about $451.6 billion falls due by the end of 2030. Beyond the hyperscalers, Anthropic holds Nvidia GPU capacity through agreements with SpaceXAI (maximum $120.5 billion) and Nscale (about $44.6 billion), plus three Lambda contracts worth around $41.3 billion through 2034. The largest Lambda agreement, worth $34.9 billion, became terminable by either party after Lambda missed a facility-lease deadline, though Anthropic said on 10 November that it does not intend to terminate. Nvidia separately agreed to buy around $10 billion of Anthropic shares in a $26 billion private placement concurrent with the listing.

Sachin Bansal backs Manav Garg's agentic commerce venture, with a $100 million round in sight

Flipkart and Navi co-founder Sachin Bansal has committed $20-30 million to a new agentic commerce startup being launched by Manav Garg, founder of Eka Software and co-founder of Together Fund, Moneycontrol reported on 13 November. The venture is aiming to raise about $100 million in total from global investors, with the final size and structure to be decided once investor interest is gauged. Bansal's commitment follows his own $100 million raise from Prosus.

The bet is that shopping increasingly gets delegated to AI agents rather than navigated by people, a shift that would reorder how marketplaces, brands and payment providers capture demand. Prosus is a common investor in Navi and Emergent, and the report ties the new venture to that circle. Neither the startup's name, its product nor a launch timeline has been disclosed.

India

Razorpay says its Vulcan payments model stops 8x more international card fraud on live traffic

Razorpay published early production results from Vulcan, its transformer-based foundation model for payments, reporting 8-10% better payment success rates and eight times more international card fraud detected and stopped. The model was trained on about 3 trillion data points across 4 billion payments and built with Nvidia and AWS, and components of it already inform routing, fraud and risk decisions in real time. Blinkit, Bachatt and redBus are among the merchants cited.

Razorpay also reports 5x more fraudulent or disputed transactions identified without an increase in the number of alerts, which is the harder half of the problem for risk teams. The figures are the company's own and have not been independently audited. Vulcan is part of a wider push by Indian fintechs to train domain-specific foundation models on transaction data rather than renting general-purpose models for payments work.

Source: ThePrint (ANI), 13 November 2026

India

Karnataka says it is open to subsidising GPU access for startups through its planned AI hub

Karnataka IT and BT Secretary N. Manjula said the state is open to looking at subsidising GPU access for startups on top of the Centre's IndiaAI Mission, after a founder asked about cheaper compute at a women entrepreneurs' mixer ahead of the Bengaluru Tech Summit. No budget, eligibility criteria or timeline has been set. The state's Rs 35 crore AI hub at IIIT Bangalore, approved in September, is due to include an AI Living Lab with bookable compute slots.

IIIT Bangalore director Debabrata Das said hardware costs have risen about fourfold in a year, driven mainly by memory, with GPUs next, and cautioned that compute and memory availability are global constraints likely to persist for another 18 months. IndiaAI Mission has allotted 62,000 GPUs, but utilisation remains marginal and access runs through the national channel rather than the state. Karnataka also said it is drafting a policy to make government datasets available to companies on request, and has committed Rs 23.19 crore to 63 women-led startups under ELEVATE Shakti 2026.

India

Astrome wins Rs 64.05 crore of government support for an AI-enabled intelligence satellite constellation

The Technology Development Board signed an agreement with Bengaluru's Astrome Technologies for Rs 64.05 crore under its Research, Development and Innovation Fund toward a sovereign space-based AI-ELINT constellation, part of a Rs 128.11 crore project. The programme combines an electronic intelligence payload, AI-based ground processing software and inter-satellite communication links, and aims to move the technology from TRL-4 to TRL-9. Support is structured as optionally convertible debt.

The plan envisages 50 kg-class AI-ELINT microsatellites in low Earth orbit, designed to deliver persistent coverage and higher revisit rates over identified areas of interest, with signals detected and analysed across a broad frequency range. It is one of the clearest examples so far of the government pairing defence requirements with domestic AI and space hardware capability. PIB announced the agreement on 12 November.

Policy

EU publishes common rules for AI regulatory sandboxes ahead of the December transparency deadline

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 was published in the Official Journal on 11 November, setting detailed rules for establishing, operating and supervising AI regulatory sandboxes under Article 58(1) of the AI Act. The Commission said common rules were needed to avoid fragmentation and ensure consistent implementation across member states. Sandboxes let developers test systems with regulators before market entry.

The regulation standardises how sandboxes are run, supervised and reported, which matters most for smaller developers who cannot afford parallel national compliance efforts. It lands as firms prepare for the AI Act's Article 50 deadline on 2 December, when generative tools deployed before 2 August 2026 must mark AI-generated audio, images, video and text in machine-readable, C2PA-compatible form. A visible label does not satisfy the requirement, and the penalty tier runs to EUR 15 million or 3% of global annual turnover.

Source: EU Official Journal, 11 November 2026

Policy

Texas lawmakers file bills to create a state Department of AI

Three bills filed ahead of Texas's 90th legislative session would add oversight of how state agencies use AI, including House Bill 605 from Rep. Salman Bhojani to create an independent Texas Department of Artificial Intelligence. The department would run risk-based reviews, maintain a searchable public registry of covered systems and coordinate procurement checks with the Department of Information Resources. None of the bills has been enacted and the session opens on 12 January.

HB 474 would require meaningful human oversight wherever AI is a controlling factor in consequential decisions, with oversight performed by a trained officer able to approve, reject, modify or terminate an action. HB 416 would bar agencies from using AI for employment decisions such as hiring, promotions and terminations, and from entering confidential information into AI systems. If passed, all three take effect on 1 September 2027, with the first statewide inventory of covered AI systems due by December 2028; none of them regulates private AI companies.

Source: GovTech, 13 November 2026

Research

Singularity-Bench asks whether agents can build agents, and one setup clears the human baseline

A new arXiv paper introduces Singularity-Bench, an open-ended benchmark of six end-to-end AI development tasks spanning pre-training, mid-training, post-training, inference, the agent harness and cluster orchestration. Across 11 agents built from seven frontier models and two harnesses, Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Code matched or beat the mature human-engineered baseline on all six tasks, scoring a Singularity-Index of 1.25; the remaining agents averaged 0.58. Every task is graded against human baselines on identical hardware.

The authors also red-teamed the conventional Harbor framework across 9 of the 10 OWASP Top 10 categories for agentic applications, found reward-hacking vulnerabilities, and released Breakwater, an extension they say blocks every attack tested in the study. The framing is deliberately provocative: the benchmark is designed to track how much of AI development agents can already automate, and when they might move from assisting to taking end-to-end responsibility for building stronger successors. All results are the authors' own.

Source: arXiv, 12 November 2026

Research

Ai2's XYBench tests whether a model can spot that the user is asking the wrong question

The Allen Institute for AI released XYBench, a benchmark of 8,115 real-world questions in which the user's stated approach rests on an implicit misconception, and which won the Best Resource Paper award at EMNLP 2026. Built with researchers at Carnegie Mellon, Nvidia and Johns Hopkins, it measures whether assistants identify the misconception and redirect the user toward their actual goal rather than answering as asked. The questions span programming, cooking and home repair.

The canonical example is a user asking how to thicken frosting that keeps sliding off cupcakes, when the real problem is that the cupcakes are still too warm to frost. Getting that right requires inferring an intent the prompt never states, a capability that matters more as assistants move into troubleshooting and agentic execution rather than single-turn question answering. Ai2 frames the gap as one of the more practical failures in deployed assistants.

Source: Allen Institute for AI, 13 November 2026

RAG

IIT Patna's READ drops the vector index and beats standard RAG on a 780-page report

Researchers at IIT Patna and the independent lab TwoSpoon built READ, an embedding-free agentic document search method that replaces chunk-and-embed retrieval with an agent holding three tools: keyword search that returns line numbers, an outline of section headings and a reader that fetches specific line ranges. On 51 questions about a 780-page Gujarat government financial report, READ answered 58.8% correctly against 15.7% for RAG-style retrieval and 27.5% for RAG inside an agentic loop, all on the same Gemini 2.5 Pro base model.

The argument is that chunking destroys the context that makes figures and legal terms meaningful, since a table's units or a defined term may sit paragraphs away from where it is used. An agent navigating an undivided document can look past the chunk boundary and cross-check clues against the outline. It is a pointed challenge to vector-first pipelines for dense financial, scientific and legal documents.

Source: The Batch (DeepLearning.AI), 13 November 2026

Vision

MBZUAI releases PAN 2, a video world model, with open weights and training code

MBZUAI's Institute of Foundation Models released PAN 2 on 11 November with model weights, training and inference code, and documentation, licensing its own components under Apache 2.0. Given a starting scene and a text description of actions, the model simulates what happens next: an LLM-based reasoner structures events, timing, actors and camera movement, a frozen Qwen3.5-9B encoder conditions a 29.2-billion-parameter diffusion backbone, and a Wan 2.2 VAE decodes the latents into video.

The released BF16 checkpoint supports text-to-video and image-to-video at 704x1248 and 24 frames per second, with clips up to 20 seconds. In MBZUAI's own evaluation covering 300 single-action cases and 100 everyday multi-step tasks, PAN 2 completed 75.1% of individual steps and 34.0% of full plans, against 75.1% and 32.5% for MiniMax-H3. The model card is candid about the limits: about two in three tested plans omit at least one step, appearance drifts across long interactions, and the repository does not include the reward-learning and model-soup stages used to produce the released weights.

Source: Data Phoenix, 13 November 2026

Tools

Constant Contact launches Catalyst, an AI marketer that plans the work and then executes it

Constant Contact announced Catalyst at its GURU Conference, an AI marketing solution that scans a small business's digital footprint to learn its voice, builds an itemised plan for review in a chat interface, then creates and executes the work across email, Facebook and Instagram, SMS, ads, Google Business Profile and the company website. Owners approve the plan and can switch off any channel or edit anything before it goes live.

The pitch is an expert the owner hired rather than another tool to learn, aimed at the crowded SMB marketing stack where most owners have neither an agency nor a media buyer. Catalyst ties execution back to business outcomes through system integrations, so results from one week inform the next. It joins a run of agentic marketing launches this week, alongside Sitecore's MarketingIQ, ActiveCampaign opening its platform to MCP connectors and Etisalat UAE's AI Marketing Hub built with Pixis.

Supermetrics puts ad campaign changes inside the chat, with ChatGPT Ads as the seventh platform

Supermetrics launched Campaign Manager on 10 November, letting marketers create campaigns and change budgets, status, targeting and creative across Google, Meta, LinkedIn, Microsoft Advertising, TikTok, Snapchat and ChatGPT Ads from inside an AI chat. Write access is switched on per ad account, budget moves can be capped, chosen changes can require sign-off, and every change is logged. The Helsinki company first described the product a week earlier with six platforms and no OpenAI product on the list.

Treating ChatGPT Ads as a standard channel suggests advertising inside assistants is now being planned as real inventory with its own reporting rather than an experiment. A Business Context feature stores targets, budgets and naming rules once so they need not be restated in each conversation, and campaign audits and monitoring can push alerts to Slack or email. The controls matter because the same conversation can move money in live accounts.
