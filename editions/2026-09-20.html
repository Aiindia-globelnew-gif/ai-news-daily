Subscribers Sue Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI Over 'Pacing the Frontier' Agreement

A proposed class action filed Friday in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California alleges the four leading AI labs violated antitrust law by agreeing to coordinate a slowdown of frontier AI development. Brought on behalf of paid ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Gemini subscribers, the suit argues the pact reduces the value consumers receive — and that safety decisions belong with government, not private agreements between rivals.

The complaint centres on 12 September, when Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a nearly 4,000-word essay urging "industry-wide coordination" to pace the frontier of AI development; OpenAI's Sam Altman, SpaceXAI's Elon Musk and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis publicly endorsed the proposal within hours. The filing also points to a July 2026 statement signed by senior employees across labs acknowledging "intense competitive pressure not to unilaterally slow" development. Lead attorney Nick Rowley warned that AI safety must not be "controlled by private self-serving agreements between the world's most powerful 'for profit' technology companies," while Sen. Josh Hawley has already rejected any antitrust exemption for cross-lab safety talks. Amodei himself had flagged the legal risk, asking the U.S. government to "issue a narrow waiver for certain kinds of safety conversations." None of the four companies immediately responded to requests for comment.

India's AI Framework to Get a MeitY Technical Secretariat, Not a Regulator

India's forthcoming AI governance framework is expected to establish a technical secretariat within the Ministry of Electronics and IT rather than a full-fledged AI regulator, reports TechShots. The body would set guidelines for data provenance, environmental metrics and safety and security, coordinate across ministries and host an AI-incident database — without enforcement powers.

The lightweight design signals New Delhi's preference for coordination over sanction as it finalises rules for one of the world's largest AI user bases, echoing institutional debates in the EU and US while stopping short of empowering a regulator. The secretariat would track AI incidents centrally — a capability most countries still lack. The approach follows reports that India has also dropped a proposal to mandate government approval before launching AI models, a shift aimed at streamlining deployment and fostering innovation.

PM Modi to Launch ₹1 Lakh Crore R&D Fund for Private AI and Deeptech on 3 November

The Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) scheme — a ₹1 lakh crore fund meant to build a private-sector-led R&D ecosystem — is set to be launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 3 November. It will offer long-term, low-interest loans or equity to startups and large companies in sunrise sectors including AI and deeptech.

The scheme is one of India's largest commitments to private R&D and responds to longstanding criticism that the country underinvests in research relative to the size of its economy. By financing AI, semiconductors and other deep technologies with patient capital, the government aims to keep strategic capability-building at home as AI competition between the US and China intensifies. It complements the ₹1.3 lakh crore India Semiconductor Mission 2.0 announced in July.

₹300 Crore Defence AI Centre of Excellence Planned with Sarvam AI

The Indian government is set to establish a ₹300 crore Defence Centre of Excellence in partnership with Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI to strengthen national security and self-reliance. The initiative focuses on developing indigenous generative AI models tailored for military applications, including surveillance and tactical decision-making.

The partnership pairs the state with the startup widely viewed as the flagship of India's sovereign AI push, which is already building inference infrastructure, voice and vision models and enterprise software — with ambitions of a trillion-parameter model. A defence-grade centre would give the armed forces domestically controlled models trained and hosted in India, a growing priority as military AI adoption accelerates globally and questions of data sovereignty sharpen.

Trump Says He Will Create an 'AI Force' and Appoint an AI Czar

US President Donald Trump said on Saturday he is creating an "AI Force" to oversee AI and will announce an AI "czar" in the near future. Writing on Truth Social, he pledged not to "in any way hinder or stifle the Growth of this incredible Industry," while saying the government "will also be looking for BAD" using existing criminal and civil justice systems.

The announcement came after Trump rejected industry calls for a slowdown, describing efforts to limit the technology as part of a "conspiracy" that, if strongly implemented, would drive AI companies "into oblivion and bankruptcy." Details of the AI Force — modelled, he said, on the Space Force — and the czar role were scant, and it remains unclear how they would interact with existing agencies, Congress, and the antitrust suit filed against the major labs. Lawmakers from both parties have warned that Congress risks forfeiting precious time on AI oversight, with the House adjourned until after the November midterms.

Policy

California Governor Orders Accelerated AI Oversight and an Emergency 'Kill Switch'

Governor Gavin Newsom issued an executive order on Friday to dramatically accelerate California's first-in-the-nation independent oversight of AI companies and advance the creation of an "AI kill switch" for frontier models. The order speeds implementation of newly signed laws SB 813 and AB 1405 and convenes national experts to recommend further strengthening of state law within two months.

Proposals under consideration include requiring independent third parties embedded onsite at frontier AI labs to write safety plans, independently verified risk assessments and transparency reports, and an emergency shutoff capability for frontier models verified on an ongoing basis. The order also updates definitions of critical safety incidents to include loss-of-control events such as the recent Hugging Face attack, and calls on Congress and President Trump to adopt California's framework as a national floor — noting that no federal law currently requires AI companies to report dangerous incidents.

'Atria Dawn Preview': Agentic Foundation Model Claims Frontier Results — With Humans as Research Partners

A new arXiv paper introduces Atria Dawn Preview, a foundation agentic language model for scientific research and engineering workflows, trained via a "Verifiable Experience Pipeline" that links tool-mediated interactions to executable environments and externally verified outcomes. Across 16 benchmarks spanning research, engineering and digital work, the model is reported to be competitive with frontier agents, with the highest reported score on five.

Unusually, the paper also studies its own development as a case study in human-AI collaboration, analysing 769 task records from 56 participants alongside agent logs. Participants rated about one-third of completed AI-assisted tasks as infeasible without AI; agents frequently proposed methods and implemented revisions, while humans retained most final decisions and guided exploration through judgment and feedback. The authors argue that progress toward more autonomous AI research must advance both the capacity for discovery and the capacity for meaningful human oversight, preserving accountable human authority over risks and direction. The work is a preprint and has not yet been peer-reviewed.

Source: arXiv via AI News Brief, 19 September 2026

ReactHuman Benchmark: Multimodal LLMs Fumble One in Three Sudden Physical Hazards

A new arXiv preprint, ReactHuman, tests whether multimodal LLMs can react safely to sudden physical dangers — a slipping plate, a falling knife — in simulated household scenarios. Across seven representative models, the authors report roughly one in three hazards was mishandled, and the failures did not shrink with model scale.

The benchmark includes 17 event categories and more than 1,000 reproducible scenarios with ground truth generated by 240 Hz rigid-body simulation, executing each model's plan in simulation to expose its physical consequences. Adversarial objects such as a foam anvil or a steel apple test whether models rely on visual appearance over motion cues. Models often followed fixed behavioural patterns and missed interception points by meter-scale distances even when selecting the correct action. "Reactive safety is far from solved," the paper concludes, offering the benchmark as both a diagnostic and a scalable training signal for embodied agents.

Google Open-Sources AutoBound, a 'Finite-Scale' Extension of Automatic Differentiation

Google Research released AutoBound, an open-source JAX library that computes polynomial upper and lower bounds on a given function over a user-specified interval — a finite-scale analogue of automatic differentiation. Alongside it, the team presents SafeRate, a meta-optimizer that uses those bounds to derive learning rates guaranteed to monotonically reduce a given loss, without time-consuming hyperparameter tuning.

The work starts from the observation that derivatives only describe a function at infinitesimal scale, while practical training decisions — like choosing a learning rate for gradient descent — depend on how the loss behaves over a finite window. AutoBound extends familiar autodiff machinery from TensorFlow, PyTorch and JAX to tight interval bounds, opening new applications in optimization and beyond. The library is available now on GitHub, and the team says its experiments so far "have only scratched the surface."

Ant Group's Venus Team Quietly Open-Sources Realtime-Venus, a 9B Full-Duplex Speech-and-Video Model

Two 9B-parameter full-duplex interaction models — Realtime-Venus-Omni for audio-visual use and Realtime-Venus-Audio for spoken interaction — appeared on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, credited to the Venus Team at Ant Group with Tsinghua University, following a technical report on arXiv. The release shipped everything except the announcement: no launch post, product page or API.

The report claims best scores on six of eight video benchmarks used, including StreamingBench 70.2, and a 97% continuation rate under backchannel interruptions on Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 — figures the authors say exceed Gemini 3.1 Live and GPT-4o, though no independent evaluation has appeared. The design delegates retrieval, complex reasoning and business API calls to a background model, keeping the duplex front end lightweight. It is the second quiet Ant Group release this year after the UI-Venus-2-9B GUI agent appeared on Hugging Face in August.

Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash Brings Agentic Audio-Video Understanding to a 1M-Token Context

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a hosted omnimodal model that ingests text, image, audio and video and pairs a near-one-million-token context with agentic tool use — built to act on long multimedia, not merely describe it. Alibaba reports audio-visual performance close to Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash, with hourly API costs for audio input down over 98% versus its predecessor.

The model uses "agentic perception" — deciding what to examine, then gathering evidence across coarse-to-fine passes — which Alibaba says lifted long-video accuracy from 63.4 to 67.8 on an internal benchmark while cutting tokens per query by roughly 46%. It accepts two-hour videos and three-hour audio in a single request, supports speech recognition in 74 languages, and is available through Qwen Chat and Alibaba Cloud Model Studio in six regions. No open weights were released for this version, though the companion Qwen-MM-Plugins toolkit is Apache 2.0. All benchmark and cost figures are vendor-reported pending independent testing.

HiDream Launches Native Omnimodal Video Model with Synchronized Audio, Ranks in Global Top 10

Beijing-based HiDream.ai announced HiDream-O1-Video-1.0, a native omnimodal video generation model that jointly models text, video and audio within a single generation process. HiDream says V1 incorporates duration into narrative planning, choosing an appropriate clip length based on how an event unfolds rather than fixing it in advance, with character consistency, physical plausibility and audiovisual synchronization as explicit design goals. "The next generation of video models will not be defined solely by higher resolution or longer duration," said CTO Yao Ting. The company also announced a Series C+ financing round backed by New Micro Capital, Jiaozi Capital and ICBC Capital; the model is in internal testing with broader availability promised soon.

AWS Maps Out Vector Store Choices for Bedrock RAG: OpenSearch, Aurora pgvector or S3 Vectors

AWS published detailed guidance for choosing the vector backend behind Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases, comparing Amazon OpenSearch Service, Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL with pgvector, and Amazon S3 Vectors across distinct RAG use cases. The three backends trade off latency, hybrid search capability, transactional consistency and cost at scale.

OpenSearch Serverless suits real-time workloads needing hybrid keyword-plus-vector search with reciprocal rank fusion; Aurora with pgvector is the balanced middle ground for conversational applications, delivering sub-100ms latency with the operational familiarity of SQL; and S3 Vectors targets cost-sensitive, large-scale workloads, cutting vector storage costs by up to 90 percent and scaling to tens of millions of vectors per index. The guidance lands as hybrid retrieval adoption accelerates and enterprises rebuild RAG stacks for agentic workloads over billions of embeddings.
