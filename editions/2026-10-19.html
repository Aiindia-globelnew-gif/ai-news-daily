★ Top Story India

India moves to buy 10,000 more AI chips for a national compute pool open to startups

India is moving to buy 10,000 more AI chips for a national compute pool that will be open to startups, according to a WION report, expanding the shared GPU capacity at the centre of the IndiaAI Mission. The programme's common compute facility has been built around roughly 18,700 GPUs, and recent tenders have let bidders also sell capacity abroad.

For founders who cannot buy or rent high-end accelerators at commercial rates, subsidised access through a national pool is the practical route to training and inference. The IndiaAI Mission runs on seven pillars: compute, an innovation centre, datasets, application development, future skills, startup financing and safe AI, under an outlay of Rs 10,372 crore cleared in March 2024. State-level centres of excellence, such as Kerala's in Thiruvananthapuram and a newly approved one in Lucknow, are designed to route that compute to early-stage teams.

The two AI rounds together account for $35.6 million of the week.

AKTU Innovation Hub secures a Rs 20-crore AI Centre of Excellence for Lucknow

AKTU Innovation Hub has secured an AI Centre of Excellence for Lucknow under the IndiaAI Mission, a Rs 20-crore project running four years and approved by MeitY. It follows Kerala's centre in Thiruvananthapuram, which opened on 30 September with a first cohort of 15 startups.

State centres of excellence are meant to give founders more than grants: cohorts, lab access and needs-based subsidised compute. Kerala's centre, run by Digital University Kerala, spans healthcare, climate and disaster resilience, e-governance and agriculture. Odisha has in-principle approval for a Bhubaneswar centre and Delhi announced two centres in April 2026; no cohort call is open yet for Lucknow. IndiaAI Mission runs on seven pillars with an outlay of Rs 10,372 crore cleared in March 2024.

Global

Google releases Gemini 4 Argon worldwide to developers and businesses

Google made its flagship Gemini 4 Argon available globally to developers and enterprises through API access, following a limited rollout to trusted cyber defenders. Google says Argon is the most powerful model DeepMind has built, with an output limit of up to 1 million tokens, at introductory API pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.

Argon arrives after months of delays and a leadership shake-up at DeepMind, and Google is pitching it at coding, research and professional workflows. Independent testers place it near the top of the frontier pack: Artificial Analysis ranks it level with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and behind Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, while Google says it scored 97.5% on ARC-AGI-3. Introductory pricing is set to double to $4/$20 per million tokens on 1 December 2026.

Meta unveils Muse Wonder and Wonder Full, claiming parity with OpenAI and Anthropic

Meta released two 'frontier' models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, saying they perform at a level comparable to OpenAI's and Anthropic's best on personal and professional agentic tasks. Meta cites benchmarking from Artificial Analysis showing Wonder Full at 56 on its Intelligence Index, matching Claude Sonnet 5.5.

Muse Wonder scores 52 on the index, on par with GPT-5.1 Sol, and is available through Meta's API at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens; the more capable Wonder Full is reserved for paid Muse Code subscribers. Meta says the models add 'Active Memory' across conversations and 'Active Focus' to recover from errors, and that Wonder will also power its personal assistant. The comparisons rest on Meta's own testing plus third-party index scores.

Policy

Newsom signs an executive order on AI kill switches and independent model verification

In his final months as California governor, Gavin Newsom issued an executive order asking for independent verification of AI models and setting up an expert committee to design kill switches for AI agents that could go rogue. The move revives measures he vetoed in 2024, when he warned they would 'thwart the promise of this technology'.

The order asks the California Emergency Services Office for recommendations by 15 November and could lead to a special legislative session. Experts are split: the Distributed AI Research Institute's Alex Hanna calls it a lame-duck signal rather than a plan, while the Center for AI and Digital Policy says the kill-switch clause is weaker than the ex-ante termination obligation in the vetoed SB 1047. California already requires large developers to disclose serious safety incidents under a 2025 law.

Policy

China proposes export controls on 23 AI technologies, sparing API access

China's Commerce Ministry proposed placing 23 categories of AI technology, including models, agents and computing clusters, on its export-control catalogue, which could require licences when companies deploy restricted technology abroad. Running a restricted Chinese model abroad would count as an export, while selling access to it over an API would not.

The draft distinguishes hosted access, local deployment and open releases, and a temporary pre-export registration requirement is already in force. The final catalogue remains under consultation until 15 November. The proposal lands amid tighter US and Taiwanese controls on AI chips and servers, and follows a September window on model-weight export controls that closed without a formal decision.

Source: Think Facility, 18 October 2026

Research

Even the best models pick the better agent path only 59.7% of the time, Taste-Bench finds

Researchers from Microsoft and City University of Hong Kong published Taste-Bench, a benchmark measuring whether an agent picks the better direction at a decision fork in a long task. The best of 14 models tested, GPT-5.6 Sol, answered 59.7% of 502 questions correctly, where random guessing scores 25%.

Forks come from real coding and ML-research runs, and a model scores only if it answers correctly in both option orders. The authors report that a larger reasoning budget does not improve accuracy, and that forks whose deciding evidence appears later are far harder: average accuracy falls to 21% when the clue only appears after extensive later work. They also show taste can be distilled, lifting agent success on 41 held-out SWE-bench Pro tasks from 14.6% to 33.7%.

Source: The Decoder, 18 October 2026

Frameworks

Google open-sources Harness2, a framework for agents that improve their own harness

Google Research released Harness2, an Apache-2.0 framework in which an agent recursively improves its own harness: the system prompt, guardrails, memory, sub-agents, skills, scripts and plugins around the model. Using a Propose-Probe-Compose scheme, a single step yielded up to an 11.5-point gain on WorkBuddy-Bench with Gemini 3.5 Flash and GLM-5.

The method tests edit variants on a single task and assembles a configuration from behavioural contrasts rather than a ready-made answer. The framework runs locally (Linux, Python 3.12, uv), its demo works without API keys, and the method is described in arXiv:2610.14224. The authors caution that contrastive improvement does not guarantee correctness and that independent verification is still required for legal and safety-critical tasks.

Source: MarkTechPost, 18 October 2026

DeepSeek open-sourced DeepSeek Harness v0.1, its first agent framework, under the permissive MIT licence. The developer-preview tool is aimed at building coding agents and automated workflows and is available through the deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness repository on GitHub.

RAG

NVIDIA ships a 1.55B multimodal embedding model for text-and-image retrieval

NVIDIA released Nemotron-3.5-Embed-1B-BF16, a 1.55-billion-parameter embedding model for multimodal and multilingual question-answering retrieval. It lets teams query a corpus with text against document pages supplied as images, text or combined inputs, targeting RAG workflows that must handle tables, charts and infographics.

The model pairs a Pixtral vision encoder with a frozen language model initialised from Nemotron-3-Embed-1B, preserving text retrieval accuracy while adding vision, and was distilled from an 8B teacher. It supports a 32,768-token context, emits a single 2,048-dimensional L2-normalised vector per input, and was evaluated on text retrieval across 34 languages and image retrieval across 18. It is available under the OpenMDW-1.1 licence for commercial or non-commercial use.

Vision

NVIDIA releases Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a 120B open multimodal agent model

NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open-weight vision-language model with 120 billion total parameters and 12 billion active per token, built for continuously running enterprise agents. It merges text, images, documents and short video clips into a single output stream with a context length of up to 1 million tokens.

The architecture is a hybrid of Mamba-2, mixture-of-experts and attention layers, and the weights ship under the OpenMDW-1.1 licence on Hugging Face and NVIDIA's NIM service. NVIDIA reports 63.44% on Hermes Bench against 56.33% for Nemotron 3 Ultra, and says Dynamo recipes deliver an 8x increase in users served on a 16-GPU B200 cluster. A compact 74B 'Bolt' variant targets local agents, with a 4GB variant due later in October.

Source: NVIDIA via ai notis, 17 October 2026

Vision

Kuaishou's Kling 4.0 goes live to all users with 30-second single-shot video

Kuaishou fully launched Kling 4.0 on klingai.com, opening its latest video generation model to all users. The upgrade adds native 30-second single-shot generation, 4K 10-bit HDR output, dual-channel stereo audio, improved lip-sync accuracy, and MCP/CLI integration.

Creators can steer a shot with up to 10 keyframes and feed up to 10 reference images, five video clips and seven subjects in a single task, with prompts up to 8,000 tokens and an auto-refinement feature Kuaishou calls 'director thinking'. Output extends to roughly two minutes through continuation passes and supports a 21:9 ultra-wide ratio. The MCP and CLI integrations, plus an enterprise API due soon, signal a shift from standalone demo to embedded production workflow.

Ahrefs introduced Letaido, an AI marketing platform that runs on the company's proprietary web index and promises to handle marketing reports, app builds and content automations in a single environment. The differentiator is native access to Ahrefs' crawl data rather than a standalone AI layer.
