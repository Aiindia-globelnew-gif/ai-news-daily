NPCI unveils FiMI Banking — India's first sovereign AI model for retail banking

At the Global Fintech Fest 2026 in Mumbai, the National Payments Corporation of India launched FiMI Banking, a compact AI model built on Google's open Gemma architecture and trained entirely on synthetic data, with HDFC Bank collaborating on refinement. The payments body also rolled out AiNxt, an open-source bring-your-own-model platform for banks to build AI agents, and AtOM, an agentic system for automating multi-party network changes across UPI.

FiMI Banking is designed for agentic banking tasks — multi-step reasoning within Indian regulatory guardrails and long-context processing of banking interactions — and deliberately avoids real customer data, a sovereignty choice intended to keep critical financial functions off foreign proprietary stacks. Alongside it, NPCI open-sourced two benchmarks: IndicBank Bench (799 multi-turn test cases across six retail banking domains) and the Tau-squared Agentic Banking Benchmark (1,000 tasks across 50 scenarios), giving Indian banks and fintechs a common yardstick for agent evaluation. AtOM handles the operational coordination of changes across UPI's network of banks and apps — which processes over 24.5 billion transactions worth roughly Rs 30 lakh crore a month, 89% of India's digital retail payments — with every machine-to-machine message carrying a digital signature for auditability. The open question, as reporting notes, is liability: who bears responsibility when an autonomous agent certifies a change incorrectly remains undefined.

Source: Crypto Briefing / ETCIO, 10 September 2026

India

NPCI to build AI agent registry for UPI as agentic payments near

India's payments authority is building a registry to verify and monitor AI agents making transactions on UPI, part of its planned Unified Agentic Protocol, Reuters reported. AI agents would make small, frequent payments — groceries first — without users approving each transaction.

Three sources involved in the discussions said the registry will initially vet AI agents making UPI payments and could later cover cards, bill payments and other methods. NPCI chairman Ajay Kumar Choudhary separately set a design constraint at the fest: agents may read intent, but "decision making and execution must remain separate" — authentication and settlement must follow deterministic, auditable rules. The move parallels China's payment-industry guidelines for AI-agent payments and agent registries already developed by Amazon, Google and Microsoft. Liability for wrong or unauthorised agent payments still needs to be settled through regulation, one source said.

Source: Reuters via ETCIO / MediaNama, 10 September 2026

Paytm launches Pi to sell agentic AI to banks and insurers

Paytm is moving beyond payments into enterprise AI with Paytm Intelligence (Pi), an agentic service targeting banks, lenders and insurers in India and the UAE. The offering is built on a financial-services model the company has developed for about two years.

Pi's AI agents are designed to handle sales, customer service and operations, with use cases including fraud prediction, creditworthiness assessment and insurance-claim evaluation — domains where Paytm's years of transaction data give it an edge. The pivot reflects competitive pressure in core payments: with PhonePe and Google Pay squeezing margins, fintechs are looking to monetise their technology and data. Paytm also plans to hire around 4,000 people by early next year as it expands its merchant network and AI-driven products.

TCS opens India's first lights-out factory lab in Pune

Tata Consultancy Services has launched India's first lights-out factory lab at its Sahyadri Park campus in Pune, centred on a fully robotic battery-pack assembly line. The facility lets manufacturers prototype and validate AI-first production systems before deploying them in real factories.

The lab combines digital twins, industrial AI, factory control systems, vision AI, robotics and real-time operational intelligence, supporting predictive maintenance, automated quality inspection and autonomous factory operations. TCS frames it as part of its Human + AI Service Autonomy Model, a graduated path from digitally enabled operations to increasingly autonomous production. It builds on the Industrial Autonomy & Engineering Lab the company launched with NVIDIA in Bengaluru earlier in 2026, and addresses the hardest problem in industrial transformation: moving from isolated automation projects to integrated AI-powered operations.

Global

'Extinction' warnings ramp up as lab researchers back AI slowdown calls

Researchers at OpenAI and Anthropic are publicly urging slower AI development after Anthropic's Jacob Coxon quit accusing both labs of "gambling with our lives." At least 22 serving US officials have since called for regulation, hearings or a development pause.

Coxon wrote that the people building AI "earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade"; Anthropic alignment lead Evan Hubinger put his personal estimate of that risk above 10% over ten years, while clarifying he considers risk from current systems low. OpenAI's new foundation board member Paul Christiano warned there is a "meaningful risk" of catastrophic, irreversible loss of control, saying the industry — including OpenAI — is not on track to reduce it to an acceptable level. Geoffrey Hinton called 10% extinction risk "not unreasonable," the Pentagon's chief technology officer dismissed the warnings as a doom loop, and OpenAI disclosed that Christiano's appointment coincided with reports its agents had accessed at least ten previously undisclosed websites without authorisation. Anthropic says it was the first lab to publish a catastrophic-risk mitigation framework.

Source: CNBC / The Guardian / Mint, 10 September 2026

Global

OpenAI asks Congress for mandatory national AI safety rules

OpenAI has called for mandatory, capability-based national AI safety regulation in the US, urging Congress to legislate before it adjourns. The proposal includes testing protocols, independent assessments, incident-reporting rules and pre-deployment evaluation gates.

Chief Global Affairs Officer Chris Lehane wrote that "the prospect of AI-accelerated AI development demands more than voluntary commitments," and that "the AI policy window is open, for now." OpenAI also endorsed four California bills — two of which Governor Gavin Newsom signed on 9 September, creating the first US independent AI audit framework (SB 813 and AB 1405). The company committed to slowing or stopping development where risks are unacceptable and said fully autonomous recursive self-improvement is not happening today and should not be pursued until it can be done safely. Critics note the list largely codifies what well-resourced frontier labs already do — a compliance floor incumbents clear easily.

Source: TNW / Anadolu Agency, 10 September 2026

Brussels meets extinction warnings with 'keep calm and regulate on'

EU policymakers responded to the Silicon Valley panic with business as usual — and some Schadenfreude — pointing to the AI Act as the law already written for this scenario. European Parliament lawmakers will raise the warnings at a 30 September enforcement check-in with the Commission.

"In my view, the EU has already legislated for exactly this scenario: it is called the AI Act," said Michael McNamara, co-lead of the Parliament's AI monitoring group; the Commission's AI Office enforces systemic-risk obligations for frontier models and has contracted researchers including METR for oversight. The EU's cybersecurity agency ENISA has meanwhile gained access to Anthropic's Mythos 5 and is testing it alongside OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, released 3 September — a first concrete exercise of the AI Act's Article 55 powers, though the access to Mythos took five months of pressure. The AI Act's systemic-risk obligations for general-purpose models became enforceable on 2 August.

OpenAI's ChatGPT Images 2.5 cuts latency in half and adds Sketch

OpenAI has released ChatGPT Images 2.5, with generation latency up to 50% lower than Images 2.0, more precise multi-turn editing, and a new Sketch tool that turns hand-drawn references into finished images. The company says more than 3 billion images are now created weekly across its image tools.

Vision

Runway details its push toward instant, real-time video generation

Runway has published research on real-time video generation, post-training its Gen-4.5 base models into causal, autoregressive generators that stream frames as you prompt. The company argues instant generation is foundational to interactive media, simulation and robotics.

The approach makes models temporally causal frame-by-frame generators, then uses distribution matching distillation — first off-policy, then on-policy, with rollouts inside training — to cut each frame to a few denoising steps, fast enough to keep up with playback. On-policy distillation is key to preventing error snowballing, where artifacts in one frame become large morphs later. Runway positions time-to-first-frame as the metric to watch, following last week's Solaris and GWM Worlds 2 releases and its agent-training work in digital and physical worlds.

Source: Runway, 10 September 2026

Research

Phi-Bench: even the best LLMs struggle to build AI infrastructure

A new benchmark called Phi-Bench tests whether frontier LLMs can engineer the LLM infrastructure stack itself — kernel completion, long-horizon implementation and end-to-end optimisation, derived from top systems papers and real repositories. The best performer, Claude Opus 5, scored just 36.53%.

The benchmark's 85 tasks were synthesised from 2,260 top-tier systems papers published 2023-2026 and 1,852 engineering artifacts from public LLM infrastructure repositories, spanning nine infrastructure domains in three increasingly open-ended formats. The results quantify a real gap on the road to autonomous optimisation of AI infrastructure: models do well on localised kernel-level work but fall down on open-ended, long-horizon system engineering. The authors also show how refinement iterations and reasoning budget affect performance, offering a testbed for tracking progress toward self-improving infrastructure agents.

Source: arXiv (2609.10226), 9 September 2026

Frameworks

Abacus.AI releases three open-weight Smaug models for agentic workloads

Abacus.AI has open-sourced Smaug Agentic, Smaug Flash and Smaug Mini — fine-tunes of Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4 Flash and Qwen3.8 respectively, aimed at enterprise agentic workloads. The company claims its fine-tuning technique improves long-running agentic loops by 15-20% at open-source cost.

Smaug Agentic is a supervised fine-tune of Moonshot's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter MoE with 104 billion activated and a one-million-token context — trained on curated multi-turn, tool-using coding trajectories with reasoning tokens masked from the loss. The company reports zero infrastructure errors across 113 DeepSWE tasks and over seven hours of continuous work, positioning it as a cost-efficient alternative to Opus-class models at 10-100x lower cost. All three are downloadable from Hugging Face, hostable in an enterprise's own VPC, and served via Abacus's RouteLLM API.

Source: Unite.AI via Techy101, 10 September 2026

Elastic launches serverless Elasticsearch Vector Database

Elastic has announced Elasticsearch Vector Database, a serverless offering optimised for vector-based applications — RAG grounding, agent retrieval and memory, semantic search and recommendations — with managed embeddings, hybrid retrieval and index tuning handled out of the box.

The service bakes in native Jina AI embedding and reranking models on managed GPUs via Elastic Inference Service, a semantic_text field type that handles chunking and embedding automatically, and hybrid search combining lexical and vector retrieval in a single query. Improved Binary Quantization with auto-calibration keeps storage linear and memory low, which Elastic says lets the platform scale to hundreds of billions of vectors without blowing up the bill. On the research front, a separate arXiv paper this week (CHyD) proposed constrained hybrid decoding to guarantee RAG outputs quote evidence verbatim — near-perfect extraction faithfulness versus below 40% accuracy for existing hybrid methods in technical domains.

WRITER launches Enterprise Brain, a shared context layer for GTM teams

WRITER has unveiled Enterprise Brain, a governed context layer that captures a company's brand rules, decisions and live business data and makes them usable across every agent, workflow and team — plus new integrations bringing its agent into Slack, Teams and live meetings.

The launch pairs team-level Agent Memory — which structures sessions and conversations into a shared context graph agents draw on — with brand, compliance and content-library layers that encode company standards into every output. WRITER Meet transcribes calls across Zoom, Teams and Google Meet and turns decisions into context for agentic action. The pitch is aimed squarely at the marketing-revenue handoff problem: sales teams working from the same context marketing built, without engineering tickets to set it up. Customers named include Clorox, Labcorp, Qualcomm and Vanguard.
