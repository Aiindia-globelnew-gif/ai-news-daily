Sarvam signs five-year defence AI pact to build for the Army, Navy and Air Force

Sarvam has signed a five-year memorandum of understanding with Headquarters Integrated Defence Staff under the Ministry of Defence, creating a single framework for AI projects across the Army, Navy and Air Force as well as joint and tri-service organisations. The work will be carried out by Chanakya, Sarvam's firewalled national-security division, running the company's own Sarvam 105B foundation model on infrastructure controlled within India. Focus areas are decision support, target acquisition and data fusion across the sensor-to-shooter loop, with human oversight retained on operational decisions. The company did not disclose the value of the agreement, the number of projects or deployment timelines.

The pact extends the government's Atmanirbharta push from equipment into military software. Because all three services and the joint organisations draw on the same foundation models, agentic infrastructure and geospatial and perception systems, doctrine and lessons from a project in one service can feed the next; Sarvam and HQ IDS also plan an AI fabric that links elements of India's defence capability. Sarvam, which became a unicorn in June 2026, says Sarvam 105B was trained from scratch in India using compute allocated under the IndiaAI Mission. As Mint noted, the deeper argument is control rather than accuracy: where the data sits, who can push a software update and who holds access permissions all carry national-security weight, and a model that can be deployed inside a controlled environment gives the services more room on each.

Oracle widens its AI build-out to multi-gigawatt and adds Google TPUs

Oracle is expanding its AI infrastructure partnership with Nvidia to multi-gigawatt scale on Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin servers, and will offer Google's tensor processing units to customers for the first time. It is also adding chips from Cerebras and SambaNova and will use its own Acceleron networking silicon in some Nvidia-based systems, reducing its reliance on Nvidia's networking. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The mix-and-match approach marks a break from the single-vendor pattern of the first AI build-out: buyers increasingly want to place each workload on whichever accelerator is cheapest for the job, and Oracle is positioning its cloud as the neutral venue that sells all of them. It lands in the same week that Google and Meta open-sourced a native PyTorch backend for TPUs and AWS put its own Trainium backend into public beta — the portability argument, moving up from the framework layer into procurement. Reuters reported that Oracle plans to use its own networking chips partly to cut dependence on Nvidia's.

Nvidia and AMD put $4 billion into Meta's $95 billion AI raise

Nvidia said it will invest $3 billion in Meta's equity offering and AMD $1 billion, a day after Meta announced plans to raise up to $95 billion to fund AI infrastructure — $35 billion through underwritten offerings and up to $60 billion later through an at-the-market programme. Meta expects capital spending of $135–145 billion this year. Nvidia added that the two companies will deepen work on Meta's Muse agent family and expand Vera Rubin and Vera CPU deployment.

"AI agents represent a new computing paradigm — software that understands our goals, reasons, uses tools and gets work done on our behalf," Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang said, adding that it "changes how we use computers, from operating applications to working with intelligent agents." Mark Zuckerberg framed the expanded partnership around delivering personal agents and frontier models to billions of people, while AMD's Lisa Su pointed to Helios racks already running at Meta and EPYC processors powering Muse. Nvidia has now taken part in 121 investment rounds this year, up from 13 in 2022 according to Dealroom — the supplier-as-shareholder pattern is becoming a defining feature of the build-out.

Vision

Google's Genie 4 world model adds native audio and live text steering

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4, its world model for real-time interactive simulation, as a research preview for trusted testers. The model generates photorealistic environments that can be steered mid-simulation in natural language — changing the weather, moving the camera, introducing a character — and is DeepMind's first real-time world model to generate audio natively, including ambient sound, music and interactive dialogue. It holds a scene coherent for about two minutes, double Genie 3's limit, with markedly better physics for aerodynamics and collisions.

Gemini 3.8 Flash sits in the loop as a guiding agent, observing the scene, breaking goals into steps and checking frames as they arrive; DeepMind also experimented with Gemini 4 Argon decomposing a task into captions for Genie to render, a route toward testing robot scenarios in simulation before they happen in the real world. The team is candid about gaps: visual detail and object permanence drift over longer sessions, audio accuracy is unresolved, and in-world text is often illegible. All generated video and audio carries SynthID watermarking. Access starts with trusted testers, then AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and early-access partners via the Gemini API and AI Studio; no pricing has been announced.

Source: Google DeepMind and TechCrunch, 27 Oct 2026

Tata Communications to spend ₹4,350 crore on a 10,000-km AI network

Tata Communications will invest $400–450 million, about ₹4,350 crore, to build roughly 10,000 km of AI-ready long-haul fibre linking India's main data-centre corridors. The first phase covers Mumbai–Pune–Hyderabad–Chennai; later phases extend to Vizag, Bhubaneswar, Jamnagar and GIFT City, tying the hubs to international cable landing stations. The company expects to add about 6 Pbps of capacity to its existing 1.25 Pbps, funded from operating cash flows, with implementation scheduled between FY2030 and FY2036.

Managing director Ganesh Lakshminarayanan said India's data centre-to-data centre connectivity could become a market exceeding $1 billion over the medium term. The bet is that connectivity, not just compute, is now the binding constraint: India's operational data-centre capacity has grown from about 375 MW in 2020 to nearly 1.8 GW in the first half of 2026, with more than $100 billion committed across hyperscalers, operators and conglomerates. Existing network utilisation is already running at roughly 70–80%, which is why the build is being phased rather than announced as a single switch-on.

Rhodium: India's data-centre boom is mostly serving foreign workloads

A Rhodium Group study published this week puts India's AI data-centre investment commitments above $300 billion over the past year, with operational capacity expected to at least triple in three years — but finds domestic compute badly underused despite subsidised rates. Yotta, which markets itself as India's sovereign AI cloud, estimates nearly 80% of its revenue already comes from foreign clients and plans to route half of its $7 billion of Nvidia GPU purchases through Nvidia's DGX Cloud Lepton marketplace. The report argues that hosting foreign compute, deploying foreign models and selling capacity to foreign enterprises sits awkwardly with a non-aligned stance.

The study also identifies where India's genuine advantage lies. Indian IT firms clocked their worst revenue growth in six years last quarter, yet annualised AI revenue grew by double digits — TCS at 30% and HCL at 60%, albeit from a small base — and AI services now contribute an estimated $12 billion in annualised revenue for the sector, or 7.5% and 8% of revenue at TCS and Infosys. Infosys puts the AI services addressable market at $300–400 billion by 2030. The build-out itself is policy-pulled: February's budget added a 20-year tax holiday for foreign hyperscalers selling overseas plus GST input credits on capital assets, and data centres were formally declared core infrastructure, easing land acquisition and cheap credit.

Policy

India weighs US-style frontier-model safeguards ahead of its AI consultation paper

IT minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said at NDTV World Summit 2026 that the government will issue a consultation paper on AI regulation in the coming weeks, pointing to a release in November. Officials told NDTV the government is examining whether to put the most capable 'frontier' models under a separate safety framework, potentially requiring developers to demonstrate adequate safeguards — including pre-release evaluation and sandbox testing — before release. Deepfakes, financial fraud, psychological harm and human control over autonomous agents are also expected to feature.

Officials drew an explicit contrast with the United States, where executive powers have carried the load: "The US has used executive powers in the absence of a legislation, the Indian system does not allow that. There have to be legislative provisions for India to enact." India currently leans on amendments to the IT Rules, in force since February 2026, that require platforms to label synthetically generated information, with loss of safe harbour as the sanction. Two questions remain open: how to define a frontier model — by training compute, by capability, or both — and how to avoid sweeping small developers and research labs into obligations built for the largest labs. Experts quoted by NDTV argued that human control has to be specified technically: blocking tool calls, revoking access, cancelling pending jobs, plus audit trails and recovery procedures.

Research

AI2 finds LLM 'novelty judges' are far less reliable than they look

The Allen Institute for AI released Old Ideas, Novel Problems, a study of whether large language models can reliably judge the novelty of research ideas, alongside a benchmark, data, code and an automated pipeline for building new evaluation sets. Across six judges — GPT-5.1 through 5.4 and Claude Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.5 and Opus 4.6 — small changes to the evaluation setup produced dramatically different verdicts; in one experiment, changing a few words in a prompt shifted accuracy by more than 50 percentage points. Retrieval and additional reasoning did not consistently help, and two purpose-built novelty systems scored worse than a plainly prompted Claude Opus 4.6 baseline.

The evaluation sets were mined from ICLR 2026 peer reviews rather than acceptance decisions: 154 high-novelty ideas from top-10% accepted papers whose reviewers positively identified originality, and 145 lower-novelty ideas from bottom-10% rejects where reviewers raised concerns. An agentic judge given an extra-high reasoning budget and live arXiv search scored 0.78 macro-F1 against 0.89 for Opus 4.6, at roughly ten times the cost per decision. The finding matters as labs and funders increasingly use model judges to triage submissions and generated ideas.

Source: Allen Institute for AI, 27 Oct 2026

Berkeley's LEANN shrinks a 60-million-chunk RAG index from 201 GB to 6 GB

UC Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab released LEANN, an open-source vector index that stores almost no embeddings and recomputes them at query time, cutting a 60-million-chunk Wikipedia index from 201 GB to 6 GB. The underlying paper won the Best Research Paper award at MLSys 2026. On Natural Questions with Qwen3-4B, LEANN matched HNSW's 25.5% downstream accuracy using 4 GB of storage instead of 188 GB.

LEANN keeps a pruned graph plus a compact product-quantisation table and re-embeds only the nodes on the search path, with two-level search and dynamic batching lifting query speed by 1.4x and 1.8x in ablations. The cost is retrieval latency — 2.48 s versus 0.05 s for HNSW on Natural Questions — though end-to-end time rises only from about 20.95 s to 23.34 s because generation dominates. It is MIT-licensed, installs with uv, and ships apps for local documents, mail, browser history and chat exports, plus a semantic-search MCP server for coding agents.

Source: MarkTechPost, 27 Oct 2026

Frameworks

Google and Meta open-source TorchTPU for native PyTorch on TPUs

At PyTorch Conference North America in San Jose, Google and Meta open-sourced TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend for Google's Tensor Processing Units meant to replace the PyTorch/XLA bridge. The pitch is that porting becomes a change from model.to("cuda") to model.to("tpu"). Four repositories shipped: TorchTPU, vLLM-TorchTPU, SGLang-TorchTPU and TorchTitan, with Hugging Face models working out of the box and Ray and llm-d scaling workloads across hosts on Kubernetes.

Because the backend plugs into PyTorch's low-level dispatcher, the vLLM and SGLang inference engines run natively and TorchTitan covers dense, mixture-of-experts and diffusion training. AWS shipped a comparable native backend, TorchNeuron, for its Trainium chips in public beta days earlier. The caveat from early coverage is that the one-line device change gets a model running, not tuned — budget for sharding and kernel work, and expect a long tail of unsupported operators. No price, benchmark or adoption figures were published with the release.

RAG

Pinecone open-sources pg_pinecone, a Postgres extension for vector search

Pinecone released pg_pinecone, an Apache 2.0 Postgres extension that indexes the pgvector columns teams already have, using the search engine Pinecone runs at scale. Rather than a graph index, it stores a compact summary of each vector as RaBitQ codes in clusters, and applies metadata filters during the search rather than after it, so a query scoped to one tenant still returns a full result set. Queries stay standard SQL and the full vectors never leave Postgres.

Three engines — native (no Pinecone account, API key or network calls), pgvector's HNSW and a remote managed index — switch with a single call, and tables, syntax and data stay put. Pinecone reports 2.3 ms against 4.7 ms and 6.7 ms for baselines at 0.99 recall, though these are vendor numbers from a first release. The extension also adds an embed() function that calls Pinecone inference, BM25 full-text search on the same table, and rerank() in SQL. It lands in a week of consolidation around filtered search: AWS added metadata pre-filtering to S3 Vectors, and Actian added IVF indexing and vector quantisation to VectorAI DB.

Sprinklr opens beta of six connected marketing AI agents

Sprinklr launched a beta of Sprinklr Marketing AI Agents, a connected set of six specialised agents covering ideation, creative, campaign execution, compliance, community and budget optimisation. The pitch is compressing the path from a live signal to an activated campaign — a process the company says typically takes weeks — while keeping people in control of strategy, creativity, approvals and brand decisions. The beta is open to customers through their account teams during CXUnifiers 2026.

Each agent has a narrow job. The Ideation Agent connects performance, listening, audience and competitive signals to propose campaigns or adjust scheduled content as conditions change. The Creative Agent scales approved assets through variations, localisation and A/B testing rather than generating from scratch. The Campaign Execution Agent turns media plans into campaigns and audiences and flags mismatches, such as creative language that does not match the target market, before compliance checks. The Compliance Agent automates repeatable checks against brand standards, policy and channel rules, escalating exceptions to humans, and the Budget Optimisation Agent recommends allocation before launch and mid-flight.

Adobe's new Composer turns a website URL into a brand concierge in minutes

Adobe redesigned the Composer in Brand Concierge so marketers can create, customise, test and manage conversational experiences starting from a single public website URL, producing a working on-brand concierge in about five minutes. Composer drafts the brand expression, profile, instructions and guardrails from the site's own content, and applies an initial visual style from its colours and fonts for practitioners to review rather than build.

The product ships four ready-made skills — site advisory, product advisory, catalog discovery and product comparison — and four integrations including knowledge base search, content AI search, product catalog and Adobe Commerce MCP, so a live catalog can answer on current price, stock and variants. A multiconcierge capability manages up to 10 independently configured concierges in one sandbox, and teams can bring their own agents through MCP and A2A. Functional, out-of-scope and safeguard evaluations let marketers test a concierge before it goes live, which is the part enterprise buyers will check first.

Source: Adobe, 26 Oct 2026
