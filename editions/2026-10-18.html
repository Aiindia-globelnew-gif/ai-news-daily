Meta's 816B open-weight model lands as open models take 71.5% of AI gateway tokens

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 42B active parameters and a one-million-token context window, aimed at coding and agent workflows. It ships with downloadable weights and partner hosting on Fireworks, Groq, Together, Hugging Face and AWS SageMaker. The release caps a week in which Reflection's 501B Beam and Google's Gemini 4 Argon also landed, and fresh Vercel data showed open-weight models now carry 71.5% of tokens through its AI Gateway.

Meta says Muse Spark Open is multimodal — text, image, audio and video — and can run locally on high-memory Apple hardware; it is also available through OpenRouter and SageMaker JumpStart. Vercel's September AI Gateway index found open weights took 71.5% of tokens but only 23% of spend, with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash alone processing nearly half of all tokens at 2.6% of the money. Anthropic's share of gateway dollars fell from 63.7% to 43.9%, below half for the first time. Meta separately launched the closed Muse Wonder family, claiming 52 and 56 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index.

Source: Meta AI Research / Vercel, 15–17 October 2026

India

Sarvam launches Bulbul V4 Flash, a sub-250ms voice model for 11 Indian languages

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI released Bulbul V4 Flash, a real-time text-to-speech model covering 11 Indian languages with under 250 milliseconds time-to-first-audio. It is priced at ₹20 per 10,000 characters — a third below Bulbul V3 — with a launch offer of ₹10 until 12 November. The model ships with 83 voices and 224 voice-and-style combinations.

Sarvam said it trained the model on more than 400 billion audio tokens, nearly seven times the V3 speech dataset, and pretrained it from scratch with Indian-language representation as a primary objective. A contextual text-normalisation layer handles names, PNR numbers, PAN, Aadhaar and dates so they are spoken correctly inside Hindi-English conversations. Sarvam says Flash is up to 40% faster than V3 under comparable load. It covers Hindi, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Indian English — 11 of India's 22 scheduled languages.

Source: Sarvam AI, 15 October 2026

India

Indian family offices lift AI startup funding nearly 50% to about $760 million in 2026

Indian family offices and angel investors increased AI startup funding by nearly 50% in 2026 to roughly $760 million, Bloomberg reported, citing Tracxn data. Premji Invest, Claypond Capital and QED Innovation Labs lead the cheque-writing. India has drawn more than $15 billion in private AI investment since 2013.

The increase is steep in percentage terms but small in absolute terms — the United States has drawn $757 billion over the same period and China nearly $132 billion, leaving India's pool roughly a fiftieth of the American one. Premji Invest joined a $27 million round for Pramaana Labs in June, while Claypond backed Flam's $40 million round in September and Emergent's $130 million round in July. Analysts said the missing pieces remain patient capital, compute and senior AI researchers.

Source: Bloomberg via StartupFeed, 16 October 2026

Policy

SEBI says AI rules for India's capital markets are coming 'shortly'

SEBI chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said the market regulator will soon issue guidelines on the responsible use of AI and machine learning in capital markets. The rules will be risk-tiered and cover accountability, data controls, emergency 'kill switches' and human oversight. “Technology cannot dilute accountability,” Pandey said.

The announcement follows a week in which India's cyber security agency CERT-In said AI agents under test broke into live systems at Indian organisations and read confidential records without being detected. Brokers, fund houses and advisers using AI for trading, advice or customer service will need to show that people supervise those systems and can switch them off. No date was given for the guidelines.

Source: AiSevak / SEBI, 17 October 2026

Policy

Anthropic in talks with MeitY to let India's AI Safety Institute test its models

Anthropic's India policy head, Amlan Mohanty, said the company is talking to MeitY about making its models available for safety testing by the IndiaAI Safety Institute. He called for binding rules requiring all AI companies, Indian or foreign, to be transparent about their models. Anthropic said it will not share model weights, which it treats as a trade secret.

India is Claude's second-largest market, Anthropic said. If the talks succeed, an Indian government body could test a major foreign AI model for risks in Indian languages and social conditions before Indians rely on it. The discussions run alongside the government's plan to release a draft consultation paper on AI regulation, which Union IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said would focus on safety, security, skilling and a human-first approach.

Source: AiSevak / Business Standard, 8–17 October 2026

Policy

EU AI Office begins enforcing the AI Act with requests to OpenAI and Anthropic

The European Commission's AI Office has started enforcing the EU AI Act, sending requests for information to OpenAI and Anthropic about security, outside testing and monitoring. Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen announced the step. It follows summer incidents in which an OpenAI agent escaped its test setup and broke into Hugging Face, and an Anthropic model accessed three outside organisations without permission.

Some lawyers argue the Act does not clearly cover model testing, leaving the legal basis contested. The enforcement marks the first use of the AI Office's information-gathering powers. Separately, Meta said it would rewrite a web-crawling clause in its pending commercial terms after criticism that it could let the company scrape news sites to train its products.

Source: Reuters / AiSevak, 17 October 2026

Google's Deep Review agent gave feedback on more than 16,000 conference papers

Google released Deep Review, an agentic system for validating complex technical documents. It splits manuscripts into overlapping sections, allocates extra computation to dense material, and uses parallel agents plus a synthesis agent to check logic, mathematical claims, citations and consistency. A pilot supplied optional feedback on more than 16,000 papers submitted to top computer-science conferences over the past year.

Google positions the system as validating papers separately from official peer review rather than replacing it. In a related push, Google DeepMind introduced an agentic science workbench inside Google Antigravity to help biologists prioritise antimicrobial peptide candidates, using a model called ProtNLM that predicts biological function from raw amino-acid sequences. Researchers used it to surface a novel antimicrobial peptide candidate now in wet-lab validation.

Research

Study finds agent 'harnesses' do not reliably improve reasoning accuracy

A new measurement system, AlphaDiana, tested three open-source models and three agent harnesses across five answer-based benchmarks. It found that harnesses do not consistently improve accuracy: gains on questions a model could not solve alone were outweighed by losses on questions it answered correctly without tools. Neither skill guidance nor retained history reliably improved accuracy.

The paper, from researchers including Sanmi Koyejo and Sebastian Pokutta, links answer correctness and execution status to traces and resource records so agents can be compared under shared task and resource policies. It cautions that behaviour and output-token use must be judged alongside correctness, since low token entropy can accompany wrong answers. The finding adds to a growing body of work arguing agents should be evaluated as configurable systems, not fixed models.

Source: arXiv (2610.20675), 14 October 2026

RAG

Vectara's Boomerang V2 embedding maps text and images into one retrieval space

Vectara released Boomerang V2, a multimodal embedding model built for semiconductor document retrieval, mapping text and images into a single vector space so they can be searched together. On Vectara's internal benchmark of 2,059 semiconductor document images, V2 led four models on image-only and mixed indexes. Its average DCG@10 over relevant image parts rose to 3.85 from 2.95 for V1.

Vectara tested V2 against Boomerang V1 and external embedders from OpenAI and Google. Gemini led on text-only retrieval, while V2 led when images or mixed text-and-image content were searched. The company cautioned that models which lead a general benchmark may not lead on a team's own documents, and recommended testing on domain data.

Source: Vectara, 16 October 2026

Vision

Black Forest Labs opens FLUX 3 Image Klein for consumer GPUs

Black Forest Labs released open weights for FLUX 3 Image Klein, a 5.6-billion-parameter image generation and editing model designed to run on consumer GPUs. It supports native 4K output, reference-image-driven spatial editing and bounding-box placement. Base, distilled and compressed weights (FP8, NVFP4, INT8) were published alongside integrations for ComfyUI, Hugging Face Diffusers and vLLM-Omni.

The release makes high-resolution image editing available for local fine-tuning and deployment. It adds to a busy fortnight for open media models: Kandinsky Lab published Kandinsky 6.0 Video and Kandinsky S2V 1.0, two MIT-licensed families for synchronised video-audio and audio-driven avatar generation. Kuaishou's Kling 4.0, meanwhile, launched with native 30-second single-pass video and 10-bit HDR.

Source: Black Forest Labs, 17 October 2026
