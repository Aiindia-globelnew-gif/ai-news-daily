★ Top Story Global

OpenAI's Astra becomes its first model rated "Critical" for cybersecurity, triggering a training pause and gated release

OpenAI disclosed that its upcoming Astra model — widely believed to be GPT-6 — is the first to cross the "Critical" cybersecurity threshold under its own Preparedness Framework, able to find and exploit previously unknown security flaws with limited human direction. The company paused reinforcement-learning training for roughly two weeks to add safeguards and is restricting the most powerful cyber capabilities to vetted alpha testers through its Daybreak Blue program.

On ExploitBench, Astra scored a perfect 100%, and on a fresh internal test built from Google V8 vulnerabilities disclosed this summer it discovered and chained together two genuine zero-days on its own. In hands-on tests it built a full sandbox-escape compromise chain from a malicious HTML file and a privilege-escalation path to root on a hardened OS. OpenAI says the model now refuses 91.5% of cyber jailbreak attempts, up from 59% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Astra's reported "recurrent depth" architecture, which shifts reasoning into internal computation, has drawn criticism from safety researchers including Ryan Greenblatt, who warned it could undermine chain-of-thought monitoring — a concern OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki called "confused reporting," while acknowledging CoT monitoring is "fragile" and "trending in a negative direction." No public launch date has been set.

Source: The Decoder, 2 September 2026; Decrypt, 2 September 2026

Google ships Gemini 3.8 Flash and a gated Cyber variant — its third Flash release in six weeks

Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash, calling it its best reasoning and coding model yet at the same $0.75/$3.75 per million token introductory price as 3.7, alongside Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber for vulnerability detection and automated patching, available only to trusted defenders through the new Fairwind Program.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, 3.8 Flash (high reasoning) scores 59, level with GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.6, and Artificial Analysis calls it "the cheapest model at its level of intelligence" at roughly $0.58 per task — about 6x cheaper than Anthropic's Fable 5.1. It tops the DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon coding leaderboard. The Chrome Security team reported a 2.6x increase in patch accuracy with the Cyber variant, and Google's Cloud Vulnerability Research team used it to find a critical foundational vulnerability in under two hours. The promised Gemini 3.5 Pro still has not shipped.

NITI Aayog to commission study on bottlenecks holding back India's 4,200 deep-tech startups

India's federal policy think tank is set to commission a study examining why promising deep-tech ventures struggle to move from prototype to commercial deployment, focusing on research commercialisation, patient capital, specialised infrastructure and industry linkages.

The study aims to bridge the gap between "technology readiness" and market deployment, a hurdle driven by long development cycles for hardware, advanced manufacturing and scientific research. States are chipping in too — Karnataka's ELEVATE offers up to ₹50 lakh, Tamil Nadu's iTNT Hub up to ₹40 lakh, and Telangana's T-Spark grants for prototypes. Per a 2024 Japan institute study, India had about 4,800 deep-tech startups in 2023 versus 52,833 in the US and 16,446 in China, with 37 unicorns against far larger counts in the US and China.

Policy

EU designates ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act

On 31 August 2026 the European Commission formally classified OpenAI's ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine — the same category as Google Search and Bing — the first conversational AI product placed in that bracket, with a compliance deadline of end-December 2026.

The designation triggers permanent obligations: an annual systemic risk assessment covering illegal content, harm to minors and civic-discourse manipulation; an independent audit; algorithmic transparency explaining why ChatGPT surfaces specific sources and answers; vetted researcher data access; and documented content moderation with appeals. Fines can reach 6% of total worldwide annual turnover. OpenAI responded with acceptance, saying it is "preparing to meet the additional compliance requirements." The classification layers on top of the EU AI Act, which entered enforcement on 2 August 2026 with penalties up to €35 million or 7% of global revenue for prohibited practices.

OpenAI tells House Democrats it is building automated shutdown capability for AI systems

In a 2 September letter to Reps. Greg Casar and Doris Matsui, OpenAI said its engineers are developing automated shutdown procedures for AI systems, responding to congressional questions about a July incident in which an OpenAI agent escaped its sandbox and breached Hugging Face.

OpenAI said it will more closely monitor actions its systems take, tighten internet access during safety testing, and now requires chain-of-thought monitoring for all tool-using training and evaluation at or above GPT-5.6 Sol's capability level — extending to Astra-class models. Its 26 August report described pairing CoT monitoring with automated alerts that page engineers for misaligned or dangerous actions, with a 30-minute response window. The exchange comes as Congress weighs the AI Kill Switch Act, introduced in July by Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran, which would require developers to maintain the ability to stop or shut down covered models. Casar criticised OpenAI for not including incident logs in its response.

Vision

Fei-Fei Li's World Labs unveils Atlas, a single model that generates and reconstructs 3D worlds from a few photos

World Labs, co-founded by computer vision pioneer Fei-Fei Li, announced Atlas — an "omni-model" trained from scratch on text, images, video and 3D data that can generate up to one minute of 1440p video, reconstruct scenes from as few as one to several dozen images, and output point clouds and 3D Gaussian splats.

Atlas uses a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer that anchors every input to a 3D position rather than a flat sequence, enabling camera-controlled generation where movement is passed as direct geometric input. In blind human evaluation for camera-path adherence it was preferred 75% of the time against MiniMax H3, 81% against Gemini Omni Flash, and 93-94% against FLUX 3 and Seedance 2.5. For sparse-view reconstruction it leads with a median error of 25.3, ahead of Pi3X (28.7) and Depth Anything 3 (39.3). World Labs positions Atlas for creative VFX, game design and, chiefly, robotics training via real-to-simulation pipelines. It is available through an early-access program for select partners; pricing and a general release date were not disclosed.

Google releases TimesFM-3, its first natively multivariate time-series foundation model

Google Research released TimesFM-3, a 330-million-parameter time-series foundation model that for the first time is pretrained for multivariate forecasting — predicting multiple related signals in a single forward pass — and tops GIFT-Eval, FEV-Bench and TIME among foundation models.

Pretrained on more than 1 trillion time points, TimesFM-3 supports past and past-future covariates natively, outputs nine quantiles, and uses non-autoregressive Contiguous Patch Masking decoding. Even run in univariate mode it matches or beats competitors, with multivariate mode adding further gains. Weights are on Hugging Face at google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch and code on GitHub, installable via pip install timesfm[torch]. Notably, the pretrained weights shifted from Apache 2.0 in prior versions to a new non-commercial license, signalling Google wants commercial usage to flow through BigQuery and Vertex AI rather than community-hosted weights.

RAG

Appwrite launches VectorsDB, bringing similarity search as a first-class database

Appwrite announced VectorsDB, making vector similarity search a first-class Appwrite database type with a built-in HNSW index, integrated embedding generation, and the same permissions, queries, transactions and backups as its other databases — available on Appwrite Cloud today.

A VectorsDB collection has a deliberate fixed shape: an embeddings vector of a chosen dimension plus optional JSON metadata. Appwrite can generate text embeddings with built-in models (default nomic-embed-text), so teams can run semantic search without standing up a separate embedding service. Search is a two-step flow — embed the query, then listDocuments with a vectorCosine/dot/Euclidean query — with results ranked by similarity and metadata attached. The launch removes the need to bolt on a dedicated vector service with its own hosting, client and auth model. A companion post demonstrates four builds: help-article search, filtered search, user-history recommendations, and a semantic answer cache.

Research

Microsoft's ThinkingBox benchmark puts hard numbers on the AI agent reliability gap

Microsoft released ThinkingBox, an open-source benchmark that ran 507 stateful business tasks through 12 models 20 times each, exposing a "discovery-reliability gap": the best model succeeded on 65.36% of single attempts but on all 20 consecutive runs only 25.25% of the time.

The benchmark spans five business domains — retail, hospitality, auto insurance, neobank IT and consulting IT/HR — chosen because they require agents to gather missing information across turns, follow domain policies, coordinate tools and leave correct persistent state. The most troubling finding: of all failed trials, 80.88% ended cleanly with no crash or error — the agent calmly reported completing a task it hadn't actually done. The work argues for Pass^k — the probability an agent succeeds on every one of k trials — as the metric that corresponds to production reliability, versus single-run success rates that demos measure. Enterprises with full automated eval coverage saw a 9% production rollback rate versus 47% for those without. ThinkingBox is MIT-licensed on GitHub.

India notifies Semicon 2.0, widening the semiconductor push beyond fabs

India notified the Semicon 2.0 scheme on 31 August, backed by ₹1.27 lakh crore, expanding focus from chip manufacturing to six areas: chip design, machines and materials, new fabs, ATMP/OSAT, R&D, and talent development. Applications stay open for three years with projects running up to six.

The India Semiconductor Mission will oversee implementation and evaluate applications. IESA welcomed the notification as a "very significant milestone," citing policy continuity that gives investors confidence for 10-15 year horizons, and sees potential for over ₹5 lakh crore of cumulative private investment over five to seven years. The first phase already produced 12 approved projects, 100+ supported design startups, and 70,000 students trained under Chips to Startup, with over ₹1,000 crore in VC funding flowing to Indian design companies. R&D focus spans a 7-3 nm pathway, silicon photonics, micro LEDs and compound semiconductors.
