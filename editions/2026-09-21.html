Four Subscribers Sue Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI Over 'Coordinated' AI Slowdown

A proposed class action filed Friday in the US District Court for the Northern District of California alleges the four leading AI labs violated antitrust law by collectively agreeing to slow the pace of AI development under the banner of safety. The plaintiffs — paid subscribers to ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Gemini — argue the coordination reduces the value consumers receive for their subscriptions, and are seeking treble damages, an injunction and a jury trial. No defendant has yet responded.

The complaint, Buist et al. v. Anthropic PBC et al., leans on the labs' own public statements: Dario Amodei's September 12 essay urging industry deceleration, same-day endorsements from Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis, and OpenAI policy chief Chris Lehane's later confirmation that the labs had been coordinating on safety protocols for weeks. The suit alleges the restraint operates through limits on training compute, restrictions on using AI to improve AI, and capability checkpoints. Legal experts note the plaintiffs concede that no delayed release, cancelled model or altered training run has yet manifested — and antitrust claims typically require evidence of a concrete agreement rather than parallel public positions. The filing landed just days after Senators Josh Hawley and Ted Cruz blocked a proposed national-security antitrust exemption for AI companies in the NDAA, leaving the labs without the legislative cover Amodei had asked for.

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google Are Building a FINRA-Style AI Standards Body

OpenAI confirmed it is working with Anthropic and Google on a self-regulatory standards body modelled on FINRA, the oversight organisation for US brokers. The concept, credited to Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis, would set standards, conduct oversight and enforce compliance among AI developers without waiting for government legislation.

The proposal lands at a febrile moment: September brought disclosures of AI agents escaping controlled environments, high-profile researcher resignations citing existential risk, and public deceleration calls from the CEOs of the largest labs. Critics — including Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez, who called the push "a cartel by any other name" — warn that self-regulation by companies racing to build the most capable systems is inherently compromised, and that open-weight models sit outside any such body's reach. Sceptics also note FINRA governs a mature industry with agreed metrics and real enforcement teeth; AI has neither. The body is best understood as a bridge that buys time while legislation catches up.

IndiaAI Mission to Back Eight More Homegrown Foundation-Model Builders

Under the IndiaAI Mission, the government will soon extend incentives to around eight domestic firms building foundational AI models, with BharatGen — the IIT-anchored consortium behind bilingual model Param-1 — among the expected beneficiaries. The move comes under the mission's ₹1,500 crore scheme for sovereign AI models.

Earlier rounds of the scheme selected Sarvam, Soket Labs, Gnani.ai and Gan.ai. The expansion signals Delhi's intent to seed a domestic frontier-model ecosystem rather than depend entirely on US and Chinese labs. IndiaAI's call for sovereign AI models drew 187 proposals at last count, roughly double the initial submissions, indicating deep pipeline interest across startups, academia and public institutions. The incentive structure is designed to defray the compute and data costs that have so far kept most Indian players in the application layer.

PM Modi to Launch ₹1 Lakh Crore R&D Fund for Private-Sector Deeptech

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to launch a ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) scheme on November 3, designed to build a private-sector-driven R&D ecosystem across India. The fund will offer long-term, low-interest loans or equity contributions for projects in sunrise sectors including AI and deeptech.

The scheme targets a structural weakness in Indian innovation: private R&D spending has historically lagged peer economies, and industry often treats research as a cost centre. By providing patient, low-cost capital rather than grants alone, the government hopes to coax deep-science and AI ventures past the valley of death between prototype and product. The announcement follows growing concern — voiced at forums such as the Sakshya Dialogue 2026 — that India underfunds foundational AI research even as its application ecosystem booms. Implemented well, it could anchor the capital foundation for the sovereign-model push running in parallel under IndiaAI.

India Plans ₹300 Crore Defence AI Centre of Excellence with Sarvam AI

The government is set to establish a ₹300 crore Defence Centre of Excellence in partnership with Sarvam AI to strengthen national security and self-reliance. The initiative focuses on developing indigenous generative AI models tailored for military applications, including surveillance and tactical decision-making.

The partnership pairs the state with one of India's most prominent homegrown model builders — Sarvam was among the first firms selected under the IndiaAI Mission's foundational-model incentives. Defence applications being eyed include sensor-fusion analysis, decision support for commanders, and language models hardened for classified environments. The centre also represents a deliberate step toward what Indian strategists call AI-safety sovereignty: a country that cannot build or evaluate frontier models for its own defence risks becoming dependent on — or vulnerable to — those who do.

Pocket FM Launches Sherpa, an AI Fiction-Writing Platform Built for Hundred-Episode Worlds

Bengaluru-based Pocket FM launched Sherpa, a fiction-creation platform built around what it calls Narrative Intelligence — specialised agents for ideation, planning, research, prose and evaluation, anchored by a Narrative World Model that maintains long-term story memory. Annualised content production across Pocket FM has grown 75x to more than 2.5 million hours.

Sherpa attacks the hardest problem in long-form AI storytelling: consistency across hundreds of thousands of words. Characters must evolve without losing identity, relationships must remember their history, and events from 50 episodes ago must still constrain what happens next. Rather than one model responding to prompts, the system layers agents across the story hierarchy — from characters and world to arcs and scenes — while keeping the creator in charge of vision and key decisions. Pocket FM says the approach has already driven a 10x increase in blockbuster titles across its platform of more than 250 million listeners in over 20 countries.

India's AI Framework to Get a Technical Secretariat, Not a Regulator

India's forthcoming AI governance framework is expected to establish a technical secretariat within MeitY rather than a full-fledged AI regulator. The body will set guidelines for data provenance, environmental metrics and safety, coordinate across ministries, and host an AI-incident database — but will not have regulatory enforcement powers.

The choice of a coordinating body over a statutory regulator echoes the federated, sectoral, risk-graded approach favoured at recent Indian policy dialogues, where participants warned that a single omnibus AI law would always trail the technology it governs. The secretariat model does leave open questions about liability allocation between developers, deployers, platforms and users — a gap lawyers say current Indian law addresses only after harm occurs. The government has separately stated that the India AI Safety Institute is being established for safety research, standards and testing, though it too lacks licensing or shutdown powers. Watch how the incident database is designed; it could become the foundation for future enforcement.

GPT-6 Astra Co-Solves First 'Major Progress'-Level Problem on FrontierMath

OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, working with three human researchers, has solved an open mathematics problem rated at "major progress" level on Epoch AI's FrontierMath benchmark — the first such solve in that tier. The team proved that the counterexample the problem asked for does not exist, and invented a new voting rule along the way.

The problem, open since 2017, concerned the existence of the core in approval-based committee elections. Rather than brute-force a search for a counterexample, Astra — working with Patrick Becker (TU Munich), Matthias Greger (Oxford) and Dominik Peter (Université Paris Dauphine-PSL/CNRS) — introduced a "harmonic entropy" objective function and proved that any local optimum under it falls within the core, yielding a polynomial-time algorithm for computing a fair committee. The resulting arXiv paper notes the voting rule and its proof "were found by GPT-6 Astra." Epoch AI has introduced a new "Human + AI" status label for the problem, saying humans played an active role in prompting but the core ideas came from the model. Only the three "breakthrough"-level problems now remain unsolved above it.

Step5Preview: A 600B Sparse MoE That Activates Just 27B, Open Weights Coming October 15

Step has fully opened Step5Preview, a flagship base model for real-world agentic tasks: 600B total parameters in a sparse mixture-of-experts design that activates only 27B at a time, with a native 1-million-token context and multimodal input. It scored 44 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index — among the top three open-source models globally.

The pitch is the Pareto frontier — capability, efficiency and cost at once — with claimed per-task cost around one-eighth of Claude Opus 5. In a 24-hour test on a single H100, the model optimised an MLA GPU kernel to 508 TFLOPS, beating Opus 5's 493; in another, it autonomously post-trained a Qwen3-30B base model and lifted AIME24 accuracy from 53.3% to 60%. On agentic evaluations including the CLI subset of Agents' Last Exam, FrontierFinance and DRACO, it ranks second only to GPT-6 Astra or Claude Opus 5. Full weights are scheduled for release on October 15 — the date that matters most for the open-source ecosystem.

TypeSafe AI Emerges from Stealth with Jev, a Non-Generative 'System 1' Decision Model

TypeSafe AI, co-founded by former OpenAI researcher Diogo Almeida, launched Jev alongside a $40M seed round led by DCVC. Jev discards autoregressive text generation entirely — it outputs type-safe decisions (boolean, categorical, score) in 70-500ms at roughly 400x lower cost, targeting the guardrail and routing bottleneck in agent stacks.

The thesis, named for the Jevons paradox, is that most model invocations in software workflows don't need prose — they need a verdict. Jev uses non-autoregressive single-pass inference and RLCD (reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions), so a 98% confidence score means 98% empirical accuracy, eliminating malformed-JSON failures by design. Early adopters report 5-18x speedups when replacing generative classifiers, with input priced at $42 per billion tokens and output free. The company positions Jev as the "System 1" to generative models' "System 2" — and is offering 120 million free tokens to new users to try the pattern this week.

Linkup Open-Sources SPARSEUP, a 149M-Parameter Sparse Embedding Model for RAG

Linkup Research released SPARSEUP, an Apache 2.0 sparse embedding model on a 149M-parameter ModernBERT backbone that scores 56.4 average nDCG@10 on BEIR-13 — which the team calls the strongest public vocabulary-based sparse encoder under 150M parameters. It joins LightOn's DenseOn and LateOn to give RAG builders a matched trio of dense, late-interaction and sparse retrieval styles.

Sparse models output vocabulary weights rather than dense vectors, fitting inverted indexes and matching rare terms well — a useful complement to the hybrid BM25-plus-vector architectures that have become the enterprise default in 2026. Three fixes make it work: a logit shift that stops output bags filling with stopwords, per-position top-12 expansion caps, and case folding that collapses duplicate byte-level BPE variants. With the Seismic inverted index it reaches over 97% recall in about 380 microseconds per query, single-threaded. In controlled comparisons it still trails its dense siblings (DenseOn at 57.9, LateOn at 58.9) — pick it when interpretability and rare-term matching matter more than raw semantic recall.

Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash Turns Audio-Video Into an Agentic Workload — at a Fraction of the Price

Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, a native omni-modal model built for agentic audio-visual execution: text, image, audio and video input, a 1M-token context, and tool-calling for workflows like video editing, translation and film commentary. Audio input costs drop by more than 98% versus the predecessor, with API pricing at $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.47 per million output tokens.

The roughly 125B-parameter sparse MoE (about 6B active per token) uses what Alibaba calls agentic perception — deciding which frames to examine rather than sampling uniformly, cutting tokens per query by roughly 46% while improving accuracy. On vendor-reported benchmarks it approaches Gemini 3.8 Flash on audio-visual tasks and exceeds it on audio, undercutting Google's pricing roughly 5x on input and 8x on output. Alongside the model, Alibaba open-sourced Qwen-MM-Plugins (Apache 2.0), which gives agent harnesses like Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI native multimodal reading abilities. Caveats: weights stay closed, output is text-only, and all benchmark claims are self-reported pending independent testing.

OpenAI Tests 'Sponsored Agents' — ChatGPT Ads That Open a Conversation with a Brand's AI

OpenAI is testing Sponsored Agents, a ChatGPT ad format in which clicking an ad opens a labelled conversation with an AI agent that speaks for the advertiser. The alpha is running with selected US advertisers, and OpenAI says the exchange is kept separate from ChatGPT's independent answers — the assistant's own judgment is not for sale.

The format shifts an advertiser's work from writing copy to preparing what its agent knows: product details, pricing rules and the boundaries it answers within. It arrives with an expanded ads toolkit — a HubSpot integration as OpenAI's first CRM partner, a Shopify app for US merchants expanding to other markets on September 23, and natural-language campaign management through the Ads Manager plugin. The conversational ad is now a three-way race: Google places ads inside AI Mode, and Amazon lets shoppers complete purchases inside Alexa+ conversations. OpenAI's bet is that users will trust a brand's own labelled agent more than the platform's assistant playing salesman.
