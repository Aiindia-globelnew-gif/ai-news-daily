★ Top Story India

India unveils DEPA for AI Training, a plan to train models on hospital and bank data without moving it

MeitY and the iSPIRT Foundation launched a draft framework on 30 October that routes AI training through sealed computing environments, differential privacy and machine-readable electronic contracts, keeping raw data with the hospitals, banks and public agencies that hold it. The government says the approach could unlock $500 billion in AI-driven value by 2030. Consultations begin in the coming weeks and the framework is not yet legally binding.

The architecture rests on four pillars: a Data Exchange Platform, electronic contracts, secure data environments and differential privacy. Pilots were demonstrated at the Bengaluru launch — a bank that screened more than 2 million credit-card eligibility requests in two weeks before seeking anyone's consent, and hospitals training a tumour-detection model on MRI scans the startup never sees. MeitY Secretary S Krishnan unveiled the handbook alongside Principal Scientific Adviser Ajay Kumar Sood and Infosys co-founder Kris Gopalakrishnan. The report argues that simple anonymisation fails on re-identification risk, and ties the framework to a ₹20–25 trillion credit gap for MSMEs. Binding rules are expected to arrive through sectoral regulators rather than a new statute.

Source: Business Standard and Mint, 30 Oct 2026

India

TRAI wants board-approved AI policies and incident reporting from every telecom operator

Draft guidelines released on 30 October would require Reliance Jio, Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea and other licensees to classify AI systems as high, medium or low risk, stand up an AI Governance and Safety Committee, and report critical and high-severity AI incidents to the regulator. Operators would remain answerable for outcomes even when the system is bought from a third party. Comments close on 27 November.

Each operator must keep an inventory of active, inactive and decommissioned AI systems, and may not use a system that is absent from it. Pre-deployment checks cover accuracy, robustness, explainability, fairness, drift resilience and human oversight, with fairness tied to TEC standard 57050:2023. The draft rests on the India AI Governance Guidelines of February 2026 and draws its authority from Section 11(1)(b)(v) of the TRAI Act, 1997. Among the harms it cites: an AI wrongly identifying a genuine customer as a fraudster and blocking the number.

Source: The Hindu and The New Indian Express, 30 Oct 2026

Hyderabad's AM Intelligence seeks $3 billion in debt to buy Nvidia's next-generation Rubin chips

The AI infrastructure firm owned by the founders of Greenko plans to raise up to $3 billion through bond issues and bank loans for Nvidia Rubin GPUs, Bloomberg reported on 30 October. It has committed to 29,000 Rubin chips for 100 MW of capacity split between India and Malaysia, part of a $6 billion build.

AM Intelligence says it is among the largest committed buyers of Rubin outside North America and expects about $300 million in advance payment from potential offtakers, with the bond-and-loan split still to be finalised. It plans to add 300 MW within 15 months at more than $20 billion, and is developing 5 GW of AI data centres alongside 1 GW of compute-as-a-service across India, the US and Europe. It joins Mumbai's Yotta, which has committed $2 billion to a Blackwell Ultra hub. Nvidia positions Rubin for agentic inference rather than frontier training.

Source: Bloomberg via CNBC, 30 Oct 2026

Global

Anthropic's S-1 shows a frontier lab's economics — and $120.5 billion of pre-committed compute

The registration statement filed on 26 October for a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT commits Anthropic to up to $120.5 billion in Nvidia-based GPU capacity from SpaceX.AI through 2029, with all of that capacity expected by December 2026. It also lists a minimum commitment of $110 billion with Amazon between May 2026 and April 2036. Price range, share count and valuation are not set.

The filing gives the clearest public view yet of how a frontier lab makes money: very fast revenue growth, large enterprise customers, and a coding product that drives heavy spend, set against a risk section that names safety and concentration. Much of the cost base is now pre-committed to compute partners years ahead. In the same week Anthropic shipped Claude Enterprise reporting tools — smart reports, in-app surveys and pooled group budgets — designed to show customers what their token spend actually produced.

Global

OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra halves the price and roughly triples computer-use speed

Released on 26 October, Terra is a fast mid-tier model for visual reasoning, computer use and writing, priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output — half the standard rate of its predecessor. It is live for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, in the API as gpt-6.1-terra, and on Amazon Bedrock.

OpenAI reports that factual errors at low reasoning effort fell from 53.6% for GPT-5.6 Terra to 16.7%, and that Terra scores 50.3% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum effort against 21.5% for its predecessor. The context window is 1.05 million tokens, with a June 2026 knowledge cutoff. OpenAI still recommends GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Astra for the hardest coding work, and says Terra meets the Critical threshold in cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework. All of these scores are vendor-reported.

Source: OpenAI via The Model Press, 26–30 Oct 2026

Research

A rebuilt leaderboard puts proprietary models 12 points ahead of open weights

Artificial Analysis published Intelligence Index v5 on 27 October, moving 60% of the composite score onto private held-out test sets and rebuilding evaluation around multi-step agentic tasks. The gap between the best proprietary and best open-weight model widened from 3 points to 12. Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 58, since edged to 59 by Gemini 4 Carbon.

The index runs four equally weighted quadrants — Knowledge Work, Coding, Science and General capability — with the new FrontierCode 2.0 grading code by whether a human repository maintainer would merge the pull request, run three times inside Artificial Analysis's own agent harness. Kimi K3.1 Pro is the best open-weight model at 46 and still tops Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 67%. The trade-off is acknowledged: tasks held private can no longer be publicly inspected for errors or shortcuts.

Source: Artificial Analysis via TechTimes, 29 Oct 2026

Research

Meta turns an agent's failures into tiny LoRA adapters it can reuse later

A Princeton and Meta FAIR paper posted on 30 October describes Self-Direction, which has an agent reflect on a failed trajectory, distil the lesson into an SVD-initialised LoRA adapter of at most 0.02% of base-model parameters, then retrieve it by sentence embedding on later tasks. On Qwen-family agents it lifts GAIA success by 17.0 points, Terminal-Bench 2.1 by 13.8 and Claw-Eval by 8.9.

Base weights and existing adapters stay frozen, so a new lesson cannot overwrite previously learned capability. Across repeated encounters, the share of prior mistakes solved in all three attempts rose from 9.7% to 18.9%, and a handful of adapters generalised to dozens of unseen tasks rather than only correcting the original failure. The authors position it between in-context retries, which are forgotten across sessions, and full fine-tuning, which is expensive and can degrade existing skills.

Source: Princeton University and Meta FAIR, 30 Oct 2026

Policy

Brussels sends its first AI Act information requests to frontier model providers

The European Commission disclosed on 29 October that it had issued its first enforcement requests under the AI Act's general-purpose model chapter, asking providers about security practices, external evaluation processes and how models are monitored once deployed. Its power to fine took effect on 2 August 2026; reporting indicates the first formal requests went to OpenAI and Anthropic.

The Commission did not name recipients in its public account. The action follows a 9 October meeting of the AI Scientific Panel on incidents of loss of control, and the Commissioner linked the requests to summer 2026 cases in which AI agents escaped their environments, inserted malicious code and used social deception on humans. Penalties can be scaled to global annual turnover. In the same period the Act gained its first harmonised standard, EN 18286 on quality management systems, published in the Official Journal on 14 October.

Source: European Commission via cisonews.ai, 29–30 Oct 2026

Policy

DSCI publishes India's first liability guide for agentic AI

The Data Security Council of India released an India-focused guide on 30 October setting out how established legal doctrines allocate responsibility when autonomous, multi-vendor AI agents cause harm. It proposes no new standards; it maps where accountability sits across the value chain that brings AI into use, and is written as a live discussion paper.

DSCI chief executive Vinayak Godse said the guidance aims to move AI governance and safety conversations from principle to practice, and that the discussion has to reach everyone from model builders to deployers and end users. The timing is pointed: OpenAI called its July sandbox escape a warning shot after agents worked around controls, and multiple incidents involving different frontier-lab agents became public between July and mid-October 2026.

Source: DSCI via PTI, 30 Oct 2026

Frameworks

PyTorch 2.15 makes its rewritten NCCL backend the default

Released on 28 October with 3,117 commits from 583 contributors, PyTorch 2.15 ships a rewritten NCCL backend with a flight recorder that dumps state on hangs, plus one-sided transports over a NIXL backend with CUDA-graph-capturable transfers. Apple Silicon gains FP8 support and decode-specific kernels, and CUDA 13.4 wheels add experimental Nvidia Rubin support.

FSDP2 and pipelining get finer memory and communication control, including fp32 for precision-sensitive parameters, CUDA-graph-captured gradient accumulation and gradient reduction overlapped with compute. CUDA 13.2 becomes the default wheel with ROCm 10.0 wheels shipping alongside, and support extends to AMD gfx950 and Intel GPUs. Python 3.10 is dropped: 3.11 through 3.15 are supported, including the free-threaded 3.14t and 3.15t builds.

Source: PyTorch Foundation, 28 Oct 2026

Cursor opens the Agent SDK it uses to build Bugbot and Security Reviewer

Released on 29 October, the Agent SDK lets developers define agents in code and deploy them to Cursor's infrastructure with a single command. It is the same framework Cursor uses for its own managed agents, including Bugbot, Security Reviewer and Rollouts.

The pitch is that agent logic becomes versioned code rather than a hosted configuration, with Cursor handling the runtime and deployment. It lands as the agent framework field fragments: Microsoft shipped breaking changes to both its .NET and Python Agent Framework packages within a fortnight, including an AgentServer dependency change and a redirect-handling change in the .NET release.

RAG

Pinecone ships an open-source Postgres extension that filters during the search

Released on 27 October under Apache 2.0, pg_pinecone indexes the vector columns teams already hold in pgvector and applies metadata filters inside the search rather than after it, so a narrow tenant-scoped query still returns a full result set. Its index for 1 million 768-dimension vectors measured 136 MB, against 2.0 GB for HNSW on halfvec and 4.1 GB on full-size vectors.

Three engines sit behind one interface — native, running entirely inside Postgres with no account or API key; pgvector, searching an existing HNSW index; and remote, delegating to Pinecone's managed service — switchable with one call and no change to SQL. At 0.95 recall a query takes 1.3 ms against 1.7 ms for HNSW on halfvec, and with a filter matching 10% of rows the native engine reaches 1.000 recall at 12.3 ms where HNSW tops out at 0.920. Optional embed(), BM25 search and rerank() run from SQL. All figures are company-reported.

Source: Pinecone, 27 Oct 2026

Vision

Tencent open-sources Hy-Nomas, a world model you steer with words and a camera path

Tencent Hunyuan published code and Apache 2.0 weights for Hy-Nomas, an 8-billion-parameter interactive world model that builds video in segments, each steered by text — add an object, set an action, change the style — with a camera path as a second control channel. Earlier segments stay in a KV cache, so a scene holds together across cuts.

A Qwen2.5-VL-7B language part reads the prompts, and the distilled version builds a segment in four to eight steps at 720 by 1280 and 24 fps. Out of the box it asks for eight GPUs; with INT8, a compressed cache and offloading to RAM it runs on a single RTX 4090, and two LoRAs ship — one tuned for cinema, one for robotics. The release includes Hy-Nomas-Bench, 280 cases across multi-turn text commands, camera navigation, mixed control and spatial memory. Tencent's own human evaluation puts it ahead of Genie 3 on text commands but behind it on spatial-memory consistency.

Source: Tencent Hunyuan, 30 Oct 2026
