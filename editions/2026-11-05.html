★ Top Story India

IndiaAI halves the entry bar to 512 compute units for its 10,000-GPU round

C-DAC has cut the minimum bid size for the IndiaAI Mission's second compute tender to 512 AI compute units at a single Indian data centre, down from 1,000 in the first round. The request for empanelment, issued on 7 October, is meant to pull up to 10,000 GPUs, TPUs and other accelerators into the mission's shared pool. Two corrigenda have moved bid closing to 13 November and technical bid opening to 14 November.

Empanelled providers will supply compute for five years on ring-fenced, sovereign terms, with the state effectively buying capacity rather than renting it round by round. The lower threshold is a response to rising chip prices as much as to sluggish supply: smaller domestic operators can now qualify, which the government hopes will widen the field beyond the large data-centre players that won the first tender. Communications Today's reading is that this round signals capacity that could arrive in 2027 rather than a near-term source of supply.

Source: Communications Today, 5 November 2026

India

CERT-In publishes a 107-page AI security auditing framework with four tiers of criticality

India's national cyber security agency has laid out security, transparency and accountability requirements for AI systems, setting minimum standards for companies building and deploying models. The guidelines mandate six-hour breach reporting and 180 days of retained data logs. They remain non-binding for now.

Titled "Artificial Intelligence Security Auditing Guidelines: Framework for Secure, Ethical and Auditable Artificial Intelligence Systems", the document applies to AI developers and service providers and proposes a classification system placing models and applications into low, moderate, high and critical tiers of criticality. Obligations scale with the tier, and auditable records are central to the design. Mint notes that the framework is voluntary in effect for the moment, which makes it a baseline that could harden into regulation.

Source: Mint, 4 November 2026

India

MeitY releases two indigenous foundation model families: EKA-1 and Dhīr

The IndiaAI Mission released EKA-1 from Soket Labs, a 26.3-billion-parameter model trained on roughly 7.5 trillion tokens across all 22 scheduled Indian languages plus English, and Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B from ZenteiQ, open-source multilingual assistants covering English and 10 Indian languages. Both were unveiled at a press event in New Delhi on 3 November.

EKA-1 is positioned for mathematics, code, reasoning and agentic workloads; the Dhīr models are built for secure, on-premise and offline-capable deployment in critical sectors, including air-gapped environments. The releases sit under the IndiaAI Innovation Centre pillar, the mission's bet that sovereign models trained on Indian language data are a strategic asset rather than a vanity project.

Source: Press Information Bureau, 3 November 2026; Hindustan Times, 3 November 2026

Advertising veteran Sanjeev Jasani and technology entrepreneur Shubham Saurabh have launched TurboAgents, an AI marketing platform bundling 39 specialised agents across seven marketing functions. Global

Gemini 4 Carbon tops Artificial Analysis' intelligence index, one point ahead of Claude Opus 5.5

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon is now the highest-ranked model on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index V5, edging Claude Opus 5.5 by a single point. It takes first place on four of the nine evaluations and does so at a lower cost per task than other high-ranking models. Google is giving cyber defenders first access through its Fairwind program before wider availability.

Google bills Carbon as its strongest model yet, with top cyber security scores alongside the claim that it is the least likely frontier model to hallucinate. Tom's Hardware cautions that the newest benchmark suites are designed to expose weaknesses rather than flatter leaders, so a one-point lead is a snapshot, not a settled hierarchy. The staged rollout to security teams follows Google's pattern for high-capability releases.

Global

OpenAI to acquire Pierre Computer Company to expand Codex

OpenAI will acquire Pierre Computer Company, the startup behind Code Storage and the Trees and Diffs libraries that already power the Codex app's file browsing and code review. Terms were not disclosed, and the deal still needs regulatory approval. Pierre's open-source projects will stay open source.

The strategic point is Git. As agents write code in parallel, the tooling around source control has to keep up, and OpenAI already runs Pando, its in-house backend built for both engineers and agents. Bringing Pierre in lets it connect the code a company ships with the decisions its people make, rather than treating review as an afterthought. Observers read the move as a shot at GitHub for the agent era.

Source: OpenAI, 4 November 2026; SiliconANGLE, 4 November 2026

China's state planner moves on life-cycle AI rules spanning data to computing power

China's National Development and Reform Commission said it is accelerating legislation to govern AI across its entire life cycle, from data and computing power through to deployment. The standards would be among the most comprehensive guardrails any government has proposed for keeping advanced models under control.

Wang Ruomeng, a senior official at the state economic planner, said the rules would cover how AI is developed and applied, reaching developers from DeepSeek to Moonshot. Bloomberg Law reports the push follows a series of global security lapses that sharpened Beijing's focus on loss-of-control risk. The scope is notable for extending past model behaviour into the infrastructure layer.

Research

Ai2's StateBench tests whether models can track state changes across long documents

Ai2 has released StateBench, a benchmark for long-context state tracking: how well a language model follows information about entities as it evolves through a document. Tasks run from tracking variable reassignment in code to following who is holding an object as a plot unfolds. Transformers have documented theoretical limits on the harder variants.

The benchmark separates easy cases, where a model only needs the most recent update, from hard chains where one missed step corrupts the final answer — for example, an object moving into a backpack before changing hands. Ai2's framing is that state tracking underlies much of what people assume long context windows already solved, and that the failure mode is silent.

Source: Allen Institute for AI, 3 November 2026

Research

OpenAI pipeline finds and patches 948 Linux kernel privilege-escalation bugs

OpenAI built an autonomous pipeline that scanned 2,308 candidate findings in the Linux kernel, reproduced 1,724 as real vulnerabilities and generated 948 distinct working local privilege-escalation exploits, each with a candidate patch. The mid-October run cost $1,213,430 in API credits, about $1,279 per exploit. Human researchers reviewed every result before reporting it to maintainers.

The exploits worked against a stock desktop distribution with standard protections enabled, and OpenAI says the agents used at least 10 previously unknown Linux exploitation techniques. It values the batch at $18.9M to $66.3M against historical kernel bug bounties, 16 to 55 times the run cost. The sobering detail is speed: within 10 days, 61 of the flaws had been patched upstream and 43 had staging patches. The report also admits an early run counted 20 "successes" that had actually reached root through a different bug, a reminder that autonomous security work needs its path verified, not just its outcome.

Source: OpenAI, 4 November 2026

Frameworks

TorchTPU goes open source, bringing native PyTorch to Google Cloud TPUs

Google and Meta's TorchTPU is now open source on GitHub with a pip install torch_tpu path, giving PyTorch eager execution on Google Cloud TPUs without the graph-capture patterns teams tolerated under PyTorch/XLA. It ships with torch.compile support and works with vLLM, Hugging Face and TorchTitan workloads.

The milestone was announced at PyTorch Conference 2026 and carried through November commits. The practical effect is that teams can move PyTorch code onto TPUs with far fewer rewrites, lowering the cost of trying a second accelerator family. As one analysis notes, the framework removes friction but the chip choice still comes down to workload.

Source: JSipe / PyTorch.org, 3 November 2026

Frameworks

OpenAI's Agents SDK ships a native-runtime preview with no Rust toolchain needed

OpenAI released Agents SDK for Python 0.24.0-alpha.6, with wheels that bundle the native runtime so teams no longer need a Rust toolchain or a source build. The preview supports CPython 3.10 to 3.14 on Linux x86_64, macOS 11 or newer on Apple Silicon, and Windows x64.

The build is distributed through GitHub Releases rather than PyPI, and no wheels are provided for Intel Macs, ARM Linux, ARM Windows or Alpine. Teams pinning to the stable line should treat this as an evaluation build; the significance is that installation friction, long a complaint about the SDK, is being removed.

Source: Patchwire, 3 November 2026

RAG

Cohere's Embed 5 and Rerank 5 retrieval models land on OpenRouter

Cohere's Embed 5 and Rerank 5 are now available through OpenRouter, usable with an existing API key and credits and no additional SDK. Embed 5 Pro and Fast share one embedding space, so teams can index with embed-5-pro for quality and query with embed-5-fast for latency.

Cohere's pitch is that a working RAG pipeline now takes a few dozen lines of Python against a single API, with embedding search as the fast first stage and a reranker reading query and candidate together for precision. The shared embedding space is the substantive detail: it removes the usual rebuild-the-index trade-off when moving between quality and speed tiers.

Source: Cohere, 4 November 2026

Vision

OpenAI's Images 2.6 adds GIF creation and tighter editing control

OpenAI released Images 2.6 across ChatGPT, Codex and the API, adding faster generation, GIF creation, more precise editing rounds and new API controls. Characters, products and brand details hold more consistently as an image is revised.

OpenAI says people now create about 4 billion images a week across its surfaces, more than half a billion a day. The GIF support targets marketers and product teams turning stills into loops — a job that previously meant a separate video tool — while the editing consistency is aimed at brand work where a redrawn mascot has to survive five rounds of feedback.
