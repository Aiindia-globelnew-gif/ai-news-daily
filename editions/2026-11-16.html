Anthropic’s IPO prices Wednesday: a $1 trillion test of the AI trade

Anthropic is expected to price its initial public offering on 18 November and begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on 19 November, in a deal that could value the Claude maker at about $1.02 trillion at the top of its range. The company set a $70–$78 range for 825 million Class A shares in an amended filing on 10 November. Seven chip and cloud partners — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC — are together putting in $26 billion at the IPO price.

At the top of the range the offering would raise about $64.35 billion, made up of 801.4 million new shares and 23.6 million sold by existing holders; underwriters led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley can take another 123.75 million. CNBC put the fully diluted valuation as high as $1.405 trillion, and early backer Amazon’s stake could be worth hundreds of billions of dollars. Reports that the book is several times oversubscribed rest on unnamed sources and have not been confirmed by the company; the final prospectus will only be filed after pricing.

Global

A datacentre developer wants Oracle and Meta to backstop grid costs before it signs AI leases

Applied Digital has asked major tenants including Oracle and Meta — and, case by case, chipmakers Nvidia and AMD — for billions of dollars in financial guarantees covering power and utility-connection costs before it will finalise new AI datacentre leases, Bloomberg reported on 14 November. The developer says it cannot carry utility grid liabilities on its own balance sheet.

Connecting a large AI datacentre can require the developer to promise to pay for grid upgrades and to keep paying for the connection even if the tenant leaves — a liability that can run into the billions. Applied Digital is also capping leasing capacity for lower-rated neocloud tenants such as CoreWeave, targeting a tenant mix of 70% investment-grade and 30% non-investment-grade. Oracle already carries a $7 billion letter-of-credit obligation in Wisconsin after a utility raised its credit threshold; Oracle declined to comment, and Meta, Nvidia and AMD gave no comment.

Source: Bloomberg, 14 November 2026

Research

GPT-6 Astra helped build a candidate lattice definition of the Standard Model

OpenAI launched a science blog on 15 November with a guest post from physicist Nathaniel Craig describing how GPT-6 Astra helped turn his strategy for defining the Standard Model of particle physics on a lattice into a construction and a detailed proof. A nonperturbative definition of the full theory has been open for more than 50 years, largely because the weak force is chiral and resists lattice formulation.

Craig, who leads the particle theory group at UC Santa Barbara and is a visiting scientist at OpenAI, says Astra suggested how to carry force fields onto successively finer grids and took the lead on the detailed proof; lattice field theorists Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir reviewed the work and asked questions. The preprint is not peer reviewed, and Craig is explicit that the result is “far more an answer in principle than an answer in practice” — reflection positivity at finite lattice spacing and the continuum limit are both still open.

Source: OpenAI, 15 November 2026

Research

NeurIPS paper: a new capability can arrive by deleting under 0.05% of a model’s weights

Bit-Mask Tuning, a NeurIPS 2026 paper from Jack Lu and colleagues at the University of Toronto, learns a binary mask over the gate projections of transformer blocks instead of updating weight values. It matched full-parameter fine-tuning on GRPO, supervised fine-tuning and on-policy distillation, while changing less than 0.05% of the model’s weights — and only whether they participate at all.

The trained mask is thousands of times smaller than a full fine-tuning checkpoint, and the gap to full fine-tuning closes monotonically as models scale from 0.5B to 8B parameters. BMT also forgets less: under GRPO training, prior-task accuracy with full fine-tuning, LoRA and random sparse fine-tuning all drifted by up to 5.4 points, while BMT stayed near the base model — a practical advantage for continual and multi-task settings.

Source: AI Beat, 15 November 2026

Sarvam confirms a $300 million round backed by Nvidia and the government

Sarvam co-founder Vivek Raghavan told the Financial Times that the Bengaluru startup has raised $300 million in a round backed by Nvidia and the Indian government, and is developing a new version of its Indian-language model. The company argues that low costs, a large home market and Indian languages give India a route to “sovereign AI” independent of US and Chinese stacks.

Sarvam announced a $234 million first close of the round in June at a $1.5 billion valuation, led by HCLTech with $150 million; Nvidia joined a $74 million tranche in August. Government support came through the IndiaAI Mission as subsidised compute and compulsorily convertible debentures, which could leave the Centre with a 1–2% stake. Raghavan acknowledged that Sarvam’s servers still depend on US semiconductors, and the FT report carries no independent benchmark of its models against rivals.

SEBI to issue AI and ML guidelines for capital markets, with human oversight made explicit

SEBI chairperson Tuhin Kanta Pandey said on 14 November that the regulator will soon issue detailed guidelines for the responsible use of AI in securities markets, built on disclosures, accountability of regulated entities and a “human-in-the-loop” requirement for critical decisions. The framework follows a June 2025 consultation paper on guiding principles for AI and ML in the markets.

Speaking at the India International Trade Fair in Delhi, Pandey said SEBI is reviewing its regulatory framework to ensure that AI use by market participants does not compromise investor protection, market safety or market integrity. The regulator has built supervisory tooling of its own, including a “Sudarshan” tool demonstrated at international regulatory forums, and introduced a structured cyber-incident response mechanism in May aligned with the Financial Stability Board’s FIRE reporting template.

Source: The Hindu, 15 November 2026; Business Standard, 15 November 2026

India

Centre tells states the AI bottleneck is content, not compute

In a note sent ahead of the sixth National Conference of Chief Secretaries, the Centre flagged AI and digital services, geospatial technology and microbiome technology as “under-governed public assets” where institutional frameworks exist but data stays fragmented and adoption on the ground is patchy. Despite 38,000 GPUs empanelled under the India AI Mission, it concluded that “what is missing is not compute but content”.

The note, reported by The Economic Times, says state datasets are scheme-specific, vendor-hosted, inconsistently coded and without metadata, with scanned PDFs still passing for digitisation and no master registries for beneficiaries or land parcels. It flags widespread “DPDP uncertainty” — most state departments lack data protection officers, consent architecture and anonymisation standards, so the default response is to share nothing — and warns that district and block staff are the least trained cadre, with no risk assessments or audit trails before AI touches entitlements.

Source: The Economic Times, 15 November 2026

Policy

EU publishes Article 50 transparency guidance as the 2 December marking deadline nears

The European Commission’s guidelines on Article 50 of the EU AI Act appeared in the Official Journal as Communication C/2026/6005 on 12 November, setting out how transparency duties apply to AI systems and the content they produce. The guidance is non-binding, but the underlying obligations have applied since 2 August 2026.

Article 50 requires disclosure when people interact with AI, when emotion recognition or biometric categorisation is used on them, and when an AI system has generated or manipulated certain content. Deepfakes and unreviewed text on matters of public interest need a clear visible label plus machine-readable marks. A further deadline on 2 December 2026 under the Digital Omnibus requires generative systems already on the EU market to comply with Article 50(2) marking, and adds bans on non-consensual intimate material and child abuse material. Penalties for covered operator duties reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover.

Source: Official Journal of the EU, 12 November 2026; JournalArta, 16 November 2026

Frameworks

Google open-sources TPU Sync, cutting KV cache transfer latency by over 90%

Google Cloud released TPU Sync, an Apache-2.0 C++ library with Python wheels for PyTorch and JAX that treats TPU HBM, host DRAM, shared storage and the data centre network as one memory hierarchy. In internal tests on 235B to 480B parameter mixture-of-experts models, KV transfer overhead fell by more than 90%.

Median KV transfer latency for Qwen3 235B dropped from several seconds to about 230 ms, and average time to first token fell 60% for the 235B model and 90% for the 480B model at low concurrency; prefix caching at 64K context saw up to 90% lower TTFT. It is the TPU KV connector behind vLLM’s KVConnector interface and the P/D transfer backend in SGLang-TorchTPU, and Google says Meta and Wayfair are evaluating the architecture. The README states it is not yet recommended for general use, and every published figure is Google’s own.

Source: MarkTechPost, 13 November 2026

RAG

IIT Patna researchers propose embedding-free agentic search for “RAG-resistant” documents

Sagar Tamang, Ayush Vyas and Tabarakul Hazarika at IIT Patna, with independent tech lab TwoSpoon, built READ — Reliable Embedding-free Agentic Document-search — which replaces vector similarity with an agentic harness and specialised search tools. It targets documents where meaning is spread across a page: financial tables whose numbers depend on units many lines away, or legal briefs that define terms paragraphs earlier.

The authors evaluated READ against RAG-style retrieval, and against RAG inside an agentic loop, on 51 questions about a 780-page financial report published by the government of Gujarat. DeepLearning.AI’s The Batch reported the work, framing the approach as a response to “RAG-resistant” documents rather than a general replacement for vector search.

Source: DeepLearning.AI, The Batch, 13 November 2026

RAG

Pooling-aware training keeps multi-vector retrieval quality at a fraction of the vectors

Hugging Face researchers trained a multi-vector embedding model with the compression step built into training rather than applied afterwards. On NanoBEIR@13, a pooling-aware model compressed documents from an average of 193 vectors to 48.68 and held 97.8% of its uncompressed score, against 94.6% for a conventionally trained baseline at identical storage.

At an aggressive cap of four tokens per document, the pooling-aware model scored 43.00 nDCG@10 against 37.22 for the baseline; with calibrated adaptive pooling it retained 97.3% of its unpooled score while cutting stored vectors by 84.7%. The experiment used 120,000 MS MARCO triplets and a ModernBERT-base initialisation, so the gains describe one carefully specified setup rather than a guarantee for every corpus or index.

Source: Hugging Face, 14 November 2026

BytePlus, ByteDance’s cloud unit, released Dreamina Seedance 2.5 Flash, which it says gives results close to flagship Seedance 2.5 at nearly twice the generation speed, priced at $0.152 per second of 720p text-to-video and image-to-video — about 34% below the flagship.
