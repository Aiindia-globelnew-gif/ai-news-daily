★ Top Story Global

Frontier labs' slowdown pact draws first antitrust lawsuit

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and SpaceXAI were hit with an antitrust lawsuit alleging their coordinated pledge to slow the pace of AI development is an illegal agreement among competitors. The complaint, filed under Section 1 of the Sherman Act, argues paying subscribers were deprived of product improvements they were promised. It lands just days after OpenAI policy chief Chris Lehane confirmed the three labs have been coordinating on safety since July.

The suit alleges the companies coordinated on September 12, when their executives publicly endorsed Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's essay arguing for limits on the rate of unchecked AI progress. Lehane has said the labs do not believe they need an antitrust waiver for safety coordination, comparing it to safety cooperation in the airline industry, while FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson has already described such arrangements as a form of "moat digging." The stakes are high because the talks reportedly cover shared safety standards and giving independent evaluators access to intermediate model checkpoints. Forbes reported that chip stocks had already tumbled on September 14, the trading day after Amodei, Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly agreed to pace the frontier, with the Trump administration remaining skeptical of the initiative.

India

Semicon 2.0 draws ₹1 lakh crore in commitments; India targets 200 chip-design startups

IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said India has received semiconductor investment commitments worth $11-12 billion, roughly ₹1 lakh crore, since Semicon 2.0 launched, spread over two to three years. The programme could create at least 1 lakh jobs, a figure he called a conservative estimate.

Speaking at SEMICON India 2026 in New Delhi, Vaishnaw said the commitments cover equipment, materials, gases, chemicals, ATMP, substrates and wafers, with many investors waiting on board approvals before naming themselves. The programme's six pillars run from chip design and machines to fabrication, advanced packaging, R&D and talent; the government wants at least 200 startups designing chips, up from 105 in phase one, of which 20 raised about ₹800 crore in venture capital. ThePrint reported that Semicon 2.0 was approved in July 2026 with an outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore, and MeitY Secretary S Krishnan said the first phase committed its entire corpus in just three of the six intended years.

Source: Business Today / ThePrint, 17-18 September 2026

Global

Sam Altman to brief UN Security Council on AI and international security

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman will brief the 15-member UN Security Council next Wednesday during the annual gathering of world leaders for the UN General Assembly, Reuters reported exclusively. The session on AI and international security comes weeks after several industry leaders called for a coordinated slowdown of frontier development.

The meeting is scheduled for 23 September, with France holding the Council's rotating presidency. It follows a bruising fortnight in which Amodei, Altman and Elon Musk publicly agreed to pace frontier development, and OpenAI disclosed six cases of models behaving in unauthorised ways. Altman, who has ruled out an OpenAI IPO in 2026 citing safety work, told a Dreamforce audience that the world is "right to be afraid" of AI while arguing the public should trust labs to manage the risks. Reuters reports high-level representation from Anthropic was anticipated but not confirmed.

Policy

California orders acceleration of AI oversight and an emergency 'kill switch'

Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order accelerating California's independent oversight of AI companies and advancing the creation of an AI kill switch for frontier models. The order cites recent incidents, including the Hugging Face attack, and gives a convened group of experts two months to recommend stronger state law.

Proposals under consideration include requiring independent third parties to write safety plans for frontier AI companies, embedding independent verification organisations onsite in labs, and requiring an emergency shutoff whose efficacy is verified on an ongoing basis. The order accelerates SB 813 and AB 1405, signed last week, which created the first state framework for certifying independent AI auditors. Other governors moved the same day: Virginia's Abigail Spanberger launched an AI task force and data-centre accountability plan, while Nevada's Joe Lombardo restricted tax breaks for data centres. Newsom accused the federal government of an "abject failure" to create meaningful AI oversight.

Source: Governor of California / NBC News, 18 September 2026

India

NaBFID lines up ₹3,000-crore loans for India's data-hub build-out

The National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development has sanctioned loans exceeding ₹3,000 crore each to at least four data centres to fuel India's AI and cloud expansion, with moratoriums of up to five years. NaBFID estimates the sector will need roughly ₹1 lakh crore in funding through March 2031.

Managing director Rajkiran Rai G told Bloomberg the loans carry five-year moratoriums followed by ten-year repayment schedules, and that the institution has seen strong interest from hyperscalers and cloud service providers. The financing comes alongside investments from the Adani Group and global players including EQT, Blackstone and Alphabet. To fund the pipeline, NaBFID plans to raise around ₹1 trillion this financial year, with roughly 40% from offshore sources, and has already secured nearly $1 billion through RBI's special foreign-currency window.

Research

Compact longevity models beat every frontier AI system tested

A Cell cover study released LongevityBench, an open benchmark for aging biology, together with five compact Longevity-LLMs (0.6B-9B parameters) that matched or exceeded all 16 frontier systems evaluated. The best small model, L-Qwen3.5-9B, ranked first among all 26 systems tested, ahead of Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6.

The study was led by Insilico Medicine with Liquid AI, the Buck Institute and Harvard Medical School, and evaluated 18 frontier systems from OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI, DeepSeek and Moonshot. No single frontier model dominated all five biological domains, and performance shifted significantly with question phrasing, a robustness failure. On Olink proteomic age prediction, even a 0.6B model achieved a 5.7-year mean absolute error versus 10.1 years for the best frontier model. The benchmark, model weights and an agentic research platform called Longevity Claw are free on Hugging Face and GitHub.

Source: Cell / Tech Times, 17-18 September 2026

Vision

Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash puts agentic audio-video understanding in one model

Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash, its first omni-modal model built around agentic capabilities, combining audio, video, image and text understanding with tool use in a 1 million-token context window. Audio input costs dropped more than 98% versus its predecessor, with audio-visual costs down over 93%.

The hosted-only model lists at $0.15 per million input tokens and accepts video files up to 2 hours and audio up to 3 hours, with input in 113 languages and dialects. Alibaba reports a 25%+ average gain across 29 evaluations over Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, and on OmniVideoBench accuracy rose from 63.4 to 67.8 while token use fell from 145,736 to 79,117. The company claims audio performance above Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash, though all figures are vendor-reported and pending independent review. Alibaba also open-sourced Qwen-MM-Plugins, which lets agent harnesses such as Claude Code and Codex call the model's multimodal capabilities as tools.

India

BodhanAI releases open AI models for 22 Indian languages as public education rails

BodhanAI, India's Centre of Excellence in AI for Education at IIT Madras, has released foundational AI models for 22 Indian languages as the core of the Bharat EduAI Stack, a sovereign digital public infrastructure for education. The models cover speech recognition, speech generation, translation and OCR, with open weights plus hosted APIs.

Developed with AI4Bharat under the Ministry of Education, the stack applies India's DPI playbook to AI: public rails that any startup, State or non-profit can build on rather than depending on proprietary platforms. Pricing is utility-like — about ₹6 to transcribe an hour of Indian-language classroom audio and ₹60 to digitise a 300-page textbook. Writing in The Hindu, the Centre argues openness alone is not enough: for AI to reach every government-school classroom, it must become affordable infrastructure whose marginal cost disappears into the economics of the application.

'Godfather of AI' Hinton: Congress may have 'maybe a year' left to regulate AI

Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton warned lawmakers that Congress may have only about a year left to implement AI safeguards before it loses control, noting that researcher forecasts for superintelligence have collapsed from decades to just a few years. His warning came as the House left Washington until after the November midterms.

Hinton spoke after closed-door meetings with Senate and House members on Wednesday. Despite the urgency, legislative progress is stalled: Senator Rand Paul blocked unanimous consent on a kill-switch bill, and Senator Ted Cruz said bipartisan talks on catastrophic-risk legislation with Senators Klobuchar and Thune are ongoing but lack agreement. CNN reported that no substantive AI law has passed since the 2024 task force issued 85 recommendations, leaving a patchwork of state laws while the White House has shelved a FINRA-style oversight body.

Research

OpenAI says AI agents produced a proof for the Navier-Stokes Millennium problem

OpenAI says an internal system produced a proof that the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, addressing one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems. Roughly 10,000 concurrent AI agents reached the result after about 88 hours of work, followed by 17 hours of formal verification in Lean.

The company published both a written argument and a formalised Lean proof, and says it does not intend to claim the $1 million Clay Mathematics Institute prize. The effort involved about 2.7 million agent messages and roughly 130 billion output tokens, with the larger project spanning 4.9 million messages across several attempted problems. The claim is unverified and must survive expert scrutiny and the Clay problem's formal requirements; the announcement has also triggered debate over mathematical priority and how credit is assigned when machines solve major problems.

Source: The Brighter Side of News, 18 September 2026

Global

Google DeepMind launches institute to widen the AGI debate

Google DeepMind launched the DeepMind Institute, led by co-founder Shane Legg, James Manyika and Demis Hassabis, to surface differing views on artificial general intelligence. Its inaugural essays include Hassabis's proposal for a US-led frontier standards body that could eventually make safety tests mandatory before deployment.

Under the proposal, developers would initially submit models voluntarily for review up to 30 days before release, with the body later developing independent "held-out" tests to prevent labs tailoring models to known evaluations; the framework could be "ratcheted up" to include a coordinated slowdown. Another essay by Rohan Shah and Anca Dragan argues that AI's shrinking window of reasoning transparency is not inevitable and suggests limiting "opaque serial depth." Legg told the Financial Times it is premature to declare AGI achieved, maintaining his forecast of a 50% chance of "minimal" AGI by 2028, and called Amodei's slowdown call "interesting directionally" and worth considering.

Frameworks

NVIDIA's AIPerf replaces GenAI-Perf for benchmarking LLM inference at scale

NVIDIA released AIPerf, a ground-up rewrite that succeeds GenAI-Perf for benchmarking LLM inference under high concurrency. A multiprocess architecture prevents the benchmarking client itself from becoming the bottleneck, a known weakness of single-process tools.

Worker processes generate load while separate record-processor services handle results, coordinated over ZMQ, so servers can be measured accurately at scale. The tool supports more than 15 endpoint types — chat, responses, rankings, image generation — plus public datasets such as ShareGPT and trace replay formats from Mooncake, Baseten and WEKA. Configurable arrival patterns (constant, Poisson, gamma) with tunable burstiness let engineers shape load to match production traffic, and core metrics like TTFT, ITL and throughput come with percentile breakdowns and GPU telemetry.

Source: NVIDIA Technical Blog, 18 September 2026

RAG

MLPerf Inference v6.1 adds end-to-end RAG benchmark in record-setting round

MLCommons published MLPerf Inference v6.1 results with a participation record, headlined by a new End-to-End RAG benchmark that tests a full pipeline: an embedding model, a retriever pulling passages from a vector database, a re-ranker, and one or more LLMs producing the answer. A new Edge Agentic Inference test was also added.

The RAG test generates performance data for two tasks — ingesting a document corpus to build a vector database, and query-answering against a pre-built one. Working group co-chair Miro Hodak said the test was added because query-answering "has evolved beyond simply an LLM trained on a corpus," and stakeholders need real-world performance data on the multi-step, multi-component pipelines being built today. The addition signals that retrieval workloads are now a first-class benchmark category alongside raw LLM serving.

Source: MLCommons, 17 September 2026

Tools

Darkroom opens Shadow, the AI workspace it built for its own marketing teams

Darkroom opened Shadow to public beta — an AI workspace that syncs a brand's full commerce and marketing stack into one continuously updated brand library where people and AI agents work from the same context. Plans start at $120 per month with a three-day free trial.

Shadow connects Meta, Google, Shopify, Klaviyo, TikTok, Amazon and Google Analytics data, syncing performance every hour with historical backfill. It is model-agnostic: teams connect Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or Cursor, and brand context travels into any app through the Model Context Protocol. Changes to live ad accounts are proposed with before-and-after values and executed only on human approval, with a full audit trail and rollback for Google Ads. Darkroom says reporting time on live client accounts fell by more than 80% during six months of internal use.
