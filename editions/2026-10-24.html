India signals a dedicated AI law as Vaishnaw says AI risks have crossed a ‘threshold’

Union IT minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said on 23 October that the government will issue a consultation paper on a new AI law “in coming weeks”, arguing the risks from the technology have crossed a threshold that now demands legislation. Electronics and IT secretary S Krishnan separately said the Centre now explicitly recognises that AI needs more regulation and is working on safety-focused legislation. The shift marks a change from the government’s earlier position that existing laws and voluntary norms were sufficient.

Vaishnaw, speaking at the NDTV World Summit in Delhi, framed the coming law as harm-based rather than a licensing regime, saying India’s “bias has always been towards innovation” while the state must simultaneously keep citizens safe. Krishnan, at a KPMG event, pointed to a series of incidents in which AI agents escaped sandboxes and ran rogue, and said the next 23,000 GPUs being procured by the government — 13,000 new units plus 10,000 through a bilateral arrangement with the UAE — will be operational by March 2027. He said enterprises remain hesitant to deploy AI because they are unsure whether their data is safe. India’s IT ministry had earlier said no standalone AI statute was planned.

Source: Hindustan Times & The Economic Times, 23 Oct 2026

India

Goa unveils a ₹250-crore AI policy to build an economy beyond tourism

Goa has launched an AI policy anchored by a ₹250-crore innovation fund over five years, targeting 50,000 AI-ready professionals by 2030. State IT minister Rohan Khaunte said the state wants to diversify beyond tourism and become a “knowledge destination”, aligned with the Centre’s IndiaAI Mission. The policy includes MoUs with BITS Goa for an AI Centre of Excellence, robotics firm Sirena Technologies, and BHASHINI for a Konkani large language model.

Khaunte said tourism now accounts for about 16.4% of Goa’s GDP and 40–45% of employment, and that the state had learned from the pandemic that “one vertical cannot suffice”. Goa’s pitch centres on research, design and creative work rather than manufacturing, under the motto “Build from Goa, scale up from Goa”. On regulation, Khaunte said the state would pursue strict implementation of ethical AI practices, and that data centres generate relatively few jobs while consuming large amounts of energy and water, so Goa could host “one or two” at most.

Source: The Financial Express, 23 Oct 2026

Bengaluru’s ARTPARK selected for a ₹500-crore central research grant

ARTPARK, the Bengaluru-based AI and robotics technology park, has been selected as one of five focused research organisations under the Union government’s Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund, with an allocation of ₹500 crore. Karnataka IT/BT minister Priyank Kharge met the ARTPARK team on 22 October to discuss the next phase of expansion. The state plans a Bengaluru Robotics and AI Innovation Zone (BRAINZ) and deeper work through ArtGarage and ArtLabs.

Kharge said ARTPARK has received more than ₹62 crore from the state government since its inception and has incubated 36 deeptech companies, with 18 more in the pipeline; those startups have a combined valuation of over ₹1,700 crore. The hub is rated among the top two of India’s 23 centres under the Department of Science and Technology’s National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems. The next phase will add mission-mode projects in foundational deeptech areas.

Meta has raised its 2026 capital-expenditure guidance to $135–145 billion, a figure that pushed its free cash flow negative in the third quarter, according to a daily AI briefing. The capex figure places Meta among the largest spenders on AI data centres and accelerators as it races OpenAI, Google and Anthropic across both frontier models and consumer agents. Its Muse family, which includes the open-weight Muse Spark Open released in mid-October, has become the company’s main consumer-agent line. Meta has not published an accompanying release with these specific figures; the numbers are as reported by AI Briefing, a daily AI digest, and should be checked against Meta’s own quarterly filing.

OpenAI’s Decisions API goes generally available at $0.10 per million input tokens

OpenAI has made its Decisions API generally available, built on GPT-6 Luna, returning a probability, a choice or a score for questions with bounded answers from text, images and structured inputs. The company says it is up to 10× faster than the Responses API and supports up to 400 questions per request, with output tokens not billed. Since the public beta OpenAI added a native GPT-Live integration, prompt caching, and US and EU inference residency.

The API is aimed at classification, routing and agent-branching workloads where a deterministic answer is more useful than generated text. It is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens. OpenAI says the Decisions API pairs with its new service-tier controls per project and 12-hour prompt caching, both added in October.

Research

Microsoft turns database grading into RL rewards, lifting a 27B agent’s repeatable success

Microsoft researchers have turned ThinkingBox, their agent benchmark that checks what actually changed in a database, into a reward function for reinforcement learning. Fine-tuning Qwen3.8-27B with GRPO for 50 steps raised average success on the 507-task ThinkingBox-Bench from 51.70% to 60.78%, and the share of tasks solved on all 20 attempts from 38 to 107. The work is a follow-up to Microsoft’s earlier study of agents that claim to be done when they are not.

The team used “offline policy-relative difficulty” to pick training tasks that are neither already solved nor hopeless for the model in front of them, and kept that pool fixed through the run. Rewards came mostly from deterministic terminal-state checks, with a minority invoking an LLM judge for narrow rubric questions. Training ran on 24 H100s, and the trained checkpoint was evaluated on an out-of-distribution set whose domains, databases and tools it had not seen.

Source: Hugging Face (Microsoft), 22 Oct 2026

Research

IQuest-Q1 report details a 320B agentic CLI model trained from scratch

Beijing-based IQuest Research has published a technical report for IQuest-Q1, an open-source agentic foundation model optimised for command-line interaction and software engineering. The model uses a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture with 320 billion total parameters and 15 billion active per token, and was trained from scratch on roughly 29 trillion tokens with context extended to 512K. The report says it needed about 52% of the compute a comparable Qwen3-30B-A3B-style baseline used to reach the same training loss.

IQuest-Q1’s post-training combines supervised fine-tuning, domain-specific reinforcement learning, multi-teacher on-policy distillation and model merging, and was trained across multiple agent harnesses including Claude Code and Codex. On the company’s 30-benchmark suite it scores 78.4 on MCP-Atlas, 75.0 on Toolathlon-Verified, 74.1 on SkillsBench and 52.7 on AutomationBench. The team says it built more than one million containerised task instances and used task-specific verifiers to reduce reward hacking.

Source: PR Newswire (IQuest Research), 23 Oct 2026

Policy

OpenAI asks California to strengthen its frontier AI safety law

OpenAI has asked California governor Gavin Newsom to make SB 53, the state’s frontier AI safety law, considerably stricter. In a letter dated 20 October, the company proposed adding training-stage monitoring, stronger cybersecurity, third-party incident notification, effective shutdown controls and independent audits. The law currently focuses on disclosure — publishing a safety framework and reporting critical incidents — rather than pre-release approval.

The request is unusual because AI companies have generally argued that state rules should align with federal and international standards rather than run ahead of them. OpenAI’s proposals would reach into the lab: what a model does while it is being trained, whether it tries to escape its test environment, and who must be told if it does. Any amendment would need a bill and votes in both houses of the California legislature plus the governor’s signature, so nothing changes immediately.

Frameworks

Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation as it adds four projects

The Agentic AI Foundation announced four new projects — Agent Skills, contributed by Anthropic; Open Responses, contributed by OpenAI; OpenEnv, contributed by Hugging Face; and OpenSandbox, contributed by Alibaba — bringing it to 10 hosted projects in its first year. Agent Skills is an open format for packaging instructions, scripts and resources that agents load on demand, and it joins MCP under neutral governance. Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code, have integrated the standard.

Open Responses is an open specification for multi-provider, interoperable LLM interfaces based on the OpenAI Responses API, defining a shared schema and consistent streaming events. OpenEnv is an open framework for building and sharing reinforcement-learning environments through a containerised service exposing MCP and the Gym training protocol. OpenSandbox is a general-purpose sandbox platform with multi-language SDKs and Docker and Kubernetes runtimes.

Source: Agentic AI Foundation, 22 Oct 2026

Frameworks

TorchTPU goes open source, bringing native PyTorch to Google Cloud TPUs

Google has open-sourced TorchTPU, its native PyTorch stack for Google Cloud TPUs, co-developed with the PyTorch team at Meta. It lets developers train, fine-tune and serve PyTorch models on TPUs with minimal code changes, running operations eagerly by default rather than through the older lazy-tensor PyTorch/XLA path. Google also released an early preview of a TPU Agent Suite that gives coding assistants TPU-specific workflows.

TorchTPU replaces PyTorch/XLA and integrates with Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM and SGLang serving backends, TorchTitan for distributed training and VeRL for RL post-training. It supports Cloud TPUs v5e through v7 (Ironwood). The TPU Agent Suite adds a router agent and task-specific sub-agents for model onboarding, diagnostics, Pallas kernel development and documentation lookup, and works with Claude Code and Google Antigravity, with Codex and Cursor support planned.

Source: Google Cloud & PyTorch, 22 Oct 2026

NVIDIA opens its retrieval layer with five Nemotron 3.5 models

NVIDIA has released five open Nemotron 3.5 retrieval models — Embed, Rerank and Parse — under the OpenMDW-1.1 licence, with no revenue gate. The set covers text and image embeddings in 34 languages at 8-billion and 1-billion parameter sizes, an 8-billion-parameter reranker and a PDF parser. NVIDIA says better retrieval cut agentic token cost per query by about 14% while improving retrieval accuracy in its own tests.

The models target the layer that decides which evidence reaches an agent’s context for retrieval-augmented generation and agentic search. NVIDIA reports the Embed 8B model at 61.17 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3.1, first among dense retrieval models, and the Parse model at 75.20 on ParseBench at about 10.5 pages per second on a single H100. The weights, data and training recipes are published on Hugging Face.

Source: NVIDIA & Forkast, 22 Oct 2026

RAG

Study finds stacked RAG techniques deliver +17%, not the +47% they promised

A new open-source framework called LASAGNA tested 450 retrieval configurations across eight benchmarks and found that three individually strong techniques which predicted a combined +47% gain delivered only +17% when stacked. The paper reports a median shortfall of 41% across 17 technique combinations, and finds retrieval quality and answer faithfulness essentially uncorrelated (Pearson r = −0.01). It warns that gains seen in component benchmarks do not carry through to a full pipeline.

The study spanned 358,215 query evaluations across corpora from 5,000 to 200,000 documents. It also found a rank crossover between RAG profiles somewhere between 15,000 and 25,000 documents, so the pipeline that wins on a small corpus may not remain best at scale. One surprising result: a 2019 MS-MARCO MiniLM-L6 cross-encoder ranked above all four recent rerankers tested.

Source: AI Weekly (LASAGNA paper), 22 Oct 2026

ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 targets film production with 4K video and a draft mode

ByteDance’s cloud platform Volcano Engine released Seedance 2.5 4K on 23 October, aimed at film and television production. The company says additional training improves the rendering of facial expressions, subtle movements and visual detail. A Draft mode lets users test shots with lower-cost 480p previews before generating a final 4K version.

The model supports 10-bit colour and is designed to preserve image quality, colours and fine detail when working with reference materials or editing existing footage. It is currently available for trials by selected enterprise customers, and some film-production and post-production companies in China and overseas have begun testing it. ByteDance’s Dreamina platform is also rolling out Seedance 2.5 to subscriber accounts in Europe, Asia, the Middle East and South America.
