Maharashtra Unveils AI Policy 2026: ₹10,000-Crore Investment Target, 1.5 Lakh Jobs

The Maharashtra Cabinet approved the state's first dedicated AI Policy 2026, modelled on the India AI Mission, aiming to attract ₹10,000 crore in investment and create over 1.5 lakh jobs by 2031. The policy proposes a Maharashtra AI Mission, AI cities across five innovation zones, and a ₹500 crore AI Startup Venture Fund.

The policy also pledges to train two lakh youth and professionals in AI, establish a State AI Data Exchange, and set up the Maharashtra Advanced Centre for Artificial Intelligence Training. To bring MSMEs into the fold, it offers a 20 per cent subsidy on AI implementation costs to 5,000 MSMEs and a central ‘Maha AI Tools Hub’. Twelve AI incubators are planned across the state. The move positions Maharashtra alongside Uttar Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh in a wave of state-level AI industrial policy this week.

Source: News on AIR (Akashvani), 28 August 2026

Uttar Pradesh Startup Policy 2026: Up to ₹40 Crore Per Startup, 50% Extra for Deep-Tech

The UP government overhauled its startup policy, allowing state investment of up to ₹40 crore in a single eligible startup and granting deep-tech ventures (AI, robotics, quantum, space tech) a 50 per cent additional incentive over standard support. A ₹1,000 crore Fund of Funds and ₹400 crore corpus fund anchor the framework.

The policy adds a 4 per cent interest subsidy on term loans up to ₹2 crore, patent and quality-certification reimbursement up to ₹2 crore, and matching grants up to ₹5 crore. AI/ML, robotics, quantum, space-tech, healthtech and agritech startups get prototype funding of ₹20 lakh, seed funding of ₹30 lakh, and up to 40 per cent research-cost support. The state reported over 24,000 startups and eight unicorns at launch.

Source: Amar Ujala, 28 August 2026

India's First Dedicated Quantum and AI University Campus to Come Up in Amaravati

Andhra Pradesh approved a NIELIT-run Quantum and AI university campus in Amaravati, a ₹730.70 crore facility on 8.5 acres, fully funded by a MeitY grant-in-aid. Academic and skill programmes begin September 2026 at a temporary Guntur facility while the permanent campus is built.

NIELIT proposes collaborations with IIT-Tirupati, IIIT-Sri City, Andhra University and JNTU for academic programmes, research and technology development. The initiative aligns with the National Quantum Mission, IndiaAI Mission and India Semiconductor Mission, and is expected to strengthen Amaravati's identity as a national destination for quantum, AI, semiconductors and deep-tech innovation.

Anthropic Opens ‘Model Hardware Standard’ Letting AI Agents Operate Lab Equipment

Anthropic opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a specification that lets AI agents like Claude discover and safely operate physical devices — microscopes, robotic arms, liquid handlers, laser systems — through a single shared interface it likens to a USB-C cable for AI. Early partners include Genentech, Carnegie Mellon, QuEra, Universal Robots and AWS.

MHS is model-agnostic, working with non-Claude agents via the Model Context Protocol, and Anthropic plans to open-source the specification after the preview. The company says integration that once took weeks of custom engineering can now happen in hours or minutes. The move marks Anthropic's first significant step into ‘physical AI’, alongside a new in-house silicon team and the hire of hardware executive Caitlin Kalinowski.

Source: Technology.org, 28 August 2026

Anthropic: Automated AI Researchers Reliably Fix Alignment Failures

Anthropic released research showing Claude can autonomously train models to improve performance across 10 categories of alignment failure — deception, sycophancy, privacy violation and more — without degrading general capabilities. In 60 hours, Claude tested over 50 solutions and reached alignment scores nearly matching production models.

The winning solution contained just over 2,000 training examples, making it roughly 15,000 times more efficient than Anthropic's production alignment procedure. The paper notes the automated researcher beat experienced humans within six hours, at roughly $4 per hour versus $150 for human researchers. Limitations remain: the failures studied were narrow, and the methods may degrade capabilities not measured.

EU AI Act’s Transparency Wave Now Live: Chatbots Must Disclose, Content Must Be Marked

From 2 August 2026, Article 50 transparency duties are in force across the EU: users must be told when interacting with AI, and synthetic audio, image, video and text must be marked in a machine-readable format. The AI Office now holds live enforcement powers, with fines up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices.

The Digital Omnibus on AI deferred the heavier high-risk regime to December 2027 and August 2028, so what is live now is disclosure, not conformity assessment. The next deadline is 2 December 2026, when the transitional marking period ends and a new ban on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery takes effect. Anthropic, Google and OpenAI have all begun shipping watermarking in response.

Source: NYU Program on Corporate Compliance and Enforcement, 28 August 2026

Federal Judge Rules Pentagon’s Anthropic Blacklisting Was Illegal

Judge Rita Lin ruled the Trump administration's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was unlawful retaliation violating the First Amendment, after the company refused to allow Claude's use for lethal autonomous warfare and mass surveillance. Lin vacated the government directives and ordered their rescission.

Lin found the actions ‘arbitrary and capricious’ and said ‘the empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics.’ The government had paradoxically sought to apply the Defense Production Act to Anthropic — deeming it essential — while branding it a threat. The administration may appeal; a separate case continues in the DC Circuit.

Source: Ars Technica, 28 August 2026

Microsoft Agent Framework 1.0 Reaches GA, Unifying Semantic Kernel and AutoGen

Microsoft shipped Agent Framework 1.0 to general availability, merging Semantic Kernel and AutoGen into a single supported production runtime with a ready-to-use Agent Harness for tool-calling, history persistence and context management. Alongside it, Microsoft published Agent Hooks, an open framework-neutral governance contract with conformance testing.

Agent Hooks specifies eight interception points bracketing the agent loop, with SDKs in Python, TypeScript, .NET, Rust and Go, and a 47-scenario conformance kit making ‘deny means deny’ a testable claim. Microsoft Agent Framework is the first framework to ship the contract in core. The release targets teams needing policy-driven observability over third-party coding agents.

Weaviate 1.39 Promotes Boost API and MMR to GA, Previews 4-Bit Quantization

Weaviate v1.39 promotes the Boost API (query-time rescoring) and Maximal Marginal Relevance diversity selection to general availability across hybrid and vector searches. New in the release: a 4-bit Rotational Quantization preview (784 bytes per 1536-dim vector, 7.84x smaller than float32), an experimental Search REST API, and automatic HNSW snapshots.

The experimental Search REST API posts JSON over plain HTTP/1.1, suiting shell scripts, edge workers and LLM tool calling where a client library is unavailable. gRPC-Web, shipped quietly in 1.38, is now covered: browsers can reach the gRPC API over ordinary HTTP. Automatic HNSW snapshots cut commit-log disk usage and speed startup, retiring five tuning knobs.

Google DeepMind Ships Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash With 4K Video and 40-Second Scene Extension

Google DeepMind released Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash, a production-ready generative video model adding 4K upscaling, scene extension up to a cumulative 40 seconds in 10-second increments, and first-and-last-frame keyframe control. A 360p draft mode generates up to 60% faster at roughly a third of 720p cost, enabling cheap iteration before final 4K finishing.

The model reasons over 10 seconds of prior context (up from one second) to keep characters, lighting and camera motion consistent across extensions, and accepts up to three seconds of reference video for character continuity. Adobe Firefly, Figma Weave and GMI Cloud are already shipping it. On the Arena leaderboard, Omni 1.1 Flash ranked first for text-to-video.
