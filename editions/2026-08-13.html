Sarvam AI Tapped to Build India's First Sovereign Large Language Model

The Indian government has entrusted Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI with developing the country's first indigenous large language model under the Rs 10,370 crore IndiaAI mission, with access to up to 4,000 GPUs for six months. The startup is building three variants — Sarvam-Large, Sarvam-Small, and Sarvam-Edge — tuned for Indian languages and voice-first interfaces.

The model programme sits inside the broader IndiaAI mission, through which the government has already selected 10 companies to supply 18,693 GPUs for domestic compute. Sarvam's mandate is explicitly sovereignty-oriented: models fully developed and trained inside India, reducing dependence on foreign technologies while handling the country's linguistic diversity. Co-founder Vivek Raghavan framed the goal as building multimodal, multi-scale AI "specifically designed for Indian citizens," with data kept onshore. The three-variant strategy — Large, Small, and Edge — mirrors the tiered deployment philosophy of frontier labs, but the differentiator is Indian-language depth and cost structure rather than raw benchmark supremacy.

The Digital India BHASHINI Division and NITI Aayog signed a statement of intent to build multilingual, voice-first AI for citizen services, integrating BHASHINI's translation APIs across NITI Aayog's digital platforms.

A key component is SMRITI, a meeting-transcription system built with BHASHINI that automates minutes and enables multilingual translation. The collaboration also covers reference voice bots for sector-specific NITI Aayog workflows and crowd-sourced linguistic data collection through BhashaDaan.

RBI Governor: AI Should Approve Loans Humans Would Reject — but Banks Stay Accountable

Reserve Bank Governor Sanjay Malhotra told the FIBAC conference that AI trained on alternative data can extend the frontier of "bankable" India far beyond manual underwriting, but he set strict governance expectations: full AI inventories, board-approved policies, explainability, red-teaming, and human oversight.

Malhotra's speech framed AI as "a capability to be responsibly harnessed and not merely as a risk to be contained," arguing models trained on cash flows, GST filings, and utility payments could widen financial inclusion at a fraction of marginal cost. But he drew a hard line on accountability: no matter how sophisticated the model, responsibility rests with the bank, and meaningful human oversight must remain a design principle. He flagged the black-box problem, geographic and occupational bias, and concentration risk from shared vendors as the three dangers banks must actively manage.

Cross-Model Replay Attack Exposes Hidden Reasoning Traces in GPT, Claude, and Gemini APIs

Researchers from ELLIS Institute Tubingen, Max Planck, MATS, and Snyk showed that encrypted reasoning envelopes from flagship LLMs can be replayed into weaker sibling models to extract private chain-of-thought traces, system prompts, and even credentials in plain text.

The attack affects the Claude, GPT, and Gemini ecosystems and requires only standard, unprivileged API access. The team confirmed identical cross-model compatibility across OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family and Google's Gemini 3 lineup. Following responsible disclosure, all three vendors deployed server-side mitigations rendering the original proof-of-concepts non-reproducible on current API builds. The finding underscores that encrypted reasoning does not equal private reasoning when a weaker sibling model can be used as a decryption oracle.

Source: Cybersecurity News, 11 August 2026

Evo-Bench: Can LLMs Rewrite Their Own Agent Harnesses?

Researchers from Renmin University of China and BOSS Zhipin released Evo-Bench, the first benchmark isolating a model's capacity to improve its own agent scaffolding. GPT-5.6-Sol topped the leaderboard at 46.3 overall, just shy of the 47.5 human-engineered baseline.

The benchmark fixes DeepSeek-V4-Flash as the policy model and gives each evolver a 48-hour, 20-iteration, 1,000-step budget across Search, Office, and General domains. On General tasks, GPT-5.6-Sol and Qwen3.7-Max both hit 59.4, strictly beating the human baseline of 56.3 — a milestone for autonomously synthesised reasoning structures. Office remained the weak spot. Cost varied wildly: GPT-5.6-Sol runs exceeded $500 each, while DeepSeek-V4-Pro produced functional improvements for under a dollar.

Meta Releases Muse Glimmer, a 30B Open-Weight Model for Local Agents

Meta shipped Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter multimodal model under the permissive Apache 2.0 license, engineered to run always-on agents on a single 24GB consumer GPU. It leads the MCP Atlas tool-orchestration benchmark at 75.5, a 13-point margin over the nearest rival.

Muse Glimmer combines a 27.9B text decoder with a 1.9B ViT vision encoder, supports 128K+ context (tested to 262K), and ships in BF16, NVFP4, GGUF, and MLX formats with a DFlash speculative-decoding drafter. The release marks Meta's first fully open model since it retired the Llama family, and Zuckerberg committed to opening weights for the larger Muse Spark 1.2 in coming weeks. The catch: Glimmer trails Qwen3.6-27B by 9 points on TerminalBench 2.1, shows a 28.4% prompt-injection attack-success rate, and the training corpus is undisclosed — a provenance question for regulated deployments.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning: Open 30B MoE Built for Always-On Agents

NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, a 30B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters, optimised for high-volume, low-latency execution in autonomous agents with up to 4x output speed versus similar-sized models.

Beyond Vector Search: Hybrid Retrieval and Reranking Become the Production RAG Default

A detailed new guide argues that vector search alone is insufficient for production RAG, laying out a retrieve-then-rerank pipeline combining BM25 keyword search, dense retrieval, Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and cross-encoder reranking as the new baseline.

The pipeline runs BM25 and dense retrieval in parallel, fuses results with RRF to avoid score-normalisation headaches, trims redundancy with MMR, and applies a cross-encoder reranker for a final high-precision pass. The guide frames MTTR (mean time to resolve) as a first-class metric alongside recall@k, noting that cross-encoder reranking is often the biggest latency add after generation itself. The practical takeaway: reranking is one of the highest-ROI improvements in production RAG once retrieval is already decent.
