The through-line is that capability is no longer the scarce thing — access, distribution and the cost of the last mile are.

★ Top Story Global

OpenAI ships GPT-6 Cyber, a vulnerability-hunting model it rates Critical and keeps behind a vetted-access gate

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber on 5 November to selected Daybreak Red customers, a model it scores Critical for cyber risk. It reached 48.5% on ExploitGym, up from 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and completed 95% of RemoteExploitBench tasks involving Jenkins, PostgreSQL and GitLab. In an internal Linux kernel audit the model produced 948 working root exploits and surfaced 81 high-severity Firefox bugs.

The gate is as much the story as the capability. Access requires Daybreak Red vetting plus a separate model approval, so general API customers cannot reach it. Pricing runs at $20 per million input tokens and $100 per million output tokens — roughly 60% and 33% more than its predecessor, and about twice Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5.1 base rates. CodeRabbit reported finding 66% more medium- and high-severity issues on internal benchmarks after adopting it. The release landed alongside the Frontier Model Forum's cyber jailbreak rubric, and follows Google's decision to hand Gemini 4 Carbon to a subset of cyber defenders first — a pattern of shipping the most dangerous capability to the smallest possible audience.

C-DAC opens a 10,000-unit IndiaAI compute tender and cuts the minimum cluster to 512 units

The Centre for Development of Advanced Computing has opened a tender for up to 10,000 additional AI compute units under the IndiaAI Mission, dropping the minimum cluster size from 1,000 units to 512 at a single Indian data centre. Bidders may offer clusters of 4,000, 1,000 or 512 units, and the eligible hardware list now covers TPUs and other accelerators, not GPUs alone.

The lower threshold is meant to widen participation, but the financial bar remains: average annual turnover of at least Rs 120 crore across FY2023-24 to FY2025-26 and positive net worth as of 31 March 2026. MeitY has also let bidders sell capacity globally to keep them profitable until domestic demand picks up. More than 38,000 GPUs are already available through the shared facility at Rs 65 per hour, against an original target of 10,000. MeitY secretary S. Krishnan told ETGovernment on 4 November that India targets an indigenous GPU chip in about 24 months under India Semiconductor Mission 2.0.

Anthropic details a memory system built from session records, model-written notes and a 'dreaming' review pass

Anthropic described a Claude memory architecture assembled from session records, model-written notes, linked knowledge structures and a small block of information loaded at the start of each session, plus a review process it calls 'dreaming' that revisits older records and updates stored material. The company reported memory raised Claude Opus 5.5's mean reward from 0.655 to 0.838 on one database task.

Those figures come from Anthropic's own test, not an independent evaluation, and the roundup that reported them says it checked each item against a lab post, product documentation or code. In a separate account, Anthropic described using Claude in research on vault particles, searching roughly 15 billion predicted proteins and testing 44 candidates; the post also disclosed errors, including a protocol problem that cost two weeks. The related preprint had been submitted but was not yet public. Persistent memory across sessions has become the competitive front this month, with Meta's Active Memory models shipping in the same window.

Research

Salesforce finds per-domain RL experts plus distillation beat joint training by 42% on compute

A Salesforce AI Research paper posted on 5 November found that training one reinforcement-learning expert per domain and distilling them into a single model matched a jointly trained model on math and code with 42.0% fewer total training FLOPs. On four domains the saving was 31.6%.

The paper, 'Unpacking the Compute Efficiency of Strong-to-Weak OPD against GRPO', also found that distilling from a large external teacher generally lost to plain RL on accuracy per FLOP. On LiveCodeBench, GRPO reached 46.4% at 0.6 EFLOP while distillation from an external 32B teacher peaked at 28.7% after 68.7 EFLOP. The result reverses when the teacher is the student's own RL checkpoint: on math, on-policy distillation recovered that checkpoint's accuracy with 50 times fewer FLOPs than GRPO needed to reach the same level. Students were Qwen3 models of 1.7B, 4B and 8B parameters; the paper is a preprint and does not report dollar costs.

Source: FourWeekMBA, citing arXiv 2611.08453, 8 November 2026

Research

Tsinghua ICML oral: forcing left-to-right unmasking during RL training lifts diffusion LLM reasoning

An ICML 2026 oral paper from Tsinghua University's LeapLab tested whether diffusion language models gain from their native flexible token ordering during reasoning. Enforcing strict left-to-right unmasking during GRPO training on LLaDA 8B Instruct reached 89.1% on GSM8K against 86.9% for the SPG baseline, and 45.1% versus 41.8% on MATH-500.

The mechanism is entropy at logical branch points. Under arbitrary-order decoding, connectives such as 'Therefore' and 'Given' get deferred until later context constrains them, producing what the authors call entropy collapse; under left-to-right decoding no future context exists, so the model must make high-entropy decisions early, guided by the RL signal. Overall average entropy is similar between the two orders, which the authors read as a localised effect rather than a vocabulary-wide one. Evaluation still used the diffusion model's native arbitrary-order decoding — only training changed. On HumanEval, 21.3% of solved problems were exclusive to the left-to-right-trained model, against 0.6% for arbitrary order.

Google ADK 2.14.0 graduates agent-to-agent, execution environments and evaluation to stable

Google released version 2.14.0 of its Agent Development Kit on 7 November, moving execution environments, the A2A protocol, evaluation modules and the core runtime out of experimental status, along with 26 auth, environment and app modules and 16 toolset features.

New in the release: MultiRunConfig for concurrent multi-run evaluation through adk eval --num_runs with a --parallelism flag, a ToolArgsMatcher for semantic argument comparison, a TeamsRunner for Microsoft Teams and an OpenAIDecisionsClassifier. RAG

Tiger Data's pg_vectorsearch builds a 100M-vector index in 1h40m where HNSW took 42 hours

Tiger Data has released pg_vectorsearch, an open-source PostgreSQL extension for vector similarity search, currently in alpha. Its PRISM index uses data partitioning and compression rather than the graph approach behind pgvector's HNSW index, cutting memory requirements and index build times.

In Tiger Data's own benchmarks on 100 million vectors, PRISM built an index in 1 hour 40 minutes on a machine with 128 GB of memory; HNSW took 42 hours on a machine with 512 GB. PRISM combines hierarchical clustering to narrow the search space, compressed vectors for sequential scanning, and RaBitQ quantization, and duplicates vectors near partition boundaries to protect recall. It supports existing pgvector columns and requires PostgreSQL 18 or later, with vectors up to 1,968 dimensions. Incremental rebalancing is not yet implemented, and filtered searches combining WHERE with ORDER BY are unsupported until general availability.

Source: InfoQ, 7 November 2026

RAG

Cohere puts Embed 5 and Rerank 5 on OpenRouter, with Context Focus retaining 96% answer quality on half the text

Cohere announced on 4 November that Embed 5 and Rerank 5 are available through OpenRouter on existing keys and credits. Embed 5 Pro builds vectors for indexed content, including text, image and mixed inputs, while Rerank 5 scores and reorders candidates before a chat model generates an answer.

Cohere described Context Focus, an optional Rerank 5 feature that selects relevant text spans and returns them with document-level relevance scores. Across 30 question-answering datasets, Cohere reported answer quality held at 96% after half the retrieved text was removed and 87% after three quarters were removed — its own evaluation, not a guarantee for any particular application. Its reference pipeline retrieves 20 candidates, reranks, keeps up to five and applies a demonstration score threshold of 0.3, skipping the model call entirely if nothing clears it. Rerank 5 is text-only, so image results must be reranked on parsed text or captions, or left out of the reranking step. OpenRouter lists no EU in-region routing for the Pro tiers.

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash extends native video to 15 seconds and cuts 720p pricing 30%

Google made Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generally available across the Gemini API, AI Studio, Vertex AI and Gemini Ultra on 5 November, extending native single-pass clips from 10 to 15 seconds. Pricing for 720p falls 30% to $0.07 per second, and Google Vids now gives any Google account three 15-second AI videos a month free.

The model conditions on character images, 3-second motion clips and 1-second voice cues for multi-speaker lip-sync, and supports conversational shot editing plus post-generation 1080p and 4K upscaling.
