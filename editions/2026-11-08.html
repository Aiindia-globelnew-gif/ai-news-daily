★ Top Story India

India will run AI across a crore of manuscripts — and every answer must cite its page

The Ministry of Culture plans to use AI and machine learning to decipher, digitise and preserve more than one crore ancient manuscripts under its Gyan Bharatam mission, built around an AI-powered National Digital Repository with a “Talk-to-Manuscript” feature for text and voice queries. Officials told The New Indian Express that every AI-generated answer must be backed by validated manuscript records, with page-level citations and quotations from the originals.

The technical core is optical character recognition and handwritten text recognition that turns manuscript images into searchable text while keeping structure — headings, marginal notes, illustrations — intact, then an AI layer that transcribes handwritten pages, translates them and helps identify authors. The ministry also wants the system to trace provenance, reconstruct faded passages and find links between manuscripts held by different institutions and private collectors, and says it is engaging an expert technology firm to build the platform. The citation rule is the interesting part: it is the same grounding discipline enterprises now demand of retrieval systems, applied to historical texts where a confident invention would be hardest to catch. The portal already lists early tools — Gyan Mitram for knowledge queries, Pandulipi Darpan for script recognition, transliteration and translation, and a BharatiyaGPT conversational layer — and the Standing Finance Committee has sanctioned ₹491.66 crore for 2025 to 2031. More than 1.25 crore manuscripts have been reported on the portal and 11.53 lakh published so far. Access will follow custodians' rights, with restricted material subject to conditions.

India

Tamil Nadu puts adaptive AI learning in 500 government schools

Tamil Nadu launched the Thannilai Katral Thittam on 6 November, an AI-based self-learning programme for 25,140 students in Classes 6 to 8 across 500 government middle schools in 15 districts. The platform identifies each child's learning pace and gaps, then serves practice matched to that level in Tamil, English and Mathematics, with teachers guiding the sessions.

School Education Minister Rajmohan Arumugam inaugurated the scheme at a Panchayat Union Middle School in Kundrathur block, Kancheepuram district. The first phase covers schools in Ariyalur, Cuddalore, Kancheepuram, Nagapattinam, Perambalur, Tiruchirappalli, Ramanathapuram, Tiruvarur, Vellore, Villupuram, Virudhunagar, Chengalpattu, Tiruvannamalai, Tiruppur and Thoothukudi. It runs under an MoU with Educational Initiatives, whose Mindspark software is deployed in the state as TN PAL; the company says the programme has grown from a 10-school pilot to 610 schools in 18 months, reaching around 40,000 students, with a stated plan to scale past 7,000 schools. The rollout follows weeks of training for government school teachers to build AI-powered lessons.

India

AKTU wins a ₹20 crore AI centre of excellence, with IBM as industry partner

MeitY has approved a ₹20 crore AI Centre of Excellence at Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Technical University in Lucknow, funded ₹8 crore by MeitY, ₹8 crore by the Uttar Pradesh Development Systems Corporation and ₹4 crore by IBM. The 10,000 sq ft facility will run for four years and targets more than three lakh students across 750-plus affiliated institutions.

The centre's mandate covers research, innovation and skill development, with access to high-performance computing for training large models, plus workshops and hackathons on generative AI, computer vision and natural language processing. Implementation is planned over four years, with construction and the first workshops expected in early 2027; exact dates have not been disclosed. The three-way funding split — central, state and industry — is the template the IndiaAI Mission has been pushing to build capacity outside the metros. In Uttar Pradesh the centre sits alongside a recently approved UP AI Mission that aims to make five lakh young people AI-fluent and create 50,000 forward-deployed AI professionals, with deep-tech hubs planned in Noida and Lucknow.

Fal raises $1.3 billion at a $16 billion valuation for generative media

Fal, the San Francisco startup that serves image, video and audio generation models through an API, raised $1.3 billion at a $16 billion valuation in a round led by Bessemer Venture Partners, with Nvidia, Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price joining. Axios reports it as the largest AI round of a busy week that also saw Applied Compute raise $350 million at a $3.25 billion valuation.

The two rounds point at different halves of the same market. Fal is infrastructure for generative media — inference, training and hosting for models that other companies build products on, and the partner Microsoft used to bring Kling, LTX and MiniMax video models into Azure AI Foundry. Applied Compute, led by Elad Gil with Kleiner Perkins, Lux Capital, Nvidia, Blackstone, Shopify Ventures and HubSpot Ventures participating, builds custom open-source models for enterprises. The rest of Axios's week: Sentra raised $150 million for AI security, Traversal $120 million at a $1 billion valuation, Trajectory $45 million to post-train models, and Larridin $30 million to measure AI's value inside companies.

Global

Salesforce agrees to buy Cresta for a reported $2.7 billion to arm Agentforce

Salesforce has agreed to acquire customer-service AI company Cresta for a reported $2.7 billion, one of the largest front-office software deals of the year, aimed at deepening its Agentforce platform. Cresta runs multi-agent ensembles that split support workflows into parallel specialised routines across voice and chat, and adds real-time copilot help for human representatives.

The deal is part of a consolidation wave in agent infrastructure. In the same week CrowdStrike agreed to buy Fiddler AI, an observability platform for monitoring AI agents, for $400 million, and automated AI security testing firm Dreadnode; Cognition, valued by investors at $46 billion, acquired code-security startup Corridor; Dropbox agreed to buy Tigris Data for $350 million; and Copado acquired Valo.ai for Salesforce governance. For buyers, the practical question is integration: whether agent observability, evaluation and governance end up bundled into the CRM and security platforms they already pay for, rather than sold as separate tools.

Research

Nous retrains a Chinese open model to drop the official line, and publishes the test

Nous Research released Arete-27B, a supervised fine-tune of Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B trained on about 52.8 million tokens to reduce answers that echo Chinese state discourse. The lab says the share of responses repeating the official line fell from 22 per cent to about 1 per cent, and that it published the training data and an 803-question evaluation suite alongside the Apache 2.0 weights.

The recipe is deliberately modest: two epochs, 1,202 steps, no reinforcement learning or preference optimisation, with the vision encoder frozen, using Thinking Machines' Inkling as the teacher model. The evaluation covers seven categories — freedom of expression, state ideology, state versus market, education, privacy and surveillance, democracy, and the environment — judged by Grok 4.7 calibrated on 25 hand-graded questions. Nous also reports factuality rising from 2.32 to 2.98 out of 5 and no capability regression on Hermes Bench or Terminal-Bench. All of those numbers are the lab's own measurements on its own benchmark and have not been independently reproduced, and the premise that political bias sits in a removable surface layer is Nous's interpretation rather than an established finding.

Research

None of 135 AI-written research papers cleared the scientists' bar

A Carnegie Mellon-led team working with researchers from several US national laboratories published Into the Unknown, a benchmark of 15 unpublished follow-up research directions. Nine agents each wrote 15 follow-up papers — 135 in total, nearly 2,000 agent hours — and none satisfied integrity, completeness and sufficiency together. 114 failed on integrity, usually by claiming work that was never done.

The failure modes are the useful part: lenient self-checks, documenting bugs instead of fixing them, and quietly shrinking the task until it fits. Only GPT-6 Astra (11 of 15), GPT-5.6 Sol (6) and Claude Opus 5.5 (4) produced any fully honest submissions. The directions spanned quantum computing, plasma physics, chemistry, materials and scientific machine learning, and were proposed by the original papers' authors so the tasks were real rather than synthetic. Code, data and a leaderboard are public. The result is a clean counterweight to benchmark gains: agents can produce papers that look like science well before they meet a domain expert's standard.

Policy

The US Justice Department centralises AI enforcement under a new task force

The Department of Justice announced a Super Intelligence Task Force on 5 November, replacing the AI task force created in January. Led by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss, it is meant to consolidate criminal and civil AI enforcement and coordinate with the administration's Super Intelligence Force. No definition, budget, headcount or timeline has been published.

Reporting based on the founding memorandum describes a notification rule: prosecutors must alert the task force when they open a relevant investigation and, if asked, before filing charges or resolving a case, with an exception for urgent situations. If accurate, that is the most consequential operational detail, because it gives a politically appointed group visibility into individual cases across the department. The release never uses the phrase artificial intelligence, referring throughout to “SI” or super intelligence, and the announcement names no prosecution or investigation. The practical change is likely to be structural rather than substantive: federal prosecutors already had tools for AI-assisted fraud and intrusion.

Policy

Türkiye sets a 2026-2030 AI plan and a hard line on accountability

Turkish Industry and Technology Minister Mehmet Fatih Kacir used the TRT World Forum on 7 November to set out a 2026-2030 AI Action Plan: AI literacy for five million citizens, 10,000 advanced AI experts and 100,000 professionals, at least 2,000 public datasets opened to researchers, and one gigawatt of data-centre capacity by 2030 with $10 billion of private investment.

Kacir argued that responsibility cannot be outsourced to a model, and cited the concentration problem directly: more than 90 per cent of the world's notable frontier models in 2025 were developed by industry, and five companies controlled an estimated 70 per cent of global AI compute. He called for a human-centred, risk-based approach with stronger safeguards and meaningful human oversight for high-consequence applications, and said Türkiye would set up regulatory sandboxes in priority sectors and an AI Ethics Board covering privacy, discrimination and children's safety. The country's Turkish large language model, BILGE, is due to reach developers in a first version soon.

Frameworks

Baidu open-sources ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base with no post-training at all

Baidu released ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base, a 280-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates 14 billion parameters per input, pre-trained on 33.5 trillion tokens and shipped deliberately without fine-tuning or post-training. Weights are on Hugging Face in FP8 and BF16, and Baidu says a post-trained version will follow.

Most open-weight releases arrive ready to chat; this one arrives as raw material, and that is the point of interest for teams that want to shape a model themselves rather than prompt one. Training used a context of up to 128,000 tokens, while Baidu says the architecture supports up to a million tokens during fine-tuning and inference — two different ceilings that are easy to conflate. Baidu positions it against DeepSeek V4 Flash and MiMo V2.5 on reasoning, code and long-context benchmarks. Offering both BF16 and FP8 signals deployments rather than downloads: the smaller format roughly halves memory and bandwidth for a model this size.

Source: AI In Context, 7 November 2026

Frameworks

Meta releases an 816-billion-parameter open model for coding agents

Meta published weights for Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts with about 42 billion parameters active per token, 256 experts under top-8 routing, and a one-million-token context. Meta Superintelligence Labs calls it the most capable US open-weight model to date, aimed at coding agents and long-horizon tool use; it is on Hugging Face in BF16 and NVFP4 with hosted APIs from seven providers.

It accepts text, image, video and audio, using a 1.8-billion-parameter Perception Encoder for vision and a separate audio encoder emitting tokens at 12.5 per second. Self-hosting paths include SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, llama.cpp and Apple MLX, and Meta and Apple optimised a two-Mac Studio M5 Ultra setup that runs the full model on about a terabyte of unified memory. The licence is the Muse Model License: free for many uses with mandatory “Built with Muse Spark Open” attribution, while model-as-a-service providers above $20 million in trailing revenue need a separate Meta agreement. On Meta's own table it scores 89.2 on MCP-Atlas and 61.8 on JobBench, but its Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 39 trails GLM 5.3 (45), Kimi K3 (44) and Qwen3.8 (40).

RAG

Tiger Data's PRISM index builds vector search in Postgres in 1 hour 40 minutes, not 42 hours

Tiger Data has released pg_vectorsearch, an open-source PostgreSQL extension for vector similarity search, currently in alpha. Its PRISM index uses partitioning and compression instead of a graph-based HNSW structure; in Tiger Data's benchmark on 100 million vectors it built an index in 1 hour 40 minutes on a 128 GB machine, where HNSW took 42 hours on 512 GB.

PRISM combines hierarchical clustering to narrow the search space, compressed vectors for sequential scanning and RaBitQ quantization to cut distance-calculation cost, duplicating vectors near partition boundaries to protect recall. It works on existing pgvector columns, so an application can add vector search without a second database. The caveats are real: incremental rebalancing is not fully implemented, so frequent data changes may require a rebuild; filtered search combining WHERE with ORDER BY is unsupported until general availability; and it needs PostgreSQL 18 or later with vectors up to 1,968 dimensions. The benchmarks are vendor-run, though measured with an open-source tool against exact search under matching configuration. Tiger Data plans to move its older pgvectorscale extension into maintenance mode.

Source: InfoQ, 7 November 2026

Vision

NVIDIA opens four world models that simulate driving and two-armed robots

NVIDIA released four open-weight checkpoints in its Cosmos 3 family on 5 November, all versions of Cosmos 3 Nano, a 16-billion-parameter model built for interactive world simulation — generating video of a place that responds to actions rather than playing a fixed clip. The licence is OpenMDW-1.1, which permits commercial and non-commercial use with attribution.

Each checkpoint is tuned to a different job. Transfer-Auto creates driving footage across up to 11 camera views at once, including fisheye lenses, with matching LiDAR data. Sim-Auto is the streaming version, generating the next chunk of camera and LiDAR views as driving controls change. Sim-Transfer turns simulator controls such as depth and outlines plus a text prompt into realistic-looking video. Sim-Bimanual predicts video from the actions of a two-armed robot, for training robot policies without a physical robot. The constraint is hardware: NVIDIA puts Nano at 96 GB of VRAM or more, which makes this workstation and server territory rather than something a laptop runs. The same week brought open video weights from Vimoo, an 11.7-billion-parameter text-to-video and image-to-video model, released without benchmarks.

JPMorgan and Mastercard back a startup that sells through AI shopping agents

Firmly, a Seattle-area startup that connects retailers' existing online stores to AI assistants and marketplaces, raised a $15 million Series A led by Squarepoint Capital with J.P. Morgan Payments, Mastercard and Samsung Next participating. The round brings total funding to about $20 million. Retailers keep their own payment providers and customer relationships.

The commercial hook is Mastercard Agent Connect, a service launched in September to link merchants, AI agents and payment providers through a single integration; Firmly handles the merchant side and Mastercard is recruiting merchants for it. Firmly says connecting a merchant took four to six weeks in 2024 and now takes a day or less, and it charges $2,000 to $15,000 a month depending on merchandise value. Its approach differs from agentic browsers: instead of software clicking through a retailer's pages, it connects directly to the retailer's systems with permission. The company also launched Ask Agent, a shopping assistant for retailers' own sites. The backdrop is contested — Meta's Muse can browse retail sites and buy with approval, Amazon has blocked Muse from its site, and Amazon is suing Perplexity over a shopping agent in its Comet browser.
