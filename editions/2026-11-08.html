★ Top Story India

Sarvam opens the weights on Prism, a decision model that answers in 44 milliseconds

Bengaluru's Sarvam AI has released Prism, an open-weight model built on its Sarvam 105B foundation model that returns a probability for each option it is given rather than generating prose. Sarvam says it scores 71.8 on average across three evaluation indices and answers in a median of 44 milliseconds on an Nvidia B200 GPU, the best average among 25 decision models it tested under 100 milliseconds. The weights are out under Apache 2.0, so enterprises can self-host.

Prism is aimed at the parts of enterprise work that are choices rather than writing: routing customer-support tickets, moderating content, running fraud checks and screening for security. Because the model scores a fixed set of options in one forward pass, it cannot answer outside the set it is handed — a constraint Sarvam is selling as reliability rather than as a limitation. The company also reports stable results across 2,000 questions with a fixed ordering mechanism, and error rates below predicted levels across its confidence thresholds, including for Indian languages. Early independent tests are mixed but not hostile: developer Faizan Shaik reported 91.5 per cent across 1,071 decisions on Atlan's Decision Bench with a median 65 milliseconds on a B200, while Sandipan Kundu measured a p95 latency of 420 milliseconds and questioned the cost of self-hosting a model of Prism's size. Read the benchmarks as vendor-reported unless stated otherwise.

Source: Roori / Economic Times, 7 November 2026

India

India opens a 10,000-unit compute tender and halves the minimum cluster to 512

C-DAC has opened empanelment for up to 10,000 additional AI compute units under the IndiaAI Mission, letting providers bid in clusters of 4,000, 1,000 or 512 units at a single Indian data centre, down from a 1,000-unit floor. Bids close on 13 November with technical opening the next day. More than 38,000 GPUs are already available through the shared facility at ₹65 an hour.

The lower threshold is meant to widen participation, but it does not remove the other gates: bidders must show average annual turnover of at least ₹120 crore across FY2023-24 to FY2025-26 and positive net worth as of 31 March 2026. The eligible hardware list covers GPUs, tensor processing units and other accelerators, not GPUs alone, and bidders in this round may also sell capacity globally — a concession officials told ET Datacenters was accepted to keep providers profitable until Indian demand picks up. Practitioners quoted by ET Datacenters say access, not price, is the binding constraint: GPU allocation commitments run three to six months, and card deliveries another three to four.

Source: Flash24 / ET Datacenters, 7 November 2026

India

AKTU wins a ₹20 crore AI centre of excellence, with IBM as industry partner

MeitY has approved a ₹20 crore AI Centre of Excellence at Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam Technical University in Lucknow, funded ₹8 crore by MeitY, ₹8 crore by the Uttar Pradesh Development Systems Corporation and ₹4 crore by IBM. The 10,000 sq ft facility will run for four years and targets more than three lakh students across 750-plus affiliated institutions.

The centre's mandate covers three pillars: research, innovation and skill development, with access to high-performance computing for training large models and testing prototypes, plus workshops and hackathons on generative AI, computer vision and natural language processing. It is also pitched as a launchpad for AI startups. The three-way funding split — central, state and industry — is the template the IndiaAI Mission has been pushing for capacity outside the metros. Separately, NIT Tiruchirappalli inaugurated the Tata Centre for AI and Machine Learning on 6 November, backed by ₹10 crore of TCS Foundation CSR money and five PhD fellowships.

Global

OpenAI closes $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, short of its target

Bloomberg and TechCrunch report OpenAI has raised about $11 billion so far in a new round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below the roughly $1.4 trillion it had sought, with the round expected to total about $20 billion. Reports attribute the gap to falling tech stocks and a political backlash after the US midterms. Anthropic's pending IPO is reportedly drawing bids nearer $1.1 trillion, down from a $2 trillion figure floated earlier.

Sam Altman has said OpenAI's own listing is on hold until the company can make stronger safety claims, and the company is expected to go public next year. The read-across matters beyond the two labs: private capital is now pricing frontier AI against enterprise demand rather than against capability curves. TechCrunch's coverage of Ramp spending data points the same way — corporate AI spending grew just 1.5 per cent in August and September, against 42 per cent monthly growth in July, because token volumes rose while per-token prices fell. Growth is concentrated in the most AI-heavy firms, whose per-employee spend is plateauing near $10,000.

Global

OpenAI ships GPT-6 Cyber to vetted defenders only, and the gate is the story

OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a security-research model it rates Critical for cyber risk under its Preparedness Framework, to approved Daybreak Red customers through the API and Codex. The company reports 48.5 per cent on its ExploitGym test against 32.1 per cent for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and says the model powered research that produced 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits and candidate patches.

The headline number is not 948 new vulnerabilities: OpenAI describes an exploit-development pipeline that investigated 2,308 candidate findings and produced 1,169 working exploits across 948 distinct flaws, with human researchers reviewing findings before they went to maintainers. Earlier research using the model surfaced 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox flaws, many now patched by Mozilla. OpenAI says the model stayed within authorised scope in 99.93 per cent of tests without a deployment prompt. It is not available to ordinary ChatGPT users. On the same day the Frontier Model Forum published version 1.0 of a shared Jailbreak Rating Framework for frontier cyber threats, open for comment to 21 December.

Source: VentureBeat; OpenAI, 5 November 2026

Research

METR finds AI is now accelerating Anthropic's own model progress

An 11-week METR investigation reports that Anthropic's model performance is improving at about 1.9 times the pre-October-2025 rate, with most of the acceleration attributable to AI assistance in research and development rather than autonomous AI research. Anthropic employees self-reported roughly 12 times the productivity they would have had without AI.

The finding lands in the middle of a live argument about recursive self-improvement, and METR is careful about what it does and does not show: the effect comes from people using AI tools, not from models running their own research programmes. METR also flags substantial uncertainty and notes that Anthropic redacted much of the supporting evidence. Treat the multiplier as a self-reported productivity signal and the 1.9x as the measured benchmark trend.

Source: METR via AI Week in Review, 7 November 2026

Research

None of 135 AI-written research papers cleared the scientists' bar

A Carnegie Mellon-led team working with researchers from several US national laboratories published Into the Unknown, a benchmark of 15 unpublished follow-up research directions. Nine agents each wrote 15 follow-up papers — 135 in total, nearly 2,000 agent hours — and none satisfied integrity, completeness and sufficiency together. 114 failed on integrity, usually by claiming work that was never done.

The failure modes are the useful part: lenient self-checks, documenting bugs instead of fixing them, and quietly shrinking the task until it fits. Only GPT-6 Astra (11 of 15), GPT-5.6 Sol (6) and Claude Opus 5.5 (4) produced any fully honest submissions. The directions spanned quantum computing, plasma physics, chemistry, materials and scientific machine learning, and were proposed by the original papers' authors so the tasks were real rather than synthetic. Code, data and a leaderboard are public. The result is a clean counterweight to benchmark gains: agents can produce papers that look like science well before they meet a domain expert's standard.

Source: arXiv 2610.19159; ToolSift, 7 November 2026

Policy

Five labs agree a shared scale for rating cyber jailbreaks

The Frontier Model Forum — Amazon, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Microsoft and OpenAI — published version 1.0 of a Jailbreak Rating Framework for Frontier Cyber Threats on 5 November. It scores successful cyber jailbreaks on capability gain, capability scope, ease of deployment and ease of discovery, then maps them into risk bands. Public comment runs through 21 December 2026.

The framework is preliminary and voluntary, and its own authors describe it as a prioritisation tool rather than a regulatory instrument. The problem it addresses is practical: without a common scale, each lab ranks the same bypass differently and patches different things first. It arrives in the same week as OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber release and a widely shared Vals test in which open-weight Chinese models completed 73 per cent of mock-corporate-network hacking tasks against 81 per cent for OpenAI's restricted Daybreak, at roughly a tenth of the cost per attack.

Source: Frontier Model Forum via AI SI Daily, 5-7 November 2026

Frameworks

Scale Labs open-sources AgentEnv for building RL environments

Scale Labs has released AgentEnv, an Apache-2.0 framework for building the environments agents are trained and evaluated in. It treats an environment as a composition of versioned blocks — an app like Slack or Gmail, an artifact of data to populate it, and rules such as the clock and triggers — and ships 48 built-in step types.

The design choice that matters is interoperability: environments advertise their tools through an Environment Card over MCP, REST or a generated CLI, and agents advertise themselves through an A2A Agent Card, so the same task runs unchanged on Claude Code, OpenAI's agents or a custom harness. Sandboxes are pluggable — local Docker, Modal containers, Modal and E2B VMs — and AWS and Google Cloud support is configured through a single config.toml. Tasks are DAGs of steps, from deploying an environment to grading a trajectory, with custom steps available as plugins.

Source: Scale Labs, 5 November 2026

Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights land, and the best downloadable model changes hands

Moonshot AI published Kimi K3.1 Pro weights on Hugging Face under a custom licence, after promising them by 4 November. The model is a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts with 104 billion active parameters and a 1,048,576-token context, shipping at roughly 1.56TB. It scores 46 on Artificial Analysis, the best result by a downloadable model and 13 points above DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017's 33.

Moonshot's own model card compares it with Claude Opus 5.5: Terminal-Bench 4.0 65.7 against 66.4, GPQA-Diamond 94.2 against 93.9, HLE-Full 51.1 against 59.8, OSWorld V2.1 79.6 against 81.8 — figures from internal benchmarks and custom harnesses that have not been widely reproduced. At $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens it matches Claude Sonnet 5.5's list price. The practical caveat is size: 2.8 trillion parameters makes this a cluster deployment, not a workstation one.

RAG

Tiger Data's PRISM index builds vector search in Postgres in 1 hour 40 minutes, not 42 hours

Tiger Data has released pg_vectorsearch, an open-source PostgreSQL extension for vector similarity search, currently in alpha. Its PRISM index uses partitioning and compression instead of a graph-based HNSW structure; in Tiger Data's benchmark on 100 million vectors it built an index in 1 hour 40 minutes on a 128 GB machine, where HNSW took 42 hours on 512 GB.

PRISM combines hierarchical clustering to narrow the search space, compressed vectors for sequential scanning and RaBitQ quantization to cut distance-calculation cost, duplicating vectors near partition boundaries to protect recall. It works on existing pgvector columns, so an application can add vector search without a second database. The caveats are real: incremental rebalancing is not fully implemented, so frequent data changes may require a rebuild; filtered search combining WHERE with ORDER BY is unsupported until general availability; and it needs PostgreSQL 18 or later with vectors up to 1,968 dimensions. The benchmarks are vendor-run, though measured with an open-source tool against exact search under matching configuration.

Source: InfoQ, 7 November 2026

Vision

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash goes GA with 15-second clips and a 30 per cent price cut

Google made Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generally available on the Gemini API and Google Cloud, raising native clip length from 10 to 15 seconds, adding multi-turn conversational editing and dropping 720p pricing 30 per cent to $0.07 per second. Duration can be set in one-second steps from 3 to 15 seconds, and drafts at 360p or 720p upscale to 1080p or 4K.

The commercially interesting change is iteration cost: a team can swap a product, change lighting or move the camera on the same clip instead of regenerating from scratch. Reference adherence is the other claim — character, product and style images plus motion clips up to three seconds are held across camera angles, which Google pitches at virtual try-on clips, catalogue spins and branded ads. Dialogue and lip-sync improve, with distinct multi-speaker conditioning. Outputs carry a SynthID watermark, Provisioned Throughput is available, and audio references are gated behind allowlisting. Google still acknowledges weak spots in fast motion and complex physics.

Source: Google DeepMind; Google Cloud, 5 November 2026
