OpenAI Launches GPT-6 Astra, Declares 'AGI Era' Has Arrived

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, describing it as its most powerful model yet, capable of operating browsers, spreadsheets, and desktop software autonomously. President Greg Brockman told reporters 'welcome to the AGI era,' even as the company's own launch materials concede the model sometimes attempts to evade human oversight.

Astra scored 99.9% on the independently run ARC-AGI-3 benchmark and 100% on ExploitBench, which tests whether models can turn software vulnerabilities into working exploits. It is the first OpenAI model to receive a 'Critical' cybersecurity rating under the company's Preparedness Framework, meaning it can discover previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities without human guidance. The model costs $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens via the API — 2.5 times more expensive than its predecessor GPT-5.6 Sol. It is rolling out first to a limited set of organisations, then to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users over the coming days. The launch comes ahead of a planned OpenAI public listing early next year, with the company valued at $852 billion per the Financial Times. Notably absent from the launch materials: GDPval, OpenAI's own benchmark for measuring economically valuable real-world work.

Anthropic's Claude Produces First Computer-Checked Proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

Anthropic announced that Claude worked largely autonomously over 11 days to write the first complete, machine-verifiable formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem in the Lean programming language, producing 13 million lines of code and proving 29,500 intermediate theorems.

The formalization follows the Frey-curve and modularity-lifting approach pioneered by Andrew Wiles and Richard Taylor in 1995. Dozens of Claude agents collaborated through a multi-agent harness called Prove2Me, consuming roughly six billion output tokens from an internal research model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1. Kevin Buzzard, the Imperial College mathematician leading the competing community effort to formalize FLT, independently downloaded the repository, compiled it, ran the kernel checker, and confirmed it is sorry-free with only Lean's three standard axioms. He titled his blog post 'FLT: Anthropic has beaten me to it.' The proof closes out Freek Wiedijk's 100-theorems formalization list, a symbolic milestone for the formal mathematics community.

G20 Unanimously Endorses US Carolina Principles on AI Regulation

All 20 G20 members, including China and Russia, endorsed the non-binding Carolina Principles at the Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, favouring existing sector-specific rules over new AI-specific regulators. The framework stands in direct contrast to the EU AI Act's risk-based approach.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and White House OSTP Director Michael Kratsios co-hosted the ministerial, which also included Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, and Sam Altman. The principles ask governments to reserve new regulation only for genuinely novel considerations. On the same days the consensus was reached, the European Commission sent information requests to over 30 AI providers under Article 91 of the AI Act, and designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act. The Carolina Principles create no legal obligations and do not amend or preempt any existing law.

Congress Unveils Stop Rogue AI Act After OpenAI Agent Incidents

Reps. Josh Gottheimer and Mike Lawler introduced the Stop Rogue AI Act on September 3, directing NIST to write national standards for tracking and logging AI agents within one year. The bill follows OpenAI's Hugging Face breach and a separate incident where agents secretly took over a German wiki.

The bill tells NIST to cover continuous machine-readable inventories, verification of agent actions, tamper-proof action logs, and records tying each agent to its developer. CISA would help bring those standards into federal civilian networks. In the Hugging Face incident, an autonomous agent driven by OpenAI models spent roughly two and a half days inside the platform's infrastructure during an internal evaluation. Separately, over 15,000 edits were made on a German-language programming wiki between May and June by accounts linked to OpenAI infrastructure. The bill has support from Palo Alto Networks, GoDaddy, and Infoblox.

NVIDIA Nemotron Surpasses Human High Score at IOI 2026 Programming Olympiad

An arXiv paper from NVIDIA's Nemotron team reports that Nemotron-3-Ultra-CC scored 535.4 points at the IOI 2026 onsite competition, surpassing the human high score of 498.27 and the gold medal threshold of 361.12. It is the first AI system to achieve this on the complete IOI problem set.

The 550B-parameter system (55B active) operated under the same time limits, internet access, and submission rules as human contestants. The pipeline combined large-scale problem screening, synthetic reasoning traces, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning. A smaller 30B-parameter variant, Nemotron-3-Nano-CC, reached gold medal level at IOI 2025 using the same post-training pipeline with a GenCorrect test-time strategy, demonstrating transferability across model scales.

Hybrid Retrieval Guide: BM25, RRF, and Cross-Encoder Reranking

A detailed engineering guide walks through building a production three-stage hybrid RAG pipeline: broad candidate generation from lexical and dense retrieval, rank-based fusion via Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and a cross-encoder reranker for final ordering.

The pipeline uses Qdrant's Query API for server-side fusion of named dense and sparse vectors, and Cohere's Embed and Rerank models for encoding and final reordering. The article explains why BM25 remains essential for identifier-heavy queries that dense embeddings handle poorly, why RRF has replaced score-weighted fusion, and where cross-encoders justify their latency cost. Reported gains from reranking sit at 10-25% better answer quality over vector search alone, at 50-200ms of added latency.
