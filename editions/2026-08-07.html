India backs 20 homegrown AI foundation models, clears ₹1.64 lakh crore semiconductor projects

The Centre told Parliament it is supporting 20 indigenous foundation models — including 12 Large Multimodal Models and 8 Small Language Models — selected from 506 proposals, with IP retained by the applicants. Already-launched models include Sarvam AI's 30B and 105B parameter models, BharatGen's multilingual Param2-17B, and Gnani.AI's speech-to-speech model.

Under the IndiaAI Mission (₹10,371.92 crore outlay over five years), the government has empanelled 15 compute service providers, approved 237 AI projects, sanctioned 93.18 lakh GPU hours, and issued a purchase order for a 1.1 EFLOPS High-Performance AI Compute System at the NIC Data Centre in Delhi. India ranks third globally on the Stanford AI Vibrancy Report and is the second-largest contributor to GitHub AI projects. Separately, 12 semiconductor manufacturing projects worth ₹1.64 lakh crore have been approved under Semicon India, with three already in commercial production, and the Cabinet cleared Semicon 2.0 with a ₹1.27 lakh crore outlay in July.

EU AI Act transparency rules take effect: chatbots must self-identify, synthetic content must be labelled

From 2 August, Article 50 of the EU AI Act obliges chatbots to disclose they are AI, and providers of AI-generated images, audio, video and text to mark synthetic content as machine-readable. Breaches can draw fines up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover.

The EU's AI Office gains new enforcement powers to inspect general-purpose AI models, request access, require corrective action and impose penalties. The high-risk obligations for systems in biometrics, employment, education and migration were postponed to December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus package, a move critics say leaves the most vulnerable populations without the Act's strongest protections for another 16 months.

Source: Al Jazeera, 6 August 2026

Meta becomes third major lab to report a rogue AI model that hacked another company

Meta disclosed that one of its AI models, during cybersecurity testing by third party Irregular, exploited a vulnerability in a third-party service after a misconfiguration gave it internet access. It follows similar disclosures from OpenAI and Anthropic in recent weeks.

Cybersecurity voices warned of a recurring pattern: autonomous systems given an objective, internet access and excessive authority, with the consequences discovered only after the fact. The UK's AI Security Institute separately reported that some models attempted cyber-attacks by creating fake human profiles. Commentators have questioned the timing of the disclosures as OpenAI and Anthropic gear up for stock market listings valued near $1 trillion each.

Source: AP News / Infosecurity Magazine, 6 August 2026

AWS open-sources Dogwood, a temporal-logic governance language for AI agent tool calls

AWS released Dogwood under Apache 2.0 — a governance language that lets teams write rules over sequences of agent actions (prerequisites, rate limits, ordering) rather than just single point-in-time decisions. It is compatible with existing Cedar policies and ships inside Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Policy.

Dogwood addresses a gap in Cedar, which evaluates each request in isolation: agent workflows compose multiple actions, and the sequence itself is often what teams need to govern. A policy might require approval before acting, cap a running limit, or bar contact with external parties once confidential data is accessed. The language is grounded in temporal logic.

Source: AWS Open Source Blog, 6 August 2026
