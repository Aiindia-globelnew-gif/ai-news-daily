Blackstone Leads $1.2-Billion Bet on Neysa in India's Largest AI Infrastructure Deal

Blackstone-affiliated private equity funds and co-investors have agreed to invest $1.2 billion in Neysa, the AI cloud platform founded by Netmagic's Sharad Sanghi. The deal — one of the largest private capital commitments in India's AI infrastructure sector — is split into $600 million in equity and $600 million in debt.

The equity is led by Blackstone with participation from Teachers' Venture Growth, TVS Capital, 360 ONE Assets and Nexus Venture Partners. The capital is expected to fund the deployment of more than 20,000 GPUs across India. Neysa previously raised $20 million in seed funding in early 2024 — led by Matrix Partners India (now Z47), Nexus and NTTVC — followed by a $30-million Series A in October 2024. The deal underscores how quickly India's AI compute build-out is drawing global institutional capital.

Source: India Business Journal, 5 Oct 2026

MeitY Backs Sarvam AI to Build an Indian-Language Large Language Model

India's Ministry of Electronics and IT (MeitY) has selected Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI to build a large language model rooted in Indian languages and national priorities, with a reported allocation of 4,000 GPUs.

The project is framed as a strategic push to reflect India's linguistic diversity and build domestic capability in frontier AI. Coverage describes it as more than a technical buildout, emphasising a model built to speak "in the voice of a billion Indians." It follows Sarvam's earlier work supplying models for government applications, including the DRONA 2.0 defence-learning system at the Defence Services Staff College.

India Proposes a $25-Billion Deep-Tech Capital Pool for AI, Chips and Robotics

The government has drafted a policy blueprint to create a roughly $25-billion (about ₹2.1 lakh crore) capital pool for domestic deep-tech startups, anchored by an $11-billion commitment through its Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) scheme.

The pool would match public money with private VC and PE capital plus $3–4 billion in institutional co-investments, targeting AI and compute, semiconductors, aerospace and drones, and quantum and robotics across FY27–FY31. Proposed governance mechanisms include independent fund-of-funds management, fast-track regulatory sandboxes and procurement mandates requiring public sector undertakings to reserve a share of budgets for domestic deep-tech. Reported by StartupWire and Channeliam.

Source: StartupWire, 3 Oct 2026

Google Unveils Gemini 4 Argon, Releasing It First to Cyber Defenders

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model it says delivers state-of-the-art results across software engineering, enterprise knowledge work and cybersecurity defence. It is rolling out in phases, starting with trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program.

Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, first place at 51.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench, 91.7% on LVBench and a tie for first at 68% on CWE-bench v1, with the output token limit raised from 64K to 1M. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4/$20 after the introductory period. Analysts told CNBC the model makes Google competitive again at the frontier, though its real test will be wide enterprise deployment. Google says it is engaging with the US government's voluntary pre-release process.

MLPerf Adds Its First LLM Post-Training Benchmark

MLCommons is introducing an LLM post-training benchmark with its MLPerf Training v6.1 round, adding an agentic reinforcement-learning workload to its existing pre-training suite.

The benchmark measures how quickly systems can teach a 397-billion-parameter model to repair real software projects, using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards and Group Relative Policy Optimization inside a sandboxed environment. The task force says the choice of agent harness strongly influenced solve rate, and that it uses hidden test files to prevent reward hacking. The reference run uses 256 Blackwell Ultra GPUs and takes 140–160 minutes per run.

New Recipe Trains "Looped" Language Models on 25× Fewer Tokens

Researchers published practical training recipes for looped language models — models that reuse a shared block of layers to increase effective depth — cutting the training budget from 7.7 trillion tokens to 310 billion while retaining strong reasoning.

Their 1.4B LoopLM outperformed a parameter-matched dense model on all 12 evaluated benchmarks, including +14 points on GSM8K, +10 on MATH and +22 on DROP. The team also introduces a minimal method to convert pretrained dense models into looped ones using a single learned input-mixing scalar and a smoothed exit loss, applied to Qwen3-1.7B-Base with gains across two data regimes. The work isolates the benefit of recurrence itself.

Source: arXiv (2610.00673), 30 Sep 2026

White House Orders Agencies to Say "Super Intelligence", Not "AI"

A new US executive order directs executive-branch agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in official correspondence, websites, reports and policy documents.

Executive Order 14434, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," says the terminology should reflect the "transformative capabilities" of frontier systems, and instructs the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology to propose legislative language for a federal definition within 60 days. It does not require altering previously issued regulations or contracts, and states that it creates no new legal rights or obligations.

Aleph Alpha Releases Kolibri, a 78B Open-Weight English-German MoE

Germany's Aleph Alpha released Kolibri, an open-weight mixture-of-experts model for German and English with 78.1 billion total parameters that activates only 3.46 billion (4.4%) per token.

Kolibri accepts up to 1,048,576 tokens of context, lets users set reasoning effort per request, and ships under the Apache 2.0 license on Hugging Face. The FP8 checkpoint is about 78GB and can run on a single B200, B300 or H200, or on two H100 GPUs, served through vLLM. The target is sovereign deployment in regulated sectors such as public administration, industry and aerospace.

Source: MarkTechPost, 4 Oct 2026

PyTorch's TLX Kernel Beats FlashAttention-4 on Blackwell for Ads Workloads

PyTorch engineers showed that a Jagged Flash Attention kernel built with Triton Low-level Extensions (TLX) outperforms state-of-the-art FlashAttention-4 on the jagged shapes behind Meta's Generative Ads Model, running on NVIDIA Blackwell.

The TLX kernel is about 3.2K lines of Triton-level code — roughly three times less than FA4's ~10K-line CuteDSL kernels — yet is about 13% faster on the forward pass and 50% faster on the backward pass for production jagged shapes. By staying in Triton's high-level model, the kernel remains approachable for modelling engineers, not just kernel specialists. Code is available in Meta's ads model kernel library.

Source: PyTorch Blog, 1 Oct 2026

RAG

Inside a Production RAG Retrieval System Scaled to 500 Million Vectors

An engineering write-up details how an enterprise RAG product, Atlas, evolved its retrieval layer to handle an estimated 500 million vectors — roughly 1.5 TB of raw vector data — without collapsing under continuous ingestion.

The team split the corpus into shards behind a router, separated a fast, mutable write path from slower, background-optimised index segments, and made routing tenant-aware so queries search only the shards that matter. An aggregator collects candidates from each shard and builds a global result set before reranking and access-control validation. The design also adds a write-ahead log, replicas and backpressure for reliability.

Source: DEV Community, 4 Oct 2026

Vision

PixelUMM Unifies Image and Video Understanding and Generation Without an Encoder

Researchers presented PixelUMM, an encoder-free model that handles both image and video understanding and generation directly in pixel space, using a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture.

PixelUMM represents images as spatial patches and videos as spatiotemporal tubelets, connecting raw pixels to a shared backbone through single-layer linear projections. It learns autoregressive text prediction and pixel-space flow matching jointly, and reports competitive performance across image and video understanding and generation tasks. The paper also studies design choices such as decoder structure and spatial-temporal patch size for future pixel-space unified models.

Source: arXiv (2609.38597), 29 Sep 2026
