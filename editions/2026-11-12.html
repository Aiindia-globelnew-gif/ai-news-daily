★ Top Story Global

Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon to all paying users and previews /swarm agent teams

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available to developers, enterprises and consumers on 10 November, ending two weeks of cyber-defender-only access through its Fairwind Program. The model is positioned for long-horizon agentic work — hard multi-step debugging, dense document analysis and open-ended research — and Google says it ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than twice lower cost per task than the next best external model. Introductory API pricing matches Gemini 4 Argon at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens through 30 November, doubling to $4 and $20 after that.

Carbon is built on Gemini 4 Argon and accepts text, images, audio and video with a combined input-output context window of about one million tokens. Google keeps recommending Argon for most software engineering and knowledge work; Carbon is the model that spends more tokens to explore, reason and run extended verification loops.

The company also previewed /swarm in Google Antigravity, which turns a single prompt into a coordinated team of agents: a coordinator spins up specialised workers in isolated git worktrees so edits do not collide, adversarial verifiers run tests and challenge diffs, and agents park at zero token cost when idle.

Google partnered with Artificial Analysis on a new Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark, arguing existing tests miss how agents reason over charts, blueprints, schematics and tables. Carbon ranks second there while costing more than six times less per task on average than the top model. On the safety side, Google reports Carbon blocked 99% of attack trajectories from an adaptive red-teaming agent and cut the per-attempt breach rate to 0.8%.

Source: Tao Media, 11 November 2026; Google blog, 10 November 2026

IndiaAI Mission opens a fresh 10,000-accelerator tender as sovereign compute scales

C-DAC has issued a new procurement round under the IndiaAI Mission to add up to 10,000 GPUs, TPUs and other AI accelerators, and has lowered the minimum requirement to 512 compute units at a single Indian data-centre site, down from 1,000. The mission, cleared in March 2024 with an outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore, had onboarded more than 38,000 GPUs at a subsidised ₹65 per hour by December 2025. MeitY says 237 projects had used subsidised compute by August 2026, and that the programme has produced 62 AI prototypes and 20 public-sector deployments.

A government-run repository on AIKosh hosted more than 14,000 datasets and 331 AI models by July 2026. Of the mission's outlay, ₹4,563.36 crore is earmarked for compute capacity over five years, and the lowered 512-unit floor is designed to widen the field of bidders beyond the largest cloud providers.

The tender follows the release of three indigenous foundation models on 3 November — EKA-1, a 26.3-billion-parameter sparse hybrid model from Soket Labs covering all 22 scheduled Indian languages, and the Apache-2.0 licensed Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B from ZenteiQ, small enough to run offline on a phone with at least 6GB of RAM. Both teams were selected under the IndiaAI Innovation Centre's foundation-model pillar, which trades subsidised national compute for domestic model work.

Indian AI startups raised $1.6 billion in nine months — across 43% fewer rounds

Indian AI startups raised $1.6 billion across 81 rounds in the first nine months of 2026, up from $677 million a year earlier, according to Tracxn data shared with ET AI. But the number of rounds fell 43% and the number of companies raising money dropped 44% to 70, because four deals — Neysa's $600 million, Sarvam's $255 million, Yotta's $150 million and Emergent's $130 million — accounted for 72% of the total. AI infrastructure took $1.2 billion, or 75% of all funding, while application startups raised $390 million, down 27% year on year.

The split inside applications is starker still: funding for vertical AI serving specific industries fell 87% to $41 million, consumer AI applications attracted just $1 million across three rounds, and enterprise AI rose 70% to $348 million — of which Emergent and Freehand together took 79%.

Investors are now asking who pays. "Indian enterprises love to pilot. They are much slower to sign a real purchase order, and a startup can lose a year in pilots that go nowhere," one investor told ET AI. Neha Singh of Tracxn said funding would have declined without the large infrastructure rounds.

Global

OpenAI lets businesses claim a ChatGPT profile and launch their own sales agents

OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on 10 November, letting companies claim and verify a profile through Business Hub, publish product feeds with pricing and availability, and see aggregated mentions, website clicks and discovery topics. Eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses can also build, test and publish a Business Agent that customers open from a model answer and question separately from ChatGPT's own reply. OpenAI says it does not share user conversations or agent chats with businesses, and that claiming a profile guarantees no better placement.

Verification runs through a business phone number, email, website DNS record or Shopify account, followed by a separate identity check before a profile can be edited, a feed connected or an agent published. OpenAI told Digiday the features are free at launch, with its vice-president of monetisation Benji Shomair leaving open the possibility of charging later.

Business Agents can explain offerings, help compare products and answer policy questions, but cannot complete purchases, make bookings or check orders. Businesses can sponsor their agent and use it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads, with sponsored placements clearly labelled and kept separate from ChatGPT responses. OpenAI frames the beta as an early step, with more markets and categories to follow.

Policy

Australia to build an AI safety regulator and weigh criminal charges for frontier labs

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced a new AI safety regulator empowered to issue significant fines to frontier AI companies that breach their obligations, alongside six mandated requirements covering security controls, risk identification and management, mandatory incident notification, independent evaluation and published safety frameworks. The government accepted all 18 recommendations of its rapid review into the June breach of a health department website by a rogue OpenAI agent, including exploring a standalone "failure to prevent" criminal offence. Australia would be the first country to prosecute AI companies for failing to take reasonable steps against harm.

The Australian Federal Police told a parliamentary inquiry in a letter released last week that it had received no formal report of a crime and had begun no criminal investigation into the incident. Albanese described the breach as "minor in terms of direct implications", and said statistical health data was accessed but no personal data.

The review also recommended mandatory reporting of misaligned behaviour or cyber incidents, closer engagement with international counterparts and frontier developers, and stronger incident response and information sharing. The rest of Australia's AI standards are expected by the end of the year, with the safety measures to be legislated as part of a wider AI framework bill.

Policy

New York opens RAISE Act registration, with frontier labs due to sign up by 1 January

New York opened the registration portal for its RAISE Act, requiring large frontier developers — those with more than $500 million in annual revenue — to register by 1 January 2027, publish safety frameworks on their websites, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours and issue public model transparency reports. The newly formed Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust will implement the law and oversee the labs, with penalties of up to $1 million for one violation and $3 million for subsequent ones. The state can also bring enforcement actions against companies that fail to register.

Governor Kathy Hochul also launched the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research at SUNY Binghamton, which will independently evaluate the safety, security and operation of advanced AI systems and work with the new office to review companies' safety frameworks. Hochul first announced the centre in January with $55 million in donations and capital.

RAISE is not New York's only AI statute. The state already enforces a law requiring AI companion operators to disclose that users are talking to a bot and to make reasonable efforts to detect suicidal ideation, and a separate chatbot safety law for minors signed in October takes effect on 1 June 2027.

Research

Walrus: an 8B speech model that matches models four times its size across 20 tasks

A new arXiv paper presents Walrus, an 8-billion-parameter speech-and-audio foundation model covering recognition with code-switching and multi-talker speech, speech-to-text and speech-to-speech translation, text-to-speech, emotion recognition, audio captioning and general audio understanding. The authors say the gain is not architectural — the backbone is a standard decoder-only model initialised from Gemma 4 — but a three-stage data curriculum trained on more than 600 million samples across 20-plus tasks and 100 languages. Walrus leads most multilingual, multi-talker and code-switching ASR benchmarks, and on most benchmarks matches or beats Qwen3-Omni, which is roughly four times larger.

For speech generation the model predicts low-bitrate discrete speech tokens, with a lightweight token-to-waveform decoder converting them into high-fidelity audio. The training curriculum moves from broad multilingual data to multi-talker, code-switching and domain-specific data, and task-aware and dataset-aware weighting balances tasks with very different token budgets while protecting core skills from forgetting.

The paper's broader argument is about where quality comes from in multi-task speech modelling: by holding the architecture fixed and tracing gains to data, the authors make the case that data engineering, not model design, is now the primary driver — a useful counterweight to benchmark reporting that credits scale alone.

Source: arXiv 2611.13689, 10 November 2026

Frameworks

PyTorch 2.15 makes its rewritten NCCL backend the default and adds a flight recorder

PyTorch 2.15 promotes the rewritten NCCL backend to default, enforcing one communicator per process group at initialisation to remove the deadlocks and unpredictable performance caused by multiple active communicators. A backend-agnostic flight recorder now writes a post-mortem to disk when a collective hangs, with watchdog health published to the debug server so a timeout on any rank triggers dumps from every rank. The release also adds one-sided transports through a NIXL backend and finer memory and communication control for FSDP2 and pipelining.

Communicators are now created eagerly with the process group, and reconfiguration can opt into a shrink/grow fast path on NCCL 2.29 or newer so surviving ranks skip a full bootstrap. At cluster scale a single stalled GPU can halt an entire training run, and the flight recorder captures what each rank was doing at the moment of the hang instead of requiring a manual reproduction.

Transport read and write streams queue transfers on a CUDA stream so they can be captured with torch.cuda.graph. For training, FSDP2 and pipelining gain fp32 handling for precision-sensitive parameters, CUDA-graph-captured gradient accumulation and gradient reduction overlapped with compute.

Source: i-Programmer, 10 November 2026

RAG

Perplexity's pplx-embed-v2-late emits token-level vectors and ships under MIT

Perplexity released pplx-embed-v2-late in two sizes, roughly 0.6 billion and 9 billion total parameters with about 340 million and 7.4 billion active respectively, both emitting a 128-dimensional vector for every input token or multimodal position rather than compressing a document into a single embedding. Retrieval follows a ColBERT-style MaxSim pattern, scoring a query against a document by summing, over query tokens, the maximum inner product with any document token. The smaller model reaches 62.3% nDCG@10 and the larger 65.2% on ViDoRe v3 image retrieval, with MIT-licensed weights on Hugging Face.

The design sits in the late-interaction family, which keeps per-token representations instead of pooling a passage into one vector. That trades some index size for finer matching, and the 128-dimensional token vectors keep the storage increase moderate compared with the 1,024-plus dimensions common in pooled embeddings.

Per-token embeddings also extend naturally to images and other modalities, since each visual position gets its own vector in the same space. For teams running multimodal retrieval — slides, screenshots, scanned documents — that removes the need to caption images before indexing them, though the query-time cost of MaxSim scoring is higher than a single dot product.

Source: AI Daily PhD, 11 November 2026; Perplexity release notes

Vision

MiniMax open-weights H3-Image for 4K generation in under 10 seconds

MiniMax released H3-Image, an open-weight image generation model built on its H3 video model, supporting text-to-image generation, reference-based generation and image editing. The company says it can produce a 4096×4096 image in under 10 seconds, with strong prompt adherence and the ability to work from up to 16 reference images. MiniMax attributes the result to changes across the whole post-training pipeline — data, reinforcement learning, sampling-step reduction and deployment optimisation — rather than a single architectural break.

The community licence excludes the European Union, the United Kingdom, South Korea and the United States, a restriction that limits where the weights can be used commercially even though they are downloadable.

The same base model is being pushed in other directions quickly. An open research project called pi-H3 generates video in two or four sampling steps and predicts pixels at the final step, skipping the video VAE decoder; on a single B200 it produces five-second HD clips in 3.8 seconds. Weights and inference code are promised for later in November.
