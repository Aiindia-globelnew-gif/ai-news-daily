★ Top Story Global

Anthropic's Amodei calls on AI labs to 'pace the frontier' as Altman and Musk agree

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a roughly 3,800-word essay urging AI companies to deliberately slow the pace of capability gains, warning that a more capable version of the autonomous-agent swarm that hacked Hugging Face could “take over the entire internet” within 6–12 months. Within hours, OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly agreed, with Altman pledging OpenAI would grant independent evaluators the same employee-level access Anthropic had committed to.

Amodei's three-part plan asks frontier labs to embed independent, employee-level safety evaluators with the right to publish findings; asks democratic-country labs to coordinate on common safety standards (and Washington for a narrow antitrust waiver so rivals can talk); and seeks international coordination, including with China, on measures like a ban on AI for biological weapons. The call comes days after researcher Jacob Coxon quit Anthropic accusing the firm and OpenAI of “gambling with our lives,” and safety lead Evan Hubinger wrote he believes AI could kill all humans at greater than 10% probability within a decade. Amodei stressed this is not a halt — he wants progress to remain “very fast” rather than “extremely fast” — but said even one or two years of bought time could meaningfully improve alignment work. The Trump administration's light-touch stance means any binding US pause would likely need congressional action.

India

Voice AI startup Navana.ai raises ₹40 crore led by Ronnie Screwvala

Voice AI startup Navana.ai raised ₹40 crore in a Series A led by upGrad co-founder Ronnie Screwvala, with Antler India and others joining. The Bengaluru startup's Bodhi speech model covers 12 Indian languages and 45 dialects, and it plans to expand deployments among BFSI firms and develop Indic speech models further.

Navana.ai offers on-premise deployments so enterprises retain control of sensitive customer data, and says it has already processed over 100 million minutes of voice interactions for clients including Bajaj Finserv, Ujjivan Small Finance Bank and Grihum Housing Finance. The company open-sourced the RESPIN speech corpus with IISc Bengaluru and has collaborated with IIT Madras and Microsoft Research. Screwvala said India's linguistic diversity creates problems global models weren't built for, making voice AI “a critical part of India's digital infrastructure.”

India

Karnataka approves ₹35 crore AI Hub at IIIT-Bangalore

The Karnataka cabinet approved a ₹35 crore AI Hub at IIIT-Bangalore, combining skilling, startup support and applied research as the foundation for a proposed state AI University. The hub will offer industry-aligned certifications, an incubation track and an “AI Living Lab” with compute and real-world testbeds.

IT/BT Minister Priyank Kharge called the hub “the foundation” for the planned Karnataka Artificial Intelligence University, and Deputy CM G Parameshwara said the allocation would eventually move to the university once established. The Living Lab is designed so researchers, startups, industry and government can co-develop and validate applications with shared compute infrastructure rather than the state having to build an institution from scratch.

Bodhan AI and AI4Bharat release open-weight Indic foundational models

Bodhan AI, an IIT Madras incubatee, released open-weight foundational models for Indic languages alongside AI4Bharat, covering speech recognition, text-to-speech, machine translation and OCR. The models were trained using NVIDIA's NeMo framework so developers can deploy, adapt and fine-tune multilingual AI.

The four models — Indic-Transcribe, Indic-Speak, Indic-Translate and Indic-OCR — target capabilities where general-purpose global models often fall short for Indian languages. Bodhan AI principal investigator Mitesh Khapra said the goal was to give India's developers “the best open models they can deploy, adapt and fine-tune,” and the NVIDIA partnership was meant to bring state-of-the-art capability to the ecosystem faster.

Research

NVIDIA Nemotron pipeline scores IMO 2026 gold with natural-language proofs

NVIDIA researchers showed an agentic pipeline built on Nemotron 3 Ultra that scored 30/42 at the 2026 International Mathematical Olympiad, clearing the gold-medal threshold using only natural-language proofs — no formal prover or external tools. The team released the full stack: two specialist checkpoints, training data and code, plus a new 200-problem benchmark.

Published on arXiv (2609.10712) on 9 September, the system uses an iterative generate-verify-refine loop over the general-availability Nemotron 3 Ultra model. The authors — Ivan Moshkov, Stephen Ge and colleagues — released Nemotron-IMO-Bench, a set of 200 novel olympiad problems designed to address contamination in reused competition benchmarks. The openness contrasts with competition entries that disclose only final scores, and gives outside teams a reproducible template for domain-specialist agents.

Research

Apple introduces SimpleDesign for joint protein sequence and structure generation

Apple researchers introduced SimpleDesign, an AI model that jointly generates protein amino-acid sequences and 3D structures in a single end-to-end process rather than separate stages. Trained on over 2 million sequence–structure pairs, it handles folding, sequence generation and co-design from partly corrupted inputs.

SimpleDesign uses a multimodal Transformer with separate streams for sequences and coordinates that share attention, trained with cross-entropy on masked tokens plus a regression objective on noisy coordinates. The paper reports competitive results across co-design, structure-generation and sequence-generation benchmarks, but the generated proteins have not been experimentally tested in a lab — so it is a model and benchmark result, not a drug-discovery product. It follows Apple's earlier SimpleFold work on protein-structure prediction.

Policy

US Senate weighs 'duty of care' AI safety bill with power to block model releases

US Senate negotiators — Majority Leader John Thune, Commerce Chair Ted Cruz and Senator Amy Klobuchar — are weighing legislation that would impose a “duty of care” on developers of the most powerful AI models, with a federal power to block unsafe releases and an appeal route through federal courts. A clause would also preempt some state AI laws.

Reuters reported the bill is still under negotiation with no published text, and the exact structure of the government's blocking power remains in dispute. The measure is the most serious bipartisan AI safety push in years and arrives after researcher Jacob Coxon's resignation and a wave of safety departures. Cruz, who last year tried to bar states from regulating AI entirely, is now co-sponsoring a safety bill — though critics note it would likely wipe out state-level rules like California's in favour of a single federal standard. The Senate has only three voting weeks left before midterms.

Frameworks

OpenAI ships free Agents API, open-sources the Codex harness

OpenAI launched an Agents API in public beta, giving developers the same harness that powers Codex — managing context, orchestrating subagents, calling tools and keeping long-running sessions alive across multiple context windows. The API supports MCP, custom functions and built-in tools like web search, and charges only for tokens and tools used, not for orchestration.

The harness is open-source, so developers can inspect the logic that coordinates model calls and tools. Recent additions include “tool search” that loads relevant tool definitions on demand to cut token usage, and programmatic tool calling for parallel and chained operations. The release signals OpenAI's belief that value has migrated from orchestration software to inference compute — a demand signal more than an earnings event, as agentic workloads are projected to dominate token consumption.

AgentJIT open-sources a just-in-time compiler for AI agent tool chains

A developer open-sourced AgentJIT, a just-in-time trajectory compiler that traces an AI agent's dynamic tool chains on a first run and compiles them into deterministic Python AST pipelines — reportedly cutting a multi-step LLM chain from roughly 37ms to 0.1ms with zero runtime token cost. It inserts runtime input guards that bail out to the original LLM agent when inputs deviate.

AgentJIT borrows ideas from V8's tiered compilation and PyTorch's torch.compile: trace, profile hot paths, and synthesise hard-wired Python calls. The author's benchmark claims a 356x speedup and 100% determinism over 100 iterations, and the library is self-contained with no mandatory external dependencies. It targets Python 3.13 and 3.14 including free-threaded no-GIL builds, though the headline numbers are author-reported and not independently reproduced.

RAG

Pinterest details cost-cutting overhaul of its billion-scale embedding retrieval

Pinterest Engineering detailed the evolution of Manas, its in-house embedding retrieval platform serving billions of embeddings across 80+ clusters for Home Feed, Search, Ads and more. To control cost as the corpus heads toward tens of billions, the team rolled out quantization, SSD-based serving and multi-embedding (ColBERT-style late-interaction) retrieval.

Quantization has been deployed across major use cases, cutting embedding-index memory by over 50% and serving cost by 20–30%; SSD-based serving shows 10x lower memory and 40% CPU savings versus in-memory HNSW in early tests. Pinterest is also moving beyond the two-tower single-vector paradigm toward late-interaction models like ColBERT, and is tracking SPANN/SPFresh and GPU-based retrieval as the next frontier.

Vision

OpenAI launches GPT Image 2.5 with Flare and Sunburst dual models and 4K output

OpenAI released GPT Image 2.5, splitting image generation into two models — Flare for speed-first workflows and Sunburst for precision editing — alongside a Sketch tool, comment-based editing and up to 4K output. Both share identical token pricing and are live across the API and all ChatGPT tiers; Adobe integrated both into Firefly with C2PA and SynthID provenance.

Flare delivers 2–4x faster throughput than its predecessor for batch and e-commerce use, while Sunburst targets campaign creative and multi-round refinement. The Sketch tool (@Sketch in ChatGPT) turns rough drawings into finished images, and Templates cover posters, merchandise and product photos. Pricing is $5 per 1M text input, $8 per 1M image input and $30 per 1M image output for both models.

Vision

Kuaishou's Kling 3.0 brings native 4K 60fps video and an 'AI Director' mode

Kuaishou launched Kling AI 3.0, a cinematic video generation platform built on a unified Multimodal Visual Language architecture that delivers native 4K at 60fps. A “Multi-Shot AI Director” can string up to six connected shots with consistent characters, Motion Control AI handles camera paths and subject choreography, and native lip sync covers five languages.

The lip-sync languages include English (with American, British and Indian accents), Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Spanish using frame-accurate phoneme mapping. Motion Control AI adds a Curve Dolly for 3D camera paths drawn on a 2D projection and Multi-Elements for assigning independent movement to two to four subjects. Kling 3.0 is available via kling.ai and the Kling API, and the Diffusion Transformer plus 3D-VAE architecture aims for temporal stability and realistic physics on complex motion.

Tools

MCP connectors turn AI assistants into marketing execution layers

A wave of marketing platforms launched Model Context Protocol connectors that let marketers act from inside AI assistants rather than exporting data. Constant Contact's Claude connector drafts email and social campaigns in conversation; Leadpages' MCP builds and publishes landing pages from a prompt in Claude or ChatGPT; and Catchr pipes live cross-channel marketing data into assistants for plain-language querying.

Constant Contact's connector generates channel-specific subject lines, captions and CTAs and pushes finished drafts back to its platform; Leadpages reads brand from an existing site and publishes to a custom domain; Catchr normalises metrics across 25+ sources including Google Ads, Meta, GA4 and Shopify so a single prompt can ask “why did CPA rise last week?” The pattern reflects MCP becoming the de-facto way AI assistants reach external tools — the assistant stops being a place you write and becomes a place you query.
